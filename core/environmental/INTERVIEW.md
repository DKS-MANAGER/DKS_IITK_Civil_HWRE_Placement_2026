## A. Basic Concept Questions

1. **What is BOD and how is it measured?**
   - Biochemical Oxygen Demand: oxygen consumed by microorganisms to decompose organic matter. Measured as BOD₅ (5 days at 20°C).

2. **What is the difference between BOD and COD?**
   - BOD: biodegradable organic matter (biological). COD: total oxidizable matter (chemical, dichromate). COD > BOD.

3. **What is the DO sag curve?**
   - Streeter-Phelps: the oxygen deficit curve downstream of a pollution source, showing deoxygenation ($k_1$) vs reaeration ($k_2$).

4. **What is the activated sludge process?**
   - Biological secondary treatment where microorganisms (MLSS) in an aeration tank consume organic matter, then settle in a clarifier with sludge recycle.

5. **What is breakpoint chlorination?**
   - Adding chlorine until free residual appears — beyond the point where combined residuals (chloramines) are destroyed.

---

## B. WHY Questions

1. **Why is BOD measured over 5 days at 20°C?**
   - Standardized to allow comparison; 20°C is typical ambient, 5 days captures the majority of carbonaceous oxidation.

2. **Why is nitrification oxygen-intensive?**
   - Nitrification requires 4.57 mg O₂ per mg NH₄-N oxidized — a significant oxygen demand in receiving waters.

3. **Why is primary treatment only 25–40% BOD removal?**
   - Primary (sedimentation) removes settleable solids; dissolved organic matter requires biological (secondary) treatment.

4. **Why is sludge digested anaerobically?**
   - Stabilizes sludge, reduces pathogens, produces biogas (60–65% CH₄) for energy recovery.

---

## C. WHAT-IF Questions

1. **What if the F/M ratio is too high?**
   - Poor settling (bulking), incomplete BOD removal. Increase tank volume or reduce loading.

2. **What if DO in the aeration tank is too low?**
   - Aerobic bacteria die, treatment efficiency drops, filamentous growth. Increase aeration.

3. **What if chlorine demand exceeds applied dose?**
   - No free residual — disinfection incomplete. Increase dose past the breakpoint.

## High-Value Interview Answers

### High-Value Q1: "Describe the water treatment train."

**30-second answer:**
"Aeration → coagulation (alum) → flocculation → sedimentation → filtration (rapid sand) → disinfection (chlorination) → storage and distribution. Each stage removes progressively finer contaminants: aeration removes gases, coagulation-flocculation aggregates colloids, sedimentation settles them, filtration removes remaining particles, and chlorination kills pathogens."

### High-Value Q2: "Explain the activated sludge process."

**30-second answer:**
"Influent wastewater enters an aeration tank where microorganisms (MLSS) consume organic matter in the presence of oxygen. The mixed liquor flows to a secondary clarifier where sludge settles. Part of the settled sludge is recycled to maintain the biomass concentration; the excess is wasted. The F/M ratio and SRT control the process performance."
