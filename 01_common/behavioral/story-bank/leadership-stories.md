## 📝 Story 1: Leading a Technical Project

> **Framing:** "In my M.Tech thesis project at IIT Kanpur, our 4-member team had to complete a CFD simulation of bridge pier scour within 8 weeks — geometry, meshing, solver setup, validation, and documentation."

**Situation:**
During my M.Tech thesis at IIT Kanpur, our 4-member team was tasked with simulating 2D pipeline/bridge pier scour using OpenFOAM. The scope included geometry creation, mesh generation, solver configuration, validation against experimental data, and final documentation. The deadline was non-negotiable — 8 weeks.

**Task:**
I was assigned team lead. My responsibility was to coordinate mesh generation, solver setup, and result validation across the team while ensuring we met our weekly milestones.

**Action:**
1. **Set a clear vision:** I told the team, "We deliver a validated, publication-ready simulation 1 week before the deadline."
2. **Assessed strengths:** Mapped each member's skills — one strong in geometry, one in meshing, one in post-processing, one in literature review.
3. **Structured work:** Created weekly milestones with clear deliverables. Set up a shared Notion workspace with a task board, documentation templates, and validation checklists.
4. **Daily rhythm:** 15-minute standups (what did you do, what's next, blockers). Weekly 1-hour sync for technical deep-dives.
5. **Empowered:** When the meshing member struggled with boundary layer inflation, I pair-programmed the fix rather than taking over.
6. **Celebrated wins:** Acknowledged each milestone completion in the team channel.

**Result:**
We completed 2 weeks ahead of schedule with 95% mesh convergence. Presented at the department seminar with faculty praise for our organization. The project template was adopted by 3 other thesis groups. This taught me that clear vision + daily rhythm + empowerment = high-performing team.

⏱️ **Target time: 90 seconds**

## 📝 Story 2: Mentoring Junior Students

> **Framing:** "Two junior students in our lab were struggling with OpenFOAM case setup for their thesis."

**Situation:**
Two junior students in our lab at IIT Kanpur were struggling with OpenFOAM case setup for their thesis — they had the physics background but lacked computational workflow experience. My guide asked me to help them get their simulations running within 4 weeks.

**Task:**
I needed to take them from "I don't know where to start" to "my simulation is running and producing results" — within 4 weeks, without derailing my own thesis progress.

**Action:**
1. **Assessed gaps:** Spent 1 hour with each understanding their specific blockers — case structure, boundary conditions, meshing workflow.
2. **Created resources:** Built a step-by-step OpenFOAM tutorial document (15 pages) covering case setup, boundary conditions, mesh generation, and common errors.
3. **Hands-on sessions:** Conducted 3 two-hour workshops: (1) Case structure & dictionaries, (2) snappyHexMesh workflow, (3) Post-processing with ParaView.
4. **Set up support:** Created a shared Slack channel for troubleshooting with a 2-hour response SLA.
5. **Gradual release:** Week 1-2: I led. Week 3: They led with me observing. Week 4: Independent with check-ins.

**Result:**
Both students successfully completed simulations within 4 weeks. The tutorial document was adopted by the lab as standard onboarding material. One student later mentored the next batch. This taught me that teaching deepens your own mastery and that the best leaders multiply themselves.

⏱️ **Target time: 90 seconds**

## 📝 Story 3: Influencing Without Authority

> **Framing:** "In a group project, I wasn't the team lead but had to resolve a critical technical disagreement."

**Situation:**
In a group project at IIT Kanpur, two teammates strongly disagreed on the choice of turbulence model — one advocated for k-ε (familiar, robust), the other for k-ω SST (better for adverse pressure gradients). The disagreement stalled our progress for a week. I was a team member, not the lead.

**Task:**
As a non-lead member with CFD experience, I needed to resolve this technical deadlock without alienating either member or overstepping my role.

**Action:**
1. **Researched objectively:** Compared both models for our specific case (adverse pressure gradient around a pier). Created an evidence table with convergence behavior, validation literature, and computational cost.
2. **Proposed data-driven path:** "Let's run both on a simplified 2D case for 2 days and compare against benchmark data."
3. **Facilitated decision:** Presented results without advocacy. The team chose SST k-ω based on evidence.
4. **Documented:** Added model selection rationale to project documentation for future reference.

**Result:**
The team aligned on SST k-ω. Simulation matched experimental data within 8% error. We avoided 2 weeks of wasted effort. The comparison framework became our lab's standard for model selection. This taught me that influence comes from preparation and process, not from position or title.

⏱️ **Target time: 75 seconds**

## 📝 Story 4: Taking Initiative Beyond Role

> **Framing:** "I noticed a gap in our batch's placement preparation and decided to fill it without being asked."

**Situation:**
Our batch of 60 M.Tech students at IIT Kanpur had no structured placement preparation program. Students were preparing in isolation, duplicating effort, and missing key topics. Before company visits, students were stressed and unprepared.

**Task:**
I decided to take initiative and create a structured preparation program without being asked by anyone.

**Action:**
1. **Surveyed needs:** Sent a Google Form to 60 students — identified top gaps: CFD interview prep, aptitude practice, behavioral preparation, company-specific guidance.
2. **Built program:**
   - Weekly 2-hour sessions over 12 weeks
   - Topic experts from the batch led each session
   - Shared resource drive with curated materials
   - Mock interview pairs with feedback forms
   - Company-specific prep tracks (PSU, Core, Analytics)
3. **Executed:** Recruited 8 peer facilitators. Scheduled sessions. Created a shared calendar.
4. **Iterated:** Weekly feedback forms. Adjusted topics based on company visit schedule.
5. **Scaled:** Opened to B.Tech students in the final month.

**Result:**
40+ students attended regularly. 85% reported improved confidence in mock interviews. Multiple students credited the program for their placement success. The program continues annually with new coordinators. This taught me that seeing a gap and filling it — without waiting for permission — creates disproportionate impact.

⏱️ **Target time: 90 seconds**

## 📝 Story 5: Leading Through Crisis

> **Framing:** "Two weeks before our thesis submission, our main CFD simulation started diverging. The team was panicking."

**Situation:**
Two weeks before our thesis submission at IIT Kanpur, our main CFD simulation of bridge pier scour started diverging after 500 time steps, producing unphysical sediment concentrations. The team was panicked — we had no backup and the deadline was immovable.

**Task:**
As team lead, I needed to diagnose and fix the divergence issue while keeping the team calm and on track for submission.

**Action:**
1. **Calmed the team:** "This is normal in CFD. We have 14 days. Let's systematically debug."
2. **Structured debugging:** Created a checklist — boundary conditions, time step, under-relaxation factors, mesh quality, solver settings.
3. **Parallel investigation:** Assigned each check to a team member. I took mesh quality (most likely culprit based on experience).
4. **Root cause found:** y+ values near the pier were 300+ (should be <5 for low-Re SST). The boundary layer mesh was too coarse.
5. **Fixed and validated:** Refined boundary layer mesh (15 layers, growth ratio 1.15). Re-ran. Converged in 3 days.
6. **Documented:** Added mesh sensitivity analysis section to thesis for reproducibility.

**Result:**
Simulation converged, thesis submitted on time with high quality. Team confidence was restored. The debugging checklist became lab standard for all future CFD projects. This taught me that calm structure + parallel work + personal ownership = crisis resolution. Leaders don't panic — they systematize.

⏱️ **Target time: 90 seconds**

## 📝 Story 6: Difficult Decision Under Pressure

> **Framing:** "Mid-project, our supervisor changed the entire research focus. I had to decide whether to resist or adapt — and execute fast."

**Situation:**
Midway through our M.Tech thesis at IIT Kanpur, our supervisor changed the research focus from pipeline scour to bridge pier scour — completely different geometry, boundary conditions, and validation data. We had 6 weeks remaining to deliver results.

**Task:**
I had to make a critical decision: resist the change (risking advisor relationship) or adapt quickly (risking quality due to compressed timeline). Then I had to execute the chosen path.

**Action:**
1. **Rapid assessment:** Listed what's reusable (solver setup, turbulence model, post-processing scripts) vs. what's new (geometry, mesh, validation data).
2. **Decided to adapt:** Created a revised project plan in 4 hours. Reallocated tasks based on reusability analysis.
3. **Communicated clearly:** "We're pivoting. Here's the new plan. Here's what each person owns. Questions?"
4. **Executed fast:** New geometry in 2 days. Literature review on pier scour started immediately. New validation data sourced from lab archives.
5. **Monitored:** Daily stand-ups. Weekly milestone reviews.

**Result:**
Completed the pier scour study on time. The pivot actually led to a stronger conference paper because pier scour has broader engineering relevance. This taught me that decisive adaptation beats resistant perfectionism. Sometimes the best decision is to commit fully to the new path.

⏱️ **Target time: 75 seconds**

## 📝 Story 7: Building a Knowledge-Sharing Culture

> **Framing:** "Our lab had valuable knowledge scattered across individual students. I created a system to consolidate and share it."

**Situation:**
Our CFD lab at IIT Kanpur had valuable but scattered knowledge — students had individual notes, scripts, and workflows that weren't shared. New students took 2 weeks to get up to speed because they had to rediscover everything.

**Task:**
Create a sustainable knowledge-sharing system that would outlast any single student's tenure in the lab.

**Action:**
1. **Audited existing assets:** Surveyed 15 students — identified 50+ scripts, 20+ workflow docs, 10+ validated simulation cases.
2. **Designed system:** GitLab repository with standardized structure (scripts/, cases/, docs/, templates/). README templates. Contribution guidelines.
3. **Seeded content:** Migrated my own validated cases and scripts first. Added issue templates for "New Case" and "Bug Report."
4. **Onboarded:** 30-minute workshop for lab members. Assigned "knowledge champions" per topic area.
5. **Incentivized:** Monthly "Best Contribution" recognition in lab meetings.

**Result:**
40+ contributions in the first month. New students onboarded in 1 day vs. 2 weeks previously. The repository became the lab's single source of truth and is still in use. This taught me that culture change requires three things: structure (the system), seeding (go first), and recognition (celebrate contributions).

⏱️ **Target time: 90 seconds**

## 📝 Story 8: Resetting Team Morale

> **Framing:** "During our final thesis push, team morale was low. Everyone was burning out. I needed to reset the culture without being preachy."

**Situation:**
During the final thesis push at IIT Kanpur, team morale was low. Members were working late but unfocused, distracted, and burning out. Energy was declining, and the quality of work was starting to slip.

**Task:**
As team lead, I needed to reset the team's work culture and energy without being preachy or adding more pressure.

**Action:**
1. **Modeled behavior:** Started "Deep Work Blocks" — 90-minute focused sessions, phone away, single task. Invited others to join rather than mandating.
2. **Structured breaks:** 15-minute break after each block. Group walk/stretch.
3. **Protected time:** "No meetings 9-12 AM and 2-5 PM" rule for the team — protected deep work hours.
4. **Visible progress:** Shared daily "Done" list in team channel — small wins create momentum.
5. **Checked in personally:** "How's your energy? Need to adjust the schedule?"

**Result:**
The team completed the thesis 3 days early with higher quality. Members reported better work-life balance during the final sprint. The "Deep Work" practice spread to other labs in our department. This taught me that culture is caught, not taught — model what you want to see.

⏱️ **Target time: 75 seconds**

## 📝 Story 9: Challenging the Status Quo

> **Framing:** "Our lab had been using outdated software despite better alternatives being available. I proposed the upgrade."

**Situation:**
Our lab at IIT Kanpur had been using a deprecated OpenFOAM version (v1912) for all simulations, despite v2412 being available with significant bug fixes, new solvers, and better performance. Everyone accepted it because "that's how it's always been."

**Task:**
I proposed upgrading the lab's OpenFOAM installation and needed to build a case that would convince the lab head.

**Action:**
1. **Documented benefits:** Created a comparison document — bug fixes relevant to our work, new solver features, performance benchmarks on our typical cases.
2. **Tested compatibility:** Ran 3 existing lab cases on the new version to verify backward compatibility and measure performance improvement.
3. **Created migration guide:** Step-by-step upgrade instructions, common issues and fixes, new features guide.
4. **Presented to lab head:** Showed the data — 30% stability improvement on our cases, 2 new solvers that enabled previously impossible simulations.
5. **Offered to lead:** "I'll handle the upgrade and support the team during transition."

**Result:**
The lab upgraded within 2 weeks. Simulation stability improved by 30%. 3 new case types became possible with the updated solver library. Other labs in the department followed suit. This taught me that challenging the status quo requires evidence, preparation, and offering to own the implementation — not just pointing out problems.

⏱️ **Target time: 75 seconds**

## 📝 Story 10: Quality Assurance Leadership

> **Framing:** "Before submitting our CFD results for publication, I noticed inconsistencies in validation data across team members' sections."

**Situation:**
Before submitting our CFD results for journal publication, I noticed inconsistencies in the validation data across different team members' sections — different mesh independence thresholds, inconsistent y+ reporting, and a missing validation plot.

**Task:**
I needed to ensure reproducibility and accuracy of all reported values before submission, without damaging team morale or delaying the deadline.

**Action:**
1. **Created a validation checklist:** Mesh independence, residual convergence, boundary condition verification, y+ compliance, experimental comparison protocol.
2. **Audited independently:** Re-ran 3 key cases from scratch. Cross-checked all reported values against raw data files.
3. **Found issues:** 2 minor calculation errors in drag coefficient reporting, 1 missing mesh independence plot.
4. **Fixed collaboratively:** Assigned fixes to responsible members with a 24-hour deadline. Verified all corrections personally.
5. **Institutionalized:** Added the validation checklist to the lab's publication workflow as a mandatory step.

**Result:**
The paper passed peer review without any data-related reviewer comments — a rare achievement. The validation checklist is now mandatory for all lab publications. This taught me that systematic quality gates prevent embarrassment and that the best time to catch errors is before they become public.

⏱️ **Target time: 75 seconds**
