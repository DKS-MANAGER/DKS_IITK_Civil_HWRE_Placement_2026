# ⚔️ Follow-Up Attack System — Prepare for the Probing

> **Interviewers don't just ask one question — they drill down with follow-ups. If your answer is shallow, follow-ups will expose it. Prepare for 3 levels of depth.**

---

## How Interviewers Probe

```
Level 1: Surface Question (30 sec answer)
    ↓ "Tell me more about..."
Level 2: Detail Question (60 sec answer)
    ↓ "How specifically did you..."
Level 3: Deep Question (90 sec answer)
    ↓ "What would have happened if..."
```

### The Iceberg Principle
Your answer is the tip of the iceberg. The interviewer can see you're hiding depth — they'll probe until they find it (or confirm you don't have it).

---

## 5 Common Follow-Up Patterns

### Pattern 1: "Tell me more about..." (Elaboration)
**Trigger:** Your answer was too brief or vague
**Defense:** Prepare 3 levels of detail for every story

**Example:**
- Q: "Tell me about a time you led a team."
- A: "I led a 4-member CFD thesis team..."
- Follow-up: "Tell me more about the challenges."
- A: "The main challenge was diverging simulation — y+ was 300+..."
- Follow-up: "How specifically did you fix it?"
- A: "I refined the boundary layer with 15 inflation layers, 1.15 growth ratio..."

### Pattern 2: "How specifically did you..." (Specificity)
**Trigger:** Your answer used generalities ("I worked on it", "We solved it")
**Defense:** Always include specific actions, tools, numbers

**Example:**
- Q: "How did you improve team communication?"
- Bad: "I set up regular meetings."
- Good: "I implemented 15-minute daily stand-ups at 9 AM — each person shared: what they did yesterday, what they're doing today, and any blockers."

### Pattern 3: "What would have happened if..." (Counterfactual)
**Trigger:** They want to test your understanding of consequences
**Defense:** Prepare the "what if I hadn't" angle for each story

**Example:**
- Follow-up: "What would have happened if you hadn't caught the data inconsistency?"
- A: "The presentation would have had conflicting boundary conditions — faculty would have caught it, damaging our credibility and potentially our grade."

### Pattern 4: "Why did you choose this approach?" (Reasoning)
**Trigger:** They want to understand your decision-making process
**Defense:** Prepare the "why" behind every action

**Example:**
- Follow-up: "Why did you choose SST k-ω over k-ε?"
- A: "Three reasons: (1) Better performance in adverse pressure gradients around the pier, (2) More recent validation literature for scour applications, (3) Better near-wall treatment for our y+ < 5 mesh."

### Pattern 5: "How would you apply this to our work?" (Transfer)
**Trigger:** They want to see if you can generalize
**Defense:** Prepare a connection to the company's work for each story

**Example:**
- Follow-up: "How would you apply this debugging approach here?"
- A: "The systematic debugging approach — checklist, parallel investigation, root cause analysis — applies to any complex system. At [Company], I'd use the same methodology for [specific application]."

---

## The 3-Level Defense Preparation

For each of your STAR stories, prepare:

### Level 1: The Hook (30-45 sec)
The main story — situation, task, key action, result.

### Level 2: The Detail (60-90 sec)
Specific steps, tools, numbers, challenges encountered.

### Level 3: The Depth (90-120 sec)
Why each decision was made, alternatives considered, broader impact, lessons learned.

### Practice Method
1. Tell your story in 45 seconds
2. Have a friend ask "Tell me more"
3. Continue for another 45 seconds
4. Have them ask "How specifically?"
5. Continue for another 30 seconds
6. Total should be ~2 minutes max

---

## Red Flags That Invite Follow-Ups

| Red Flag | Follow-Up It Invites | How to Prevent |
|----------|---------------------|----------------|
| "We worked together" | "What specifically did YOU do?" | Use "I" and describe YOUR actions |
| "It went well" | "How do you measure that?" | Quantify results with numbers |
| "I learned a lot" | "What specifically did you learn?" | State the specific lesson |
| "I'm a team player" | "Give me an example" | Lead with the example, not the label |
| "I'm detail-oriented" | "How so?" | Give a specific instance |

---

## Proactive Depth Strategy

Don't wait for follow-ups — volunteer the depth:

**Instead of:** "I led a team project."
**Say:** "I led a 4-member CFD thesis team — I divided tasks by individual strengths, set weekly milestones, and conducted daily 15-minute stand-ups. When we hit a divergence issue, I diagnosed the root cause as y+ = 300+ and fixed it with boundary layer refinement."

This satisfies the curiosity that would otherwise become a follow-up question.
