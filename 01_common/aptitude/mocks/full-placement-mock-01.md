> The architecture of modern distributed computing is increasingly constrained not by raw floating-point arithmetic throughput, but by the physical thermodynamic limits of memory bandwidth and inter-chip interconnect interconnectivity—a phenomenon known in computer architecture as the "memory wall." > Consequently, the industry is undergoing a paradigm shift toward "compute-in-memory" (CIM) and near-memory spatial architectures. However, CIM systems introduce severe non-idealities: analog signal drift, device-to-device conductance variations, and non-linear ADC quantization noise. Thus, the ultimate scalability of next-generation hardware accelerators depends not merely on lithographic scaling, but on algorithmic noise-resilient training frameworks that tolerate underlying physical hardware stochasticity.
