### Q15 (Trap / Multi-Concept: Flaw in Causal Generalization)
**Premise:**  
A municipal survey examined 50 wards in a metropolitan city and observed that wards with the highest density of asphalt road resurfacing contracts also reported the highest rates of surface water stagnation and pothole complaints within 6 months. A local newspaper editorial concluded: *"Asphalt resurfacing directly damages urban road durability; municipal engineers must cease asphalt road paving immediately."*  
**Which of the following illustrates the primary logical flaw in the newspaper's reasoning?**  
- (A) It treats a common symptom of poor underlying drainage as a destructive causal property of the surfacing material itself, ignoring that municipal engineers prioritized asphalt resurfacing in low-lying wards that already suffer chronic seasonal waterlogging.
- (B) It uses emotive language rather than presenting structural pavement deflection measurements via falling weight deflectometer.
- (C) It relies on survey feedback from residents rather than structured interviews with certified asphalt batching plant operators.
- (D) It assumes that 6 months is an adequate time window to assess bituminous pavement fatigue cracking under heavy axle loads.

---

## Master Solutions & Explanations

### Q1 — Solution: (D) Both Assumptions I and II are implicit
- **Analysis:**  
  - For an evacuation advisory to be logically issued by the authority, the authority must assume that the impending event poses genuine risk (Assumption I is implicit; without it, there is no rationale for an emergency advisory).
  - The authority must also assume that the target population has the feasible means/time to comply with the directive before midnight (Assumption II is implicit; an authority does not issue actionable deadlines assuming compliance is universally impossible).
- **Correct Choice:** (D)

---

### Q2 — Solution: (B) Statement II is the cause and Statement I is its effect
- **Analysis:**  
  - Anti-dumping tariffs on imported steel billets and clinker directly restrict low-cost imported raw materials, forcing domestic manufacturers and EPC contractors to purchase domestic or tariff-inflated supplies.
  - This tariff policy (Cause, Statement II) directly leads to the observed 18% surge in procurement costs for infrastructure EPC firms over subsequent quarters (Effect, Statement I).
- **Correct Choice:** (B)

---

### Q3 — Solution: (A) Only Course of Action I logically follows
- **Analysis:**  
  - Course of Action I is targeted, proportionate, and directly tackles prominent sources of winter particulate emissions ($PM_{2.5}$) during emergencies.
  - Course of Action II is disproportionate, extreme, and unfeasible as an immediate mandatory course of action across all corporate/academic sectors indefinitely without regard to indoor air filtration.
- **Correct Choice:** (A)

---

### Q4 — Solution: (C) Both Argument I and Argument II are strong
- **Analysis:**  
  - Argument I is strong because it addresses a major public interest dimension (resource conservation, national carbon commitments, 30% urban consumption footprint).
  - Argument II is also strong because it addresses an established economic reality in infrastructure: a 5–10% increase in upfront capital expenditure directly impacts developer viability and homebuyer affordability in developing markets.
  - Both arguments present valid, non-trivial, evidence-based perspectives from opposing policy priorities.
- **Correct Choice:** (C)

---

### Q5 — Solution: (C) Both Assumptions I and II are implicit
- **Analysis:**  
  - To claim that SCADA telemetry will eliminate non-revenue water (NRW) losses within 18 months, the speaker must assume that NRW is driven by leaks/tapings that sensors can detect (Assumption I). If NRW were purely caused by administrative billing fraud or unmetered public standposts, telemetry alone could not eliminate it.
  - The speaker must also assume operational responsiveness (Assumption II); detection without repair capacity cannot eliminate physical losses.
- **Correct Choice:** (C)

---

### Q6 — Solution: (B) Confounding Factor / Selection Bias
- **Analysis:**  
  - The partner concludes that *BIM software itself* directly caused the reduction in overruns.
  - Option (B) demonstrates a classic confounding variable (selection bias): firms adopting BIM also had superior management budgets and contractor qualification. Thus, the management caliber may be the true driver, weakening the claim of sole BIM causality.
- **Correct Choice:** (B)

  - Argument II is a strong corporate finance argument: abrupt complete divestment destroys the cash engine (85% OCF) that finances renewable transition before commercial parity is established.
  - Argument III is weak because private investment in hybrid wind-solar does not justify complete divestment of refineries into green hydrogen specifically.

### Q9 — Solution: (A) Formal Syllogistic Deduction
- **Analysis:**  
  - Let $H$: Hydrogeologists who conduct pumping test analyses.
  - Let $D$: Understands Darcy's law. Premise 1: $H \subseteq D$.
  - Let $V$: Verifies boundary recharge conditions. Premise 2: If $D \implies V$ ($D \subseteq V$). Thus, $H \subseteq D \subseteq V$.
  - Premise 3: Some Environmental Auditors ($E$) are $\neg V$ ($\exists x \in E \text{ such that } x \notin V$).
  - Since $D \subseteq V$, any entity outside $V$ must also be outside $D$ ($\neg V \implies \neg D$).
  - Therefore, the environmental auditors who are $\neg V$ cannot be in $D$. Hence, "Some environmental auditors do not understand Darcy's law" is a strictly valid deduction.
- **Correct Choice:** (A)

---

### Q10 — Solution: (A) Reverse Causality / Confounding Forecast Variable
- **Analysis:**  
  - The activist falsely assumes desilting caused the flood damage ($A \to B$). In reality, impending extreme rainfall triggers emergency desilting budgets ($C \to A$ and $C \to B$). High rainfall causes both higher spending and higher flooding.
- **Correct Choice:** (A)

---

### Q11 — Solution: (B) Only Assumptions I and II are implicit
- **Analysis:**  
  - Assumption I is necessary: if induced demand immediately overwhelms added capacity (Braess's Paradox / Lewis-Mogridge Law), the 40% reduction cannot materialize.
  - Assumption II is necessary: if the gridlock is caused by downstream bottlenecks, adding an elevated bridge merely transfers the queue faster into the same blockage.
  - Assumption III is NOT implicit: temporary or permanent commercial relocation on ground level does not prevent the elevated lanes from reducing vehicle transit times.
- **Correct Choice:** (B)

---

### Q12 — Solution: (B) Argument Structure & Function
- **Analysis:**  
  - The first boldface sentence states the specific administrative policy proposal that the author evaluates.
  - The second boldface sentence presents concrete, factual empirical evidence (5x tariff and 35% billing impact) supporting the author's final conclusion that the policy is economically sound.
- **Correct Choice:** (B)

---

### Q13 — Solution: (B) Bottleneck Shift in Integrated Supply Chain
- **Analysis:**  
  - Doubling crane lift speed does not reduce ship turnaround if the container yard and evacuation gates are paralyzed. When the yard saturates, cranes must idle, lengthening offshore ship anchorage queues. Option (B) resolves the paradox completely.
- **Correct Choice:** (B)

---

### Q14 — Solution: (B) Modus Tollens in Conditional Logic
- **Analysis:**  
  - Premise 1: $G \implies (F \text{ only if } S) \equiv G \implies (F \implies S)$.
  - Premise 2: $S \implies E$.
  - By contrapositive of Premise 2: $\neg E \implies \neg S$.
  - We are given $\neg E$ is true. Therefore, $\neg S$ is true (sirens did not sound).
  - We are given $F$ is true (floodway was filled).
  - Evaluate $(F \implies S)$: Since $F$ is True and $S$ is False, $(F \implies S)$ is **False**!
  - From Premise 1, $G \implies (F \implies S)$.
  - Since the consequent $(F \implies S)$ is False, by Modus Tollens, the antecedent $G$ must be **False** ($\neg G$).
  - Therefore, the upstream barrage sluice gates were definitively not opened.
- **Correct Choice:** (B)

---

### Q15 — Solution: (A) Spurious Correlation / Omitted Variable Bias
- **Analysis:**  
  - The editorial commits the fallacy of false cause: roads resurfaced most frequently were in flood-prone, low-lying wards that already suffered waterlogging. The waterlogging caused both the resurfacing tenders and the subsequent potholes; the asphalt was not the primary cause of damage.
- **Correct Choice:** (A)
