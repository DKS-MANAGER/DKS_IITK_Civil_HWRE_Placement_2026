### Questions 46–50: Reading Comprehension (Market Microstructure & Order Book Dynamics)

**Passage:**
In modern continuous double-auction equity markets, the limit order book ($LOB$) serves as the fundamental mechanism for price discovery and liquidity provision. Market participants face a structural trade-off: liquidity providers submit passive limit orders that earn the bid-ask spread but bear the severe hazard of *adverse selection*—the risk of being executed against informed traders possessing superior private signals. Conversely, liquidity demanders deploy aggressive market orders that execute instantaneously against existing book quotes, paying the spread premium to guarantee execution immediacy.

The proliferation of high-frequency trading (HFT) firms operating deterministic automated algorithms has fundamentally reshaped this market microstructure. By deploying colocation architectures and ultra-low-latency microwave networks, algorithmic market makers continuously update quotes in response to stochastic order flow shocks. When order book imbalance metrics signal incoming directional momentum, HFT algorithms instantaneously cancel resting passive quotes, avoiding toxic inventory accumulation. 

However, this microstructural agility creates a paradox. While headline bid-ask spreads during benign macroeconomic regimes have compressed to historic minimums, the *depth* of the order book—the aggregate liquidity resting within multiple price tiers—has become fragile and fleeting. In periods of extreme volatility, algorithmic market makers systematically withdraw passive liquidity across the entire book within milliseconds. This sudden evaporation of resting limit orders precipitates severe liquidity black holes and flash crashes, demonstrating that apparent continuous market depth is often an ephemeral illusion generated by algorithmic ghost quotes.
