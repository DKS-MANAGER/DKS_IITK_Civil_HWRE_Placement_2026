## 1. The Executive Phrasing & Sentence Improvement Framework

Sentence Improvement questions evaluate an applicant's ability to edit professional, legal, macroeconomic, and scientific prose for maximum **precision**, **grammatical integrity**, **syntactic parallelism**, and **rhetorical economy**.

In top-tier corporate recruitment (McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Goldman Sachs, WorldQuant, Google PM), testing goes far beyond catching simple grammar blunders. Questions deliberately present options that are grammatically permissible in spoken English but violate the formal standards of executive register through **unnecessary passive voice**, **nominalization**, **faulty comparisons**, **dangling modifiers**, or **subtle shifts in intended meaning**.

### 1.1 The Four Axes of Sentence Improvement

| Evaluation Axis | Description | Operational Placement Heuristic |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **1. Grammatical Validity** | Strict compliance with subject-verb agreement, subjunctive mood, tense sequencing, and pronoun cases. | Eliminate any option with dangling modifiers, ungrammatical subjunctive endings, or faulty case. |
| **2. Syntactic Parallelism** | Coordinate elements in lists, comparisons, and correlative pairs must share identical grammatical forms. | Check 'not only... but also', 'either... or', and serial lists for matching parts of speech. |
| **3. Rhetorical Economy** | Direct active verbs and concise prepositional phrasing must replace bureaucratic nominalizations. | Prefer 'to reduce' over 'due to the fact that it desired the reduction of'. |
| **4. Semantic Fidelity** | The improved phrasing must preserve the original intended meaning without altering conditions or causality. | Beware of options that change 'if' to 'because' or reverse modifier relationships. |

### 1.2 The 4-Step Sentence Correction Algorithm

```text
Step 1: Read the full prompt sentence to grasp the core intended meaning.
Step 2: Inspect the bolded/underlined segment for primary structural defects:
   - Is there a dangling modifier?
   - Is there an illogical comparison (missing 'that of' / 'those of')?
   - Is there a parallel structure violation or bloated nominalization?
Step 3: Scan the options to categorize differences (e.g. 2 options use 'who', 2 use 'whom').
Step 4: Select the most concise, grammatically flawless option that preserves exact semantic meaning.
```

## 5. Rapid Revision & Strategic Traps

### 5.1 The 7 Deadly Traps in Sentence Improvement

| Trap Name | Why Candidates Fall Into It | The Linguistic Reality | Strategic Antidote |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **The Brevity Mirage Trap** | Selecting the shortest option without verifying grammatical completeness | The shortest option often drops a necessary preposition or creates a dangling modifier | Conciseness is a virtue only *after* grammatical validity is satisfied |
| **The Nominalization Camouflage** | Believing complex nouns ('the execution of the implementation') sound more academic | Executive English strictly favors strong, direct active verbs over multi-layered abstract nouns | Unpack noun phrases into direct verbs whenever possible |
| **The Apples-to-Oranges Trap** | Comparing an attribute directly to an entity ('unlike X, Y's revenue...') | Comparisons must be strictly between like grammatical entities | Always insert 'that of' (singular) or 'those of' (plural) when comparing attributes |
| **The Semantic Alteration Trap** | Choosing an option that is grammatically flawless but alters the conditional/causal relationship | Shifting 'if' to 'because' or 'although' changes the factual meaning of the statement | Verify that the chosen option conveys the identical factual proposition of the prompt |
| **The Subjunctive Reluctance** | Adding '-s' to third-person verbs in mandative clauses | Mandative verbs ('require that', 'stipulate that') mandate the bare infinitive ('he be', 'she relinquish') | Train your ear to accept the bare infinitive following mandative trigger verbs |
| **The Prepositional Drift** | Accepting non-standard preposition combinations ('results to', 'capable to', 'independent from') | High-register English strictly enforces fixed idiomatic prepositional regimens | Memorize core collocations: *results in*, *capable of*, *independent of*, *prefer to* |
| **The False Error Trap (Rejecting NO CHANGE)** | Assuming the prompt sentence must have an error | 15% to 20% of elite test questions are completely correct as written | If Option (A) violates zero grammatical or rhetorical rules, select it with confidence |

### 5.2 The 30-Second Sentence Correction Heuristic

1. **Seconds 0-10:** Identify what is being tested in the bolded segment (Subject-verb? Modifier? Comparison? Parallelism?).
2. **Seconds 10-20:** Group the options into 2x2 or 3x2 splits (e.g., two options with 'that of', three without; or two with active verbs, two with passive).
3. **Seconds 20-30:** Eliminate the defective splits. Among the survivors, pick the most concise and idiomatic phrasing.
