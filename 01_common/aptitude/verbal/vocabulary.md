## 1. Etymological Architecture & Precision Diction Framework

Elite corporate recruitment tests do not evaluate vocabulary through rote memorization of dictionary wordlists. Rather, they test **semantic precision**, **etymological roots**, **contextual connotation**, and the ability to avoid deliberate **'False Friend' traps**.

### 1.1 The Six High-Frequency Placement Vocabulary Traps

| Trap Name | The False Assumption | The Linguistic Reality | Operational Example |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **The 'False Friend' Trap** | Assuming a word means what it sounds like | Etymological divergence creates meanings opposite to appearance | *Salutary* != salute (means beneficial); *Noisome* != noise (means stinking); *Restive* != restful (means unruly) |
| **The Confusing Twins Trap** | Treating distinct words as interchangeable synonyms | High-register English strictly enforces legal and functional separation | *Disinterested* (impartial) vs *Uninterested* (bored); *Flout* (defy laws) vs *Flaunt* (show off) |
| **The Connotation Inversion** | Ignoring positive vs pejorative nuance | Words sharing denotation differ sharply in evaluation | *Parsimony* (positive minimalism in science) vs *Stinginess*; *Fatuous* (foolish) vs *Innocent* |
| **The Greek/Latin Root Pivot** | Failing to dissect classical morphemes | Prefix and root combinations unlock precise definitions | *Mal-* (bad) vs *Bene-* (good); *Loqu-* (speak); *Sed-/Assid-* (sit/diligent); *Tenuis* (thin) |
| **The Shift from Archaic to Modern** | Assuming obsolete definitions still apply | Literary words possess highly specific modern philosophical/scientific roles | *Chimera* (in behavioral economics: an impossible theoretical illusion) |
| **The Degree of Intensity Trap** | Choosing words that overstate or understate the prompt's context | Formal prose distinguishes between mild and extreme severity | *Equivocal* (ambiguous) vs *Deceptive*; *Platitudinous* (trite) vs *Erroneous* |

### 1.2 The 30-Second Vocabulary Decryption Protocol

```text
Step 1: Inspect the Context Clues
   - Look for contrast signposts ('Rather than...', 'Despite...', 'Far from...').
   - Identify whether the sentence requires a positive (+), negative (-), or neutral charge.

Step 2: Dissect the Word's Etymology
   - Break down Latin/Greek prefixes, roots, and suffixes.
   - Screen against known 'False Friends' (e.g. restive, noisome, enervate).

Step 3: Test for Register & Tone Concord
   - Verify that the word matches the formal macroeconomic, legal, or scientific register of the passage.
```

## 4. Rigorous Step-by-Step Deductive Solutions & Etymological Post-Mortem

### Level 1: Foundation

#### Q1 Solution
- **Correct Option:** **(A)**
- **Target Term:** **SALUTARY**
- **Definition & Etymology:** Beneficial, wholesome, promoting health or favorable outcomes.
- **Deductive Solution Proof:** 'Salutary' means beneficial or wholesome. The direct opposite is 'deleterious' (causing harm or damage).
- **Distractor Post-Mortem:**
  - **Option (B) is Incorrect:** Option (B) 'benevolent' (kind/well-meaning) is synonymous with positive intent.
  - **Option (C) is Incorrect:** Option (C) 'judicious' (wise/prudent) is positive.
  - **Option (D) is Incorrect:** Option (D) 'salubrious' (health-giving) is a near-synonym of salutary.

#### Q2 Solution
- **Correct Option:** **(B)**
- **Target Term:** **PUNCTILIOUS**
- **Definition & Etymology:** Showing great attention to detail, strict adherence to rules or etiquette.
- **Deductive Solution Proof:** 'Punctilious' means showing rigorous, meticulous attention to detail and standards. 'Meticulous' is an exact synonym.
- **Distractor Post-Mortem:**
  - **Option (A) is Incorrect:** Option (A) 'perfunctory' (carried out with minimum effort or thought) is an antonym.
  - **Option (C) is Incorrect:** Option (C) 'negligent' (careless) is a direct antonym.
  - **Option (D) is Incorrect:** Option (D) 'cursory' (hasty and superficial) is an antonym.

#### Q3 Solution
- **Correct Option:** **(B)**
- **Target Term:** **TACIT**
- **Definition & Etymology:** Understood or implied without being stated openly.
- **Deductive Solution Proof:** The sentence contrasts with 'explicit collusion', meaning the pricing was unspoken, implied, or unstated: 'tacit' pricing.
- **Distractor Post-Mortem:**
  - **Option (A) is Incorrect:** Option (A) 'overt' (open/explicit) contradicts 'rather than explicit'.
  - **Option (C) is Incorrect:** Option (C) 'candid' (frank/open) contradicts the covert signaling.
  - **Option (D) is Incorrect:** Option (D) 'blatant' (flagrantly obvious) contradicts the subtle signaling.

#### Q4 Solution
- **Correct Option:** **(C)**
