# Irrigation Engineering

## Scope

Irrigation engineering deals with the planning, design, and management of water supply systems for agricultural crops. It bridges hydrology, soil science, crop physiology, and water resources engineering.

## Crop Water Requirements

### Consumptive Use
- Consumptive use (Cu): Water used by vegetation through transpiration and evaporation from soil
- Penman equation and modified Penman methods for reference crop evapotranspiration (ETo)
- Crop coefficients (Kc) to convert ETo to crop-specific evapotranspiration (ETc)

### Water Requirements
- Net irrigation requirement: Water needed at the field to replace ETc minus effective rainfall
- Field irrigation requirement: Net requirement plus application and distribution losses
- Canal irrigation requirement: Field requirement plus conveyance losses

## Soil-Water Relationships

### Field Capacity & Permanent Wilting Point
- Field capacity (FC): Water content after gravitational drainage ceases
- Permanent wilting point (PWP): Water content at which plants permanently wilt
- Available moisture: FC - PWP
- Depth of irrigation: d = (FC - existing moisture) × bulk density × root zone depth

### Infiltration
- Infiltration rate and cumulative infiltration
- Horton's infiltration equation: ft = fc + (f₀ - fc)e^(-kt)
- Kostiakov equation: I = at^b

## Irrigation Methods

### Surface Irrigation
- **Furrow irrigation:** Water flows in small channels between crop rows
- **Border strip irrigation:** Water advances across a leveled strip bounded by levees
- **Basin irrigation:** Water spreads over a nearly level area completely surrounded by dikes

### Sprinkler Irrigation
- Spray application mimicking rainfall
- Advantages: Suitable for uneven terrain, high uniformity
- Disadvantages: High initial cost, wind sensitivity

### Drip (Trickle) Irrigation
- Slow, frequent application near the plant root zone
- Highest water use efficiency among irrigation methods
- Suited for high-value crops and saline water

## Canal Design

### Lined Canals
- Types: Concrete lining, brick lining, shotcrete, geomembrane
- Advantages: Reduced seepage, higher velocities, lower maintenance
- Design criteria: Lacey's regime theory and Kennedy's theory

### Unlined Canals
- Permissible velocity concept: V = 0.5√(R×S) (Lacey)
- Bed slope and cross-section design to prevent erosion and siltation

## Drainage & Waterlogging

### Need for Drainage
- Waterlogging: Rise of water table to within the root zone, reducing soil aeration
- Salt accumulation: Capillary rise brings salts to the surface

### Drainage Systems
- Surface drainage: Open ditches and graded land
- Subsurface drainage: Tile drains, pipe drains, drainage wells
- Drain spacing calculation using steady-state and transient flow equations

## Irrigation Efficiencies

### Definitions
- **Conveyance efficiency (Ec):** (Water delivered - seepage loss) / Water diverted
- **Application efficiency (Ea):** (Water stored in root zone) / Water delivered to field
- **Overall efficiency (Eo):** Eo = Ec × Ea
- Typical values: Surface irrigation 40-60%, sprinkler 70-85%, drip 85-95%

## Water Logging & Salinity Control

### Causes
- Over-irrigation and poor drainage
- Seepage from canals and waterlogged areas

### Reclamation Methods
- Tile drainage installation
- Surface and subsurface drainage improvement
- Irrigation scheduling and soil salinity monitoring
