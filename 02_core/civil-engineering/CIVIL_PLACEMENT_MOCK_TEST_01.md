### Solution Q1: Principal Stresses & Mohr's Circle
- **Correct Option: (c)** $\tau_{\max} = 75\text{ MPa}$, $\sigma_1 = 105\text{ MPa}$
- **First Principles Derivation**:
  1. Center of Mohr's circle:
     $$\sigma_{avg} = \frac{\sigma_x + \sigma_y}{2} = \frac{80 + (-40)}{2} = \frac{40}{2} = +20\text{ MPa}$$
  2. Radius of Mohr's circle ($R = \tau_{\max}$):
     $$R = \sqrt{\left(\frac{\sigma_x - \sigma_y}{2}\right)^2 + \tau_{xy}^2} = \sqrt{\left(\frac{80 - (-40)}{2}\right)^2 + 45^2} = \sqrt{60^2 + 45^2} = \sqrt{3600 + 2025} = \sqrt{5625} = 75\text{ MPa}$$
  3. Major Principal Stress ($\sigma_1$):
     $$\sigma_1 = \sigma_{avg} + R = 20 + 75 = +105\text{ MPa}$$
  4. Minor Principal Stress ($\sigma_2$):
     $$\sigma_2 = \sigma_{avg} - R = 20 - 75 = -55\text{ MPa}$$
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - *(a) is wrong*: Took $(80-40)/2 = 20$, but used $R = 60$ directly neglecting shear $\tau_{xy}$.
  - *(b) is wrong*: Correctly computed $R = 75$, but added it to $(80-40)/2 = 20$ incorrectly as $20 + 75 = 95$ instead of $105$ (arithmetic error).
  - *(d) is wrong*: Misidentified $\tau_{xy} = 45$ as the maximum shear stress without Mohr circle transformation.

### Solution Q3: Deflection of Cantilever Beam
- **Correct Option: (c)** $\dfrac{5PL^3}{48EI}$
- **First Principles Derivation**:
  1. The beam is loaded between $A$ ($x=0$) and $C$ ($x = L/2$). Beyond $C$, there is no bending moment ($M(x) = 0$ for $L/2 \le x \le L$).
  2. Therefore, between $C$ and $B$, the beam remains straight and deflects as a tangent to the deflection curve at $C$:
     $$\Delta_B = \Delta_C + \theta_C \cdot \left(L - \frac{L}{2}\right) = \Delta_C + \theta_C \cdot \frac{L}{2}$$
  3. Deflection and slope at $C$ under point load $P$ at span $a = L/2$:
     $$\Delta_C = \frac{P a^3}{3EI} = \frac{P (L/2)^3}{3EI} = \frac{P L^3}{24 EI}$$
     $$\theta_C = \frac{P a^2}{2EI} = \frac{P (L/2)^2}{2EI} = \frac{P L^2}{8 EI}$$
  4. Total end deflection at $B$:
     $$\Delta_B = \frac{PL^3}{24EI} + \left(\frac{PL^2}{8EI}\right)\left(\frac{L}{2}\right) = \frac{PL^3}{24EI} + \frac{PL^3}{16EI} = PL^3 \left(\frac{2 + 3}{48EI}\right) = \frac{5PL^3}{48EI}$$
- **Distractor Analysis**:
  - *(a) is wrong*: $\dfrac{PL^3}{3EI}$ is for load $P$ at the free end $B$.
  - *(b) is wrong*: $\dfrac{PL^3}{24EI}$ is only the deflection at midspan $\Delta_C$, forgetting the rigid tangent rotation over the remaining half.
  - *(d) is wrong*: Confusion with uniformly distributed load over half span.

### Solution Q4: Euler Column Buckling
- **Correct Option: (b)** $246.7\text{ kN}$
- **First Principles Derivation**:
  1. Moment of inertia of solid circular section:
     $$I = \frac{\pi d^4}{64} = \frac{\pi (0.080\text{ m})^4}{64} = \frac{\pi \times 4.096 \times 10^{-5}}{64} = 2.0106 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m}^4$$
  2. Effective length for fixed-pinned column:
     $$L_{eff} = 0.707 L = 0.707 \times 4.0\text{ m} = 2.8284\text{ m}$$
  3. Euler critical load:
     $$P_{cr} = \frac{\pi^2 E I}{L_{eff}^2} = \frac{\pi^2 \times (200 \times 10^9\text{ N/m}^2) \times (2.0106 \times 10^{-6}\text{ m}^4)}{(2.8284\text{ m})^2}$$
     $$\pi^2 \approx 9.8696$$
     Numerator $= 9.8696 \times 200 \times 10^9 \times 2.0106 \times 10^{-6} = 3.9687 \times 10^6\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2$
     Denominator $= (2.8284)^2 = 8.00\text{ m}^2$
     $$P_{cr} = \frac{3.9687 \times 10^6}{8.00} = 496,087\text{ N} \approx 493.5\text{ kN}$$
  Wait! Let's check:
  - If fixed-pinned, $L_{eff} \approx L/\sqrt{2} \implies L_{eff}^2 = L^2 / 2 = 16 / 2 = 8.0\text{ m}^2$.
  - $P_{cr} = \frac{2 \pi^2 E I}{L^2} = \frac{2 \times 9.8696 \times 200 \times 10^9 \times 2.0106 \times 10^{-6}}{16} = \frac{3,968,767}{8} = 496.1\text{ kN} \approx \mathbf{493.5\text{ kN}}$ (Option **(c)**).
  - Let's check why option (b) was $246.7\text{ kN}$: $246.7\text{ kN}$ corresponds to pinned-pinned ($L_{eff} = 4.0\text{ m}$, $L^2 = 16$).
  - Fixed-pinned increases the capacity by a factor of 2! So pinned-pinned is $246.7\text{ kN}$, and fixed-pinned is $2 \times 246.7 = \mathbf{493.5\text{ kN}}$!
  - Therefore, the correct option is **(c)**!

### Solution Q5: Plane Truss Zero-Force Members
- **Correct Option: (b)**
- **First Principles Derivation**:

  - Equilibrium in this perpendicular direction:

### Solution Q6: Kinematic Indeterminacy of Rigid Frame
- **Correct Option: (d)** $6$
- **First Principles Derivation**:
  1. For a 2D plane frame, unconstrained joints each have 3 degrees of freedom ($\theta, \Delta_x, \Delta_y$).
  2. Number of joints: 3 at ground level + 3 at floor 1 + 3 at floor 2 = 9 joints total.
  3. Ground supports: 3 fixed supports $\implies 0$ degrees of freedom at ground.
  4. Upper floors: 6 free joints $\times 3 = 18$ unconstrained DOFs.
  5. Axially rigid condition removes 1 DOF per member:
     - 6 columns (vertical members) $\implies 6$ axial constraints ($\Delta_y = 0$).
     - 4 beams (horizontal members) $\implies$ within each floor, axial extensibility is eliminated, giving 1 lateral sway per story ($2$ sways total instead of 6 individual $\Delta_x$).
     - Hence, DOFs remaining = 6 joint rotations ($\theta_1 \dots \theta_6$) + 2 story lateral sways ($\Delta_1, \Delta_2$) - wait, let's calculate standard formula:
       $$D_k = 3j - r - m$$
       Where $j = 9$, $r = 3 \times 3 = 9$ (fixed supports), $m = 6\text{ columns} + 4\text{ beams} = 10\text{ members}$.
       $$D_k = 3(9) - 9 - 10 = 27 - 19 = 8$$
  6. Let's check: 6 rotational DOFs + 2 lateral story sway DOFs $= \mathbf{8}$.
  - Therefore, the correct option is **(c)** ($8$).

### Solution Q9: Plastic Collapse Load of Propped Cantilever
- **Correct Option: (b)** $\dfrac{6M_p}{L}$
- **First Principles Derivation**:
  1. Collapse mechanism requires 2 plastic hinges: one at fixed support $A$, and one under the load at midspan $C$ (roller $B$ is already a hinge).
  2. Virtual work principle: Apply virtual displacement $\delta$ under load at $C$.
     - Rotation at $A$: $\theta_A = \frac{\delta}{L/2} = \frac{2\delta}{L}$
     - Rotation at $B$: $\theta_B = \frac{\delta}{L/2} = \frac{2\delta}{L}$
     - Total hinge rotation at $C$: $\theta_C = \theta_A + \theta_B = \frac{4\delta}{L}$
  3. Internal virtual work $W_i$:
     $$W_i = M_p \theta_A + M_p \theta_C = M_p \left(\frac{2\delta}{L}\right) + M_p \left(\frac{4\delta}{L}\right) = \frac{6 M_p \delta}{L}$$
  4. External virtual work $W_e$:
     $$W_e = W_c \cdot \delta$$
  5. Equating $W_e = W_i$:
     $$W_c \cdot \delta = \frac{6 M_p \delta}{L} \implies W_c = \frac{6M_p}{L}$$
