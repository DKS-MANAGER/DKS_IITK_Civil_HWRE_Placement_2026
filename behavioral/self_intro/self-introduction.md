# Self-Introduction

## Purpose of the Question

"Tell me about yourself" is often the opening question in interviews. It serves as your elevator pitch and sets the tone for the entire conversation. Interviewers use it to quickly understand your background, assess communication skills, and identify alignment with the role.

## Framework: Present-Past-Future

Structure your answer chronologically across three acts:

1. **Present** — Start with your current role, key responsibilities, and most relevant skills.
2. **Past** — Briefly mention prior experiences, projects, or education that led you here.
3. **Future** — Explain why you are excited about this specific opportunity and what you will contribute.

Keep the total response between 60 and 90 seconds.

## Example Response

> "I'm currently an M.Tech student in Civil Engineering at IIT Kanpur, specializing in Hydraulics and Water Resources Engineering. My work focuses on CFD modeling of turbulent flows and sediment transport using OpenFOAM and Python. Previously, I completed my B.Tech where I led a team project on open-channel flow design that improved prediction accuracy by roughly 15 percent. I'm excited about this role because it combines my background in fluid mechanics with the company's focus on sustainable water infrastructure, and I look forward to applying my simulation and data-analysis skills to real-world design challenges."

## Common Variations

- **Tell me about yourself in one minute.** Use the same Present-Past-Future structure, but compress each section to 15–20 seconds.
- **Walk me through your resume.** Focus on one or two high-impact bullets per role and explicitly connect them to the job requirements.
- **What are your strengths?** Tie each strength to a concrete example using the STAR or CAR method.

## Tips

- Avoid reciting your entire resume verbatim.
- Do not lead with personal details (hometown, family) unless culturally relevant to the role.
- Tailor the story to the company and position; a PSU interview may emphasize reliability and field-readiness, while an analytics interview should highlight coding and business impact.
- Practice aloud and record yourself to check pacing and clarity.
