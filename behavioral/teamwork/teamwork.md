# Teamwork

## Why Interviewers Ask

Teamwork questions assess whether you can collaborate effectively, resolve interpersonal friction, and contribute to a group's success without undermining others. Interviewers look for evidence that you balance individual accountability with collective outcomes.

## Key Themes

1. **Collaboration under constraints** — tight deadlines, distributed teams, or limited resources.
2. **Supporting peers** — helping a struggling teammate without taking over.
3. **Cross-functional alignment** — working with different departments or stakeholder groups.
4. **Conflict navigation** — disagreements over approach, priority, or quality.

## Framework: STAR (Situation-Task-Action-Result)

Use this structure to keep answers concise and evidence-based.

- **Situation:** Set the scene in 1–2 sentences. Define the team, project, and stakes.
- **Task:** State your specific responsibility, not the team's.
- **Action:** Describe 2–3 concrete steps you took. Emphasize listening, proposing, or coordinating.
- **Result:** Quantify the outcome and mention a lesson learned.

## Example Prompts and Approach

**Prompt:** Tell me about a time you worked as part of a team to achieve a common goal.

- Situation: A final-year project with a hard deadline and uneven task distribution.
- Task: Ensure the deliverable met quality standards despite a teammate's delay.
- Action: Asked what was blocking them, paired to resolve the bottleneck, then re-synced with the rest of the group.
- Result: Submitted on time; teammate's confidence improved; you learned to check in earlier.

**Prompt:** Describe a time when team members disagreed with your ideas. What did you do?

- Situation: Two approaches were proposed for a design iteration.
- Task: Evaluate both objectively and steer the team toward the best outcome.
- Action: Listed pros and cons with data, invited critique, and proposed a small prototype to validate assumptions.
- Result: A hybrid approach was chosen; the solution passed review with fewer rework cycles.

## Do's and Don'ts

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Highlight both your contribution and the team's success | Badmouth or name specific coworkers negatively |
| Focus on how you facilitated, not how you "saved" the project | Claim sole credit for a team deliverable |
| Mention what you learned | Drift into generic statements like "I love teamwork" |

## Questions to Prepare

- Describe a time you motivated a peer or co-worker.
- Tell me about a situation where you had to align different teams or stakeholders.
- What do you do if you disagree with a co-worker?
- Have you ever handled a difficult situation with another department?
- Describe a time when a colleague did not deliver on a promise. How did you address it?

## Company-Specific Angle (Civil/HWRE)

In PSU and core-engineering interviews, emphasize:
- Site-execution safety coordination
- Interdisciplinary reviews between structures, geotech, and hydraulics
- Client or consultant communication during project delivery
- Mentoring junior team members during field campaigns or lab work
