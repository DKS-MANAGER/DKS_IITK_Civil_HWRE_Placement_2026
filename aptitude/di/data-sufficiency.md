# Data Sufficiency

> **Priority:** P1 · **Role relevance:** Important (all roles), Critical (Consulting, Analytics, Finance)
> **Difficulty range:** Medium → Hard · **Time per question:** 1 – 2 min

---

## 1. What You Need to Know

Data Sufficiency asks whether the given statements are ENOUGH to answer the question. You don't solve — you determine sufficiency.

---

## 2. Standard Options

- **A)** Statement 1 alone is sufficient
- **B)** Statement 2 alone is sufficient
- **C)** Both together are sufficient
- **D)** Either statement alone is sufficient
- **E)** Both together are NOT sufficient

---

## 3. Strategy

1. **Read the question** — what's being asked?
2. **Test Statement 1 alone** — can you answer?
3. **Test Statement 2 alone** — can you answer?
4. **Test both together** — if neither alone works
5. **Choose the option**

---

## 4. Worked Examples

### Q1 (Medium)
**Q:** What is the value of x?
**Statement 1:** x + y = 10
**Statement 2:** x - y = 4
**Solution:** Neither alone gives x. Together: 2x = 14, x = 7. **Answer:** C) Both together.

### Q2 (Medium)
**Q:** Is x > 5?
**Statement 1:** x > 3
**Statement 2:** x < 8
**Solution:** Statement 1: x could be 4 or 6. Not sufficient. Statement 2: x could be 4 or 6. Not sufficient. Together: 3 < x < 8. Still could be 4 or 6. Not sufficient. **Answer:** E) Both together NOT sufficient.

### Q3 (Hard)
**Q:** What is the average of 5 numbers?
**Statement 1:** The sum of the numbers is 100.
**Statement 2:** The numbers are consecutive integers.
**Solution:** Statement 1: Average = 100/5 = 20. Sufficient. Statement 2: Consecutive integers alone don't give the average. Not sufficient. **Answer:** A) Statement 1 alone.
