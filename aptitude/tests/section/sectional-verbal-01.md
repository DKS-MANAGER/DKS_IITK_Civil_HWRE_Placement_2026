## Part E: Dense Academic Reading Comprehension (Q21 – Q25)

**Read the following passage carefully and answer questions 21 to 25:**

The epistemology of Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) in civil and environmental hydrodynamics occupies a contested territory between pure mathematical derivation and empirical phenomenological modeling. At the heart of this tension lies the closure problem of the Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) equations. When the non-linear convective acceleration term of the Navier-Stokes equations is decomposed into time-averaged and fluctuating components through Reynolds decomposition, the averaging operation introduces a second-order tensor representing the turbulent momentum flux—the Reynolds stress tensor $\left(-\rho \overline{u_i' u_j'}\right)$. Because this tensor introduces six independent unknown stress components without providing auxiliary governing equations, the mathematical system is fundamentally unclosed.

To circumvent this indeterminacy, classical engineering hydraulics has leaned heavily upon the Boussinesq turbulent eddy viscosity hypothesis, which posits an analogy between molecular momentum transfer and macroscopic turbulent eddy mixing. In Boussinesq-based linear eddy viscosity models—such as the ubiquitous standard $k-\varepsilon$ and Shear Stress Transport ($k-\omega\text{ SST}$) formulations—the anisotropic Reynolds stress tensor is modeled as linearly proportional to the mean rate-of-strain tensor through an isotropic scalar turbulent viscosity ($\nu_t$). 

While computationally frugal and robust for attached equilibrium boundary layers, this linear formulation harbors profound physical deficiencies. Foremost among these is its inability to capture anisotropic normal stress differences ($\overline{u'^2} \neq \overline{v'^2} \neq \overline{w'^2}$), which drive secondary currents of the second kind in non-circular conduits and open channels. In an open channel, turbulent kinetic energy is systematically redistributed near the free surface and corner sidewalls, creating transverse secondary circulation cells that shift the maximum velocity dip below the water surface. Linear eddy viscosity models, by strictly linking Reynolds stresses to the symmetric mean strain rate, predict identically zero secondary circulation in straight rectangular channels, thereby miscalculating boundary shear distributions and bed scour potential around bridge piers.

Consequently, modern computational hydraulics faces a paradigm shift. Practitioners tackling complex scour, hydraulic jumps, and aerated chute flows must either transition toward non-linear eddy viscosity models (NLEVM), second-moment Reynolds Stress Models (RSM)—which solve transport equations for each individual component of the stress tensor—or adopt scale-resolving approaches such as Large Eddy Simulation (LES). However, the computational expense of wall-resolved LES scales asymptotically with the Reynolds number ($N \propto Re^{2.4}$), rendering it prohibitive for field-scale river engineering. The pragmatic frontier therefore rests on hybrid methods: Detached Eddy Simulation (DES) and Wall-Modeled LES (WMLES), wherein the near-bed viscous layer is modeled via cost-effective RANS closures while large, energy-containing eddies in the detached shear layer are resolved directly.

## Comprehensive Worked Solutions

### Q1: Subject-Verb Concordance with *Neither... Nor*
- **Correct Answer:** **(B)**
- **Explanation:** When subjects are joined by *neither... nor*, the verb must agree in number with the subject closer to the verb (the proximity rule). Here, the closer subject is *the municipal design engineers* (plural). Therefore, the singular verb *was willing* is grammatically incorrect and must be revised to the plural **were willing**.

### Q2: Dangling Modifier Elimination
- **Correct Answer:** **(B)**
- **Explanation:** The introductory participial phrase *"Having conducted rigorous mesh refinement studies"* must be immediately followed by the agent who performed the action. A numerical divergence cannot conduct studies. Option (B) correctly places **the research team** immediately following the modifier in active voice.

### Q3: Mandative Subjunctive Mood
- **Correct Answer:** **(B)**
- **Explanation:** Verbs of demand, request, or mandate (*demanded that, recommended that, insisted that*) require the subjunctive mood in the following clause, which uses the uninflected base form of the verb without *s*, *must*, or *would*. Thus, *"demanded that the contractor pay"* is correct.

### Q4: Parallelism with *Not only... but also*
- **Correct Answer:** **(A)**
- **Explanation:** Correlative conjunctions require structural symmetry. In (A), *not only* is followed by a verb phrase (*highlighted severe liquefaction risks*) and *but also* is followed by a parallel verb phrase (*recommended deep vibro-replacement stone columns*). In (B), *highlighted* precedes *not only*, unbalancing the syntax.

### Q5: Prepositional Objective Case Pronoun
- **Correct Answer:** **(A)**
- **Explanation:** The word *between* is a preposition, and objects of prepositions must take the objective pronoun case (*me*, not *I*). The standard idiom is *"Between you and me"*.

### Q6 – Q8: Cloze Passage in Computational Fluid Dynamics
- **Q6 Correct Answer:** **(B) skepticism** (Field practitioners viewed CFD with doubt/hesitation, not worship or joy).
- **Q7 Correct Answer:** **(A) inherently** (Turbulence in natural coastal bathymetry is intrinsically/naturally chaotic).
- **Q8 Correct Answer:** **(B) irrefutable** (High-resolution LiDAR and validated WMLES provide unassailable/conclusive proof that renders opposing claims obsolete).
