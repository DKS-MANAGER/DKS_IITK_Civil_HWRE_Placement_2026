---

## 2. Core Concepts

- **Average** = `Sum / Count`.
- **Weighted average** accounts for groups of different sizes.
- **Alligation** finds the ratio in which two quantities must be mixed.
- **AP average** = `(first + last)/2` for evenly spaced numbers.

---

## 3. Formula Sheet

| Concept | Formula |
|:--------|:--------|
| Average | `Sum/Count` |
| Weighted average | `Σ(wᵢxᵢ)/Σwᵢ` |
| AP average | `(first+last)/2` |
| Alligation | `(Cheaper qty)/(Dearer qty) = (Mean-Cheaper)/(Dearer-Mean)` |
| Repeated dilution | `Final = Initial(1 - x/V)^n` |

---

## 4. Standard Methods

1. **Find total** = average × count.
2. **For missing value** — use total difference.
3. **For weighted average** — multiply each value by its weight.
4. **For alligation** — set up the cross diagram.

---

## 5. Fast Methods / Shortcuts

### Shortcut 1: AP Average
**When useful:** Consecutive/evenly spaced numbers.
**Why it works:** Symmetric distribution — middle value is the average.
**Example:** Average of even numbers 104–148 = `(106+146)/2 = 126`.
**When NOT to use:** Unevenly spaced data.

### Shortcut 2: Alligation
**When useful:** Mixing two quantities with known averages.
**Why it works:** The ratio of quantities is inversely proportional to their distance from the mean.
**Example:** Mix 20% and 40% solutions to get 30%. Ratio = `(40-30):(30-20) = 1:1`.
**When NOT to use:** More than two components (use weighted average).

## 6. Worked Examples

### Example 1 (Easy)
**Q:** Average of 5, 7, 9, 11, 13?
**Method:** AP average. **Calculation:** `(5+13)/2 = 9`. **Answer:** 9.

### Example 2 (Medium)
**Q:** Average of 6 numbers is 15. One number 20 is removed. New average?
**Method:** Total = 90. **Calculation:** New total = 70, count 5. New avg = 14. **Answer:** 14.

### Example 3 (Medium)
**Q:** A class has 20 boys (avg 18) and 10 girls (avg 15). Class average?
**Method:** Weighted. **Calculation:** `(20×18 + 10×15)/30 = (360+150)/30 = 17`. **Answer:** 17.

### Example 4 (Hard)
**Q:** Average of 11 numbers is 50. Average of first 6 is 49, last 6 is 52. 6th number?
**Method:** Sum first 6 = 294, last 6 = 312, total = 550. **Calculation:** 6th = 294+312-550 = 56. **Answer:** 56.

### Example 5 (Very Hard)
**Q:** A vessel contains 40 L of milk. 4 L removed and replaced with water, repeated 3 times. Final milk?
**Method:** Repeated dilution. **Calculation:** `40(1 - 4/40)³ = 40(0.9)³ = 40×0.729 = 29.16 L`. **Answer:** 29.16 L.

---

## 7. Common Traps

| Trap | Why it's wrong | Correct approach |
|:-----|:---------------|:-----------------|
| Simple average of two averages | Ignores group sizes | Use weighted average |
| Forgetting to adjust count | Total changes | Recompute count after removal |
| Alligation with >2 components | Wrong ratio | Use weighted average |
| AP average on non-AP data | Not symmetric | Use Sum/Count |

## 11. Placement Questions

### Q1 (Easy, 30 sec)
**Q:** Average of first 20 natural numbers?
- A) 10  B) 10.5  C) 11  D) 9.5
- **Answer:** B) 10.5
- **Explanation:** Sum = `20×21/2 = 210`. Average = `210/20 = 10.5`.
- **Fast method:** `(1+20)/2 = 10.5`.
- **Concept:** AP average. **Difficulty:** Easy. **Time:** 30 sec. **Trap:** Choosing 10.

### Q2 (Medium, 60 sec)
**Q:** The average of 5 numbers is 27. If one number is excluded, the average becomes 25. The excluded number is?
- A) 30  B) 35  C) 32  D) 28
- **Answer:** B) 35
- **Explanation:** Total = 135. New total = 100. Excluded = 135-100 = 35.
- **Fast method:** `5×27 - 4×25 = 135-100 = 35`.
- **Concept:** Average with exclusion. **Difficulty:** Medium. **Time:** 60 sec. **Trap:** None.

### Q3 (Hard, 2 min)
**Q:** A mixture contains milk and water in ratio 4:1. How much of the mixture must be replaced by water so that the ratio becomes 1:1?
- A) 1/4  B) 3/8  C) 1/2  D) 2/5
- **Answer:** B) 3/8
- **Explanation:** Let total 5 units, milk 4. After replacement milk = 2.5. Milk removed = 1.5. Fraction = 1.5/4 = 3/8.
- **Fast method:** Alligation on milk concentration.
- **Concept:** Mixtures. **Difficulty:** Hard. **Time:** 2 min. **Trap:** Confusing fraction of mixture vs milk.
