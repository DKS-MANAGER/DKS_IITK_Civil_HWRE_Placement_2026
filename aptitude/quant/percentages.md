# Percentages

> **Priority:** P0 · **Role relevance:** Critical (all roles)
> **Difficulty range:** Easy → Very Hard · **Time per question:** 30 sec – 3 min

---

## 1. What You Need to Know

Percentages are the single most-tested aptitude topic in placement screening. Master:
1. Fraction ↔ percentage conversion (memorize the table)
2. Percentage increase/decrease
3. Successive percentage change
4. Reverse percentage (finding the original)
5. Product stability rule
6. Population growth / depreciation

---

## 2. Core Concepts

- **Percentage**: A fraction with denominator 100, denoted `%`.
- **Base Value**: The original number — always the denominator in a change formula.
- **Percentage Change**: `(Change / Original) × 100`.
- **Successive Change**: Changes compound multiplicatively, not additively.

---

## 3. Formula Sheet

| Concept | Formula |
|:--------|:--------|
| % change | `(Change/Original) × 100` |
| Successive change (a%, b%) | `a + b + (ab/100)` |
| Product stability (A↑x%) | B must ↓ `(x/(100+x))×100`% |
| A is x% more than B | B is less by `(x/(100+x))×100`% |
| A is x% less than B | B is more by `(x/(100-x))×100`% |
| Population growth/decay | `P(1 ± r/100)^n` |

## 4. Standard Methods

1. **Identify the base** — what is the "original" value?
2. **Convert to fraction** — use the table for fast mental math
3. **Apply the formula** — change, successive, or reverse
4. **Verify** — does the answer make sense?

---

## 5. Fast Methods / Shortcuts

### Shortcut 1: Successive Percentage Change
**When useful:** A value changes by two percentages in sequence.
**Why it works:** A 20% increase multiplies by 1.2, then a 10% decrease multiplies by 0.9. Net = 1.2 × 0.9 = 1.08 = +8%. The formula `a+b+ab/100` is the algebraic expansion of `(1+a/100)(1+b/100) - 1`.
**Example:** Price ↑20% then ↓10% → `20 - 10 + (20×-10)/100 = 10 - 2 = +8%`
**When NOT to use:** When the base changes between steps (e.g., different quantities).

### Shortcut 2: Product Stability Rule
**When useful:** Two quantities multiply to a constant (price × quantity = expenditure).
**Why it works:** If `A×B = C` and A increases by x%, B must decrease by `x/(100+x)` to keep C constant.
**Example:** Price ↑25% → consumption must ↓ `25/125 = 20%`
**When NOT to use:** When the product is NOT constant (expenditure changes).

### Shortcut 3: Reverse Percentage
**When useful:** You know the final value and the % change, need the original.
**Why it works:** If final = original × (1 + r/100), then original = final / (1 + r/100).
**Example:** After 20% increase, value is 120. Original = 120/1.2 = 100.
**When NOT to use:** When multiple successive changes occurred (use the product).

## 6. Worked Examples

### Example 1 (Easy)
**Q:** What is 25% of 240?
**Given:** 25% = 1/4. **Method:** 240 × 1/4. **Calculation:** 60. **Answer:** 60.

### Example 2 (Medium)
**Q:** A number is increased by 20% and then decreased by 20%. Net change?
**Given:** a=+20, b=-20. **Method:** Successive formula. **Calculation:** `20-20+(20×-20/100) = -4`. **Answer:** 4% decrease.

### Example 3 (Medium)
**Q:** If A's salary is 20% less than B's, by what % is B's salary more than A's?
**Given:** A = 0.8B. **Method:** B more than A = `(B-A)/A × 100`. **Calculation:** `(0.2B/0.8B)×100 = 25%`. **Answer:** 25%.

### Example 4 (Hard)
**Q:** Due to a 20% price reduction, a person buys 5 kg more sugar for Rs. 600. Original price?
**Given:** New price = 0.8P. **Method:** `600/0.8P - 600/P = 5`. **Calculation:** `750/P - 600/P = 5 ⟹ 150/P = 5 ⟹ P = 30`. **Answer:** Rs. 30/kg.

### Example 5 (Very Hard)
**Q:** In an exam, 35% failed English, 40% failed Math, 15% failed both. % passed both?
**Given:** Set theory. **Method:** `P(E∪M) = 35+40-15 = 60%`. **Calculation:** Passed both = `100-60 = 40%`. **Answer:** 40%.

## 7. Common Traps

| Trap | Why it's wrong | Correct approach |
|:-----|:---------------|:-----------------|
| Adding percentages for successive change | Changes compound, not add | Use `a+b+ab/100` |
| Wrong base in "more/less than" | Denominator changes | "More than B" uses B as base |
| Discount on CP instead of MP | Discount is on marked price | Discount always on MP |
| Treating 20%↑ then 20%↓ as 0% | Net is -4% (compounding) | Use successive formula |

## 11. Placement Questions

### Q1 (Easy, 30 sec)
**Q:** 40% of 250 + 25% of 400 = ?
- A) 200  B) 210  C) 220  D) 230
- **Answer:** A) 200
- **Explanation:** 40% of 250 = 100; 25% of 400 = 100; total = 200.
- **Fast method:** 40% of 250 = 0.4×250 = 100; 25% of 400 = 100.
- **Concept:** Basic percentage. **Difficulty:** Easy. **Time:** 30 sec. **Trap:** None.

### Q2 (Medium, 60 sec)
**Q:** The price of a commodity is increased by 25%. By what % should consumption be reduced so that expenditure remains unchanged?
- A) 20%  B) 25%  C) 30%  D) 15%
- **Answer:** A) 20%
- **Explanation:** Product stability: `25/125 × 100 = 20%`.
- **Fast method:** `x/(100+x) = 25/125 = 1/5 = 20%`.
- **Concept:** Product stability. **Difficulty:** Medium. **Time:** 60 sec. **Trap:** Choosing 25% (adding).

### Q3 (Hard, 2 min)
**Q:** A's salary is 40% less than B's. By what % is B's salary more than A's?
- A) 40%  B) 66.67%  C) 60%  D) 33.33%
- **Answer:** B) 66.67%
- **Explanation:** A = 0.6B. B more than A = `(0.4B/0.6B)×100 = 66.67%`.
- **Fast method:** `x/(100-x) = 40/60 = 2/3 = 66.67%`.
- **Concept:** Reverse percentage. **Difficulty:** Hard. **Time:** 2 min. **Trap:** Choosing 40% (wrong base).
