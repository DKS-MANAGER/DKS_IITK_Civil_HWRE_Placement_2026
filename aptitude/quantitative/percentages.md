# Percentages

## Concept Definitions

- **Percentage**: A fraction with denominator 100, denoted by `%`.
- **Base Value**: The original number before any increase or decrease. It is always the denominator in a percentage change formula.
- **Percentage Change**: The relative change of a value expressed as a fraction of the original value.
- **Product Stability Rule**: When two quantities are multiplied to give a constant product, an increase in one corresponds to a proportional decrease in the other.

---

## Key Formulas & Shortcuts

### Conversions
- **Percentage to Fraction**: Divide by 100. E.g., $40\% = \frac{40}{100} = \frac{2}{5}$.
- **Fraction to Percentage**: Multiply by 100. E.g., $\frac{3}{4} = 75\%$.

### Percentage Increase / Decrease
$$\text{Percentage Increase} = \frac{\text{Increase}}{\text{Original Value}} \times 100$$
$$\text{Percentage Decrease} = \frac{\text{Decrease}}{\text{Original Value}} \times 100$$

### Product Stability Rule (Comparison)
- If $A$ is $x\%$ more than $B$, then $B$ is less than $A$ by:
  $$\left( \frac{x}{100 + x} \times 100 \right)\%$$
- If $A$ is $x\%$ less than $B$, then $B$ is more than $A$ by:
  $$\left( \frac{x}{100 - x} \times 100 \right)\%$$

### Successive Percentage Change
If a quantity is changed by $a\%$ and then by $b\%$:
$$\text{Net Change} = \left( a + b + \frac{a \times b}{100} \right)\%$$
Use positive values for increases and negative values for decreases.

### Population / Depreciation
Let present value be $P$ and rate be $R\%$ per annum.
- **After $n$ years**: $P_n = P \left( 1 \pm \frac{R}{100} \right)^n$
- **$n$ years ago**: $P_0 = \frac{P}{\left( 1 \pm \frac{R}{100} \right)^n}$
Use $+$ for growth, $-$ for decay.

## Practice Problems

### Problem 1
If the price of petrol increases by 25%, by what percentage must consumption be reduced to keep expenditure unchanged?

### Problem 2
A man spends 75% of his income. His income rises by 20% and his expenditure rises by 15%. By what percentage do his savings increase?

### Problem 3
The length of a rectangle increases by 20% and its breadth decreases by 10%. Find the net percentage change in area.

### Problem 4
The population of a town grows at 10% per annum. If the present population is 20,000, find the population 2 years hence.

### Problem 5
Due to a 20% reduction in the price of sugar, a person buys 5 kg more sugar for Rs. 600. Find the original price per kg.

---

## Step-by-Step Solutions

### Solution 1
1. The price increases by $x = 25\%$.
2. To keep expenditure constant, apply the product stability rule:
   $$\text{Reduction} = \frac{25}{100 + 25} \times 100 = \frac{25}{125} \times 100 = 20\%$$
3. **Answer**: Consumption must be reduced by **20%**.

### Solution 2
1. Assume initial income $= 100$. Expenditure $= 75$. Savings $= 25$.
2. New income $= 120$. New expenditure $= 75 + 15\% \text{ of } 75 = 75 + 11.25 = 86.25$.
3. New savings $= 120 - 86.25 = 33.75$. Increase in savings $= 33.75 - 25 = 8.75$.
4. Percentage increase:
   $$\frac{8.75}{25} \times 100 = 35\%$$
5. **Answer**: Savings increase by **35%**.

### Solution 3
1. Length change $a = +20\%$, breadth change $b = -10\%$.
2. Apply successive change formula:
   $$\text{Net Change} = 20 - 10 + \frac{20 \times (-10)}{100} = 10 - 2 = 8\%$$
3. **Answer**: The area increases by **8%**.

### Solution 4
1. Present population $P = 20{,}000$, rate $R = 10\%$, time $n = 2$.
2. Apply growth formula:
   $$P_2 = 20{,}000 \left(1 + \frac{10}{100}\right)^2 = 20{,}000 \times (1.1)^2 = 20{,}000 \times 1.21 = 24{,}200$$
3. **Answer**: The population will be **24,200**.

### Solution 5
1. Let original price be Rs. $X$ per kg.
2. Original quantity for Rs. 600 $= \frac{600}{X}$ kg.
3. New price $= 0.80X$. New quantity $= \frac{600}{0.80X} = \frac{750}{X}$ kg.
4. Difference in quantities:
   $$\frac{750}{X} - \frac{600}{X} = 5 \implies \frac{150}{X} = 5 \implies X = 30$$
5. **Answer**: Original price was **Rs. 30 per kg**.
