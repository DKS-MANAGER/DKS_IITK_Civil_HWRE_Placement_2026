## 1. Argumentative & Logical Reasoning Framework

### 1.1 The Anatomy of an Argument
A logical argument is not merely a collection of opinions; it is a structural mechanism designed to compel an epistemic conclusion from stated premises.

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                        THE FORMAL ANATOMY OF AN ARGUMENT                          |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Structural Element     | Functional Role                  | Indicator Words       |
|------------------------+----------------------------------+-----------------------|
| Premise ($P$)          | Stated factual evidence; treated | Because, since, given |
|                        | as incontrovertibly true         | that, for, in view of |
| Counter-Premise ($CP$) | Evidence acknowledging an        | Although, despite,    |
|                        | opposing viewpoint or caveat     | while, granted that   |
| Intermediate Concl ($IC$)| A claim derived from premises    | Thus, shows that,     |
|                        | that serves as support for $MC$  | which indicates that  |
| Main Conclusion ($MC$) | The core claim the author seeks  | Therefore, clearly,   |
|                        | to prove; the ultimate thesis    | hence, consequently   |
| Assumption ($A$)       | An unstated, essential bridge    | Unwritten; required   |
|                        | linking premise to conclusion    | for valid deduction   |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1.2 Core Question Archetypes & Dedicated Analytical Toolkits

#### 1. The Assumption Toolkit: The Formal Negation Test
An assumption is an unstated premise **necessary** for the conclusion to stand.
- **The Negation Rule:** To test whether an option is a necessary assumption, logically negate (reverse) it.
- **Decision Criterion:** If the negated statement completely shatters, invalidates, or makes impossible the author's main conclusion, the option is an **absolute necessary assumption**. If the conclusion remains logically viable despite negation, the option is merely sufficient or irrelevant.

#### 2. Causal Reasoning & The Alternative Explanation Protocol
Most critical reasoning arguments assert causal links: $X \implies Y$.
- **Inherent Vulnerabilities in Causal Claims:**
  1. **Reverse Causality:** Could $Y$ be causing $X$?
  2. **Confounding Third Variable:** Does an unobserved factor $Z$ simultaneously cause both $X$ and $Y$?
  3. **Post Hoc Ergo Propter Hoc:** Did $X$ merely precede $Y$ chronologically without physical causality?
  4. **Coincidence / Data Artifact:** Is the association a statistical anomaly?
- **To Weaken:** Show that $Z \implies Y$, or show $Y$ occurs without $X$, or show $X$ occurs without $Y$, or demonstrate reverse causality ($Y \implies X$).
- **To Strengthen:** Eliminate an alternative cause $Z$, show that when $X$ is absent $Y$ does not occur, or verify data accuracy.

#### 3. Paradox Resolution: The Dual-Fact Compatibility Bridge
A paradox presents two observed empirical facts ($Fact_A$ and $Fact_B$) that appear mutually contradictory.
- **Golden Rule:** Never choose an option that denies or refutes either fact. Both facts are treated as undeniable truths.
- **Resolution Strategy:** Identify an unstated contextual condition ($Factor_C$) that explains how $Fact_A$ and $Fact_B$ can simultaneously co-exist without contradiction.

#### 4. Evaluate the Argument: The Variance (Two-Path) Test
Evaluate questions ask: *"Which inquiry is most useful to evaluate the validity of the argument?"*
- **Application:** Formulate two extreme, opposing answers (e.g., "Yes" vs "No", or "High" vs "Low") to the question posed in the option.
- **Decision Criterion:** If one answer strongly validates/strengthens the conclusion while the opposite answer severely dismantles/weakens the conclusion, the option is the uniquely correct evaluative inquiry.

#### 5. Boldface / Argument Role Identification
- Map the global architecture before evaluating options:
  - Is Boldface 1 a Fact, a Premise, a Counter-Argument, an Intermediate Conclusion, or the Main Conclusion?
  - Does the author agree or disagree with Boldface 1?
  - What is the relationship between Boldface 1 and Boldface 2?

### Level 1: Foundation (Q1–Q5)

#### Q1
**Argument:** Municipal governments should install automated traffic light cameras at all busy intersections. Studies demonstrate that intersections equipped with automated cameras report 35% fewer red-light violations. Furthermore, the revenue generated from traffic citations can fund municipal road repair budgets. Therefore, installing these cameras will both enhance urban public safety and provide essential fiscal revenue.  
**Question:** Which of the following best expresses the main conclusion of the argument?
- (A) Intersections with cameras report 35% fewer red-light violations.
- (B) Automated camera fines should be dedicated exclusively to road repair.
- (C) Municipalities should deploy automated traffic cameras because they will improve safety and generate municipal funds.
- (D) Traffic violations are the primary cause of municipal budget shortfalls.

#### Q2
**Argument:** A corporate recruitment software firm asserts: "Our new algorithmic resume screening tool evaluates applicants exclusively based on quantifiable credentials and technical assessment scores. By deploying this software, our corporate clients will eliminate human interviewer bias and dramatically improve the quality of their engineering hires."  
**Question:** The argument depends on which of the following unstated assumptions?
- (A) Human recruiters always deliberately discriminate against candidates.
- (B) The quantifiable credentials and technical scores measured by the algorithm are accurate indicators of on-the-job engineering excellence.
- (C) Engineering candidates never include inaccuracies on their submitted resumes.
- (D) The recruitment software costs less to operate than employing human human-resource professionals.

#### Q3
**Argument:** In January, Retailer X increased the retail price of its premium coffee beans by 20%. In February, the total revenue generated from coffee bean sales declined by 15%. The regional sales manager concluded that the price hike drove away customers and directly caused the drop in sales revenue.  
**Question:** Which of the following, if true, most seriously weakens the sales manager’s conclusion?
- (A) A competing supermarket three miles away maintained its coffee prices throughout January and February.
- (B) The cost of packaging materials for the coffee beans increased by 5% in December.
- (C) Retailer X launched an advertising campaign for its bakery division in February.
- (D) An unprecedented regional blizzard in February shut down urban transit and reduced overall customer foot traffic to Retailer X by 40%.

#### Q4
**Argument:** A pharmaceutical startup tested a novel cognitive-enhancement compound on a cohort of 50 healthy college students. After four weeks, the participants demonstrated a 25% improvement on standardized spatial memory examinations. The startup asserts that the compound effectively enhances cognitive memory in the general adult population.  
**Question:** Which of the following, if true, most strongly strengthens the startup’s assertion?
- (A) The baseline biological mechanisms governing spatial memory in healthy college students do not differ significantly from those of the broader adult population.
- (B) Several students reported experiencing minor headaches during the second week of the trial.
- (C) Standardized spatial memory exams are computerized rather than written by hand.
- (D) The startup plans to price the compound at a competitive market rate once regulatory approval is granted.

#### Q5
**Argument:** All enterprise clients that purchase CyberDefense Suite X receive automated threat detection. Some enterprise clients that receive automated threat detection also purchase dedicated cloud backup storage. None of the clients that purchase dedicated cloud backup storage experience catastrophic ransomware data loss.  
**Question:** If the statements above are true, which of the following must also be true?
- (A) All enterprise clients that purchase CyberDefense Suite X avoid catastrophic ransomware data loss.
- (B) Any client that avoids catastrophic ransomware data loss must have purchased dedicated cloud backup storage.
- (C) Some enterprise clients that receive automated threat detection avoid catastrophic ransomware data loss.
- (D) No enterprise client that purchases CyberDefense Suite X avoids catastrophic ransomware data loss.

#### Q7

**Question:** The financial comptroller's projection is based on which of the following essential assumptions?
- (A) The cost of diesel fuel for the bus fleet will decline by 25% over the next fiscal year.
- (B) All bus drivers will agree to freeze their hourly wages during the current contract negotiations.
- (C) Municipal bus ridership consists entirely of high-income professionals who prefer buses to personal cars.
- (D) The 25% fare hike will not cause bus ridership volume to decline or drive commuters toward alternative transport modes.

#### Q8

However, statistical traffic data collected over the subsequent three years revealed that the total number of vehicle collisions per 10,000 drivers remained virtually unchanged.  
**Question:** Which of the following, if true, best explains the apparent discrepancy described above?
- (A) Anti-lock braking systems slightly increase the manufacturing cost of entry-level compact vehicles.
- (B) Electronic stability control requires specialized diagnostic equipment for routine maintenance.
- (C) Drivers aware that their vehicles possessed superior safety technologies drove faster in adverse weather and maintained shorter following distances (risk compensation).

#### Q9
**Argument:** Immediately following the appointment of CEO Marcus Vance, TechCorp's quarterly software sales expanded by 40%. Clearly, Vance's visionary restructuring of the corporate sales team is the direct driver of TechCorp's commercial success.  
**Question:** The reasoning in the argument is most vulnerable to criticism on the ground that it:
- (A) Infers that one event caused another merely because the second event followed the first in chronological time (post hoc fallacy).
- (B) Relies on testimony from employees who have a personal financial interest in Vance's retention.

- (D) Assumes that all corporate CEOs in the software industry possess visionary leadership capabilities.

#### Q10
**Argument:** **Many environmental economists argue that carbon taxation is the most effective policy mechanism to accelerate the clean energy transition.** However, carbon taxes impose a disproportionate financial burden on low-income rural households that must drive long distances for employment. **Therefore, policymakers should reject universal carbon taxation in favor of targeted clean-technology subsidies and clean electricity standards.**  
**Question:** In the argument above, what roles do the two boldface portions play?
- (A) The first is the author's main conclusion; the second is a premise supporting that conclusion.
- (B) The first is an opposing position acknowledged by the author; the second is the author's main conclusion.
- (C) The first is an intermediate conclusion; the second is an unverified factual observation.
- (D) The first is evidence supporting the author's stance; the second is a counter-argument dismissed by the author.

#### Q13

#### Q14
**Argument:** In an industrial manufacturing facility, engineers upgraded all compressed-air generation systems with variable-speed drives, reducing the electrical energy consumed per cubic meter of compressed air by 40%. Unexpectedly, the facility’s monthly total electrical consumption attributable to the compressed air system increased by 15% over the subsequent six months.  
**Question:** Which of the following best resolves the apparent paradox described above?
- (A) The price of grid electricity increased by 5% during the monitoring period.
- (B) Because compressed air became drastically cheaper per unit, shop-floor technicians expanded its usage to blow away dust, run pneumatic tools continuously, and neglected minor line leaks that were previously repaired immediately (Jevons paradox).

- (D) Compressed-air systems require annual oil filter replacements to maintain peak efficiency.

#### Q15

**Question:** Which of the following, if true, most clearly exposes a flaw in the chief engineer's conclusion?

#### Q20
**Argument:** In a metropolitan police jurisdiction of 2,000,000 residents, an automated facial-recognition surveillance camera system boasting a 99% accuracy rate (correctly identifying a wanted felon 99% of the time, and misidentifying an innocent civilian as a felon only 1% of the time) alerts officers that a pedestrian on a downtown sidewalk is a wanted fugitive. The police commander asserts that there is a 99% probability that the alerted individual is indeed the fugitive.  
**Question:** The commander's assertion commits which of the following classical probabilistic/logical fallacies?

- (B) The Base Rate Fallacy, by ignoring that because the absolute number of innocent civilians in the city (roughly 1,999,900) vastly exceeds the number of wanted fugitives (say, 100), a 1% false positive rate will generate roughly 20,000 false alarms, meaning the vast majority of positive alerts are innocent civilians.
- (C) Circular Reasoning, by assuming that an individual is a fugitive in order to prove that the individual is on the sidewalk.
- (D) Ad Hominem, by attacking the moral character of the pedestrian rather than evaluating the camera.

### Level 6: Extreme (Q26–Q30)

#### Q26

1. All commercial banks subject to Tier-1 capital adequacy regulations must hold at least 30% of their liquid reserves in sovereign government bonds.

## 5. Rapid Revision & Strategic Exam Traps

1. **The Negation Test for Assumptions:**
   - Negate the choice: if the conclusion shatters, it is a necessary assumption.
2. **Correlation $\neq$ Causality:**
   - Always check for Reverse Causality ($Y \implies X$) and Confounding Variables ($Z \implies X \text{ and } Z \implies Y$).
3. **The Two-Path Test for Evaluate Questions:**
   - The correct inquiry must produce opposite conclusions when answered with extreme positive vs negative responses.
4. **Scope Shift Vigilance:**
   - Watch out for options that subtly swap domains (e.g., from *hospital surgical mortality* to *overall healthcare costs*).
5. **Beware of False Dichotomies:**
   - Reject arguments that assume disproving Option A automatically forces the adoption of Option B without evaluating intermediate alternatives.
