## 1. Discourse Architecture: Summary & Inference Framework

In premier recruitment examinations (McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Goldman Sachs, WorldQuant, Google PM, and PSU Executive Trainee Cadres), **Paragraph Summary** and **Critical Inference** questions evaluate an applicant's ability to extract the irreducible logical core of dense technical prose, distinguish necessary deductions from external assumptions, and dismantle subtle distractor traps.

### 1.1 The Four Evaluative Archetypes

| Question Archetype | Cognitive Objective | Operational Placement Heuristic |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **1. Best Summary (Précis)** | Identifies the single statement that synthesizes the author's primary premise, counter-premise, and ultimate conclusion. | Must encompass the overarching argument; eliminate options that capture only a subordinate detail or omit the core resolution. |
| **2. Must Be True (Deductive Inference)** | Derives a proposition that is mathematically and logically guaranteed by the text's explicit premises. | Requires zero external assumptions. If a statement requires even a highly plausible outside fact, it is NOT an inference. |
| **3. Primary Purpose / Stance** | Identifies the author's underlying rhetorical objective (critique, reconcile, explain, defend). | Focus on structural verbs (*reconcile, demonstrate, caution*) and the author's calibrated tone. |
| **4. Role of Boldface Sentence** | Evaluates the structural function of an individual sentence within the dialectical argument. | Determine whether the sentence serves as an empirical premise, a counter-premise concession, an objection, or the main conclusion. |

### 1.2 The 4-Step Distractor Elimination Algorithm

```text
Step 1: Isolate the Logical Skeleton
   - Premise 1 (Baseline Context) -> Complication / Counter-Premise -> Author's Resolution / Conclusion.

Step 2: Check for the 4 Fatal Distractor Flaws
   - The Narrow Trap: Accurately summarizes one sentence but ignores the author's resolution.
   - The Extreme Trap: Shifts nuanced modal qualifiers ('can occasionally constrain') into absolutes ('inevitably destroys').
   - The Out-of-Scope Trap: Introduces real-world facts not stated in the passage.
   - The Inversion Trap: Flips cause and effect or misattributes the critics' argument to the author.

Step 3: Test the Scope Boundary
   - The correct option must match the exact analytical register and institutional scope of the passage.
```

## 5. Rapid Revision & Strategic Traps

### 5.1 The 7 Deadly Traps in Summary & Inference Tests

| Trap Name | Why Candidates Stumble | The Structural Flaw | Antidote |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **The Keyword Mirage Trap** | Distractor contains 5-6 verbatim phrases from the passage | Verbatim words are rearranged to express an inverted or distorted claim | Do not match words; match the logical proposition and causal direction |
| **The Partial Summary Trap** | Option perfectly summarizes the opening paragraph problem | Completely omits the second half containing the turnaround or engineering solution | Ensure the summary bridges both the complication and the ultimate resolution |
| **The Extreme Language Trap** | Candidate fails to notice subtle quantifier shifts | Shifts 'often', 'can', 'may' into 'always', 'inevitably', 'never', 'sole' | Check modal auxiliaries: the option's certainty cannot exceed the text's certainty |
| **The Prior Knowledge Trap** | Statement is an indisputable real-world scientific or economic fact | The passage never mentioned or substantiated that specific fact | If it cannot be derived strictly from within the four corners of the text, eliminate it |
| **The Perspective Inversion** | Candidate confuses the critics' cited objection with the author's stance | The author introduces an argument specifically to refute or qualify it | Identify the pivot ('However, history refutes...') to determine the author's true stance |
| **The Causal Reversal** | Reverses the direction of cause and effect | Asserts that X caused Y when the text argued Y caused X | Map cause -> effect explicitly before scanning the options |
| **The Extrapolation Trap** | Extends a valid short-term conclusion into an unconstrained future claim | The author analyzed a specific case study; the option generalizes to all time and space | Bound your inference strictly to the passage's stated operational scope |

### 5.2 The 45-Second Summary & Inference Heuristic

1. **Seconds 0-15:** Read the passage actively. Map the dialectic: Thesis -> Complication -> Resolution.
2. **Seconds 15-25:** Formulate the single-sentence core takeaway in your own words before reading options.
3. **Seconds 25-35:** Scan options for qualifiers and extreme words (*always, solely, completely*). Eliminate extreme and partial summaries.
4. **Seconds 35-45:** Verify that the winning option captures both the operational constraint and the author's ultimate conclusion.
