# Reading Comprehension

> **Priority:** P0 · **Role relevance:** Important (all roles), Critical (Consulting, Product)
> **Difficulty range:** Easy → Very Hard · **Time per passage:** 5 – 10 min

---

## 1. Core Question Types & Strategies

### 1.1 Main Idea / Primary Purpose
- **Goal:** Identify the central theme or author's motive.
- **Strategy:** Read first and last paragraphs. Focus on the thesis statement.
- **Wrong answer traps:** Too broad, too narrow, or directly copied from passage.

### 1.2 Inference
- **Goal:** Draw conclusions not explicitly stated but logically supported.
- **Strategy:** Locate the relevant facts, choose what *must* be true.
- **Wrong answer traps:** Requires speculation, copies the passage verbatim, introduces outside knowledge.

### 1.3 Vocabulary in Context
- **Goal:** Determine the meaning of a word as used in the passage.
- **Strategy:** Replace the word with each option — which one fits the context?
- **Wrong answer traps:** The word's most common meaning (out of context).

### 1.4 Tone / Attitude
- **Goal:** Identify the author's attitude.
- **Strategy:** Look for descriptive words and the overall message.
- **Common tones:** Objective, critical, laudatory, cynical, didactic, neutral, pessimistic.

### 1.5 Specific Detail
- **Goal:** Find a fact stated in the passage.
- **Strategy:** Go back to the passage, locate the specific line.

## 2. Passage 1 — Technology & Burnout (Medium)

**Read the following passage and answer questions 1–5.**

While the technological advancements of the 21st century have undeniably streamlined communication and increased access to global markets, they have simultaneously birthed an insidious epidemic of digital exhaustion. Constant connectivity, once hailed as a liberation from the confines of the traditional nine-to-five office, has morphed into a psychological leash. Employees are now subtly coerced into responding to emails late at night, fearing that a delay will be misconstrued as a lack of professional commitment. This blur between professional availability and personal time has slowly eroded the cognitive downtime necessary for creative thought and mental restoration. Despite corporate wellness programs that offer yoga classes and meditation apps, the core issue remains unaddressed: the expectation of perpetual responsiveness. Until organizations dismantle the structural culture that rewards constant availability, burnout will continue to masquerade as individual failure rather than a systemic failure.

---

### Q1 (Main Idea)
Which of the following best expresses the main idea?
- A) Corporate wellness programs are completely ineffective at resolving worker stress.
- B) Constant digital connectivity has created a system of workplace expectations that leads to burnout.
- C) Employees should stop responding to emails after traditional office hours to protect their mental health.
- D) Technology in the 21st century has done more harm than good to global business communication.

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The passage argues that constant connectivity has created workplace expectations leading to burnout — a systemic issue, not individual failure. A is too narrow (one sentence), C is not the author's suggestion (passage criticizes the system, not employees), D is too broad (technology did streamline communication too).

---

### Q2 (Inference)
It can be inferred that the author believes "cognitive downtime" is:
- A) A luxury that only highly creative professionals can afford.
- B) Incompatible with the demands of modern global markets.
- C) Essential for generating creative ideas and maintaining mental well-being.
- D) Successfully cultivated by corporate meditation and yoga programs.

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The passage states downtime is "necessary for creative thought and mental restoration" — so C must be true. A contradicts the passage's egalitarian tone. B is never suggested. D is contradicted by "core issue remains unaddressed."

---

### Q3 (Tone)
The author's tone in the passage can best be described as:
- A) Objective and detached
- B) Critically concerned
- C) Optimistic and hopeful
- D) Indifferent and dismissive

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** Words like "insidious epidemic," "psychological leash," "subtly coerced," "eroded" show concern and criticism. The passage is not neutral (A), not hopeful (C), and not indifferent (D).

---

### Q4 (Vocabulary in Context)
The word "insidious" in the first sentence most nearly means:
- A) Visible and obvious
- B) Gradually harmful but not immediately apparent
- C) Intentionally malicious
- D) Mildly annoying

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** "Insidious" means proceeding harmfully but subtly. The context ("epidemic of digital exhaustion") confirms it's gradual and not immediately obvious. A is opposite. C is too strong. D is too weak.

---

### Q5 (Specific Detail)
According to the passage, what do corporate wellness programs fail to address?
- A) The lack of employee training on digital tools
- B) The structural culture that rewards constant availability
- C) The physical health problems caused by sitting at desks all day
- D) The need for better email management software

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** "The core issue remains unaddressed: the expectation of perpetual responsiveness" and "Until organizations dismantle the structural culture that rewards constant availability" — directly supports B. A, C, D are not mentioned.

## 3. Passage 2 — Urban Infrastructure (Hard)

**Read the following passage and answer questions 6–10.**

The rapid urbanization of developing nations has exposed a critical gap between infrastructure demand and delivery capacity. By 2030, an estimated 60% of the global population will reside in urban areas, yet the infrastructure pipelines of most developing cities are designed for populations half that size. This mismatch manifests not only in overcrowded transportation networks and inadequate water supply systems but also in the subtler erosion of urban livability — longer commute times, deteriorating air quality, and the progressive displacement of low-income communities to the periphery. Engineering solutions alone cannot bridge this gap; what is required is an integrated approach that combines civil engineering innovation with policy reform, community engagement, and sustainable financing models. The challenge is not merely technical — it is fundamentally a question of political will and equitable resource allocation.

---

### Q6 (Main Idea)
The passage primarily argues that:
- A) Urban infrastructure in developing nations needs more engineering solutions.
- B) The global population will reach 60% urbanization by 2030.
- C) Bridging the urban infrastructure gap requires a multi-dimensional approach beyond engineering.
- D) Low-income communities are the primary victims of infrastructure failure.

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The passage's core argument is that "Engineering solutions alone cannot bridge this gap" — an integrated approach is needed. A contradicts the passage. B is a supporting detail, not the main argument. D is a supporting detail.

---

### Q7 (Inference)
The author would most likely agree with which statement?
- A) The problem of urban infrastructure is primarily a funding issue.
- B) Engineers are solely responsible for infrastructure failures in cities.
- C) Technical solutions must be complemented by policy and social reforms.
- D) Urbanization should be slowed down to reduce infrastructure pressure.

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** "What is required is an integrated approach that combines civil engineering innovation with policy reform, community engagement, and sustainable financing models" — directly supports C. A is too narrow. B contradicts the passage. D is not suggested.

---

### Q8 (Tone)
The author's attitude toward the urban infrastructure challenge can best be described as:
- A) Fatalistic and resigned
- B) Analytically urgent
- C) Technically optimistic
- D) Politically neutral

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The passage presents facts analytically but with urgency ("critical gap," "erosion of urban livability," "fundamentally a question of political will"). Not fatalistic (A), not technically optimistic (C), not politically neutral (D — the passage explicitly calls for political will).

---

### Q9 (Specific Detail)
According to the passage, the infrastructure mismatch in developing cities manifests in all of the following EXCEPT:
- A) Overcrowded transportation networks
- B) Inadequate water supply systems
- C) Rising crime rates in urban areas
- D) Deteriorating air quality

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The passage mentions transportation, water supply, air quality, commute times, and displacement — but NOT crime rates.

---

### Q10 (Inference)
The phrase "subtler erosion of urban livability" suggests that:
- A) The problems are invisible and cannot be measured.
- B) The negative effects are real but develop gradually and are less immediately obvious than infrastructure shortages.
- C) Urban livability is a concept that has no practical meaning.
- D) The erosion is primarily caused by natural disasters.

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** "Subtler" means less obvious; "erosion" means gradual decline. The passage contrasts these with the more visible infrastructure shortages. A is too extreme. C is wrong. D is not mentioned.

## 4. Passage 3 — Water Resource Management (Hard)

**Read the following passage and answer questions 11–15.**

Water scarcity is no longer a regional problem confined to arid geographies — it has become a global crisis that intersects with energy production, food security, and geopolitical stability. The construction of large dams, once considered the definitive solution to water management, has come under scrutiny for its ecological and social costs. Downstream ecosystems are disrupted, upstream communities are displaced, and the promised benefits of irrigation and hydropower often materialize unevenly. Meanwhile, groundwater extraction has accelerated at unsustainable rates, with aquifer depletion rates in parts of India, China, and the United States exceeding natural recharge by factors of three to five. The emerging consensus among water resource engineers is that sustainable water management demands a portfolio approach: rainwater harvesting, wastewater recycling, demand-side management, and community-based governance structures must complement traditional infrastructure. The engineering profession must evolve from building single mega-projects to designing adaptive, decentralized systems that can withstand climate variability.

---

### Q11 (Main Idea)
The central argument of the passage is that:
- A) Large dams should be completely abandoned as a water management strategy.
- B) Water scarcity is caused primarily by climate change.
- C) Sustainable water management requires a diversified, adaptive portfolio of solutions.
- D) Groundwater extraction is the most serious threat to global water security.

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** The passage argues for a "portfolio approach" combining multiple strategies. A is too extreme (complementary, not replacement). B is not the focus. D is one factor, not the central argument.

---

### Q12 (Inference)
The author's criticism of large dams implies that:
- A) Engineering projects are always harmful to communities.
- B) Mega-projects alone are insufficient for sustainable water management.
- C) Dams should only be built in developed countries.
- D) Hydropower is always less efficient than solar energy.

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The passage says dams have "ecological and social costs" and benefits "often materialize unevenly" — implying they're insufficient alone. A, C, D are not supported.

---

### Q13 (Tone)
The author's tone regarding current water management practices is best described as:
- A) Completely dismissive
- B) Analytically critical with constructive suggestions
- C) Indifferent
- D) Overly optimistic

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** The passage critiques current practices (critical) but offers a constructive portfolio approach (constructive). Not dismissive (A), indifferent (C), or optimistic (D).

---

### Q14 (Specific Detail)
The passage states that groundwater extraction in parts of India, China, and the US exceeds natural recharge by:
- A) Factors of two to three
- B) Factors of three to five
- C) Factors of five to ten
- D) More than ten times

**Answer:** B
**Explanation:** "aquifer depletion rates in parts of India, China, and the United States exceeding natural recharge by factors of three to five."

---

### Q15 (Main Idea)
The author concludes that the engineering profession must:
- A) Stop building infrastructure entirely
- B) Focus exclusively on small-scale solutions
- C) Evolve from mega-projects to adaptive, decentralized systems
- D) Leave water management to politicians and communities

**Answer:** C
**Explanation:** "The engineering profession must evolve from building single mega-projects to designing adaptive, decentralized systems that can withstand climate variability."

---

## 5. Rapid Revision

- **Main idea:** First/last paragraph, thesis statement
- **Inference:** What *must* be true based on facts
- **Tone:** Look for descriptive words
- **Vocabulary:** Replace word in context
- **Specific detail:** Go back to passage, locate exact line
- **Trap:** Options too broad/narrow, or directly copied
