### Questions 49–54: Reading Comprehension (Deep Learning Interpretability & Mechanistic Interpretability)

**Passage:**
The exponential scaling of deep neural networks—epitomized by dense transformer-based Large Language Models (LLMs)—has catalyzed a fundamental epistemological crisis in artificial intelligence. Operating as high-dimensional non-linear statistical approximators with hundreds of billions of unconstrained parameters, these models function effectively as opaque computational black boxes. While their empirical proficiency across natural language understanding, algorithmic coding, and multimodal synthesis is indisputable, their internal cognitive representations remain largely uninterpretable via classical analytic techniques.

In response, the nascent subfield of *Mechanistic Interpretability* seeks to reverse-engineer neural networks from empirical weights into human-understandable algorithmic circuits. Unlike traditional post-hoc feature attribution methods (such as saliency maps or Integrated Gradients), which merely highlight input tokens correlated with output predictions without elucidating internal reasoning, mechanistic interpretability investigates the polysemanticity of individual neurons. In standard architectures, individual neurons exhibit superposition: a single artificial neuron activates in response to wildly disparate, semantically unrelated concepts (e.g., Arabic script, base-64 encoding, and academic economics jargon). This superposition emerges as a direct geometric consequence of models representing more independent features than available orthogonal dimensions in the hidden activation space.

To resolve this dimensional entanglement, researchers deploy Dictionary Learning via Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs). By projecting dense, entangled activation vectors into higher-dimensional sparse latent spaces constrained by $L_1$ regularization, SAEs successfully isolate monosemantic features. These isolated latent features correspond to discrete, human-interpretable concepts—such as specific logical induction heads, syntactic agreement trackers, and safety-relevant deceptive reasoning circuits. However, substantial engineering bottlenecks impede the comprehensive mechanistic auditing of production-grade frontier models. Current sparse autoencoders require vast memory overheads, scale poorly to non-linear multilayer cross-attention blocks, and capture only a fraction of total network variance, leaving the critical question of autonomous safety verification open.
