## 1. Section 1 — Consulting Math Test (10 Questions)

### Question 1 (Easy)
A retail store's revenue increased from ₹40 Lakhs to ₹50 Lakhs, while costs increased from ₹30 Lakhs to ₹42 Lakhs. What is the percentage change in net profit?
* A) +20%
* B) -20%
* C) -40%
* D) +10%

#### Solution:
* $\text{Initial Profit} = 40 - 30 = ₹10\text{ Lakhs}$.
* $\text{New Profit} = 50 - 42 = ₹8\text{ Lakhs}$.
* $\text{Change \%} = \frac{8 - 10}{10} \times 100 = \mathbf{-20\%}$.
* **Correct Answer**: **B**

---

### Question 2 (Medium)
A SaaS product has an Average Order Value of ₹12,000/year, customer churn of 20%/year (average customer lifespan = 5 years), and customer acquisition cost (CAC) of ₹15,000. What is the LTV/CAC ratio?
* A) 2.0
* B) 3.0
* C) 4.0
* D) 5.0

#### Solution:
* $\text{LTV} = 12,000 \times 5 = ₹60,000$.
* $\text{LTV/CAC} = \frac{60,000}{15,000} = \mathbf{4.0}$.
* **Correct Answer**: **C** (Healthy ratio > 3.0).

---

### Question 3 (Medium)
If a market doubles in size every 4 years, what is its approximate annual compound growth rate (CAGR)?
* A) 12%
* B) 18%
* C) 24%
* D) 36%

#### Solution:
* Using Rule of 72: $\text{Years to Double} = \frac{72}{\text{CAGR \%}} \implies 4 = \frac{72}{\text{CAGR \%}} \implies \text{CAGR} = \mathbf{18\%}$.
* **Correct Answer**: **B**

## 2. Section 2 — Guesstimate Test

### Question G1: Estimate the total annual consumption of milk (in liters) in India.
* **Difficulty**: Medium
* **Time**: 10 Minutes
* **Step-by-Step Evaluation Checklist**:
  - [ ] Did the candidate start with Total Population = 1.4 Billion (~350M households)?
  - [ ] Did they split into direct consumption vs commercial consumption (sweet shops, tea/coffee, cheese)?
  - [ ] Did they assume average household consumption of 1.5 liters/day?
  - [ ] Calculation Check: $350\text{M households} \times 1.5\text{ L/day} \times 365\text{ days} \approx \mathbf{191\text{ Billion Liters/year}}$ (matches official Indian dairy production ~210B Liters).

## 3. Section 3 — Mini Case Test

### Case M1: Logistics Fleet Delivery Delays
* **Prompt**: A e-commerce logistics firm in Bengaluru experiences a **25% surge in customer complaints** regarding delayed package deliveries.
* **Time Limit**: 15 Minutes
* **Expected Solution Steps**:
  1. **Structure Value Chain**: Order Processing → Hub Sorting → Dispatch → Last-Mile Delivery.
  2. **Isolate Segment**: Data reveals last-mile delivery drivers in 3 specific zones account for 80% of delays.
  3. **Root Cause**: Construction on major arterial roads increased traffic congestion; static navigation routes failed to account for road closures.
  4. **Recommendation**: Implement real-time traffic-aware routing software for last-mile delivery mobile apps.
