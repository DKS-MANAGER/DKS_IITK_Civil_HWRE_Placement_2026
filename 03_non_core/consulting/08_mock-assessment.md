## 1. Section 1 — Consulting Math Diagnostic Test (10 Questions)

* **Time Limit**: 20 Minutes (2 minutes per question).
* **Instructions**: No calculators or spreadsheets. Solve using mental math or scratch paper.

---

### Question 1: Profit Margin & Percentage Change
A retail distributor's revenue grew from ₹40 Crore to ₹50 Crore (+25%), while operating expenses escalated from ₹30 Crore to ₹42 Crore (+40%). What was the percentage change in operating profit?
- A) +10%
- B) -20%
- C) -40%
- D) +20%

#### Solution & Verification:
- $\text{Initial Profit} = 40 - 30 = \mathbf{₹10\text{ Crore}}$.
- $\text{New Profit} = 50 - 42 = \mathbf{₹8\text{ Crore}}$.
- $\Delta \text{Profit \%} = \frac{8 - 10}{10} \times 100 = \mathbf{-20\%}$.
- **Correct Answer**: **B**

---

### Question 2: SaaS Unit Economics & LTV/CAC
An enterprise SaaS company has an Average Revenue Per User (ARPU) of ₹60,000/year, an 80% gross margin on software delivery, an annual customer churn rate of 16.67% (1/6), and an average Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) of ₹72,000. What is the LTV/CAC ratio?
- A) 2.5x
- B) 3.33x
- C) 4.0x
- D) 5.0x

#### Solution & Verification:
- $\text{Average Lifespan} = \frac{1}{\text{Churn}} = \frac{1}{1/6} = \mathbf{6\text{ years}}$.
- $\text{Annual Contribution Margin} = 60,000 \times 0.80 = \mathbf{₹48,000/\text{year}}$.
- $\text{LTV} = 48,000 \times 6 = \mathbf{₹288,000}$.
- $\text{LTV / CAC} = \frac{288,000}{72,000} = \mathbf{4.0\times}$.
- **Correct Answer**: **C**

---

### Question 3: Rule of 72 & Market Doubling
If an Indian industrial robotics market is projected to expand at an 18% Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR), approximately how many years will it take for the market to double in size?
- A) 3.0 years
- B) 4.0 years
- C) 5.5 years
- D) 6.0 years

#### Solution & Verification:
- Using Rule of 72: $\text{Doubling Time} \approx \frac{72}{\text{CAGR \%}} = \frac{72}{18} = \mathbf{4.0\text{ years}}$.
- **Correct Answer**: **B**

---

### Question 4: Break-Even Volume under Fixed Cost Inflation
A manufacturing plant incurs ₹60 Lakhs in monthly fixed overheads. The product sells for ₹500 per unit with a variable cost of ₹350 per unit. If raw material inflation increases variable costs to ₹380 per unit while price remains unchanged, how many additional units must the plant sell per month to maintain break-even?
- A) 5,000 units
- B) 10,000 units
- C) 15,000 units
- D) 20,000 units

#### Solution & Verification:
- $\text{Initial Unit CM} = 500 - 350 = \mathbf{₹150}$.
  - $\text{Initial Break-Even Volume} = \frac{₹6,000,000}{150} = \mathbf{40,000\text{ units/month}}$.
- $\text{New Unit CM} = 500 - 380 = \mathbf{₹120}$.
  - $\text{New Break-Even Volume} = \frac{₹6,000,000}{120} = \mathbf{50,000\text{ units/month}}$.
- $\Delta \text{Volume Needed} = 50,000 - 40,000 = \mathbf{+10,000\text{ units/month}}$.
- **Correct Answer**: **B**

### Question 5: Percentage Points vs. Percentage Growth
In FY24, a business unit achieved an EBITDA margin of 12% on ₹200 Crore revenue. In FY25, revenue grew to ₹250 Crore, and the EBITDA margin expanded to 15%. What was the increase in EBITDA margin in percentage points, and what was the percentage growth in absolute EBITDA?
- A) 3 pp increase; +25% EBITDA growth
- B) 3 pp increase; +56.25% EBITDA growth
- C) 25 pp increase; +56.25% EBITDA growth
- D) 3 pp increase; +30% EBITDA growth

#### Solution & Verification:
- Margin change in percentage points = $15\% - 12\% = \mathbf{3\text{ pp}}$.
- $\text{EBITDA}_{\text{FY24}} = 12\% \times 200\text{ Cr} = \mathbf{₹24\text{ Crore}}$.
- $\text{EBITDA}_{\text{FY25}} = 15\% \times 250\text{ Cr} = \mathbf{₹37.5\text{ Crore}}$.
- $\Delta \text{EBITDA \%} = \frac{37.5 - 24}{24} \times 100 = \frac{13.5}{24} \times 100 = \mathbf{+56.25\%}$.
- **Correct Answer**: **B**

---

### Question 6: Price Elasticity & Revenue Sensitivity
A premium quick-service beverage chain sells 20,000 artisanal coffees daily at ₹250. An operational test increases the price by 8% to ₹270, causing daily volume to contract by 4% to 19,200. What is the price elasticity of demand ($E_d$), and what is the change in daily gross revenue?
- A) $E_d = -0.50$; Revenue decreases by ₹1.84 Lakhs
- B) $E_d = -2.00$; Revenue increases by ₹1.84 Lakhs
- C) $E_d = -0.50$; Revenue increases by ₹1.84 Lakhs
- D) $E_d = -0.80$; Revenue increases by ₹2.50 Lakhs

#### Solution & Verification:
- $E_d = \frac{\% \Delta Q}{\% \Delta P} = \frac{-4\%}{+8\%} = \mathbf{-0.50}$ (Inelastic).
- $\text{Initial Revenue} = 20,000 \times 250 = \mathbf{₹50.00\text{ Lakhs}}$.
- $\text{New Revenue} = 19,200 \times 270 = \mathbf{₹51.84\text{ Lakhs}}$.
- $\Delta \text{Revenue} = 51.84 - 50.00 = \mathbf{+₹1.84\text{ Lakhs / day}}$.
- **Correct Answer**: **C**

---

### Question 7: Working Capital & Cash Conversion Cycle (CCC)
A supply-chain logistics company has Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO) = 45 days, Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) = 60 days, and Days Payable Outstanding (DPO) = 40 days. If supply-chain optimization reduces DIO to 35 days and accelerates collections so DSO improves to 50 days, while keeping supplier terms constant, what is the new Cash Conversion Cycle?
- A) 65 days
- B) 55 days
- C) 45 days
- D) 35 days

#### Solution & Verification:
- $\text{Initial CCC} = \text{DIO} + \text{DSO} - \text{DPO} = 45 + 60 - 40 = \mathbf{65\text{ days}}$.
- $\text{New CCC} = 35 + 50 - 40 = \mathbf{45\text{ days}}$ (Improvement of 20 days of operating cash flow).
- **Correct Answer**: **C**

---

### Question 8: Little's Law & Queue Throughput
A tech-support service center receives an average of 120 client query tickets per hour. On average, a ticket spends 30 minutes (0.5 hours) in the resolution pipeline. By applying Little's Law ($L = \lambda W$), how many active tickets are currently in the system?
- A) 40 tickets
- B) 60 tickets
- C) 120 tickets
- D) 240 tickets

#### Solution & Verification:
- $\lambda = 120\text{ tickets / hour}$.
- $W = 0.5\text{ hours}$.
- $L = \lambda \times W = 120 \times 0.5 = \mathbf{60\text{ tickets}}$.
- **Correct Answer**: **B**

---

### Question 9: Discounted Cash Flow (NPV) Quick Assessment
A port automation initiative requires a CapEx outlay of ₹50 Crore at $t=0$. It is projected to yield ₹15 Crore in net operational cash flows annually for 5 years. Assuming a 10% discount rate (5-year annuity factor = 3.79), what is the project's Net Present Value (NPV)?
- A) +₹6.85 Crore
- B) -₹3.15 Crore
- C) +₹15.00 Crore
- D) +₹25.00 Crore

#### Solution & Verification:
- $\text{PV of Cash Inflows} = ₹15\text{ Crore} \times 3.79 = \mathbf{₹56.85\text{ Crore}}$.
- $\text{NPV} = 56.85 - 50.00 = \mathbf{+₹6.85\text{ Crore}}$.
- **Correct Answer**: **A**

---

### Question 10: Multi-Entity Revenue & Margin Consolidation
Company A generated ₹500 Crore revenue at a 20% EBITDA margin (₹100 Cr EBITDA). Company B generated ₹800 Crore revenue at a 15% EBITDA margin (₹120 Cr EBITDA). Company A acquires Company B and realizes ₹30 Crore in operational cost synergies. What is the combined post-merger EBITDA margin?
- A) 16.50%
- B) 18.00%
- C) 19.23%
- D) 21.00%

#### Solution & Verification:
- $\text{Combined Revenue} = 500 + 800 = \mathbf{₹1,300\text{ Crore}}$.
- $\text{Combined EBITDA} = 100\text{ (A)} + 120\text{ (B)} + 30\text{ (Synergies)} = \mathbf{₹250\text{ Crore}}$.
- $\text{Combined Margin} = \frac{250}{1,300} \times 100 = \frac{25}{130} \times 100 = \mathbf{19.23\%}$.
- **Correct Answer**: **C**

## 2. Section 2 — Guesstimate Assessment (3 Structured Problems)

* **Time Limit**: 10 Minutes per problem.

### Guesstimate G1: Annual Fluid Milk Consumption in India (Top-Down)
- **Problem Statement**: Estimate total annual domestic and commercial liquid milk consumption (in Billion Liters) across India.
- **Evaluation Criteria & Solution Architecture**:
  1. **Population Foundation**: 1.40 Billion people $\rightarrow \approx 350\text{ Million households}$ (4 persons/HH).
  2. **Direct Household Consumption**:
     - Urban (35% = 120M HH): High daily milk purchase $\rightarrow 1.5\text{ Liters / household / day} \times 120\text{M} = 180\text{M L/day}$.
     - Rural (65% = 230M HH): Mix of subsistence livestock & packaged milk $\rightarrow 1.2\text{ Liters / household / day} \times 230\text{M} = 276\text{M L/day}$.
     - Subtotal Household = $180\text{M} + 276\text{M} = \mathbf{456\text{ Million Liters / day}}$.
  3. **Commercial & Institutional Usage**: Sweet shops (halwais), chai stalls, restaurants, bakeries consume $\approx 30\%$ of total liquid milk $\rightarrow \frac{456}{0.70} \approx \mathbf{650\text{ Million Liters / day}}$.
  4. **Annualized Output**:
     $$\text{Annual Milk Consumption} = 650\text{M L/day} \times 365 \approx \mathbf{237\text{ Billion Liters / year}}$$
  5. **Sanity Check**: Ministry of Fisheries, Animal Husbandry & Dairying reports national annual milk production of ~230–240 Billion Liters (FY23–FY24). Model error is within 5%.

---

### Guesstimate G2: Daily Ride-Hailing Trips in Mumbai (Demand & Supply)
- **Problem Statement**: Estimate the number of daily cab trips completed via app-based ride-hailing services (Ola/Uber) in Greater Mumbai.
- **Evaluation Criteria & Solution Architecture**:
  1. **Demand-Side Approach**:
     - Population of Mumbai MMR = 22 Million.
     - Working/Commuting population (20–60 yrs) = 60% = 13.2 Million.
     - Target socio-economic class (Upper/Middle using cabs) = 25% = 3.3 Million potential riders.
     - Modal split: Local trains (70%), Buses/Metro (15%), Two-wheelers/Autos (10%), Cabs/Ride-hail (5%).
     - Daily cab commuter riders = $3.3\text{M} \times 5\% \approx 165,000\text{ riders}$.
     - Trip frequency: Commuters make 2 trips/day (to and from work) = 330,000 trips.
     - Add non-commute trips (airport runs, leisure, shopping = +30%) = $\sim \mathbf{430,000\text{ trips / day}}$.
  2. **Supply-Side Sanity Check**:
     - Registered active app-cabs in Mumbai = ~50,000 vehicles.
     - Average trips per cab per 12-hour shift = 8 to 9 trips.
     - Total capacity = $50,000 \times 8.5 \approx \mathbf{425,000\text{ trips / day}}$.
  3. **Conclusion**: Both independent approaches converge on **420,000 to 450,000 daily trips**.

---

### Guesstimate G3: Number of Commercial Aircraft Operating in India
- **Problem Statement**: Estimate the active commercial passenger aircraft fleet flying for all Indian scheduled carriers (IndiGo, Air India group, SpiceJet, Akasa).
- **Evaluation Criteria & Solution Architecture**:
  1. **Passenger Demand Derivation**:
     - Annual domestic air passengers in India $\approx 150\text{ Million travelers / year}$.
     - Daily domestic passengers = $\frac{150\text{M}}{365} \approx \mathbf{410,000\text{ passengers / day}}$.
  2. **Aircraft Utilization & Capacity**:
     - Average narrow-body aircraft seating (A320 / B737 family) = 180 seats.
     - Average passenger load factor (occupancy) = 85% $\rightarrow 180 \times 0.85 \approx 153\text{ passengers / flight}$.
     - Daily flights needed = $\frac{410,000}{153} \approx \mathbf{2,680\text{ flight legs / day}}$.
  3. **Turnaround & Utilization**:
     - Indian carriers operate high fleet utilization: Average aircraft flies **4.0 to 4.5 legs per day**.
     - Active fleet required = $\frac{2,680\text{ legs}}{4.2\text{ legs/aircraft}} \approx \mathbf{638\text{ aircraft}}$.
  4. **International Fleet Addition & Maintenance Buffer**:
     - Add wide-body and international operations (+20%) = ~125 aircraft.
     - Add 10% spare/maintenance/AOG buffer = ~75 aircraft.
     - **Estimated Fleet** = $638 + 125 + 75 \approx \mathbf{840\text{ aircraft}}$.
  5. **Sanity Check**: DGCA records indicate ~800 to 850 active commercial aircraft in India (FY24–FY25).

## 3. Section 3 — Mini-Case Diagnostic Scenarios (2 Cases)

* **Time Limit**: 15 Minutes per case.
* **Format**: Structure the problem, prioritize hypotheses, and provide synthesis.

---

### Mini-Case 1: Logistics Platform Delivery Delay Surge
- **Prompt**: An e-commerce last-mile logistics provider in Bengaluru experiences a **25% surge in customer complaints** regarding delayed package deliveries over the past 6 weeks.
- **Candidate Framework & Diagnostic Path**:
  1. **Clarification**: Are delays widespread across all Bengaluru PIN codes or concentrated? *(Interviewer reveal: 75% of delays originate from 3 specific peripheral delivery hubs in Whitefield and Bellandur).*
  2. **Value Chain Decomposition**:
     $$\text{Total Delivery Cycle} = \text{Hub Sorting} + \text{Driver Dispatch} + \text{Transit Routing} + \text{Doorstep Handover}$$
  3. **Data Analysis**:
     - Hub sorting throughput and rider attendance are normal.
     - Transit duration increased by 42 minutes per route.
     - Investigation reveals major civic infrastructure construction (flyover closures) caused severe congestion. The dispatch algorithm utilized static distance-based routing rather than live GPS traffic data.
  4. **Recommendation**:
     - Integrate real-time traffic APIs (Google Maps Platform) into the courier dispatch app.
     - Re-cluster peripheral hubs into smaller, localized micro-fulfillment sorting nodes to reduce average delivery radius from 9 km to 4.5 km.
     - Introduce customer ETA notification updates with proactive SLA delay credits to curb grievance complaints.

---

### Mini-Case 2: Diagnostic Pathology Chain Margin Contraction
- **Prompt**: A chain of 80 pathology collection centers across North India saw revenue grow 12% while EBITDA margins fell from 24% to 17%. Diagnose and recommend.
- **Candidate Framework & Diagnostic Path**:
  1. **Structure (EBITDA Tree)**:
     $$\text{EBITDA Margin} = \frac{\text{Test Volume} \times \text{Avg Price} - (\text{Consumables} + \text{Technician Salaries} + \text{Logistics} + \text{Rent})}{\text{Revenue}}$$
  2. **Data Reveal**:
     - Overall patient volume grew 18%, but Average Realized Price per test fell 5%.
     - **Mix Shift Discovery**: The chain aggressively marketed home sample collection, which surged from 10% to 45% of total bookings.
     - **Cost Escalation**: Home collection requires dedicated phlebotomists traveling by two-wheeler. Fuel, travel allowances, and phlebotomist idle transit time escalated operating costs by ₹140 per home sample compared to center walk-ins. The home-collection convenience fee was priced at only ₹50.
  3. **Synthesis & Action Plan**:
     - **Reprice Home Collection**: Increase the home collection surcharge from ₹50 to ₹150, or make it complimentary only for premium master health checkup packages (>₹2,500).
     - **Route Optimization**: Batch home sample collection appointments by geographical pincode cluster rather than on-demand dispatch, doubling phlebotomist stops per hour from 1.4 to 2.8.

## 4. Diagnostic Scorecard & Remediation Framework

Evaluate performance using this objective diagnostic breakdown. Do not interpret these practice scores as official recruitment predictions or external percentiles.

| Assessment Dimension | Maximum Score | Passing Benchmark | Targeted Remediation if Benchmark Not Met |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Section 1: Consulting Math** | 10 Points | $\ge 8$ Points | Review [`05_consulting-math.md`](05_consulting-math.md); complete daily speed arithmetic drills on fractions, percentage points, and EBITDA bridges. |
| **Section 2: Guesstimate Structuring** | 15 Points (5 pts/Q) | $\ge 11$ Points | Study [`04_guesstimates.md`](04_guesstimates.md); practice explicit justification of segmentation filters and verify units at every step. |
| **Section 3: Mini-Case Reasoning** | 10 Points (5 pts/case)| $\ge 7$ Points | Review [`02_problem-solving.md`](02_problem-solving.md); focus on isolating geographic/operational segments before proposing solutions. |
| **Total Comprehensive Score** | **35 Points** | **$\ge 26$ Points** | Ready to proceed to full-length integrated partner simulations in [`12_mock-assessment.md`](12_mock-assessment.md). |

### Skill Dimension Scoring Rubric:

- **Mathematical Accuracy (0–10)**: Deduct 1 point for every calculation or conversion error (e.g. Lakhs vs Crores).
- **Structuring & MECE (0–10)**: Reward clear, non-overlapping branches; penalize premature brainstorming or jumping to conclusions.
- **Hypothesis Prioritization (0–5)**: Reward data-driven pruning of branches; penalize pursuing irrelevant branches after data confirms normalcy.
- **Executive Communication (0–10)**: Reward top-down Pyramid Principle synthesis, structured recommendations, and proactive risk identification.
