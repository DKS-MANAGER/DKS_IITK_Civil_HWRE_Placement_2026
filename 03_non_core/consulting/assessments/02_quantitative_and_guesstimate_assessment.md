## 2. Section A: Fast Mental Math & Financial Bridges (5 Questions — 15 Minutes)

### Question A1: Contribution Margin & Price Sensitivity
A subscription fitness app charges $30/month. Cloud infrastructure and payment gateway fees are $6/month/subscriber. Fixed corporate overhead is $1,200,000/year. 
- *Task*: If marketing lowers the subscription price by 20% to $24/month, by what percentage must the active subscriber base expand to maintain existing annual operating profit?

---

### Question A2: Three-Factor CAGR Rule of 72
A renewable solar EPC firm generated ₹40 Crore in FY21 and ₹160 Crore in FY27 (6-year compounding period).
- *Task*: (1) Using the Rule of 72 doubling logic, estimate the approximate CAGR. (2) Formulate the exact mathematical formula. (3) State whether linear approximation is valid for this growth.

---

### Question A3: Working Capital Cash Flow Release
An industrial distributor has:
- Annual Sales = $730M
- Annual COGS = $400M
- Days Sales Outstanding (DSO) = 60 days
- Days Inventory Outstanding (DIO) = 75 days
- Days Payables Outstanding (DPO) = 45 days
- *Task*: If an operational transformation compresses DSO by 10 days and expands DPO by 15 days, calculate the net one-time cash released onto the balance sheet.

---

### Question A4: Operating Leverage Margin Expansion
A manufacturing company has a Degree of Operating Leverage (DOL) of 3.5. In FY25, sales volume expands by 12% while prices and fixed costs remain unchanged.
- *Task*: Calculate the percentage growth in operating profit (EBIT).

---

### Question A5: LTV/CAC with Churn and Discount Rate
A B2B SaaS platform has:
- Annual Revenue per Account = $12,000
- Cost of Service Gross Margin = 80%
- Annual Account Logo Churn = 15%
- Cost of Capital (Discount Rate) = 10%
- Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) = $18,000
- *Task*: Calculate the discounted Customer Lifetime Value (LTV) and the LTV/CAC ratio. Is the customer acquisition economically viable?

## 3. Section B: Exhibit Interpretation & Profitability Bridge (15 Minutes)

### Exhibit 1: Regional Airline Route Economics (Per 100-Seat Regional Jet Flight)

| Operational Metric | Route A: Metro-to-Metro (Delhi–Mumbai) | Route B: Regional Connector (Delhi–Dehradun) |
| :--- | :---: | :---: |
| **Flight Distance** | 1,200 km | 250 km |
| **Available Seats** | 100 seats | 100 seats |
| **Passenger Load Factor** | **85% (85 passengers)** | **65% (65 passengers)** |
| **Average Fare per Passenger** | ₹6,000 | ₹3,500 |
| **Jet Fuel (ATF) Burn per Flight** | 3,200 Liters @ ₹100/L = ₹320,000 | 1,100 Liters @ ₹100/L = ₹110,000 |
| **Airport Landing & Parking Fees**| ₹80,000 | ₹25,000 |
| **Crew & Ground Handling (Fixed)**| ₹60,000 | ₹50,000 |
| **Aircraft Lease Depreciation** | ₹100,000 (allocated) | ₹40,000 (allocated) |

#### Required Analysis:
1. Calculate the Flight Contribution Margin (Revenue minus Fuel, Airport Fees, and Crew/Ground handling) for Route A and Route B.
2. Calculate the operating profit (EBIT) per flight after aircraft lease allocation for each route.
3. If oil prices spike by 25% (fuel increases to ₹125/L), what happens to Route B's operating profit? What strategic operational change should the airline make?

## 5. Complete Model Solutions & Marking Rubric

### Solution to Section A:
1. **A1 (Contribution Margin)**:
   - Initial CM = $30 - 6 = \$24/\text{subscriber}$.
   - New CM = $24 - 6 = \$18/\text{subscriber}$.
   - Ratio of old to new CM = $\frac{24}{18} = \frac{4}{3} \approx 1.333$.
   - The subscriber base must expand by **+33.33%** to deliver identical total contribution dollars.
2. **A2 (CAGR & Rule of 72)**:
   - ₹40 Cr to ₹160 Cr is a 4x increase (two doublings: $40 \to 80 \to 160$).
   - 2 doublings in 6 years $\implies$ doubles every 3 years.
   - Using Rule of 72: $\text{CAGR} \approx \frac{72}{3} = \mathbf{24.0\%}$.
   - Exact formula: $\text{CAGR} = (160/40)^{1/6} - 1 = 4^{0.1667} - 1 = \mathbf{25.99\%}$.
   - Linear approximation ($\frac{300\%}{6} = 50\%$) is completely invalid due to compounding.
3. **A3 (Working Capital Cash Release)**:
   - Daily Sales = $\$730\text{M} / 365 = \$2.0\text{M/day}$.
   - Daily COGS = $\$400\text{M} / 365 = \$1.096\text{M/day}$.
   - Compressing DSO by 10 days releases: $10 \times \$2.0\text{M} = \mathbf{\$20.0\text{M}}$.
   - Expanding DPO by 15 days retains: $15 \times \$1.096\text{M} = \mathbf{\$16.44\text{M}}$.
   - **Total Net Cash Released** = $\$20.0\text{M} + \$16.44\text{M} = \mathbf{\$36.44\text{ Million}}$.
4. **A4 (Operating Leverage)**:
   - $\% \Delta \text{EBIT} = \text{DOL} \times \% \Delta \text{Revenue} = 3.5 \times 12.0\% = \mathbf{+42.0\%}$.
5. **A5 (LTV/CAC)**:
   - Annual Gross Contribution = $\$12,000 \times 0.80 = \$9,600$.
   - Discounted LTV = $\frac{\$9,600 \times (1 - 0.15)}{0.10 + 0.15} = \frac{\$9,600 \times 0.85}{0.25} = \frac{\$8,160}{0.25} = \mathbf{\$32,640}$.
   - $\text{LTV / CAC} = \frac{\$32,640}{\$18,000} = \mathbf{1.81\times}$.
   - *Verdict*: Marginally viable ($>1.0\times$) but below the healthy growth benchmark of $\ge 3.0\times$.

### Solution to Section B (Exhibit 1):
1. **Route A (Metro-to-Metro)**:
   - Revenue = $85 \times ₹6,000 = \mathbf{₹510,000}$.
   - Direct Costs = Fuel (₹320k) + Airport (₹80k) + Crew (₹60k) = ₹460,000.
   - **Flight Contribution Margin** = $510,000 - 460,000 = \mathbf{+₹50,000}$.
   - Operating Profit (after ₹100k lease) = $50,000 - 100,000 = \mathbf{-₹50,000}$ (Loss).
2. **Route B (Regional Connector)**:
   - Revenue = $65 \times ₹3,500 = \mathbf{₹227,500}$.
   - Direct Costs = Fuel (₹110k) + Airport (₹25k) + Crew (₹50k) = ₹185,000.
   - **Flight Contribution Margin** = $227,500 - 185,000 = \mathbf{+₹42,500}$.
   - Operating Profit (after ₹40k lease) = $42,500 - 40,000 = \mathbf{+₹2,500}$ (Profitable).
3. **Oil Price Spike Impact on Route B**:
   - New fuel cost = $1,100\text{ L} \times ₹125 = ₹137,500$ (+$27,500).
   - Direct costs rise to ₹212,500 $\implies$ Contribution drops to ₹15,000.
   - Operating profit flips from +₹2,500 to **-₹25,000**.
   - *Strategic Recommendation*: Implement fuel surcharges or increase regional load factors from 65% to 75% via corporate tier partnerships.

### Solution to Section C (E-Rickshaw Battery Market):
- India installed base of electric 3-wheelers (E-Rickshaws + L5 autos): ~2.4 Million vehicles.
- Battery technology: 70% Lead-Acid (replaced every 12 months) + 30% Lithium-Ion (replaced every 36 months).
- Annual replacement demand:
  - Lead-Acid (70% = 1.68M): $1.68\text{M} \times 1.0 = 1,680,000\text{ pack sets/year}$.
  - Lithium-Ion (30% = 0.72M): $0.72\text{M} / 3 = 240,000\text{ packs/year}$.
- Total Annual Pack Replacements = $1.68\text{M} + 0.24\text{M} = \mathbf{1.92\text{ Million battery packs/year}}$.
- Blended price per pack replacement (Lead-acid set ~₹28,000; Li-ion ~₹65,000):
  - Lead-acid: $1.68\text{M} \times ₹28,000 = ₹4,704\text{ Cr}$.
  - Li-ion: $0.24\text{M} \times ₹65,000 = ₹1,560\text{ Cr}$.
  - **Total Market Value** = $₹4,704\text{ Cr} + ₹1,560\text{ Cr} = \mathbf{₹6,264\text{ Crore INR/year}}$ (~$750M USD).
