# Tools Lab 02: PowerPoint Executive Storylining & Slide Design

> **Lab Objective**: Master top-down Pyramid Principle slide architecture, transform descriptive headers into action titles, and design executive-ready charts for consulting presentations.  
> **Prerequisites**: None.  
> **Estimated Lab Time**: 1.5 hours.

---

## 1. The Executive Slide Reader Reality

Enterprise CEOs, Board Directors, and Partners do not read slides sequentially like a novel. They scan slides under severe time constraints:
- **3-Second Test**: The executive scans the slide title and the chart takeaway. If the title is merely a label (*"Q3 Sales Overview"*), the executive does not know what conclusion to draw.
- **Answer-First (Pyramid Principle)**: Always state the core recommendation or insight at the very top, followed by 3 structured supporting arguments, backed by empirical data exhibits.

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        PYRAMID PRINCIPLE SLIDE ARCHITECTURE                           │
├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ ACTION LEAD TITLE (The Answer / Insight)                                               │
│ "Expanding Regional Production to Gujarat Unlocks ₹180 Cr in Logistics Savings..."    │
```

## 2. Before-and-After Slide Makeovers

### Makeover 1: Operational Cost Analysis

#### ❌ Before (Weak / Descriptive Topic Header):
> **Header**: *Cost Breakdown of Manufacturing Operations (2021–2024)*  
> - We analyzed various cost centers across the manufacturing line.
> - Raw material prices have fluctuated over the last three years.
> - Labor costs are relatively stable across shifts.
> - Energy and electricity costs increased during summer peak months.
> - Packaging resin costs have also gone up.

*Why It Fails*: It provides a laundry list of observations without an overarching conclusion. The reader has no idea which cost driver matters or what action to take.

#### ✅ After (Executive Action Title with Quantified Hierarchy):
> **Action Title**: *Packaging Resin Inflation Drove 72% of Total Manufacturing Cost Escalation Since 2021; Power & Labor Remained Controlled*  
> 
> **Structured Slide Layout**:
> - **Visual Exhibit**: A 4-step cost waterfall showing baseline manufacturing cost ($100M) $\to$ Resin Inflation (+$18M) $\to$ Power (+$4M) $\to$ Labor (+$3M) $\to$ Ending Cost ($125M).
> - **Executive Callout Box**:
>   1. **Root Cause**: Procurement relied on spot-market resin purchases rather than negotiating 12-month forward contracts.
>   2. **Immediate Remedy**: Execute a consolidated volume contract across all 4 plants, locking in a 14% bulk discount.
>   3. **P&L Impact**: Recovers $2.5M in annual EBITDA starting in Q2.

### Makeover 2: Market Entry Feasibility

#### ❌ Before:
> **Header**: *German Renewable Energy Market Overview*  
> - Germany is located in Western Europe and has strong green policies.
> - The solar energy market is growing due to subsidies.
> - There are many local competitors already in the market.
> - Grid connection rules are strict and complicated.
> - We should think carefully before entering.

#### ✅ After:
> **Action Title**: *Entering Germany’s Commercial Solar Sector Unlocks €120M in Revenue, but Regulatory Feed-in Tariffs Sunset in 2026, Requiring a PPA Model*  
> 
> **Structured Slide Layout**:
> - **Left (Market Size & Growth)**: Bar chart showing commercial rooftop capacity expanding at 18% CAGR to 8.4 GW by 2027.
> - **Center (Regulatory Cliff)**: Highlight callout showing government feed-in subsidies expire in December 2026.
> - **Right (Strategic Go-To-Market Decision)**: Conclude that entering as a subsidized asset-owner is risky; recommend a **Corporate Power Purchase Agreement (PPA) developer model** with long-term industrial off-takers (BMW, BASF) to guarantee 15-year revenue certainty.

## 3. Chart Selection Decision Tree for Consultants

Never select charts randomly. Choose charts based on the analytical relationship you need to communicate:

| Analytical Relationship | Optimal Chart Type | Design Rule of Thumb |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Component Share of Total** | 100% Stacked Bar or Waterfall | Avoid pie charts with $>4$ slices; humans struggle to compare 2D angles. |
| **Stepwise Cost/Revenue Walk**| Horizontal/Vertical Waterfall | Color additions green, deductions red, and subtotals grey. |
| **Time-Series Metric Trend** | Clean Column Chart or Line Chart | Use column charts for $<8$ periods; use line charts for continuous multi-year data. |
| **Two-Dimensional Matrix** | 2x2 Bubble Matrix | Label quadrants clearly (e.g., Quick Wins vs. Strategic Bets). |
| **Correlation between Metrics**| Scatter Plot with Trendline | Annotate outliers with descriptive callout flags. |

## 4. The 5 Cardinal Rules of Slide Design

1. **One Key Takeaway per Slide**: If a slide contains two unrelated major ideas, split it into two slides.
2. **Action Titles Must Pass the "Read-Only" Test**: If an executive reads only the slide titles from Slide 1 to 20, they should understand the entire argument, evidence, and final recommendation without reading a single bullet.
3. **Data Labels Over Y-Axis Eye Tracking**: Always print the data value directly on top of the bar or line point; do not force the reader to trace horizontal gridlines back to the Y-axis.
4. **Color as an Analytical Tool, Not Decoration**: Use muted grey for baseline/context data; use single prominent accent color (Navy, Teal, or Orange) exclusively for the key finding you want the eye to follow.
5. **Eliminate Chartjunk**: Delete decorative 3D effects, drop shadows, heavy dark gridlines, and redundant legends.
