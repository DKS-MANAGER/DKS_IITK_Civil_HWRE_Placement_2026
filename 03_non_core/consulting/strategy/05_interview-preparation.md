# 05. Corporate Strategy: Selection Process & Interview Playbook

> **Document Type**: Tactical Recruitment Playbook & Interview Simulation Guide
> **Scope**: The 4 Selection Rounds → The Strategic Memo / CXO Case Format → Strategic Fit & Leadership Questions.

---

## 1. The 4 Stages of Corporate Strategy Recruitment

1. **Stage 1: Resume Screen & Analytical Assessment**:
   - High cutoff on academic consistency and quantitative rigor.
   - Cognitive aptitude and business logic screening test.
2. **Stage 2: Written Strategic Case / Presentation Memo (Take-Home or 90-Min Onsite)**:
   - Candidate is given a 10–15 page dossier of a conglomerate or disrupted business.
   - Deliverable: A 3-slide executive presentation or 2-page strategic memo recommending an actionable capital allocation, M&A acquisition, or market entry strategy.
3. **Stage 3: Live Strategic Case Interview**:
   - 45–60 minute interactive discussion with an Engagement Manager or Corporate Strategy Director.
   - Evaluates structured thinking, industry economics, and trade-off defense.
4. **Stage 4: Managing Director / CXO Partner Round**:
   - Strategic vision, cultural alignment, long-term leadership potential, and deep resume defense.

---

## 2. Navigating the CXO Strategic Presentation Round

When presenting a strategic recommendation to C-suite leaders:
1. **Never Walk Chronologically Through Your Slide Deck**:
   - Start immediately with the decision: *"We recommend acquiring Target B for ₹450 Cr to secure captive EV battery cell manufacturing, capturing ₹60 Cr in annual synergies with a 4.2-year payback."*
2. **Present Clear Alternatives**:
   - CXOs want to know what options were rejected and why. Always present a 3-way evaluation:
     - Option 1: Organic Greenfield Build (Too slow; 48-month lead time).
     - Option 2: Acquisition of Target B (**Recommended**; accelerates time-to-market by 3 years).
     - Option 3: Joint Venture with Global Supplier (High partner friction; low control).
3. **Acknowledge Key Risks Proactively**:
   - State the top 2 execution risks and their mitigations before the interviewer raises them.
