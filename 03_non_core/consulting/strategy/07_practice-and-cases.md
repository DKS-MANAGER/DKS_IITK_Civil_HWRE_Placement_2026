## Case 1: Conglomerate Portfolio Carve-Out & EVA Optimization

### Candidate Briefing & Problem Statement
> *“Our client is the Chairman of a diversified infrastructure and capital goods conglomerate generating ₹25,000 Crore in annual revenue. The conglomerate's stock has underperformed the Nifty 50 index by 40% over the past 4 years. An activist shareholder has acquired a 4% equity stake and demanded the board immediately divest the Heavy Industrial Forgings division (₹7,000 Cr revenue). The board wants to know: Does carving out this division create sustainable long-term value, how should stranded overhead be treated, and where should capital be redeployed?”*

### Step 1: Strategic Diagnostic & Economic Value Added (EVA)
1. **Analyze Return on Invested Capital (ROIC) vs. Cost of Capital (WACC)**:
   - Group WACC = **11.0%**.
   - **Heavy Industrial Forgings Division**:
     - Revenue = ₹7,000 Crore; EBIT = ₹560 Crore (8.0% margin).
     - Net Operating Capital Employed = **₹7,000 Crore** (Asset turnover = 1.0x).
     - Post-tax NOPAT (25% tax) = $560 \times 0.75 = \mathbf{₹420\text{ Crore}}$.
     - $\text{Post-tax ROIC} = \frac{420}{7,000} = \mathbf{6.0\%}$.
     - **Economic Value Added (EVA)**:
       $$\text{EVA} = \text{Capital} \times (\text{ROIC} - \text{WACC}) = ₹7,000\text{ Cr} \times (6.0\% - 11.0\%) = \mathbf{-₹350\text{ Crore / year}}$$
   - *Strategic Conclusion*: The Forgings division destroys ₹350 Crore of economic shareholder wealth every year. The activist investor's thesis is mathematically justified.

2. **Evaluate Stranded Corporate Overheads**:
   - Total group corporate headquarters overhead = ₹400 Crore/year.
   - Forgings was allocated ₹120 Crore of this corporate overhead based on revenue share.
   - However, audit reveals that only ₹40 Crore of these overheads are directly tied to Forgings (dedicated legal, HR, IT licenses).
   - **₹80 Crore represents stranded overhead** (group corporate jets, headquarters real estate, executive salaries) that will not disappear upon divestment and will burden remaining divisions unless actively eliminated.

### Step 2: Carve-Out Options & Valuation Analysis
1. **Option A: Trade Sale to Global Strategic Buyer**:
   - Valuation multiple: Peer industrials trade at 5.5x EV/EBITDA.
   - Forgings EBITDA = ₹750 Crore $\rightarrow \text{Enterprise Value} \approx \mathbf{₹4,125\text{ Crore}}$.
2. **Option B: Independent Spin-Off (IPO / Demerger)**:
   - Existing shareholders receive shares in the new standalone entity.
   - Eliminates conglomerate discount on remaining business immediately.
3. **Capital Redeployment Strategy**:
   - Deploy ₹4,000 Crore divestiture proceeds into the conglomerate's **Renewable Power Transmission EPC** division, where post-tax ROIC is **19.5%** and backlog is expanding at 25% CAGR.

### Step 3: Executive Recommendation to the Board
> *“We recommend the board **proceed with a structured trade sale of the Forgings division targeting ₹4,200 Crore**, combined with three mandatory execution safeguards:*  
> 1. **Establish a 24-Month Transitional Service Agreement (TSA)**: Charge the acquirer ₹60 Crore/year for shared ERP and procurement services to cushion stranded overhead.  
> 2. **Eliminate ₹80 Crore in Stranded Group Overheads**: Restructure headquarters leadership layers to ensure the parent company’s margins do not degrade post-divestment.  
> 3. **Ring-Fence Capital for Transmission EPC**: Channel 100% of net proceeds into the 19.5% ROIC Transmission business, unlocking over **₹6,500 Crore in incremental equity value** within 3 years.”*

## Case 2: Enterprise Platform Disruption Defense (SaaS vs. On-Premise)

### Candidate Briefing & Problem Statement
> *“Our client is an established enterprise Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) software provider in India with ₹4,000 Crore annual revenue. For 15 years, they have operated a traditional On-Premise software model (selling large perpetual licenses upfront with 20% annual maintenance contracts). Over the past 18 months, nimble cloud-native Software-as-a-Service (SaaS) challengers have begun winning 35% of mid-market procurement RFPs by offering monthly subscription pricing and rapid 4-week deployment. The CEO is asking: How should we defend our franchise, and what are the financial trade-offs of migrating to a pure SaaS model?”*

### Step 1: Disruption Analysis (Hamilton Helmer's 7 Powers Framework)


### Step 2: The SaaS Migration "Valley of Death" (Cash Flow J-Curve)
Why has the incumbent delayed transitioning to SaaS? The candidate must model the **revenue transition valley**:
- **On-Premise Model per Customer**:
  - Year 1: ₹10 Crore upfront perpetual license + ₹2 Crore maintenance = **₹12 Crore cash collected**.
  - Years 2–5: ₹2 Crore annual maintenance contract (AMC).
  - 5-Year Cumulative Cash = $12 + (4 \times 2) = \mathbf{₹20\text{ Crore}}$.
- **Pure SaaS Subscription Model per Customer**:
  - Subscription fee = **₹3.6 Crore / year**.
  - Year 1 Cash = **₹3.6 Crore** (a **70% cash flow drop in Year 1** compared to On-Premise!).
  - 5-Year Cumulative Cash = $5 \times 3.6 = \mathbf{₹18\text{ Crore}}$.
- **Corporate Implication**: If the incumbent switches all new sales to SaaS overnight, annual revenue drops from ₹4,000 Cr to ~₹1,800 Cr in Year 1, causing public stock price collapse and debt covenant breaches.

### Step 3: Strategic Dual-Track Defense Architecture
1. **Segment Separation**:
   - **Tier-1 Large Enterprise Clients (>₹5,000 Cr revenue)**: High switching costs protect the core. Keep them on On-Premise / Private Cloud, increasing annual maintenance fees by 5% while providing enhanced cyber-security patches.
   - **Mid-Market Segment (₹200 Cr – ₹2,000 Cr revenue)**: Vulnerable to SaaS. Build a distinct cloud-native multi-tenant SaaS version (branded as *Apex CloudCore*) hosted on AWS/Azure.
2. **Hybrid Commercial Bridge**:
   - Allow enterprise clients to convert existing perpetual license credits into SaaS subscriptions, removing balance-sheet friction while locking in 5-year contracts.
3. **Monetize the Ecosystem**:
   - Open APIs to third-party developers, creating a proprietary B2B app marketplace (charging 20% platform take-rate).

## Case 3: Cross-Border Strategic M&A Screening & Synergy Due Dilution

### Candidate Briefing & Problem Statement
> *“Our client is a top-3 Indian pharmaceutical company generating ₹18,000 Crore in revenue, with a war chest of ₹6,000 Crore in cash. They are evaluating acquiring **BioSterile SA**, a specialized French sterile injectable manufacturer, for **€500 Million (~₹4,500 Crore)**. BioSterile generates €140M in revenue and €35M in EBITDA (25% margin). The Board has tasked us with answering: Is the valuation justified, what are the hard vs. soft synergies, and what regulatory pitfalls could destroy deal value?”*

### Step 1: Valuation Multiple & Financial Screening
- **Purchase Price**: €500 Million.
- **Target EBITDA**: €35 Million.
- **Implied Entry Multiple**:
  $$\text{EV / EBITDA Multiple} = \frac{€500\text{M}}{€35\text{M}} = \mathbf{14.3x}$$
- **Industry Benchmark**: European specialty generic injectables trade between **11.0x and 13.0x EBITDA**. The deal carries an implied **~15% acquisition control premium**.

### Step 2: Rigorous Synergy Quantification (Hard vs. Soft)

| Synergy Category | Annual Run-Rate Impact | Realization Horizon | Confidence Level & Risk |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Cost Synergy 1: API Sourcing Arbitrage** | **+€8.0 Million / year** | 12–18 months | **High (Hard Synergy)**: Substitute expensive EU active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs) with client's USFDA-approved Indian API plants. |
| **Cost Synergy 2: SG&A & Regulatory Filings** | **+€4.5 Million / year** | 18–24 months | **High (Hard Synergy)**: Consolidate duplicate European regulatory filing teams and pharmacovigilance software. |
| **Revenue Synergy 1: Cross-Selling to US/UK** | **+€12.0 Million gross revenue**<br>*(at 30% margin = **+€3.6M EBITDA**)* | 24–36 months | **Moderate (Soft Synergy)**: Distribute BioSterile injectables through client's hospital network in the US. |
| **Total Net EBITDA Synergies** | **+€16.1 Million / year** | By Year 3 | Blended post-synergy EBITDA = $35 + 16.1 = \mathbf{€51.1\text{ Million}}$. |

#### Effective Post-Synergy Entry Multiple:
$$\text{Post-Synergy EV / EBITDA} = \frac{€500\text{M}}{€51.1\text{M}} = \mathbf{9.78x}$$
*(The transaction multiple compresses from 14.3x to an attractive 9.8x once hard synergies are executed).*

### Step 3: Key Due Diligence Risks & Deal Breakers
1. **European Works Council & Labor Rigidity**: French labor regulations impose strict restrictions on headcount rationalization. Attempting aggressive workforce layoffs will trigger union strikes and French Ministry of Economy scrutiny.
2. **Manufacturing Regulatory Compliance (EU-GMP)**: Sterile injectables carry severe contamination risks. A single Form 483 / Warning Letter from the European Medicines Agency (EMA) can halt the main plant, erasing €30M in revenue overnight.
3. **Key Person Retention**: The top 12 formulation scientists possess the proprietary know-how for complex lipid nanoparticle injectables.

### Step 4: Deal Structuring Recommendation
- **Proceed with the Acquisition**, but structure the transaction using an **Earn-Out Structure**:
  - **Upfront Cash**: €400 Million (80%).
  - **Contingent Earn-Out**: €100 Million (20%) tied to EMA regulatory plant compliance and achieving €45M in EBITDA by Year 2.
  - Implement a 3-year golden handcuff retention pool (€15M) for key R&D formulation scientists.
