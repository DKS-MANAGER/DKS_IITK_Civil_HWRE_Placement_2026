## 1. Case Overview & Candidate Instructions

### Candidate Briefing & Problem Statement
> *“Our client is Apex Industries, a diversified Indian industrial conglomerate generating **₹35,000 Crore in annual revenue** across three business divisions:*  
> 1. **Division A: Thermal Power Equipment** (Heavy boilers, coal plant engineering).  
> 2. **Division B: Specialty Advanced Materials** (Aerospace composites, high-performance polymers).  
> 3. **Division C: Commercial Infrastructure & Fleet Logistics** (Port logistics, heavy freight).  
> *Apex trades at a conglomerate discount of **6.5x EV/EBITDA**, compared to specialized pure-play peers that trade at 11x–14x.*  
> *The Chairman and Group Executive Board have commissioned a comprehensive 5-year portfolio review to address two strategic dilemmas:*  
> 1. **Divestment Decision**: A global private equity fund has submitted an unsolicited offer of **₹6,000 Crore** to acquire Division A (Thermal Power). Should Apex divest this legacy cash cow?  
> 2. **Capital Reallocation & M&A**: Apex's corporate development team proposes acquiring **H2-Volt GmbH**, a German green hydrogen electrolyzer technology pioneer, for **€600 Million (~₹5,400 Crore)**. Does this M&A transaction create genuine economic value (ROIC > WACC), or does it destroy shareholder capital?”*

## 4. Section 3 — Step-by-Step Quantitative Solution & Analysis

### Step 1: Evaluate Division A (Thermal Power) Divestiture Offer
1. **Analyze Return on Capital**:
   - Division A consumes **₹12,500 Crore** of capital employed (41% of group capital!).
   - Post-tax NOPAT = ₹1,125 Crore $\rightarrow \text{Post-tax ROIC} = \frac{1,125}{12,500} = \mathbf{9.0\%}$.
   - Since **9.0% ROIC < 11.5% WACC**, Division A is actively destroying shareholder economic value ($\text{Economic Value Added / EVA} = 9.0\% - 11.5\% = -2.5\% \rightarrow \mathbf{-₹312.5\text{ Cr/year}}$).
2. **Evaluate the ₹6,000 Crore PE Acquisition Offer**:
   - Standalone Thermal Power EBITDA = ₹1,960 Crore.
   - Market peer multiple for thermal equipment (Exhibit 3) = **4.0x EV/EBITDA**.
   - Market enterprise valuation = $1,960 \times 4.0 = \mathbf{₹7,840\text{ Crore}}$.
   - **Interviewer Counter-Push**: The PE offer of ₹6,000 Cr represents a multiple of $\frac{6,000}{1,960} = \mathbf{3.06x\text{ EBITDA}}$, which is an **18% discount** to fair market peer multiples.
   - **Strategic Verdict on Divestiture**: *Accept divestment in principle to eliminate EVA destruction, but counter-negotiate or run a competitive auction targeting **₹7,500 Crore** (3.8x EBITDA).*

---

### Step 2: Evaluate the H2-Volt Acquisition Economics
1. **Total Capital Invested in M&A**:
   $$\text{Total Capital Outlay} = ₹5,400\text{ Cr (Purchase Price)} + ₹1,200\text{ Cr (Giga-factory CapEx)} = \mathbf{₹6,600\text{ Crore}}$$

2. **Projected Year 3 Operating Return**:
   - Projected Year 3 EBITDA = ₹567 Crore.
   - D&A estimated at 20% of EBITDA $\approx$ ₹113 Crore $\rightarrow \text{EBIT} \approx ₹454\text{ Crore}$.
   - Post-Tax NOPAT (at 25% tax) = $454 \times 0.75 = \mathbf{₹340.5\text{ Crore}}$.

3. **Year 3 Return on Invested Capital (ROIC)**:
   $$\text{ROIC}_{\text{Year 3}} = \frac{₹340.5\text{ Crore NOPAT}}{₹6,600\text{ Crore Capital}} = \mathbf{5.16\%}$$

4. **Strategic Deduction**:
   - Even under optimistic revenue scaling (€120M $\rightarrow$ €350M), Year 3 ROIC is only **5.16%**, far below Apex's hurdle rate of **11.5% WACC**.
   - The transaction would require over 7 years to reach economic value creation, carrying immense execution risk in a rapidly evolving electrolyzer market with Chinese OEM pricing pressure.

---

### Step 3: SOTP Multiple Arbitrage & Portfolio Capital Reallocation
What happens if Apex divests Division A and reallocates capital to **Division B (Specialty Materials)** instead of the risky M&A?

1. **Division B Economics**:
   - Post-tax ROIC = **18.75%** (far exceeds 11.5% WACC).
   - Historical CAGR = **+18.0%**.
   - Trading peer multiple = **14.0x EV/EBITDA**.
2. **SOTP Valuation Impact**:
   - If Division B is scaled with the ₹6,000 Cr divestiture proceeds, expanding EBITDA from ₹2,240 Cr to ₹3,200 Cr over 3 years:
   $$\text{Division B Standalone EV at 14x} = ₹3,200\text{ Cr} \times 14.0 = \mathbf{₹44,800\text{ Crore}}$$
   - Division B alone would be worth more than the entire current conglomerate market capitalization (₹37,440 Cr)!

## 5. Section 4 — Executive Recommendation & Boardroom Synthesis

### Spoken 60-Second Recommendation to the Board:
> *“Chairman and Members of the Board, our recommendation is structured in three decisive strategic moves:*  
> 1. **Divest Division A via Competitive Auction**: Division A’s 9.0% ROIC is destroying economic value against our 11.5% WACC. We recommend rejecting the opportunistic ₹6,000 Cr bilateral offer and running a structured auction to secure ₹7,500 Cr (3.8x EBITDA) from sovereign or strategic buyers.  
> 2. **Reject the 100% Acquisition of H2-Volt**: At €600M plus ₹1,200 Cr CapEx, H2-Volt yields a meager 5.2% ROIC by Year 3. Instead, negotiate a **15% strategic minority stake with an exclusive India manufacturing licensing agreement** for €75M, securing technology transfer without balance-sheet dilution.  
> 3. **Reallocate Capital to Specialty Materials**: Deploy ₹5,000 Cr of divestment proceeds into expanding Division B’s aerospace and electronics composite manufacturing, where our 18.75% ROIC and 14.0x market multiple will unlock over **₹15,000 Crore in net shareholder enterprise value** within 36 months.*  
> *Next Steps: Appoint investment bankers for the Division A auction and form an integration team for the H2-Volt licensing partnership.”*
