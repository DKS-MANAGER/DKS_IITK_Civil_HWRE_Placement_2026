### Q1 [Corporate Strategy — Conglomerate Parenting Advantage]
**Problem**: A diversified Indian conglomerate with operations in Textiles (EBITDA margin 8%, ROIC 7%) and IT Services (EBITDA margin 22%, ROIC 30%) trades at a 30% conglomerate discount compared to the sum of standalone peer valuations. The board asks strategy: Should we keep, restructure, or spin off Textiles?  
**Solution**:
1. Evaluate EVA of Textiles: Since ROIC (7%) < WACC (~10%), Textiles **destroys economic value** and drags down the corporate parent's market multiple.
2. Strategic Options:
   - *Spin-off / Carve-Out*: Creates shareholder value immediately by eliminating the conglomerate discount and allowing capital to be reinvested into high-ROIC IT Services.
   - *Operational Turnaround*: Only viable if Textiles can reach >11% ROIC within 18 months via technical automation.
3. **Recommendation**: Execute an IPO carve-out or private equity sale of Textiles; reallocate capital to scaling IT digital capabilities.

### Q2 [Competitive Strategy — Counter-Positioning Moat]
**Problem**: Define Hamilton Helmer's Counter-Positioning moat. Why was Kodak unable to prevent bankruptcy despite inventing the digital camera in 1975?  
**Solution**:
- **Definition**: Counter-Positioning occurs when an entrant adopts an innovative business model that the incumbent cannot copy without inflicting collateral damage on its own existing high-margin core business.
- **The Kodak Dilemma**: Kodak earned 70% gross margins selling analog chemical film and photo paper (the razor-and-blades consumables). Transitioning to digital cameras would eliminate film processing entirely, cannibalizing its core multi-billion dollar profit pool. Kodak was economically paralyzed while digital newcomers with zero legacy film revenue captured the consumer market.

### Q3 [M&A Strategy — Synergy Realization & Deal Breakeven]
**Problem**: Acquirer pays ₹2,000 Cr to acquire Target with standalone Net Income of ₹100 Cr (20x P/E). Transaction is funded with 100% debt at 9.0% pre-tax interest (tax rate = 25%). Calculate the minimum annual pre-tax cost synergies needed to make the acquisition breakeven to earnings.  
**Solution**:
1. After-tax interest cost on debt: $2,000 \times 0.09 \times (1 - 0.25) = ₹135\text{ Cr}$.
2. Target Net Income contribution: ₹100 Cr.
3. After-tax earnings shortfall: $135 - 100 = ₹35\text{ Cr}$.
4. **Required Pre-Tax Synergies**: $\frac{35}{1 - 0.25} = \frac{35}{0.75} = \mathbf{₹46.67\text{ Crore / year}}$.

### Q4 [Capital Allocation — Economic Value Added (EVA)]
**Problem**: Division A has Invested Capital of ₹500 Cr and NOPAT of ₹60 Cr. Division B has Invested Capital of ₹200 Cr and NOPAT of ₹30 Cr. WACC is 10.0%. Which division creates greater economic wealth?  
**Solution**:
- Division A: $\text{ROIC} = \frac{60}{500} = 12.0\%$. $\text{EVA} = 500 \times (12\% - 10\%) = \mathbf{+₹10.0\text{ Cr}}$.
- Division B: $\text{ROIC} = \frac{30}{200} = 15.0\%$. $\text{EVA} = 200 \times (15\% - 10\%) = \mathbf{+₹10.0\text{ Cr}}$.
- *Strategic Insight*: Both divisions create the exact same absolute economic wealth (+₹10 Cr), but Division B is far more capital-efficient (15% ROIC vs 12%), making it the superior candidate for marginal growth capital.
