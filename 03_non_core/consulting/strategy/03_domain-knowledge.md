## 1. Corporate Strategy vs. Business-Unit (Competitive) Strategy

The central distinction in strategic management:

```text
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                        THE TWO LEVELS OF STRATEGIC DECISION-MAKING                     │
├───────────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. CORPORATE STRATEGY                     │ 2. BUSINESS-UNIT (COMPETITIVE) STRATEGY   │
│    "Which businesses should we be in?"    │    "How do we win in this specific market?"│
├───────────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Portfolio scope & multi-business mix    │ • Cost leadership vs. Differentiation      │
│ • Capital allocation across divisions     │ • Pricing power & customer willingness-to-pay│
│ • Mergers, Acquisitions & Divestitures    │ • Competitive advantage & structural moats │
│ • Corporate parenting advantage & synergy │ • Go-to-market execution & product features│
└───────────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### The Corporate Parenting Advantage Test
A multi-business corporation (e.g., Tata Group or General Electric) creates value if and only if:
$$\text{Value of Multi-Business Portfolio} > \sum_{i} \text{Value of Standalone Individual Businesses}$$
If the corporate parent adds overhead, bureaucracy, and misallocates capital across divisions, the businesses are worth more broken apart $\implies$ **The Conglomerate Discount**.

## 2. Industry Structure & Industry Economics (Bain & Porter Foundations)

Why are some industries consistently lucrative (e.g., software, commercial aerospace) while others destroy capital for decades (e.g., commercial airlines, bulk shipping)?

### 1. Structural Industry Determinants
1. **Capital Intensity & Sunk Costs**: High unrecoverable asset investments (e.g., building a ₹15,000 Cr semiconductor fab) create extreme exit barriers, leading to destructive price wars during downturns.
2. **Product Differentiation**: Commodity industries without switching barriers compete purely on marginal cost.
3. **Capacity Lumpy Cycles**: Adding capacity in multi-billion dollar lumps creates recurring boom-and-bust cycles.

## 3. Portfolio Strategy & Capital Allocation Frameworks

Corporate strategy teams allocate finite parent company capital (equity cash and debt borrowing capacity) across competing business units.

### 1. Economic Value Added (EVA) & ROIC vs. WACC
$$\text{EVA} = \text{NOPAT} - (\text{Invested Capital} \times \text{WACC}) = \text{Invested Capital} \times (\text{ROIC} - \text{WACC})$$
- A business unit with ₹1,000 Cr in capital generating 15% ROIC against a 10% WACC generates **₹50 Cr in positive EVA**.
- A business unit growing revenue at 25% but earning an 8% ROIC against a 10% WACC **destroys ₹20 Cr in economic value every year**. Growth without economic spread accelerates corporate ruin!

### 2. The Growth-Share Portfolio Matrix (BCG Reimagined)
```text
┌──────────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────────┐
│ HIGH MARKET SHARE, HIGH GROWTH       │ LOW MARKET SHARE, HIGH GROWTH        │
│          [ STARS ]                   │      [ QUESTION MARKS ]              │
│ Self-sustaining, invest aggressively │ Heavy cash burn; must build moat or  │
│ to maintain market leadership.       │ divest before capital is depleted.   │
├──────────────────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────────────┤
│ HIGH MARKET SHARE, LOW GROWTH        │ LOW MARKET SHARE, LOW GROWTH         │
│        [ CASH COWS ]                 │           [ DOGS ]                   │
│ Minimal reinvestment needed;         │ Harvest cash, freeze capital, or     │
│ milk surplus cash to fund Stars.     │ execute strategic carve-out/sale.    │
└──────────────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 4. Inorganic Growth: M&A Strategy, Synergies & Due Diligence

Corporate strategy teams evaluate whether to enter a market via **Organic Greenfield Build**, **Joint Venture / Partnership**, or **M&A Acquisition**.

### 1. The Build vs. Buy vs. Partner Framework
- **Build (Organic)**: Adopt when the firm already possesses core capabilities, industry change is slow, and IP cannot be acquired.
- **Buy (M&A)**: Adopt when speed-to-market is existential, scale economies dominate, or target possesses a cornered resource (patents/licenses).
- **Partner (JV/Alliance)**: Adopt when regulatory restrictions prevent full ownership, or when sharing capital risk in uncertain frontier technology.

### 2. Synergy Categorization & Realization Haircuts
$$\text{Net Deal Value Created} = \text{PV of Standalone Target} + \text{PV of Synergies} - \text{Purchase Price Paid}$$
1. **Cost Synergies (High Confidence - 80% Realization Rate)**:
   - Eliminating duplicate corporate headquarters, redundant C-suite, back-office IT consolidation.
   - Bulk procurement discounts on combined raw material purchases.
2. **Revenue Synergies (Low Confidence - 30% Realization Rate)**:
   - Cross-selling target products through acquirer's global distribution footprint.
   - Joint product bundling.
