## 1. Business Metrics & Funnel Frameworks

Analytics professionals translate ambiguous corporate performance into structured mathematical driver trees.

```text
                               AARRR PIRATE METRIC FUNNEL
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                                                         │
│  ACQUISITION    ──>  How do users find you? (CAC, App Downloads, CTR, CPC)              │
│       │                                                                                 │
│  ACTIVATION     ──>  Do users experience the core value? (Signup %, Onboarding %, Time) │
│       │                                                                                 │
│  RETENTION      ──>  Do users come back? (D1, D7, D30 Retention, Churn %, Cohort Decay) │
│       │                                                                                 │
│  REVENUE        ──>  How do you make money? (GMV, AOV, Take Rate, ARPU, LTV, CM2)       │
│       │                                                                                 │
│  REFERRAL       ──>  Do users tell others? (Viral Coefficient K, Net Promoter Score NPS)│
│                                                                                         │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Essential Business Metrics & Equations
| Metric | Mathematical Formula | `[PREPARATION HEURISTIC]` Strategic Meaning & Context |
|:---|:---|:---|
| **Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)**| $\frac{\text{Total S\&M Spend}}{\text{New Customers Acquired}}$ | Total blended marketing spend required to acquire one incremental paying user. |
| **Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)** | $\frac{\text{ARPU} \times \text{Gross Margin } \%}{\text{Monthly Churn Rate}}$ | Total net gross profit generated by a customer over their entire lifecycle. |
| **LTV / CAC Ratio** | $\frac{\text{LTV}}{\text{CAC}}$ | `[RULE OF THUMB]` $<1.0$ (Unsustainable), $3.0\times$ (Healthy benchmark), $>5.0\times$ (Under-investing in growth). |
| **DAU / MAU Ratio (Stickiness)** | $\frac{\text{Daily Active Users}}{\text{Monthly Active Users}}$ | Measures user engagement frequency ($>20\%$ is good, $>50\%$ is world-class social media). |
| **Net Revenue Retention (NRR)** | $\frac{\text{Starting ARR} + \text{Expansion} - \text{Contraction} - \text{Churn}}{\text{Starting ARR}} \times 100$ | SaaS growth metric measuring expansion from existing cohorts ($>100\%$ indicates net expansion). |
| **Viral Coefficient ($K$)** | $i \times c$ | Where $i$ = Invites sent per user, $c$ = Conversion rate per invite. If $K > 1.0$, organic viral loop exists. |

### 1. Sample Size Calculation Formula & Derivation
For a two-proportion test comparing control ($p_1$) and treatment ($p_2$) with equal allocation ($50/50$ split):
$$n = \frac{2 \cdot (Z_{\alpha/2} + Z_{\beta})^2 \cdot p(1-p)}{\Delta^2}$$
Where:
- $p$: Pooled baseline proportion $\frac{p_1 + p_2}{2} \approx p_{\text{baseline}}$.
- $\Delta$: **Absolute Minimum Detectable Effect (MDE)** ($|p_2 - p_1|$).
- $\alpha$: Type I error rate (Significance level, typically $\alpha = 0.05 \implies Z_{\alpha/2} \approx 1.960$).
- $1 - \beta$: Statistical Power (Probability of detecting a true effect, typically $80\% \implies Z_{\beta} \approx 0.842$).

**The Standard "Rule of 16" Approximation**:
$$(Z_{0.025} + Z_{0.20})^2 = (1.960 + 0.842)^2 = (2.802)^2 \approx 7.85 \implies 2 \times 7.85 \approx 15.7 \approx 16$$
$$\implies n \approx \frac{16 \cdot p(1-p)}{\Delta^2} \quad \text{[PREPARATION HEURISTIC APPROXIMATION]}$$

* **Absolute vs. Relative Lift Distinction**:
  - *Baseline*: $p = 5.0\% = 0.05$.
  - *Relative Lift ($r$)*: $+10\% \implies p_{\text{treatment}} = 0.05 \times (1 + 0.10) = 0.055$.
  - *Absolute Lift ($\Delta$)*: $\Delta = 0.055 - 0.050 = 0.005$ (0.5 percentage points).
  - *Sample Size Required*:
    $$n \approx \frac{16 \times 0.05 \times 0.95}{(0.005)^2} = \frac{0.76}{0.000025} = \mathbf{30,400 \text{ users per variant (Total: 60,800)}}$$

### 2. Primary, Secondary & Guardrail Metrics
* **Primary Metric**: The decision driver directly tied to the experiment's goal (e.g., Checkout Conversion Rate, Add-to-Cart Rate).
* **Secondary Metrics**: Diagnostic signals explaining user behavior along the funnel (e.g., Search Click-Through Rate, Cart Size).
* **Guardrail Metrics**: Critical operational and business health constraints that must NOT degrade (e.g., Page Load Latency $< 200\text{ms}$, Cancellation Rate, App Crash Frequency, Customer Unsubscribe Rate). A test with statistically significant conversion lift must still be killed if guardrail metrics degrade unacceptably.

### 3. Sample Ratio Mismatch (SRM) Diagnosis
* **Test**: Chi-Square Goodness-of-Fit test on observed ($O$) vs expected ($E$) variant traffic allocations:
  $$\chi^2 = \sum_{i} \frac{(O_i - E_i)^2}{E_i} \quad \text{with } df = k - 1$$
* **Decision Rule**: If $p < 0.001$, significant SRM exists $\implies$ **the experiment results are fundamentally invalid**.
* **Common Root Causes**: Traffic redirection latency, bot-filtering filtering one variant disproportionately, hashing key collisions, or browser caching bugs.

### 4. Experimentation Methodological Pitfalls
* **The Peeking Problem (Fixed-Horizon vs Sequential Testing)**: Evaluating standard p-values daily and stopping early when $p < 0.05$ inflates the true false positive rate from $5\%$ to $>30\%$. Experiments must run for their pre-calculated sample size and full weekly business cycles (minimum 14 days to capture weekday/weekend variance). If continuous monitoring is necessary, apply Sequential Testing (e.g., Wald's SPRT or Alpha Spending Functions).
* **Novelty & Primacy Effects**: Existing users initially click a new feature out of curiosity (temporary novelty spike) or reject it due to habit friction (temporary primacy dip). Running tests for at least 2 full weeks allows user novelty to stabilize.
