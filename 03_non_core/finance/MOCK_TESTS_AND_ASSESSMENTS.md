### Mock 01: Complete Solutions & Marking Scheme

- **Q1**: IS: Depreciation ₹40L $\implies$ Operating Income -₹40L $\implies$ Tax -₹10L $\implies$ Net Income **-₹30L**. CFS: Net Income -₹30L + D&A ₹40L $\implies$ CFO **+₹10L**. CFI: 0. CFF: 0. Net Cash: **+₹10L**. BS: Cash +₹10L, Net PP&E -₹40L $\implies$ Assets **-₹30L**. Retained Earnings **-₹30L**. Balances.
- **Q2**: Net Profit Margin $= 50 / 500 = 10.0\%$. Asset Turnover $= 500 / 400 = 1.25\times$. Equity Multiplier $= 400 / 250 = 1.60\times$. **ROE** $= 10.0\% \times 1.25 \times 1.60 = \mathbf{20.0\%}$.
- **Q3**: $\text{CCC} = \text{DSO} + \text{DSI} - \text{DPO} = 45 + 60 - 35 = \mathbf{70\text{ days}}$.
- **Q4**: $\text{PVIFA}_{10\%, 4} = \frac{1 - (1.10)^{-4}}{0.10} = \frac{1 - 0.683013}{0.10} = 3.169865$. $\text{PV} = 10 \times 3.169865 = \mathbf{₹31.70\text{ Lakhs}}$.
- **Q5**: $\text{PV} = \frac{40}{1.10} + \frac{50}{1.21} + \frac{60}{1.331} = 36.364 + 41.322 + 45.079 = ₹122.765\text{L}$. $\text{NPV} = 122.765 - 100 = \mathbf{+₹22.77\text{ Lakhs}}$.
- **Q6**: $K_d(\text{after-tax}) = 8\% \times (1 - 0.25) = 6.0\%$. $WACC = \left(\frac{2}{3} \times 14\%\right) + \left(\frac{1}{3} \times 6\%\right) = 9.333\% + 2.0\% = \mathbf{11.33\%}$.
- **Q7**: $\text{Enterprise Value} = 80 \times 8.5 = ₹680\text{ Cr}$. $\text{Equity Value} = \text{EV} - \text{Net Debt} = 680 - 120 = \mathbf{₹560\text{ Cr}}$.
- **Q8**: Bond trades at a **Discount** (Price $< 100$). Coupon is 6% while required yield is 7%. Current Yield $= \frac{6}{\text{Price}} > 6.0\%$.
- **Q9**: Interest Income $= 1000 \times 0.085 = ₹85\text{ Cr}$. Interest Expense $= 800 \times 0.05 = ₹40\text{ Cr}$. $\text{NII} = 85 - 40 = ₹45\text{ Cr}$. $\text{NIM} = \frac{45}{1000} = \mathbf{4.50\%}$.
- **Q10**: $\text{EL} = \text{PD} \times \text{LGD} \times \text{EAD} = 0.04 \times 0.45 \times 200 = \mathbf{₹3.60\text{ Cr}}$.
- **Q11**:
  - (a) Book Value $= 100 - 70 = ₹30\text{L}$. Gain on Sale $= 40 - 30 = \mathbf{+₹10\text{L}}$.
  - (b) IS: Pre-tax Gain $+₹10\text{L} \implies$ Tax $(25\%) = +₹2.5\text{L} \implies$ Net Income **$+₹7.5\text{L}$**.
  - (c) CFS: CFO: Net Income $+7.5\text{L}$ less Gain on Sale $-10.0\text{L} = \mathbf{-₹2.5\text{L}}$. CFI: Entire gross cash proceeds $= \mathbf{+₹40.0\text{L}}$. CFF: ₹0. Net Cash Change $= -2.5 + 40.0 = \mathbf{+₹37.5\text{L}}$.
  - (d) BS: Cash $+₹37.5\text{L}$, Net PP&E $-₹30.0\text{L} \implies$ Total Assets **$+₹7.5\text{L}$**. Retained Earnings **$+₹7.5\text{L}$**. Balances perfectly.
- **Q12**:
  - (a) $\text{PVIFA}_{10\%, 4} = 3.169865$.
    - $\text{NPV}_A = (55 \times 3.169865) - 150 = 174.343 - 150 = \mathbf{+₹24.34\text{ Lakhs}}$.
    - $\text{NPV}_B = (78 \times 3.169865) - 220 = 247.250 - 220 = \mathbf{+₹27.25\text{ Lakhs}}$.
  - (b) Payback $A = 150 / 55 = \mathbf{2.73\text{ years}}$. Payback $B = 220 / 78 = \mathbf{2.82\text{ years}}$.
  - (c) Incremental Cash Flows $(B - A)$: Outlay $= -(220 - 150) = -₹70\text{L}$. Annual Inflow $= 78 - 55 = +₹23\text{L}$.

### Mock 02: Complete Solutions & Marking Scheme

- **Q1**: FCFF represents cash flow available to **all capital providers** (debt + equity) $\implies$ must be discounted at WACC (weighted cost of all capital). FCFE belongs strictly to equity holders after debt service $\implies$ discounted at Cost of Equity. If FCFF is discounted at $K_e$ (which is higher than WACC), firm value will be **severely understated**.
- **Q2**: $\beta_U = \frac{\beta_L}{1 + (1 - T)(D/E)} = \frac{1.30}{1 + (1 - 0.25)(0.80)} = \frac{1.30}{1 + 0.60} = \frac{1.30}{1.60} = \mathbf{0.8125}$.
  Relevered beta at $D/E = 0.40$: $\beta_L' = \beta_U [1 + (1 - T)(D/E)'] = 0.8125 [1 + 0.75 \times 0.40] = 0.8125 \times 1.30 = \mathbf{1.056}$.
- **Q3**: $\text{TV}_5 = \frac{150 \times (1 + 0.035)}{0.095 - 0.035} = \frac{155.25}{0.060} = \mathbf{₹2,587.5\text{ Cr}}$. Implied Multiple $= \frac{2587.5}{250} = \mathbf{10.35\times\text{ EBITDA}}$.
- **Q4**: EV/EBITDA is capital-structure neutral because EV includes both Debt and Equity, and EBITDA is computed before interest payments. P/E compares Equity Value against Net Income (which has interest deducted), making it vulnerable to leverage distortion. EV/Sales becomes misleading for companies with thin or negative margins (e.g., two retail firms with identical ₹10,000 Cr sales, but one has 10% operating margin and the other has -5% margin).
- **Q5**: Circularity: Higher cash $\implies$ higher interest income $\implies$ higher Net Income $\implies$ higher cash. In production models: (1) Use average cash balance or beginning cash balance for interest calculation to break circularity, or (2) Enable iterative calculation in Excel settings with an explicit circularity breaker toggle switch.
- **Q6**:
  - (a) FCFF Schedule:
    - **Year 1**: Rev $= 1000 \times 1.15 = 1150$. EBIT $(20\%) = 230$. NOPAT $(75\%) = 172.5$. D&A $(5\%) = 57.5$. CapEx $(7\%) = 80.5$. NWC $= 1150 \times 0.10 = 115 \implies \Delta NWC = 115 - 100 = 15$. $\mathbf{\text{FCFF}_1} = 172.5 + 57.5 - 80.5 - 15 = \mathbf{₹134.50\text{ Cr}}$.
    - **Year 2**: Rev $= 1150 \times 1.12 = 1288$. EBIT $= 257.6$. NOPAT $= 193.2$. D&A $= 64.4$. CapEx $= 90.16$. NWC $= 128.8 \implies \Delta NWC = 13.8$. $\mathbf{\text{FCFF}_2} = 193.2 + 64.4 - 90.16 - 13.8 = \mathbf{₹153.64\text{ Cr}}$.
    - **Year 3**: Rev $= 1288 \times 1.10 = 1416.8$. EBIT $= 283.36$. NOPAT $= 212.52$. D&A $= 70.84$. CapEx $= 99.176$. NWC $= 141.68 \implies \Delta NWC = 12.88$. $\mathbf{\text{FCFF}_3} = 212.52 + 70.84 - 99.176 - 12.88 = \mathbf{₹171.30\text{ Cr}}$.
  - (b) Mid-Year Discounting ($WACC = 10\%$):
    - Year 1 ($t = 0.5$): $DF_1 = (1.10)^{-0.5} = 0.953463 \implies PV_1 = 134.50 \times 0.953463 = \mathbf{₹128.24\text{ Cr}}$.
    - Year 2 ($t = 1.5$): $DF_2 = (1.10)^{-1.5} = 0.866784 \implies PV_2 = 153.64 \times 0.866784 = \mathbf{₹133.17\text{ Cr}}$.
    - Year 3 ($t = 2.5$): $DF_3 = (1.10)^{-2.5} = 0.787986 \implies PV_3 = 171.30 \times 0.787986 = \mathbf{₹134.98\text{ Cr}}$.
    - **Sum of PV of Discrete FCFF** $= 128.24 + 133.17 + 134.98 = \mathbf{₹396.39\text{ Cr}}$.
  - (c) Terminal Value:
    - $\text{FCFF}_4 = 171.304 \times 1.04 = 178.156\text{ Cr}$.
    - $\text{TV}_3 = \frac{178.156}{0.10 - 0.04} = \frac{178.156}{0.06} = \mathbf{₹2,969.27\text{ Cr}}$.
    - Present Value of TV $= \frac{2969.27}{(1.10)^3} = \frac{2969.27}{1.331} = \mathbf{₹2,230.86\text{ Cr}}$.
  - (d) Valuation:
    - **Enterprise Value** $= 396.39 + 2230.86 = \mathbf{₹2,627.25\text{ Cr}}$.
    - Net Debt $= 350 - 100 = ₹250\text{ Cr}$.
    - **Equity Value** $= 2627.25 - 250 = \mathbf{₹2,377.25\text{ Cr}}$.
    - **Fair Value per Share** $= \frac{2377.25}{10} = \mathbf{₹237.73 / \text{share}}$.
- **Q7**:
  - (a) Multiples:
    - LTM multiples: 8.0, 9.5, 10.0, 11.0 $\implies$ Median $= \frac{9.5 + 10.0}{2} = \mathbf{9.75\times}$, Mean $= \mathbf{9.625\times}$.
    - NTM multiples: 7.0, 8.0, 8.5, 9.0 $\implies$ Median $= \frac{8.0 + 8.5}{2} = \mathbf{8.25\times}$, Mean $= \mathbf{8.125\times}$.
  - (b) Implied EV:
    - LTM Basis: $120 \times 9.75 = \mathbf{₹1,170.0\text{ Cr}}$.
    - NTM Basis: $140 \times 8.25 = \mathbf{₹1,155.0\text{ Cr}}$.
    - Implied EV Range: **₹1,155 Cr to ₹1,170 Cr**.
  - (c) Implied Equity Value per share:
    - LTM Equity Value $= 1170 - 220 = ₹950\text{ Cr} \implies \frac{950}{4} = \mathbf{₹237.50 / \text{share}}$.
    - NTM Equity Value $= 1155 - 220 = ₹935\text{ Cr} \implies \frac{935}{4} = \mathbf{₹233.75 / \text{share}}$.
    - Implied Fair Value Range: **₹233.75 to ₹237.50 / share**.
  - (d) Current price is ₹180. Since fair value is ₹234–₹238, the stock trades at an attractive **23%–24% discount to intrinsic peer value**. **Recommendation: BUY** (undervalued).

### Mock 03: Complete Solutions & Marking Scheme

- **Q1**: Target P/E with 20% premium $= 14 \times 1.20 = 16.8\times$. Acquirer P/E $= 22\times$. Since Acquirer P/E $(22\times) >$ Offer P/E $(16.8\times)$, the deal is **Accretive**.
  *Proof*: Acquirer pays ₹16.80 per ₹1.00 of target earnings. For ₹16.80, Acquirer issues $\frac{16.80}{22.0} = 0.7636$ shares. New EPS yield $= \frac{1}{0.7636} = ₹1.31 > ₹1.00$.
- **Q2**: Cost of Equity $= \frac{1}{\text{P/E}} = \frac{1}{18} = 5.56\%$. After-tax Cost of Debt $= 8.0\% \times (1 - 0.25) = 6.00\%$. Since Cost of Equity $(5.56\%) <$ After-tax Cost of Debt $(6.00\%)$, **Stock financing is cheaper than Debt financing and therefore MORE accretive**.
- **Q3**: Allocable excess $= 1200 - 400 = ₹800\text{ Cr}$. Pre-tax write-ups $= 200 + 150 = ₹350\text{ Cr}$. **DTL Created** $= 350 \times 0.25 = \mathbf{₹87.5\text{ Cr}}$. Net asset write-up $= 350 - 87.5 = ₹262.5\text{ Cr}$. **Goodwill** $= 800 - 262.5 = \mathbf{₹537.5\text{ Cr}}$.
- **Q4**: After-tax interest cost $= 700 \times 0.10 \times (1 - 0.25) = ₹52.5\text{ Cr}$. Target Net Income $= ₹50.0\text{ Cr}$. Net deficit $= 52.5 - 50.0 = ₹2.5\text{ Cr}$ after-tax. **Required Pre-Tax Synergies** $= \frac{2.5}{1 - 0.25} = \frac{2.5}{0.75} = \mathbf{₹3.33\text{ Cr}}$.
- **Q5**:
  - (a) Target Offer Price $= 100 \times 1.25 = \mathbf{₹125.00 / \text{share}}$. Total Purchase Price $= 5\text{ Cr shares} \times 125 = \mathbf{₹625\text{ Cr}}$.
  - (b) Funding:
    - Cash $(40\%) = 625 \times 0.40 = \mathbf{₹250\text{ Cr}}$.
    - Debt $(30\%) = 625 \times 0.30 = \mathbf{₹187.5\text{ Cr}}$.
    - Stock $(30\%) = 625 \times 0.30 = ₹187.5\text{ Cr} \implies \text{New shares} = \frac{187.5}{250} = \mathbf{0.75\text{ Cr shares}}$.
  - (c) Combined Net Income:
    - Standalone Alpha: ₹500.0 Cr

### Mock 07: Complete Solutions & Marking Scheme

- **Q1**: VaR is the threshold loss not exceeded at $(1 - \alpha)$ confidence. Expected Shortfall is the expected loss *given* that the loss exceeds the VaR threshold. FRTB replaced VaR with ES because VaR failed to capture extreme tail risk during the 2008 crash and violates subadditivity.
- **Q2**: Delta $= \partial V / \partial S$; Gamma $= \partial^2 V / \partial S^2$; Vega $= \partial V / \partial \sigma$. A Delta-neutral, short-Gamma portfolio experiences catastrophic accelerating losses during large sudden market moves because negative Gamma rapidly turns the position net long in falling markets and net short in rising markets.
- **Q3**: Axioms: Monotonicity, Translation Invariance, Positive Homogeneity, Subadditivity. Two independent short digital options each having a 0.8% default probability will each have a 99% VaR of 0. Combined, the probability of at least one default is $1 - (0.992)^2 = 1.59\% > 1.0\% \implies \text{Combined 99\% VaR} = \text{Payout} > 0 + 0$. VaR violates Subadditivity.
- **Q4**: Minimum CET1 $= 4.5\%$. Capital Conservation Buffer (CCB) $= 2.5\%$. Total baseline $= \mathbf{7.0\%}$. Countercyclical Buffer (CCyB) is $0\%$ to $2.5\%$ set by regulators during credit booms.
- **Q5**: LCR $= \frac{\text{HQLA}}{\text{30-day net cash outflows}} \ge 100\%$ (short-term survival against bank runs). NSFR $= \frac{\text{Available Stable Funding}}{\text{Required Stable Funding}} \ge 100\%$ (long-term structural funding match over 1 year).
- **Q6**:
  - (a) Standalone VaR:
    - $\text{VaR}_1 = 120 \times 2.326 \times 0.012 = \mathbf{₹3.349\text{ Cr}}$.
    - $\text{VaR}_2 = 80 \times 2.326 \times 0.020 = \mathbf{₹3.722\text{ Cr}}$.
    - Undiversified Sum $= 3.349 + 3.722 = \mathbf{₹7.071\text{ Cr}}$.
  - (b) Combined Portfolio:
    - Dollar variance $= \sigma_{1,\$}^2 + \sigma_{2,\$}^2 + 2 \rho \sigma_{1,\$} \sigma_{2,\$}$
    - $\sigma_{1,\$} = 120 \times 0.012 = 1.44\text{ Cr}$. $\sigma_{2,\$} = 80 \times 0.020 = 1.60\text{ Cr}$.
    - Variance $= (1.44)^2 + (1.60)^2 + 2(0.70)(1.44)(1.60) = 2.0736 + 2.5600 + 3.2256 = 7.8592$.
    - Combined Dollar $\sigma_p = \sqrt{7.8592} = \mathbf{₹2.8034\text{ Cr}}$.
    - **1-Day 99% Portfolio VaR** $= 2.326 \times 2.8034 = \mathbf{₹6.521\text{ Cr}}$.
  - (c) **Diversification Benefit** $= 7.071 - 6.521 = \mathbf{₹0.550\text{ Cr}}$ (₹55 Lakhs saved through imperfect correlation).
  - (d) Scaling:
    - **10-Day 99% VaR** $= 6.521 \times \sqrt{10} = 6.521 \times 3.1623 = \mathbf{₹20.62\text{ Cr}}$.
    - **10-Day Expected Shortfall** $= 20.62 \times 1.35 = \mathbf{₹27.84\text{ Cr}}$.
- **Q7**:
  - Part 1 (Backtesting):
    - Exceptions $= 7$. Under Basel 250-day 99% rules: Green (0–4), Yellow (5–9), Red ($\ge 10$).
    - The desk is in the **Yellow Zone**.
    - Supervisory scaling factor increases from base $k = 3.0$ up to $k = 3.65$ (an extra capital charge of 0.65x VaR), triggering internal model audit.
  - Part 2 (Bank Capital):
    - Total RWA:
      - Credit RWA $= ₹80,000\text{ Cr}$.
      - Market RWA $= 1200 \times 12.5 = ₹15,000\text{ Cr}$.
      - Operational RWA $= 800 \times 12.5 = ₹10,000\text{ Cr}$.
      - **Total RWA** $= 80000 + 15000 + 10000 = \mathbf{₹105,000\text{ Cr}}$.
    - Capital Tiers:
      - CET1 $= 5000\text{ (equity)} + 3000\text{ (retained)} = \mathbf{₹8,000\text{ Cr}}$.
      - Tier 1 $= \text{CET1} = \mathbf{₹8,000\text{ Cr}}$.
      - Tier 2 $= 450\text{ (reval)} + 2500\text{ (sub-debt)} = \mathbf{₹2,950\text{ Cr}}$.
      - Total Capital $= 8000 + 2950 = \mathbf{₹10,950\text{ Cr}}$.
    - Capital Ratios:
      - **CET1 Ratio** $= \frac{8000}{105000} = \mathbf{7.62\%}$ (Above 7.0% min).
      - **Total CRAR** $= \frac{10950}{105000} = \mathbf{10.43\%}$.
    - Compliance: Since $10.43\% < 11.50\%$, the bank **FAILS to meet the RBI CRAR requirement of 11.5%** and must raise additional Tier 1 capital or retain earnings before paying dividends.
