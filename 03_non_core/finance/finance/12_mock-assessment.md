### Section 1 Solutions (25 Marks)

#### Solution 1 (8 Marks)
1. **Book Value of Asset Sold**:
   $$\text{Book Value} = \text{Original Cost} - \text{Accumulated Depreciation} = 180 - 100 = ₹80\text{ Cr}$$
2. **Gain on Sale**:
   $$\text{Pre-Tax Gain} = \text{Sale Price} - \text{Book Value} = 120 - 80 = +₹40\text{ Cr}$$
3. **Three-Statement Impacts**:
   - **Income Statement** (2 Marks):
     - Pre-tax operating gain of +₹40 Cr.
     - Taxes increase by $₹40 \times 25\% = ₹10\text{ Cr}$.
     - Net Income increases by **+₹30 Cr**.
   - **Cash Flow Statement** (3 Marks):
     - $CFO$: Starts with Net Income +₹30 Cr. Subtract non-cash gain on sale of -₹40 Cr $\implies CFO = -₹10\text{ Cr}$ (representing the tax paid on the gain).
     - $CFI$: Entire cash proceeds from asset disposal recorded as cash inflow: **+₹120 Cr**.
     - $CFF$: ₹0.
     - Net Change in Ending Cash: $-10 + 120 = \mathbf{+₹110\text{ Cr}}$.
   - **Balance Sheet** (3 Marks):
     - **Assets**: Cash increases by **+₹110 Cr**. Net PP&E drops by book value of asset removed: **-₹80 Cr**. Net change in Total Assets: $+110 - 80 = \mathbf{+₹30\text{ Cr}}$.
     - **Liabilities & Equity**: Liabilities = ₹0. Retained Earnings increases by **+₹30 Cr** (via Net Income).
     - **Check**: Total Assets ($+₹30\text{ Cr}$) = Total Liabilities ($₹0$) + Equity ($+₹30\text{ Cr}$). Balances.

#### Solution 2 (6 Marks)
- **(a) On October 1, 2025** (2 Marks):
  - Income Statement: ₹0 revenue recognized.
  - Balance Sheet: Cash increases by +₹240 Cr ($CFO$ inflow +₹240 Cr). Current Liabilities / Deferred Revenue increases by +₹240 Cr.
- **(b) On March 31, 2026 (6 months elapsed out of 24 months)** (4 Marks):
  - Revenue Recognized: $\frac{6}{24} \times 240 = ₹60\text{ Cr}$.
  - Pre-tax income = ₹60 Cr. Tax at 25% = ₹15 Cr. Net Income = **+₹45 Cr**.
  - Cash Flow Statement: Net income = +₹45 Cr. Change in Deferred Revenue = $-₹60\text{ Cr}$. Net $CFO = 45 - 60 = -₹15\text{ Cr}$ (cash tax paid).
  - Balance Sheet: Cash drops by -₹15 Cr. Deferred Revenue liability drops from ₹240 Cr to **₹180 Cr** ($\Delta = -₹60\text{ Cr}$). Retained earnings increases by **+₹45 Cr**. Total Liabilities & Equity change: $-60 + 45 = -₹15\text{ Cr}$. Balances.

#### Solution 3 (5 Marks)
- **Distinction** (2 Marks): Expensed interest is deducted immediately on the Income Statement as a financing cost. Capitalized interest is added to the carrying cost of a long-term qualifying asset (e.g., self-constructed manufacturing facility or plant) on the Balance Sheet during the construction period under Ind-AS 23 / IFRS.
- **Distortionary Impact** (3 Marks):
  1. *EBITDA*: Artificially inflated because interest that would normally appear below operating income is omitted, and does not hit P&L until capitalized asset depreciation begins.
  2. *Cash Flow*: The cash outflow for interest is classified as Capital Expenditure under **Cash Flow from Investing ($CFI$)** rather than $CFO$, artificially inflating reported Operating Cash Flow.

#### Solution 4 (6 Marks)
$$\text{CFO (Indirect Method)}:$$
$$\text{Net Income} = +₹85\text{ Cr}$$
$$+ \text{D\&A (Non-Cash Addback)} = +₹25\text{ Cr}$$
$$- \text{Gain on Sale of Land (Investing Inflow Adjustment)} = -₹10\text{ Cr}$$

$$+ \text{Decrease in Inventory (Cash Source)} = +₹12\text{ Cr}$$
$$- \text{Decrease in Accounts Payable (Liability Paid Down)} = -₹15\text{ Cr}$$
$$\mathbf{CFO} = 85 + 25 - 10 - 20 + 12 - 15 = \mathbf{+₹77\text{ Cr}}$$

### Section 2 Solutions (25 Marks)

#### Solution 5 (8 Marks)
- **(a) NPV Computations at 10.0%** (4 Marks):
  $$\text{NPV}_{\text{Alpha}} = -500 + \frac{300}{1.10} + \frac{350}{(1.10)^2} = -500 + 272.73 + 289.26 = \mathbf{+₹61.98\text{ Cr}}$$
  $$\text{NPV}_{\text{Beta}} = -500 + \frac{100}{1.10} + \frac{200}{(1.10)^2} + \frac{450}{(1.10)^3} = -500 + 90.91 + 165.29 + 338.09 = \mathbf{+₹94.29\text{ Cr}}$$
- **(b) Crossover Rate Computation** (4 Marks):
  Set $\text{NPV}_{\text{Alpha}} = \text{NPV}_{\text{Beta}} \implies \text{NPV}(\text{Beta} - \text{Alpha}) = 0$.
  Incremental Cash Flows ($\Delta = \text{Beta} - \text{Alpha}$):
  - Year 0: $-500 - (-500) = ₹0$
  - Year 1: $100 - 300 = -₹200\text{ Cr}$
  - Year 2: $200 - 350 = -₹150\text{ Cr}$
  - Year 3: $450 - 0 = +₹450\text{ Cr}$
  Let $x = \frac{1}{1 + r}$:
  $$-200x - 150x^2 + 450x^3 = 0 \implies 450x^2 - 150x - 200 = 0 \implies 9x^2 - 3x - 4 = 0$$
  Using quadratic formula:
  $$x = \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{(-3)^2 - 4(9)(-4)}}{2(9)} = \frac{3 \pm \sqrt{9 + 144}}{18} = \frac{3 + \sqrt{153}}{18} = \frac{3 + 12.3693}{18} = \frac{15.3693}{18} = 0.85385$$
  $$1 + r = \frac{1}{0.85385} = 1.17116 \implies r = \mathbf{17.12\%}$$
  - *Answer*: Crossover discount rate is **17.12%**. Below 17.12%, Project Beta has higher NPV; above 17.12%, Project Alpha has higher NPV.

#### Solution 11 (6 Marks)
- **Enterprise Multiples vs Equity Multiples** (4 Marks):
  Enterprise Value represents claims of all capital providers (debt and equity), matching denominators like EBITDA, EBIT, and Revenue which are computed before interest expense deductions. In contrast, P/E uses Net Income, which is distorted by interest expense: a highly levered firm will show depressed Net Income and an artificially elevated or distorted P/E multiple. EV multiples neutralize capital structure choices.
- **Condition for EV < Equity Value** (2 Marks):
  $$\text{Enterprise Value} = \text{Equity Value} + \text{Total Debt} - \text{Cash} = \text{Equity Value} + \text{Net Debt}$$
  Enterprise Value will be strictly lower than Equity Value whenever **Cash exceeds Total Debt** (i.e., the company has negative Net Debt / Net Cash balance).

#### Solution 12 (6 Marks)
- **(a) Gordon Growth Terminal Value** (3 Marks):
  $$\text{TV}_5 = \frac{\text{FCFF}_5 \times (1 + g)}{\text{WACC} - g} = \frac{150 \times (1 + 0.04)}{0.12 - 0.04} = \frac{156.0}{0.08} = \mathbf{₹1,950.0\text{ Cr}}$$
- **(b) Exit Multiple Terminal Value** (3 Marks):
  $$\text{TV}_5 = \text{EBITDA}_5 \times (\text{EV/EBITDA}) = 210 \times 9.0 = \mathbf{₹1,890.0\text{ Cr}}$$
  - *Comparison*: The **Exit Multiple method (₹1,890 Cr)** is more conservative than Gordon Growth (₹1,950 Cr).

### Section 4 Solutions: Comprehensive Valuation Caselet (25 Marks)

#### Solution 13 (25 Marks)

**(a) Cash Flow Projections (FY26–FY30)** (8 Marks):

$$\text{NOPAT} = \text{EBIT} \times (1 - 0.25)$$
$$\text{FCFF} = \text{NOPAT} + \text{D\&A} - \text{CapEx} - \Delta \text{NWC}$$

| Line Item (₹ Cr) | FY26E | FY27E | FY28E | FY29E | FY30E |
|:---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Revenue | 300.0 | 360.0 | 420.0 | 470.0 | 510.0 |
| EBIT Margin | 15.0% | 16.0% | 17.0% | 18.0% | 18.0% |
| **EBIT** | 45.0 | 57.6 | 71.4 | 84.6 | 91.8 |
| Less: Taxes (25%) | (11.25) | (14.40) | (17.85) | (21.15) | (22.95) |
| **NOPAT** | **33.75** | **43.20** | **53.55** | **63.45** | **68.85** |
| Plus: D&A | 15.0 | 18.0 | 21.0 | 23.5 | 25.5 |
| Less: CapEx | (25.0) | (28.0) | (30.0) | (28.0) | (25.5) |
| Less: Investment in NWC | (10.0) | (12.0) | (12.0) | (10.0) | (8.0) |
| **FCFF** | **13.75** | **21.20** | **32.55** | **48.95** | **60.85** |

**(b) Present Value of Discrete Cash Flows (WACC = 12.0%)** (4 Marks):
- Discount factors: $(1.12)^1 = 1.1200$, $(1.12)^2 = 1.2544$, $(1.12)^3 = 1.4049$, $(1.12)^4 = 1.5735$, $(1.12)^5 = 1.7623$.
- $\text{PV}_1 = \frac{13.75}{1.1200} = ₹12.28\text{ Cr}$
- $\text{PV}_2 = \frac{21.20}{1.2544} = ₹16.90\text{ Cr}$
- $\text{PV}_3 = \frac{32.55}{1.4049} = ₹23.17\text{ Cr}$
- $\text{PV}_4 = \frac{48.95}{1.5735} = ₹31.11\text{ Cr}$
- $\text{PV}_5 = \frac{60.85}{1.7623} = ₹34.53\text{ Cr}$
- **Sum of PV(Discrete FCFF)**:
  $$\text{PV}_{\text{discrete}} = 12.28 + 16.90 + 23.17 + 31.11 + 34.53 = \mathbf{₹117.99\text{ Cr}}$$

**(c) Terminal Value & PV of Terminal Value** (5 Marks):
$$\text{TV}_{30} = \frac{\text{FCFF}_{30} \times (1 + g)}{\text{WACC} - g} = \frac{60.85 \times (1 + 0.04)}{0.12 - 0.04} = \frac{63.284}{0.08} = \mathbf{₹791.05\text{ Cr}}$$
$$\text{PV}(\text{TV}) = \frac{\text{TV}_{30}}{(1.12)^5} = \frac{791.05}{1.7623} = \mathbf{₹448.87\text{ Cr}}$$

**(d) Valuation Bridges & Share Price** (5 Marks):
- **Enterprise Value (EV)**:
  $$\text{EV} = \text{PV}_{\text{discrete}} + \text{PV}(\text{TV}) = 117.99 + 448.87 = \mathbf{₹566.86\text{ Cr}}$$
- **Net Debt**:
  $$\text{Net Debt} = \text{Total Debt} - \text{Cash} = 110.0 - 35.0 = \mathbf{₹75.0\text{ Cr}}$$
- **Equity Value**:
  $$\text{Equity Value} = \text{EV} - \text{Net Debt} = 566.86 - 75.0 = \mathbf{₹491.86\text{ Cr}}$$
- **Implied Share Price**:
  $$\text{Implied Price per Share} = \frac{\text{Equity Value}}{\text{Shares Outstanding}} = \frac{₹491.86\text{ Cr}}{4.0\text{ Cr shares}} = \mathbf{₹122.97\text{ per share}}$$

**(e) Investment Recommendation** (3 Marks):
- **Recommendation**: **Sell / Overvalued (Reject target acquisition at current market price)**.
- **Rationale**:
  The DCF intrinsic value is **₹122.97 per share**, whereas the stock currently trades at **₹130.00 per share** in the public market (a 5.7% premium over fundamental fair value). An acquirer paying market price without verified, material synergies would destroy shareholder value. Target acquisition should only proceed if purchase price can be negotiated below ₹115 or if post-merger cost synergies exceed ₹15 Cr annually.
