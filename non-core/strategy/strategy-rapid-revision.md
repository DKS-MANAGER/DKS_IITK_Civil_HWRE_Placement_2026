## Framework 1: Market & Competitive Analysis

### Market Sizing (TAM/SAM/SOM)
```
TAM = Total Addressable Market (whole market)
SAM = Serviceable Addressable Market (segment you can serve)
SOM = Serviceable Obtainable Market (realistic share)
```

### Market Sizing Template (Top-Down)
```
Population
  × Target segment % = Addressable population
  × Purchase frequency = Total units
  × Average price = Market size
```

### Porter's Five Forces
```
1. Threat of new entrants
2. Bargaining power of suppliers
3. Bargaining power of buyers
4. Threat of substitutes
5. Competitive rivalry
```

### SWOT
```
Strengths (internal, positive)   Weaknesses (internal, negative)
Opportunities (external, positive)  Threats (external, negative)
```

### PESTEL
```
Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal
```

## Framework 2: Business Model & Financial Analysis

### Business Model Canvas
```
Value Proposition → Customer Segments → Revenue Model
→ Cost Structure → Channels → Key Resources/Activities
```

### Unit Economics
```
Contribution Margin = Price - Variable Cost
LTV = Avg Revenue per Customer × Gross Margin × Lifetime
CAC = Total Acquisition Cost / New Customers
Payback = CAC / Monthly Contribution
Good LTV/CAC ratio ≥ 3
```

### Break-Even
```
Break-even Units = Fixed Costs / (Price - Variable Cost)
```

### Key Margins
```
Gross Margin = (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue
Operating Margin = Operating Profit / Revenue
Net Margin = Net Income / Revenue
```

### Valuation Basics
```
DCF: EV = Σ FCF/(1+WACC)ᵗ + TV/(1+WACC)ⁿ
Multiples: P/E, EV/EBITDA, P/B, P/S
```

## Framework 3: Strategic Frameworks

### Ansoff Matrix
| | Existing Market | New Market |
|:--|:----------------|:-----------|
| **Existing Product** | Market Penetration | Market Development |
| **New Product** | Product Development | Diversification |

### BCG Matrix
```
Star     = High growth, high share → invest
Cash Cow = Low growth, high share → harvest
Question Mark = High growth, low share → decide
Dog      = Low growth, low share → divest
```

### Porter's Generic Strategies
```
Cost Leadership (lowest cost)
Differentiation (unique value)
Focus (niche segment)
```

### Growth Options
```
Organic = Build internally (slower, lower risk)
Inorganic = M&A (faster, higher risk)
Synergies: Cost (overlap) + Revenue (cross-sell) + Capability
```

### M&A Evaluation
```
Strategic fit → Synergies → Valuation → Integration risk → Deal structure
```

## Framework 4: Case Study Structure

### Case Framework
```
1. Clarify the objective
2. Build framework (MECE issue tree)
3. Quantify (use numbers, estimate)
4. Diagnose root causes
5. Recommend (prioritized, feasible)
6. Define next steps
```

### Profitability Framework
```
Profit = Revenue - Cost
Revenue = Volume × Price (mix, channels)
Cost = Fixed + Variable (raw material, energy, labor, logistics)
```

### Market Entry Framework
```
Attractiveness (size, growth, margins, competition)
× Feasibility (capability, capital, regulatory, execution)
= Entry decision
```

### Hypothesis-Driven Approach
```
State hypothesis → Test with data → Confirm/revise → Recommend
```

## 10 Quick-Fire Interview Answers

**Q1: What is the difference between strategy and tactics?**
A: Strategy is the overall direction — where to play and how to win. Tactics are the specific actions to execute the strategy. Strategy answers "what and why"; tactics answer "how and when."

**Q2: What is TAM/SAM/SOM?**
A: TAM is the total addressable market, SAM is the segment you can actually serve, and SOM is the realistic share you can capture. They cascade from the full market to your achievable revenue.

**Q3: Explain Porter's Five Forces.**
A: A framework for industry attractiveness: threat of new entrants, supplier power, buyer power, threat of substitutes, and competitive rivalry. Stronger forces = less attractive industry.

**Q4: What is the Ansoff Matrix?**
A: A growth framework with four strategies: market penetration (existing product/market), market development (new markets), product development (new products), and diversification (both new).

**Q5: What is a good business model?**
A: One with a clear value proposition, strong unit economics (LTV/CAC ≥ 3, positive contribution margin), a defensible moat, and a path to scale profitably.

**Q6: How do you value a company?**
A: Use DCF (project cash flows, discount at WACC) for intrinsic value, and multiples (P/E, EV/EBITDA) for relative value. For startups, use revenue multiples or VC method.

**Q7: What is the BCG Matrix?**
A: A portfolio tool classifying businesses by market growth and market share: Stars (invest), Cash Cows (harvest), Question Marks (decide), Dogs (divest).

**Q8: How do you handle uncertainty in strategy?**
A: Use scenario planning (base/bull/bear), stress-test assumptions, gather more data where feasible, and make decisions that are robust across scenarios.

**Q9: How does a civil engineering background help in strategy?**
A: Research and analytical training, structured problem-solving, quantitative rigor, systems thinking, and deep understanding of infrastructure — a major strategic sector in India.

**Q10: A company's market share is declining. What's your strategy?**
A: Diagnose first — is it price, product, distribution, or competition? Then respond: fix the root cause, defend the core, and build a moat (cost, differentiation, or focus).
