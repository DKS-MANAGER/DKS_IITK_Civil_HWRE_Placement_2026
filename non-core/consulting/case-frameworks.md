# Consulting Case Frameworks

> The core frameworks every consulting candidate must know — with structure, questions, analysis steps, and common pitfalls.

---

## Golden Rule

**Do NOT memorize frameworks mechanically.** Learn the logic behind each framework and adapt it to the specific case. Interviewers can spot a rehearsed framework instantly.

**Framework = A structured way to think about a problem, not a template to fill in.**

---

## 1. Profitability Framework

**When to use:** "Profits are declining" / "How can we improve profitability?"

```
Profit = Revenue - Costs

Revenue
├── Price × Volume
│   ├── Price changes?
│   │   ├── Inflation / cost pass-through?
│   │   ├── Discounting strategy?
│   │   └── Competitive pressure on pricing?
│   └── Volume changes?
│       ├── Fewer customers?
│       │   ├── Market shrinking?
│       │   ├── Lost to competitors?
│       │   └── Customer churn?
│       └── Lower usage per customer?
│           ├── Seasonal?
│           ├── Usage pattern change?
│           └── Product issues?

Costs
├── Fixed Costs
│   ├── Rent, salaries, equipment
│   └── Any unusual increases?
└── Variable Costs
    ├── Materials, shipping, labor
    ├── Cost per unit changed?
    └── Efficiency of operations?
```

**Key questions to ask:**
- Is the issue on the revenue side or cost side?
- Is this recent or a trend?
- Is it company-specific or industry-wide?
- What's the magnitude of the change?

**Common insights:**
- Revenue decline is usually volume, not price
- Cost increases are often in variable costs (materials, labor)
- The fix depends on which component drives the gap

## 2. Market Entry Framework

**When to use:** "Should we enter [market]?" / "Is [country] attractive for [product]?"

```
Market Attractiveness
├── Market Size & Growth
│   ├── TAM / SAM / SOM
│   ├── Growth rate (CAGR)
│   └── Growth drivers
├── Competitive Landscape
│   ├── Number of competitors
│   ├── Market share distribution
│   ├── Barriers to entry
│   └── Competitive advantages
├── Customer Analysis
│   ├── Customer segments
│   ├── Unmet needs
│   ├── Willingness to pay
│   └── Buying behavior
├── Regulatory Environment
│   ├── Government policies
│   ├── Licensing requirements
│   └── Trade barriers

Company Capabilities
├── Do we have the right product/service?
├── Do we have distribution channels?
├── Do we have brand recognition?
├── Do we have capital?
├── What's our competitive advantage?
└── Can we sustain it?

Entry Mode
├── Organic (build from scratch)
├── Acquisition (buy existing player)
├── Partnership / JV
└── Franchise / Licensing

Financial Viability
├── Investment required
├── Expected returns
├── Break-even timeline
└── Risk assessment
```

**Common insight:** Market attractiveness alone isn't enough — you must match it with company capabilities.

## 3. Market Sizing (Guesstimate)

**When to use:** "Estimate X" / "How many Y in Z?"

→ Detailed guide: [guesstimate-guide.md](../guesstimates/guesstimate-guide.md)

**Core approach:**
1. **Top-down:** Start from a large number and narrow down
2. **Bottom-up:** Start from individual units and multiply up
3. **Demand-side:** How many people need this?
4. **Supply-side:** How much can be produced?

**Example structure:**
> "I'd like to estimate [X]. Let me break this down..."
> "[Population] × [penetration rate] × [usage frequency] = ..."
> "So my estimate is approximately [number]. Does that seem reasonable?"

## 4. Growth Strategy Framework

**When to use:** "How can we grow?" / "Revenue is flat — what do we do?"

```
Growth Options
├── Organic Growth
│   ├── Market Penetration (sell more to existing customers)
│   │   ├── Increase frequency of use
│   │   ├── Increase basket size
│   │   └── Convert non-users
│   ├── Market Development (sell existing product to new markets)
│   │   ├── New geographies
│   │   ├── New segments
│   │   └── New channels
│   ├── Product Development (new product to existing market)
│   │   ├── Line extensions
│   │   ├── Complementary products
│   │   └── Premium versions
│   └── Diversification (new product to new market)
│       ├── Related diversification
│       └── Unrelated diversification

├── Inorganic Growth
│   ├── Acquisitions
│   ├── Joint ventures
│   ├── Partnerships
│   └── Licensing

└── Which path? Based on:
    ├── Company capabilities
    ├── Risk appetite
    ├── Capital availability
    └── Time horizon
```

**Common insight:** Most companies should exhaust organic options before considering inorganic.

## 5. Pricing Framework

**When to use:** "How should we price X?" / "Should we raise/lower prices?"

```
Pricing Analysis
├── Cost-Based (floor)
│   ├── What does it cost to produce?
│   ├── What margin do we need?
│   └── Break-even price?
├── Value-Based (ceiling)
│   ├── What's the value to the customer?
│   ├── What alternatives exist?
│   ├── What's willingness to pay?
│   └── How price-sensitive are customers?
├── Competition-Based (anchor)
│   ├── How do competitors price?
│   ├── Are we premium, parity, or discount?
│   └── What's the price elasticity?
└── Pricing Strategy Options
    ├── Cost-plus (simple, safe)
    ├── Value-based (maximizes revenue)
    ├── Competitive (market-driven)
    ├── Dynamic (time/segment-based)
    ├── Penetration (low price to gain share)
    └── Skimming (high price, then lower)
```

**Key question:** Is demand elastic or inelastic? This determines whether raising price helps or hurts.

## 6. Operations Improvement Framework

**When to use:** "How can we improve efficiency?" / "Our process is too slow."

```
Operations Analysis
├── Current State
│   ├── Map the process (end to end)
│   ├── Identify bottlenecks
│   ├── Measure cycle time
│   └── Identify waste (7 types)
├── Waste Identification (Lean)
│   ├── Overproduction
│   ├── Waiting
│   ├── Transportation
│   ├── Over-processing
│   ├── Inventory
│   ├── Motion
│   └── Defects
├── Root Cause Analysis
│   ├── 5 Whys
│   ├── Fishbone diagram
│   └── Pareto analysis (80/20)
└── Improvement Options
    ├── Eliminate waste
    ├── Automate manual steps
    ├── Redesign process flow
    ├── Cross-train workers
    └── Implement quality controls
```

**Civil advantage:** You likely have direct experience with construction operations — use that.

## 7. M&A (Mergers & Acquisitions) Framework

**When to use:** "Should we acquire X?" / "Is this a good acquisition?"

```
M&A Analysis
├── Strategic Fit
│   ├── Does it complement our business?
│   ├── Does it give us new capabilities?
│   ├── Does it give us market access?
│   └── Synergy potential?
├── Target Assessment
│   ├── Financial health (revenue, profit, growth)
│   ├── Market position
│   ├── Team quality
│   ├── Technology/IP
│   └── Customer base
├── Valuation
│   ├── DCF (Discounted Cash Flow)
│   ├── Comparable companies
│   ├── Precedent transactions
│   └── Premium over market price
├── Integration Risk
│   ├── Cultural fit
│   ├── Systems integration
│   ├── Talent retention
│   └── Customer retention
└── Recommendation
    ├── Proceed / Don't proceed
    ├── Valuation range
    ├── Integration plan
    └── Key risks
```

## 8. Competitive Strategy Framework

**When to use:** "How should we compete?" / "What's our competitive advantage?"

```
Competitive Analysis (Porter's Five Forces)
├── Threat of New Entrants
│   ├── Barriers to entry (capital, regulation, brand)
│   └── How strong are they?
├── Bargaining Power of Suppliers
│   ├── How many suppliers?
│   └── How unique is their product?
├── Bargaining Power of Buyers
│   ├── How many buyers?
│   └── How price-sensitive are they?
├── Threat of Substitutes
│   ├── What alternatives exist?
│   └── How do they compare on price/value?
└── Competitive Rivalry
    ├── Number of competitors
    ├── Industry growth rate
    └── Differentiation level

Competitive Positioning
├── Cost Leadership (lowest cost)
├── Differentiation (unique value)
└── Focus (niche market)
```

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Bad | Fix |
|:--------|:-------------|:----|
| Jumping to solution without structure | Shows lack of analytical rigor | Always start with framework |
| Using framework without adapting | Sounds rehearsed | Customize to the specific case |
| Ignoring the specific industry | Generic advice is useless | Ask about industry dynamics |
| Not quantifying | Vague recommendations | Always back with numbers |
| Not synthesizing at the end | Missing the "so what" | End with clear recommendation |
| Asking too many clarifying questions | Wastes time | Ask 3-5 key questions, then start |
