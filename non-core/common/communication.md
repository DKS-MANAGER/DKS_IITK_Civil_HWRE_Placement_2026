# Business Communication for Non-Core Roles

> Structured thinking, synthesis, executive communication, and stakeholder management — the communication skills non-core interviews actually test.

---

## Why Communication Is a Non-Negotiable Skill

In non-core roles, communication isn't "soft" — it's the primary deliverable:
- **Consulting:** You communicate recommendations to C-suite
- **PM:** You align engineering, design, and business teams
- **Analytics:** You translate data into business decisions
- **Operations:** You coordinate across departments daily

## 1. Structured Thinking

### Pyramid Principle (Minto)

**Start with the answer, then support it.**

```
RECOMMENDATION (Answer)
├── Supporting Argument 1
│   ├── Evidence A
│   └── Evidence B
├── Supporting Argument 2
│   ├── Evidence C
│   └── Evidence D
└── Supporting Argument 3
    └── Evidence E
```

**Example:**
> **Q: Should we enter the Indian EV market?**
>
> **Answer:** Yes, but through a partnership model.
>
> **Why:**
> 1. Market is growing at 35% CAGR (Evidence: industry reports)
> 2. Our technology advantage is strong (Evidence: patent portfolio)
> 3. Partnership reduces capital risk (Evidence: competitor case studies)

### MECE Principle

**Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive** — the gold standard for structuring:
- No overlap between categories (mutually exclusive)
- All possibilities covered (collectively exhaustive)

**Example — Market Segmentation:**
- ❌ Not MECE: "Young people, urban dwellers, tech users" (overlap)
- ✅ MECE: "By age: 18-25, 26-35, 36-50, 50+" (no overlap, complete)

### Issue Trees

Break a problem into sub-components until each branch is actionable.

**Example — "Why are profits declining?"**
```
Profits = Revenue - Costs
├── Revenue declining?
│   ├── Volume declining?
│   │   ├── Fewer customers?
│   │   └── Lower frequency?
│   └── Price declining?
│       ├── Discounting more?
│       └── Competitor pressure?
└── Costs increasing?
    ├── Fixed costs up?
    └── Variable costs up?
```

## 2. Synthesis vs. Summary

### Summary (Weak)
> "We analyzed the market, looked at competitors, and surveyed customers."

### Synthesis (Strong)
> "The Indian EV market is attractive but requires a partnership entry strategy. Market growth (35% CAGR) and our technology advantage support entry, but capital intensity and regulatory uncertainty favor partnerships over wholly-owned operations."

**Rule:** Synthesis = Facts + Your Interpretation + Recommendation

### The "So What?" Test

After every data point, ask: "So what? Why does this matter?"

| Observation | So What? | Synthesis |
|:------------|:---------|:----------|
| Market grew 20% | = Large addressable opportunity | Growth supports market entry |
| Competitor X failed | = Barriers exist but are surmountable | Partnership mitigates similar risks |
| Customer survey: 60% interested | = Demand exists | Pre-launch marketing should target this segment |

## 3. Executive Communication

### The 30-Second Rule

If you can't explain your point in 30 seconds, you don't understand it well enough.

**Framework:**
1. **Headline** (5 seconds): State your conclusion
2. **Why** (15 seconds): Give 2-3 reasons
3. **So what** (10 seconds): State the implication/action

### Written Communication

| Element | Rule |
|:--------|:-----|
| Subject line | Specific, not generic ("Q3 Revenue Analysis" not "Update") |
| Opening | State the purpose in 1 sentence |
| Body | Use bullet points, not paragraphs |
| Data | Highlight the insight, not the number |
| Close | State next steps / ask clearly |

### Presentation Structure

```
1. Opening (30 sec)
   - Who you are
   - What you'll cover
   - Why it matters

2. Context (2 min)
   - Background
   - Problem statement
   - Scope

3. Analysis (5-8 min)
   - Key findings (3 max)
   - Supporting data
   - Implications

4. Recommendation (2 min)
   - Clear recommendation
   - Why this approach
   - Risks and mitigations

5. Next Steps (1 min)
   - Actions required
   - Timeline
   - Owners
```

## 4. Interview-Specific Communication

### Thinking Aloud (Case Interviews)

**What interviewers want to hear:**
- Your thought process, not just the answer
- How you structure ambiguity
- How you handle dead ends

**Template:**
> "Let me take a moment to structure my thoughts..."
> "I'd like to approach this by breaking it into..."
> "Let me check if my understanding is correct..."
> "Based on what we've discussed, my recommendation is..."

### Answering Behavioral Questions

**Bad:** Monologue for 3 minutes without structure
**Good:** 90-second STAR answer with clear transitions

**Transitions that signal structure:**
- "The situation was..."
- "My specific task was..."
- "What I did was..."
- "The result was..."
- "What I learned was..."

### Answering Curveball Questions

**Bad:** Panic, long pause, rambling
**Good:** Buy time, then structure

> "That's an interesting question. Let me think about that for a moment..."
> "I'd approach this in three steps..."

## 5. Stakeholder Communication Matrix

| Stakeholder | What They Want | How to Communicate |
|:------------|:---------------|:-------------------|
| CEO / Senior Leader | Bottom line first | Headlines, 1-page summaries |
| Product Manager | User impact + feasibility | Data + trade-offs |
| Engineer | Technical specifics | Detailed specs, clear requirements |
| Designer | User needs + constraints | User stories, constraints |
| Client | ROI + timeline | Business cases, progress updates |
| Team | Clarity + context | Regular updates, clear action items |

---

## 6. Active Listening

### In Interviews
- **Paraphrase:** "So what you're saying is..."
- **Clarify:** "When you say X, do you mean Y?"
- **Connect:** "That connects to what I mentioned about..."
- **Don't interrupt:** Let the interviewer finish their thought

### In Team Settings
- Take notes during discussions
- Summarize key points before responding
- Ask clarifying questions before assuming

---

## Communication Checklist for Interviews

- [ ] Can explain your background in 90 seconds
- [ ] Can explain any resume bullet in 60 seconds
- [ ] Can structure an answer in 5 seconds
- [ ] Can synthesize data into insights (not just report numbers)
- [ ] Can adjust communication style for different audiences
- [ ] Can think aloud without losing coherence
- [ ] Can ask clarifying questions before diving into problems
- [ ] Can give a clear recommendation with supporting logic
