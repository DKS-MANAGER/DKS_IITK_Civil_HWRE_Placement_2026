# Non-Core HR Questions

> 60+ HR questions specific to non-core placement interviews, with model answer frameworks for each category.

---

## Category 1: Self-Introduction & Background

### Q1: Tell me about yourself.
**Framework:** Present-Past-Future (90 seconds)
- **Present:** Current role/student at IIT Kanpur, M.Tech/B.Tech Civil
- **Past:** Key experiences that built relevant skills (research, projects, leadership)
- **Future:** Why this role, why this company, what you bring

**Template:**
> "I'm currently [year] at IIT Kanpur, pursuing [degree] in Civil Engineering. During my time here, I [2-3 key experiences — research, projects, leadership]. Through [specific experience], I developed [skill relevant to role]. I'm now looking to [target role] because [genuine reason connecting your background to the role]."

### Q2: Why are you switching from Civil to [non-core role]?
**Framework:** PPP (Past → Present → Future)
- Acknowledge your Civil background as a strength
- Explain the natural progression (not a "switch" — an "extension")
- Connect specific Civil skills to the target role

**Model Answer (for consulting):**
> "My Civil Engineering background has given me strong quantitative and analytical skills. Through my research in [area], I developed structured problem-solving abilities — analyzing complex systems, breaking them into components, and deriving actionable insights. I realized these are exactly the skills consulting demands. My thesis on [topic] was essentially a consulting project — understanding the problem, analyzing data, and recommending solutions."

### Q3: What do you know about our company?
**Preparation Method:**
1. Revenue and size (annual report, LinkedIn)
2. Recent news (last 3 months)
3. Business model (how they make money)
4. Key leadership (CEO, business head)
5. Why they recruit from IIT

### Q4: Why this role specifically?
**Structure:**
1. What the role involves (show you understand)
2. Why it matches your skills (specific evidence)
3. Why it excites you (genuine interest)
4. Why at this company (company-specific angle)

### Q5: What are your strengths?
**Rule:** Pick 2 strengths with evidence, not generic adjectives.

**Template:** "[Strength]. For example, [specific story with outcome]."

## Category 2: Motivation & Fit

### Q6: Why should we hire you?
**Structure:** Skills + Evidence + Value
> "I bring [skill 1] demonstrated through [evidence], [skill 2] from [evidence], and [skill 3] which helped me [outcome]. I'm particularly excited about [company-specific reason] because [connection to your interests]."

### Q7: Where do you see yourself in 5 years?
**Framework:** Ambition + Realism + Company Alignment
> "In 5 years, I see myself having [genuine career goal] while contributing to [company's mission]. Specifically, I'd want to [concrete aspiration] — which is why starting in [this role] makes sense as it builds [relevant skills]."

### Q8: What motivates you?
- Pick genuine motivators (impact, learning, problem-solving, team achievement)
- Connect to the role
- Avoid money as primary motivator

### Q9: Are you comfortable with relocation / travel / long hours?
**Be honest but positive:**
> "I understand [role] requires [specific demand]. My experience with [relevant experience] has prepared me for this — [specific example of working under similar conditions]."

### Q10: What's your biggest weakness?
**Framework:** Genuine weakness + Mitigation + Progress
- Pick a real weakness (not a humble brag)
- Show awareness and active improvement
- Never say "I work too hard"

## Category 3: Behavioral (Non-Core Specific)

### Q11: Tell me about a time you solved a complex problem.
**Use STAR framework.** Pick a problem with quantitative/business relevance.

### Q12: Describe a time you worked under a tight deadline.
**Emphasize:** Prioritization, efficiency, quality under pressure.

### Q13: Tell me about a time you had to influence without authority.
**Critical for consulting/PM roles.** Show stakeholder management.

### Q14: Describe a situation where you had to make a decision with incomplete information.
**Key:** How you structured your thinking, what assumptions you made, the outcome.

### Q15: Tell me about a time you failed.
**Framework:** CARL (Context → Action → Result → Learning)
- Real failure, not disguised success
- Genuine learning and behavioral change

### Q16: How do you handle ambiguity?
**Connect to:** Research experience (thesis had no clear answers), project management (uncertain timelines), data analysis (incomplete datasets).

### Q17: Describe a time you disagreed with your team.
**Emphasize:** Constructive disagreement, data-driven argument, respect for team decision.

### Q18: Tell me about a time you led a team.
**Quantify:** Team size, duration, challenge, outcome.

### Q19: How do you prioritize multiple tasks?
**Show:** Structured approach (urgency × importance matrix), communication, follow-through.

### Q20: Describe your most challenging project.
**Pick:** A project with genuine difficulty, technical + interpersonal challenges, clear outcome.

## Category 4: Role-Specific Questions

### For Consulting:
- Q21: How would you approach a client who disagrees with your recommendation?
- Q22: What business book/article have you read recently?
- Q23: How would you structure a market entry analysis?

### For Analytics/BA:
- Q24: What's the difference between correlation and causation?
- Q25: How would you investigate a sudden drop in a KPI?
- Q26: What metrics would you track for [product/service]?

### For Product:
- Q27: What product do you use daily? How would you improve it?
- Q28: How would you prioritize features for a new product?
- Q29: Design a feature for [user group].

### For Operations:
- Q30: How would you reduce turnaround time in a process?
- Q31: Describe a time you optimized a process.
- Q32: How do you handle bottlenecks?

### For Finance:
- Q33: What's the difference between NPV and IRR?
- Q34: How would you value a company?
- Q35: Explain EBITDA to a non-finance person.

## Category 5: Logistics & Closing

### Q36: What are your salary expectations?
**Research:** Check Glassdoor, campus placement data, role benchmarks.
**Response:** Give a range, not a fixed number. "Based on my research, roles like this typically range from [X to Y]. I'm open to discussion based on the complete package."

### Q37: When can you join?
**Be honest.** "I can join [specific date]. If earlier is needed, I can discuss with my department."

### Q38: Do you have other offers?
**Be honest but strategic.** "I'm in discussions with a few companies for similar roles. I'm particularly interested in [this company] because [genuine reason]."

### Q39: What are your hobbies?
**Pick hobbies that:** Show leadership, teamwork, communication, or genuine passion.
**Avoid:** Passive activities (watching TV, sleeping)

### Q40: Do you have any questions for us?
**Always have 3+ prepared.** See [Questions to Ask](../../prep/behavioral/questions-to-ask-interviewer.md).

## Category 6: Curveball Questions

### Q41: If you were an animal, which would you be?
**Strategy:** Pick an animal → explain why (skills/traits that match the role)

### Q42: How many tennis balls fit in this room?
**This is a guesstimate.** Stay calm, structure your approach, estimate systematically.
→ See [Guesstimate Guide](../guesstimates/guesstimate-guide.md)

### Q43: Sell me this pen.
**Framework:** Features → Benefits → Close
"Let me understand what you need first..." (consulting mindset)

### Q44: What would you do on your first day?
**Show:** Proactive thinking, learning orientation, team integration.

### Q45: If you could have dinner with anyone, who?
**Pick someone relevant** to your interests/role. Explain why — it reveals your values.

## Quick Answer Templates

### "Tell me about yourself" (30-second version)
> "I'm [name], [year] at IIT Kanpur, [degree]. I've worked on [key experience 1] and [key experience 2], developing [relevant skill]. I'm targeting [role] because [reason]."

### "Why this company?"
> "Three reasons: [1] [Business reason — their market position/growth], [2] [Role reason — what you'll learn/do], [3] [Personal reason — culture/values/alignment]."

### "Why this role?"
> "It combines my strengths in [skill 1] and [skill 2] with my interest in [area]. Through [experience], I've seen how [relevant impact], and I want to do more of that."

### "What's your weakness?"
> "I tend to [genuine weakness]. I've been working on it by [specific action]. For example, [recent improvement]."
