# Interview Frameworks for Non-Core

> Which framework to use when — a decision guide for behavioral, case, and role-specific interviews.

---

## Framework Decision Guide

| Question Type | Best Framework | When to Use |
|:--------------|:---------------|:------------|
| "Tell me about a time..." | **STAR** | Behavioral questions — your default |
| "Describe a failure..." | **CARL** | Learning-oriented questions |
| "Why this role/company?" | **PPP** | Motivation questions |
| "What would you do if..." | **SOAR** | Situational/hypothetical questions |
| "What do you think about..." | **PREP** | Opinion-based questions |
| "How would you approach..." | **Issue Tree** | Case/analytical questions |
| "Design a product..." | **User → Need → Solution** | Product questions |
| "Estimate X..." | **Top-Down / Bottom-Up** | Guesstimate questions |

---

## 1. STAR Framework (Primary — Use for 70% of Questions)

**Best for:** Behavioral questions ("Tell me about a time...")

| Component | What to Say | Time | Tips |
|:----------|:------------|:----:|:-----|
| **S** — Situation | Context, challenge, stakes | 15 sec | Be specific, not generic |
| **T** — Task | Your specific responsibility | 10 sec | "I was responsible for..." |
| **A** — Action | What YOU did (step by step) | 45 sec | Use "I" not "we" |
| **R** — Result | Quantified outcome + learning | 20 sec | Numbers make it real |

**Total:** 90 seconds

### STAR Examples by Role

**Consulting behavioral:**
> **S:** "Our consulting project team was analyzing why a client's profit margins were declining."
> **T:** "I was responsible for the cost analysis — identifying where costs had increased disproportionately."
> **A:** "I built a cost waterfall comparing 3 years of data. Identified that raw material costs increased 15% while the client hadn't adjusted pricing. Recommended a value-based pricing strategy with phased implementation."
> **Result:** "Recommendation was adopted. Client achieved 8% margin improvement within 2 quarters. I learned that small pricing adjustments can have outsized profit impact."

**PM behavioral:**
> **S:** "During my research project, we needed to decide between two analytical approaches."
> **T:** "As project lead, I needed to make the call while keeping the team aligned."
> **A:** "I created a comparison matrix — time, accuracy, resources for each approach. Consulted with both team members individually. Presented the trade-offs to the full team and facilitated a decision."
> **Result:** "Team chose approach A. Completed the project 1 week ahead of schedule. The structured decision process became our standard for future disagreements."

## 2. CARL Framework (Learning-Oriented Questions)

**Best for:** Failure questions, weakness questions, "what did you learn?"

| Component | What to Say | Time |
|:----------|:------------|:----:|
| **C** — Context | What happened (the failure/mistake) | 20 sec |
| **A** — Action | What you did wrong / what went wrong | 15 sec |
| **R** — Result | The consequence | 15 sec |
| **L** — Learning | What you changed / how you grew | 30 sec |

**Total:** 90 seconds

**Key:** The "L" is the most important part. Spend the most time here.

**Example:**
> **C:** "In my second year, I submitted a research proposal for a competitive scholarship."
> **A:** "I rushed the methodology section without getting feedback from my advisor."
> **R:** "The proposal was rejected. Feedback said the methodology lacked rigor."
> **L:** "I learned three things: (1) always get feedback before submitting high-stakes work, (2) methodology is where reviewers look hardest, (3) rushing creates more work later. I rewrote the proposal over 2 months, got 3 rounds of feedback, and won the scholarship on the second attempt."

## 3. PPP Framework (Present-Past-Future)

**Best for:** Self-introduction, "Why this role?", motivation questions

| Component | What to Say | Time |
|:----------|:------------|:----:|
| **Present** | Where you are now | 20 sec |
| **Past** | Key experiences that led here | 30 sec |
| **Future** | Where you're going + why this role fits | 30 sec |

**Total:** 80 seconds

**Example (Why consulting?):**
> **Present:** "I'm a final year M.Tech student at IIT Kanpur in Civil Engineering."
> **Past:** "Through my research in [area], I developed analytical problem-solving skills — breaking complex systems into components, analyzing data, and recommending solutions. Leading a 4-person team taught me how to coordinate diverse stakeholders."
> **Future:** "I want to apply these skills to solve business problems at scale. Consulting lets me work on different industries, tackle varied challenges, and learn faster than any other role. My technical background gives me a quantitative edge that I want to combine with business strategy."

## 4. SOAR Framework (Situational Questions)

**Best for:** "What would you do if..." questions

| Component | What to Say | Time |
|:----------|:------------|:----:|
| **S** — Situation | Restate the scenario (show understanding) | 10 sec |
| **O** — Options | Consider 2-3 possible approaches | 20 sec |
| **A** — Action | Choose one and explain why | 30 sec |
| **R** — Result | Expected outcome | 15 sec |

**Total:** 75 seconds

**Example:**
> **S:** "So the situation is that a key stakeholder disagrees with our data-driven recommendation."
> **O:** "I see three options: (1) present more data to convince them, (2) find a compromise that partially addresses their concerns, (3) understand their underlying objection and reframe the recommendation."
> **A:** "I'd start with option 3 — dig into why they disagree. Often resistance isn't about data but about implementation concerns or personal stakes. Once I understand the root objection, I can reframe the recommendation to address it."
> **R:** "This typically leads to buy-in because the stakeholder feels heard, and the revised recommendation is usually stronger because it addresses real-world constraints."

## 5. PREP Framework (Opinion Questions)

**Best for:** "What do you think about X?", "Is Y effective?"

| Component | What to Say | Time |
|:----------|:------------|:----:|
| **P** — Point | State your opinion clearly | 10 sec |
| **R** — Reason | Give your reasoning | 20 sec |
| **E** — Example | Provide a concrete example | 20 sec |
| **P** — Point | Restate your opinion | 5 sec |

**Total:** 55 seconds

**Example (Is data more important than intuition?):**
> **P:** "I believe data and intuition are complementary, not competing."
> **R:** "Data tells you what happened; intuition helps you interpret why and predict what's next. The best decisions combine both."
> **E:** "In my research, statistical analysis identified patterns in the data, but my domain knowledge helped me ask the right questions and identify which patterns were meaningful vs. noise."
> **P:** "So data should inform decisions, but intuition and domain expertise should guide which data to trust and how to act on it."

## 6. Issue Tree Framework (Case/Analytical Questions)

**Best for:** "How would you approach..." or "Why is X happening?"

```
Issue
├── Branch 1 (MECE)
│   ├── Sub-branch 1a
│   └── Sub-branch 1b
├── Branch 2 (MECE)
│   ├── Sub-branch 2a
│   └── Sub-branch 2b
└── Branch 3 (MECE)
    └── Sub-branch 3a
```

**Key principles:**
- **MECE:** Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive
- **Hypothesis-driven:** Start with a hypothesis, then test
- **Actionable:** Each branch should lead to a clear action or insight

## 7. Product Sense Framework

**Best for:** "Design X", "Improve Y", "How would you build..."

```
1. WHO — Define the user
   - Primary user segment
   - Use case / context

2. WHAT — Define the need
   - Problem statement
   - Current alternatives
   - Pain points

3. HOW — Define the solution
   - Core features (MVP)
   - Differentiation
   - Trade-offs

4. SUCCESS — Define metrics
   - How to measure impact
   - Key metrics to track
```

## 8. Guesstimate Framework

**Best for:** "Estimate X", "How many Y in Z?"

```
1. CLARIFY — Ask questions, define scope
2. STRUCTURE — Break into components
3. ASSUME — State assumptions clearly
4. CALCULATE — Work through math step by step
5. SANITY CHECK — Does the answer make sense?
6. STATE — Give final answer with range
```

## Framework Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | Why It's Bad | Fix |
|:-------------|:-------------|:----|
| Memorizing framework verbatim | Sounds robotic | Understand the logic, adapt the language |
| Using same framework for every question | Shows no flexibility | Match framework to question type |
| Skipping to the answer | Interviewers want to see thinking | Show your process |
| Being too rigid | Real problems don't fit neat boxes | Adapt and adjust as you learn more |
| Not acknowledging uncertainty | Overconfidence = red flag | "I don't know, but here's how I'd approach it" |

---

## Framework Practice Drill

1. Pick 5 random behavioral questions
2. For each, choose the appropriate framework
3. Write your answer using the framework structure
4. Time yourself (aim for 90 seconds)
5. Record and review
