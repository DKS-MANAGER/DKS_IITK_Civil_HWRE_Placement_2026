## What Is a Guesstimate?

A guesstimate is an estimation problem where you don't have exact data. You break the problem into components, make reasonable assumptions, and calculate an approximate answer.

**What interviewers test:**
- Structured thinking
- Comfort with ambiguity
- Ability to make reasonable assumptions
- Mental math speed
- Sanity-checking your answer

---

## 6 Estimation Methods

### Method 1: Top-Down (Population Method)

**Start large → narrow down using filters**

```
Total Population
→ Filter 1 (demographics)
→ Filter 2 (eligibility)
→ Filter 3 (behavior)
= Final Estimate
```

**Example: How many smartphones are sold in India annually?**
- Population: 1.4 billion
- Age eligible (15-65): 65% → 910 million
- Can afford smartphone: 60% → 546 million
- Already own one: 70% → need replacement/new = 30% of 546M = 164 million
- Annual replacement rate: ~20% → 164M × 20% = 33M new
- Plus new buyers: 546M × 10% = 55M
- **Total: ~88-90 million/year**

### Method 2: Bottom-Up (Capacity Method)

**Start small → multiply up**

```
Individual Unit
→ Multiply by # of units
→ Scale to market/region
= Final Estimate
```

**Example: How many cups of coffee are sold in India daily?**
- Coffee shops in India: ~5,000 branded + 50,000 unbranded
- Branded: 5,000 × 200 cups/day = 1,000,000
- Unbranded: 50,000 × 50 cups/day = 2,500,000
- Office/home: 100M households × 10% drink coffee × 1 cup = 10,000,000
- **Total: ~13-14 million cups/day**

### Method 3: Demand Method

**How many people want this × how often × how much**

```
# of potential users
× Usage frequency
× Duration/period
= Total demand
```

**Example: How many liters of paint are sold in India annually?**
- Households: 300 million
- Repaint frequency: every 5 years → 60M households/year
- Average house: 3 rooms + exterior ≈ 50 liters
- New construction: 3M houses × 50 liters = 150M liters
- **Total: 60M × 50 + 150M = 3.15 billion liters/year**

### Method 4: Revenue Method

**Price × Quantity → work backwards**

```
Market revenue
÷ Average price
= Volume
```

**Example: How many movie tickets are sold in India annually?**
- Box office revenue: ~₹20,000 crore
- Average ticket price: ₹150
- **Total: 20,000 Cr / 150 = ~133 million tickets/year**

### Method 5: Analogy Method

**Use known benchmarks for similar markets**

```
Known market (India)
→ Compare to similar market (country X)
→ Adjust for differences
= Estimate
```

**Example: How many ATMs does India need?**
- US: 400,000 ATMs for 330M people = 1 ATM per 825 people
- India: 1.4B / 825 = ~1.7 million ATMs needed
- Current: ~250,000 → gap of ~1.45 million

### Method 6: Break-Down Method (Component Method)

**Break the whole into non-overlapping parts, estimate each**

```
Whole = Part A + Part B + Part C
```

**Example: What is the daily water demand of Mumbai?**
- Population: 20 million
- Per capita usage: 150 liters/day
- **Total: 20M × 150 = 3 billion liters/day**
- Add industrial: +20% = **3.6 billion liters/day**

## Guesstimate Structure (Use for Every Question)

```
1. CLARIFY (30 sec)
   "Before I start, let me clarify the scope..."
   - Define what exactly we're estimating
   - Time period, geography, segment

2. STRUCTURE (1 min)
   "I'd like to break this down using [method]..."
   - Choose your method
   - Draw the breakdown

3. ASSUME (1 min)
   "Let me make some assumptions..."
   - State each assumption clearly
   - Use round numbers
   - Base assumptions on logic, not guessing

4. CALCULATE (2 min)
   "So the calculation would be..."
   - Work step by step
   - Show your math
   - Use mental math shortcuts

5. SANITY CHECK (30 sec)
   "Let me check if this makes sense..."
   - Compare to known benchmarks
   - Is the order of magnitude right?

6. STATE (15 sec)
   "So my estimate is approximately [X]..."
   - Give a clean number
   - Optionally give a range
```

**Total time: 5-7 minutes**

## Mental Math Shortcuts

| Operation | Shortcut |
|:----------|:---------|
| × 15 | × 10 + × 5 |
| × 11 | Split digits, sum middle (23×11 = 253) |
| Square ending in 5 | n × (n+1) append 25 → 35² = 1225 |
| ÷ 5 | × 2, move decimal left |
| 15% of X | 10% + 5% |
| 25% of X | ÷ 4 |
| 33% of X | ÷ 3 |
| 12.5% of X | ÷ 8 |
| Growth ~72/r | Rule of 72: doubles in 72/r years |

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Bad | Fix |
|:--------|:-------------|:----|
| Jumping to calculation without structure | Shows no methodology | Always state your approach first |
| Using precise numbers (3.7 million) | False precision | Use round numbers (3.5-4 million) |
| Not stating assumptions | Interviewer can't follow | Verbalize every assumption |
| Losing track of units | Dimensional errors | Track units throughout |
| Forgetting sanity check | Clearly wrong answer | Always compare to benchmarks |
| Taking too long (>8 min) | Poor time management | Practice speed; aim for 5-7 min |
| Not engaging the interviewer | Monologue | Pause, ask if they want to continue |

## Practice Method

1. **Daily:** Do 2 guesstimates mentally (during commute, before sleep)
2. **Weekly:** Do 3 guesstimates on paper with full structure
3. **Bi-weekly:** Practice with a friend (they ask, you answer)
4. **Record** yourself and review pacing
5. **Time yourself** — aim for 5-7 minutes per guesstimate
