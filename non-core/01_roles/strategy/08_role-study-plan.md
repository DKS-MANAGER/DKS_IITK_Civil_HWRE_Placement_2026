# Strategy — Role Study Plan

## Role Overview

The Strategy role targets **strategy analyst/associate positions** at consulting firms (McKinsey, BCG, Bain, Deloitte, PwC), **corporate strategy teams** (Tata, Reliance, Adani, Infosys), **banks** (Barclays, HSBC), and **tech companies** (Google, Amazon, Flipkart). The role covers market analysis, competitive analysis, business model evaluation, financial modeling, and strategic planning. Civil engineers with research and analytical training transition well — strategy is structured problem-solving under uncertainty.

**Who targets this role:** B.Tech/M.Tech graduates with strong analytical skills, students with research experience, GATE qualifiers with business interest, those who enjoy market/competitive analysis.

## 3–4 Highest-Value Preparation Blocks

### Topic 1: Market & Competitive Analysis

#### Why This Matters
Every strategy interview tests your ability to analyze markets and competition. Market sizing (TAM/SAM/SOM), Porter's Five Forces, and competitive positioning are the core frameworks.

#### What to Learn
- [ ] Market analysis: Size, growth (CAGR), trends, drivers, segments
- [ ] TAM / SAM / SOM: Total, Serviceable, Obtainable market
- [ ] Porter's Five Forces: New entrants, suppliers, buyers, substitutes, rivalry
- [ ] SWOT analysis: Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities, Threats
- [ ] Competitive benchmarking: Positioning, pricing, market share
- [ ] PESTEL: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Environmental, Legal
- [ ] Market entry analysis: Attractiveness × Feasibility
- [ ] Data sources: Industry reports, company filings, surveys

#### Worked Example
**Problem:** Analyze the Indian electric vehicle (EV) market for a company considering entry. Use TAM/SAM/SOM and Porter's Five Forces.

**Solution:**
1. **Market sizing (TAM/SAM/SOM):**
   - TAM: India sells ~4 million passenger vehicles/year. If EVs reach 30% by 2030 → **1.2 million EVs/year** at avg ₹12 lakh = **₹1.44 lakh Cr/year**
   - SAM: Two-wheeler + passenger EV segment the company can serve → ~60% = **₹86,400 Cr**
   - SOM: Realistic 5% share in 3 years → **₹4,320 Cr**

2. **Porter's Five Forces:**
   - **New entrants:** High threat (many startups, government incentives)
   - **Suppliers:** Battery suppliers have power (limited cell manufacturing in India)
   - **Buyers:** Moderate power (price-sensitive, many options)
   - **Substitutes:** High (ICE vehicles, public transport)
   - **Rivalry:** Intense (Tata, Mahindra, Ola, Ather, startups)

3. **Recommendation:** Enter the two-wheeler EV segment (higher growth, lower entry barrier) with a focus on battery supply partnerships.

**Interview insight:** "I'd size the market top-down with clear assumptions, then use Porter's Five Forces to assess attractiveness. The key insight is that battery supply is the critical constraint — securing supply partnerships is more important than brand building initially."

**Basic (3–5):**
1. What is TAM, SAM, and SOM?
2. Explain Porter's Five Forces.
3. What is a SWOT analysis?
4. What is CAGR? How do you calculate it?
5. What is the difference between strategy and tactics?

**Intermediate (3–5):**
6. Analyze a market using Porter's Five Forces.
7. Size the Indian EV market (top-down).
8. How do you analyze a competitor?
9. What is PESTEL analysis?
10. How do you evaluate a market entry opportunity?

**Interview-Level (5+):**
11. A company's market share is declining. What's your strategy?
12. How do you decide between entering two markets?
13. What makes a market attractive vs unattractive?
14. How do you handle limited data in market analysis?
15. Use your civil background to analyze the infrastructure market.

#### Interview Questions (What Interviewer Tests)
| Question | What They're Testing |
|:---------|:---------------------|
| Size this market | Structured thinking |
| Analyze this industry | Framework application |
| Should we enter this market? | Judgment |

| What's the growth strategy? | Strategic thinking |

#### Common Mistakes
- **Jumping** to numbers without a framework
- **Confusing** TAM/SAM/SOM
- **Forgetting** to sanity-check market size estimates
- **Treating** Porter's Five Forces as a checklist, not an analysis
- **Ignoring** the feasibility side (can we actually win?)

#### Completion Criterion
✅ Can size a market (TAM/SAM/SOM) with clear assumptions
✅ Can apply Porter's Five Forces to any industry
✅ Can analyze competitors and market attractiveness

### Topic 2: Business Model & Financial Analysis

#### Why This Matters
Strategy roles require understanding how businesses make money. Business model analysis, unit economics, and financial fundamentals are tested in case interviews.

#### What to Learn
- [ ] Business model canvas: Value proposition, segments, revenue, cost, channels, resources
- [ ] Revenue models: Subscription, transaction, advertising, licensing
- [ ] Unit economics: Contribution margin, LTV, CAC, payback period
- [ ] Profitability: Gross margin, operating margin, net margin
- [ ] Financial statements: Income statement, balance sheet, cash flow
- [ ] Break-even analysis: Fixed costs / (Price - Variable cost)
- [ ] Valuation basics: DCF, multiples (P/E, EV/EBITDA)
- [ ] Cost structure: Fixed vs variable, economies of scale

| [`business-fundamentals.md`](../common/business-fundamentals.md) | Business fundamentals | Full |

#### Worked Example
**Problem:** A SaaS startup has 10,000 customers paying ₹500/month. Monthly costs: ₹30 lakh fixed, ₹100 per customer variable. (a) What is monthly revenue? (b) What is the contribution margin per customer? (c) What is the break-even customer count? (d) If CAC is ₹15,000, what is the payback period?

**Solution:**
1. **Monthly revenue:** 10,000 × ₹500 = **₹50 lakh**
2. **Contribution margin per customer:** ₹500 - ₹100 = **₹400**
3. **Break-even customers:** Fixed costs / Contribution margin = 30,00,000 / 400 = **7,500 customers**
4. **Payback period:** CAC / Monthly contribution = 15,000 / 400 = **37.5 months**

**Interview insight:** "The company is above break-even (10,000 vs 7,500 customers) with a healthy contribution margin of 80%. However, the 37.5-month CAC payback is long — I'd investigate churn and whether LTV justifies the acquisition cost. If LTV/CAC < 3, the model needs work."

#### Practice
**Basic (3–5):**
1. What is a business model?
2. What is unit economics?
3. What is the difference between gross and net margin?
4. What is break-even analysis?
5. What is LTV and CAC?

**Intermediate (3–5):**
6. Compute break-even for a given cost structure.
7. Analyze a company's unit economics.
8. What is the difference between a subscription and transaction model?
9. How do you evaluate a company's profitability?
10. What is the contribution margin? Why does it matter?

**Interview-Level (5+):**
11. A company's margins are falling. Diagnose.
12. How do you value a startup with no profits?
13. What makes a good business model?
14. How do you decide between subscription and one-time pricing?
15. Analyze the unit economics of a food delivery platform.

#### Common Mistakes
- **Confusing** revenue with profit
- **Ignoring** variable costs in margin analysis
- **Not** computing payback period or LTV/CAC
- **Treating** break-even as static — it changes with costs and price
- **Forgetting** that fixed costs don't scale linearly

#### Completion Criterion
✅ Can analyze a business model using the canvas
✅ Can compute unit economics (LTV, CAC, payback)
✅ Can perform break-even analysis
✅ Can evaluate profitability and valuation basics

### Topic 3: Strategic Frameworks & Growth Strategy

#### Why This Matters
Strategy interviews test your ability to structure decisions: growth, diversification, market entry, and resource allocation. Frameworks like Ansoff, BCG matrix, and M&A logic are essential.

#### What to Learn
- [ ] Ansoff Matrix: Market penetration, market development, product development, diversification
- [ ] BCG Matrix: Stars, Cash Cows, Question Marks, Dogs
- [ ] Growth strategies: Organic vs inorganic (M&A)
- [ ] M&A logic: Synergies, market access, capability acquisition
- [ ] Portfolio analysis: Where to invest, divest, hold
- [ ] Competitive strategy: Cost leadership, differentiation, focus (Porter's generic strategies)
- [ ] Blue Ocean vs Red Ocean strategy
- [ ] Scenario planning and strategic uncertainty

#### Study Material
| File | Focus Area | Depth |
|:-----|:-----------|:------|
| [`strategy-overview.md`](strategy-overview.md) | Growth strategy frameworks | Full |
| [`case-frameworks.md`](../consulting/case-frameworks.md) | Case frameworks | Reference |

#### Worked Example
**Problem:** A construction company generates 80% of revenue from residential projects in one city. Growth has stalled. Develop a growth strategy using the Ansoff Matrix.

**Solution:**
1. **Market Penetration (low risk):** Increase share in existing city — better marketing, competitive pricing, repeat customers
2. **Market Development:** Expand to 2-3 new cities with similar demand profiles
3. **Product Development:** Add commercial/industrial construction, or prefabricated construction
4. **Diversification (high risk):** Enter real estate development (own projects) or infrastructure (roads, bridges)

**Recommendation:**
- **Short term:** Market penetration + market development (adjacent cities)
- **Medium term:** Product development (commercial + prefab)
- **Long term:** Selective diversification into infrastructure (leverages civil expertise)

**Interview insight:** "I'd sequence the strategy by risk — penetrate and expand geographically first (lower risk), then add new product lines, and only consider diversification once the core is strong. The company's civil engineering capability is a real asset for infrastructure diversification."

#### Practice
**Basic (3–5):**
1. What is the Ansoff Matrix?
2. What is the BCG Matrix?
3. What are Porter's generic strategies?
4. What is the difference between organic and inorganic growth?
5. What is a Blue Ocean strategy?

**Intermediate (3–5):**
6. Apply the Ansoff Matrix to a company.
7. Classify a company's portfolio using the BCG Matrix.
8. How do you evaluate an M&A target?
9. What are the sources of synergy in M&A?
10. How do you decide between focus and diversification?

**Interview-Level (5+):**
11. A company should diversify or focus? How do you decide?
12. How do you handle uncertainty in strategic decisions?
13. Design a growth strategy for a company entering India.
14. How do you evaluate a divestiture decision?
15. How would you build a competitive moat for a construction company?

#### Common Mistakes
- **Recommending** diversification without assessing core strength
- **Ignoring** execution capability — strategy without execution is worthless
- **Treating** frameworks as answers, not thinking tools
- **Forgetting** to sequence recommendations by risk and priority
- **Not** considering the competitive response

#### Completion Criterion
✅ Can apply Ansoff, BCG, and Porter's frameworks
✅ Can evaluate M&A logic and synergies
✅ Can sequence a growth strategy by risk
✅ Can handle strategic uncertainty

### Topic 4: Strategy Case Studies & Guesstimates

#### Why This Matters
Strategy interviews use case studies to test structured problem-solving. You must be able to break down complex problems, quantify, and recommend — the same skills as your research training.

#### What to Learn
- [ ] Case structure: Clarify → Framework → Quantify → Recommend
- [ ] Issue trees and MECE structuring
- [ ] Profitability cases: Revenue - Cost decomposition
- [ ] Market entry cases: Attractiveness × Feasibility
- [ ] Guesstimates: Top-down, bottom-up, sanity checks
- [ ] Hypothesis-driven problem solving
- [ ] Data interpretation and chart reading
- [ ] Communicating recommendations persuasively

#### Worked Example
**Problem:** "A cement company's profits have declined 20% despite stable revenue. Diagnose and recommend."

**Solution (Structured):**
1. **Clarify:** Is it gross margin, operating margin, or one-time items?
2. **Framework (Profit = Revenue - Cost):**
   - Revenue: Volume × Price (stable per the prompt)
   - Cost: Raw material (clinker, coal), energy, logistics, labor, fixed overheads
3. **Hypotheses:**
   - Coal/energy prices rose (energy is 30-40% of cement cost)
   - Logistics costs increased (fuel prices)
   - Capacity utilization fell (fixed costs spread over less output)
   - Price competition compressed margins
4. **Quantify:** If energy cost rose 15% and is 35% of cost → cost up ~5% → margin compression
5. **Recommend:**
   - Energy efficiency (waste heat recovery, alternative fuels)
   - Logistics optimization (rail vs road, plant proximity)
   - Pricing discipline / premium products
   - Capacity utilization improvement

**Interview insight:** "With stable revenue, the problem is cost-side. I'd decompose cost into energy, logistics, raw material, and fixed — energy is the biggest lever in cement. I'd quantify each hypothesis before recommending, then prioritize by impact and feasibility."

**Basic (3–5):**
1. What is the structure of a strategy case?
2. What is an issue tree?
3. What is MECE?
4. How do you structure a profitability case?
5. What is a hypothesis-driven approach?

**Intermediate (3–5):**
6. "A company's market share is declining. Diagnose."
7. "Should a company enter a new market?" Structure the analysis.
8. "A company should diversify or focus?" Build the framework.
9. "How would you value a company?" Walk through the approach.
10. "A competitor cut prices 20%. What do you do?"

**Interview-Level (5+):**
11. "Design a strategy for a company entering India."
12. "A company's revenue grew but profit fell. Diagnose."
13. "How do you handle a case with no data?"
14. "A client wants to double in 5 years. Is it feasible?"
15. "Use your civil background to analyze the infrastructure sector."

#### Common Mistakes
- **Jumping** to recommendations without analysis
- **Not** quantifying — strategy needs numbers
- **Using** frameworks mechanically without adapting
- **Ignoring** feasibility and implementation
- **Not** communicating a clear, structured recommendation

#### Completion Criterion
✅ Can structure any strategy case (clarify → framework → quantify → recommend)
✅ Can build issue trees and use MECE
