# Case Interview Communication

> How to communicate effectively during case interviews — thinking aloud, clarifying, structuring, hypothesizing, mental math, and synthesizing.

---

## Why Communication Is 50% of the Case

In a case interview, your answer matters less than how you got there. Interviewers evaluate:
- **Structure** — Can you break down ambiguity?
- **Logic** — Is your reasoning sound?
- **Communication** — Can you explain your thinking clearly?
- **Business judgment** — Do your insights make sense?

---

## 1. Thinking Aloud

### What It Means
Verbalize your thought process as you work through the case. Don't solve silently.

### How to Do It

**Bad (silent):**
> *[Candidate stares at paper for 2 minutes, then says "I think the answer is X"]*

**Good (thinking aloud):**
> "Let me think about this. The question is about [restate]. I'd like to break this into [components]. First, let me consider [branch 1]..."

### Templates

| Situation | What to Say |
|:----------|:------------|
| Starting | "Let me take a moment to structure my thoughts..." |
| Breaking down | "I'd like to approach this by breaking it into..." |
| Analyzing | "Looking at this data, I notice..." |
| Stuck | "Let me reconsider. Another way to look at this is..." |
| Concluding | "Based on what we've discussed, my recommendation is..." |

---

## 2. Clarifying Questions

### Why Ask
- Shows you understand the problem before solving
- Prevents solving the wrong problem
- Demonstrates structured thinking

### What to Ask (3-5 questions max)

| Question | Why It Matters |
|:---------|:---------------|
| "What is the client's objective?" | Defines success |
| "What is the time horizon?" | Short vs. long term |
| "What data do we have?" | Scope of analysis |
| "Are there constraints?" | Budget, timeline, resources |
| "What has been tried before?" | Avoid repeating failures |

### How to Ask

**Bad:** "Can you tell me everything about the company?"
**Good:** "To focus my analysis, I'd like to understand: (1) the client's primary objective, (2) the time horizon, and (3) any constraints I should be aware of."

---

## 3. Structuring

### The 30-Second Structure

Before diving into analysis, state your structure:

> "I'd like to structure this problem into three areas:
> 1. Market attractiveness — is the market worth entering?
> 2. Company capabilities — can we compete?
> 3. Financial viability — does it make economic sense?
>
> Let me start with market attractiveness..."

### MECE Principle

**Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive**

- No overlap between branches
- All possibilities covered

**Example — Revenue breakdown:**
- ❌ Not MECE: "Price, volume, and customers" (customers overlap with volume)
- ✅ MECE: "Price × Volume" or "Revenue by product × Revenue by region"

## 4. Hypothesis Formation

### What It Is
State a hypothesis early, then test it with data.

**Example:**
> "My initial hypothesis is that the profit decline is driven by cost increases, not revenue decline, because revenue has been flat while costs have risen. Let me test this by looking at the cost breakdown..."

### Why It Matters
- Shows you can form a view with limited information
- Demonstrates hypothesis-driven thinking (consulting gold standard)
- Lets you be wrong and adjust (shows adaptability)

### How to Do It

```
1. State hypothesis: "I hypothesize that..."
2. Test with data: "The data shows..."
3. Confirm or revise: "This confirms/revises my hypothesis..."
4. Synthesize: "So the key insight is..."
```

---

## 5. Mental Math

### Why It Matters
- Cases involve calculations (growth rates, margins, break-even)
- Speed and accuracy signal quantitative ability
- Interviewers watch how you handle numbers

### Tips

| Tip | Example |
|:----|:--------|
| Use round numbers | 1,247 → 1,250 |
| Verbalize steps | "So 15% of 200 is 30..." |
| Check reasonableness | "That seems high — let me recheck" |
| Use shortcuts | 25% = ÷4, 15% = 10% + 5% |
| Don't rush | Accuracy > speed |

### Common Calculations

| Calculation | Shortcut |
|:------------|:---------|
| 15% of X | 10% + 5% |
| 25% of X | ÷ 4 |
| Growth rate | (New - Old) / Old |
| CAGR | (End/Start)^(1/n) - 1 |
| Break-even | Fixed Cost / Contribution Margin |
| Margin | (Price - Cost) / Price |

## 6. Synthesizing

### What It Is
Summarize your analysis into a clear, actionable recommendation.

### Structure

```
1. Recommendation (1 sentence)
   "My recommendation is to [action]."

2. Supporting logic (2-3 points)
   "This is because (1) [reason], (2) [reason], and (3) [reason]."

3. Risks and mitigations
   "The key risk is [risk], which can be mitigated by [action]."

4. Next steps
   "The next steps would be [step 1], [step 2], and [step 3]."
```

### Example

> "My recommendation is to enter the EV charging market through a phased partnership approach.
>
> This is because (1) the market is large and growing rapidly, (2) our capital and land assets give us a competitive advantage, and (3) partnerships reduce execution risk.
>
> The key risk is regulatory uncertainty, which we can mitigate by engaging with policymakers early.
>
> Next steps: (1) pilot 50 stations in 2 cities, (2) measure utilization and economics, (3) scale based on learnings."

## 7. Handling Pressure

### When You're Stuck

| Situation | What to Say |
|:----------|:------------|
| Don't know the answer | "I don't have the exact data, but here's how I'd approach it..." |
| Made a math error | "Let me recheck that calculation..." |
| Interviewer challenges you | "That's a fair point. Let me reconsider..." |
| Running out of time | "Let me synthesize what we've covered and give a recommendation..." |

### When the Interviewer Pushes Back

**Don't:** Get defensive or argue
**Do:** Acknowledge, reconsider, and adjust

> Interviewer: "I don't think that recommendation makes sense."
> You: "That's interesting — can you help me understand which part concerns you? Is it the market assumption or the execution approach?"

## Bad vs. Good Responses

### Example 1: Structuring

**Bad:**
> "I think we should look at the market and the company and the finances."

**Good:**
> "I'd structure this into three areas: (1) Market attractiveness — size, growth, and competition; (2) Company fit — capabilities and competitive advantage; (3) Financial viability — investment, returns, and risks. Let me start with market attractiveness..."

### Example 2: Analysis

**Bad:**
> "The revenue is ₹10 crore and costs are ₹8 crore, so profit is ₹2 crore."

**Good:**
> "Revenue is ₹10 crore with costs of ₹8 crore, giving a 20% margin. This is below the industry benchmark of 30%, suggesting either pricing pressure or cost inefficiency. Let me dig into the cost breakdown to identify the driver..."

### Example 3: Recommendation

**Bad:**
> "I think they should enter the market."

**Good:**
> "My recommendation is to enter the market through a phased approach — pilot in 2 cities, validate economics, then scale. This balances the large opportunity (₹500 Cr market, 40% growth) with execution risk, and our existing assets (land, capital) give us a head start over competitors."

---

## Practice Method

1. **Record yourself** solving a case
2. **Review** — did you think aloud? Structure? Synthesize?
3. **Practice with a friend** — they play interviewer, you solve
4. **Time yourself** — aim for 25-30 minutes per case
5. **Get feedback** — ask "Was my communication clear?"
