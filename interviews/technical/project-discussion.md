# Project Discussion Guide

## How to Present Your Projects in Interviews

### The 3-Layer Answer Framework

When asked about your projects, structure your answer in three layers:

1. **Concept** — What problem were you solving? Why does it matter?
2. **Application** — What methods, tools, and approaches did you use?
3. **Caveat** — What were the limitations? What would you improve?

### Project Selection

Choose 2-3 projects that demonstrate:
- Technical depth in your core domain (Civil/HWRE)
- Variety of skills (analysis, coding, design, experimentation)
- Relevance to the target role (PSU, core design, analytics)

### Presentation Checklist

- [ ] Project title and context (1 sentence)
- [ ] Problem statement and motivation (2-3 sentences)
- [ ] Your specific role and responsibilities
- [ ] Methodology / approach overview
- [ ] Key results / outcomes (quantified if possible)
- [ ] Challenges faced and how you overcame them
- [ ] What you learned / would do differently

### Common Pitfalls

- **Too much detail** — Interviewers don't want a full thesis defense
- **Too vague** — "I worked on a water project" is not enough
- **Blaming teammates** — Stay positive about team dynamics
- **Ignoring failures** — Mention challenges and what you learned

### Role-Specific Angles

| Target Role | Project Angle |
|-------------|---------------|
| **PSU** | Code compliance, IS standards, field applicability, safety |
| **Core Design** | Design choices, trade-offs, assumptions, validation |
| **Analytics** | Data pipeline, metrics, reproducibility, insight communication |
| **Research** | Methodology rigor, novelty, limitations, future work |

### Sample Structure

```text
"My final year project focused on [problem]. I was responsible for [role].
We used [method/tool] to achieve [result]. The main challenge was [challenge],
which we solved by [solution]. If I were to do it again, I would [improvement]."
```
