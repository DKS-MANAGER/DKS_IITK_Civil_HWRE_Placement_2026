## 📝 Story 1: Cross-Functional Study Group

**Situation:**
Our placement preparation group at IIT Kanpur included 8 students from Civil, Mechanical, and Electrical departments with different technical strengths, study schedules, and target companies.

**Task:**
Create a unified study schedule and resource-sharing system that worked for everyone.

**Action:**
1. Surveyed needs — each member listed top 3 prep areas, preferred hours, target companies
2. Found common ground — all needed aptitude + behavioral; technical varied by department
3. Designed unified schedule: Mon/Wed/Fri shared sessions, Tue/Thu department-specific, Sat mock interviews
4. Built shared Notion workspace with topic trackers, resource links, mock schedules
5. Rotated facilitation — each department led their own technical sessions

**Result:**
All 8 members improved mock scores by 2+ points. Cross-department mock interviews were especially valued — Civil students learned coding from CS peers, CS students gained domain knowledge. The resource hub became the batch's primary prep reference.

⏱️ **Target time: 75 seconds**

## 📝 Story 2: Supporting a Struggling Teammate

**Situation:**
A teammate in our placement prep group was excellent at Python but struggling with SQL — a key requirement for analytics roles. They were avoiding practice and falling behind.

**Task:**
Help them build SQL confidence without taking significant time from my own preparation.

**Action:**
1. Understood the block — "Joins and window functions feel abstract compared to pandas"
2. Created a bridge — mapped SQL concepts to Python/pandas equivalents (merge = JOIN, groupby = GROUP BY)
3. Designed "SQL Challenge of the Day" — 1 problem each, 30 minutes, compare solutions
4. Paired practice 3x/week, 45 minutes — I explained via Python analogies, they helped me with optimization
5. Progress tracking via shared spreadsheet

**Result:**
Their SQL improved from basic to intermediate in 3 weeks. They cracked the Accenture analytics round. I also improved my Python through teaching. The best way to learn is to teach.

⏱️ **Target time: 75 seconds**

## 📝 Story 3: Resolving Presentation Approach Conflict

**Situation:**
For our final semester project presentation at IIT Kanpur, two members wanted a detailed technical deep-dive (30 slides), while two wanted a high-level overview (10 slides) for the mixed audience.

**Task:**
As the designated presenter, create a unified presentation satisfying both approaches.

**Action:**
1. Identified the core conflict — it was about audience adaptation, not content quality
2. Proposed hybrid structure — 10-slide executive summary + 20-slide technical appendix
3. Delegated by strength — technical members built appendix, overview members built summary
4. Practiced both modes — 15-min summary + 5-min Q&A with appendix references
5. Delivered — faculty questions answered from appendix, industry guests praised clarity

**Result:**
Won "Best Project Presentation" award. Both subgroups felt represented. The hybrid template became department standard.

⏱️ **Target time: 75 seconds**

## 📝 Story 4: Cross-Department Collaboration

**Situation:**
Our Civil Engineering team needed structural analysis expertise (FEA) for a bridge scour project, but no one in our team had strong FEA background. The Mechanical department had the expertise.

**Task:**
Initiate and manage collaboration with Mechanical Engineering students who had different timelines.

**Action:**
1. Identified right partners through professor introduction
2. Defined scope clearly — "stress analysis on pier foundation for 3 scour depths, 3-week timeline"
3. Set up weekly 30-minute sync with shared Google Drive
4. Integrated work — Civil did CFD, Mechanical did FEA, we jointly interpreted results
5. Co-authored — included both teams in conference paper submission

**Result:**
Project completed on time. Paper accepted at national conference. Both teams gained cross-disciplinary experience. The collaboration model was replicated for 2 other projects.

⏱️ **Target time: 75 seconds**

## 📝 Story 5: Balancing Individual and Team Goals

**Situation:**
During thesis period at IIT Kanpur, I had a personal goal to publish a first-author paper while the team needed me to handle mesh generation for everyone's simulations.

**Task:**
Balance personal publication goal with team's mesh needs without compromising either.

**Action:**
1. Transparent communication — "I want to submit a paper by Month X. The team needs meshes by Week Y. Let's plan both."
2. Time-boxed — Mon-Wed for team meshes, Thu-Fri for paper writing
3. Delegated strategically — trained one teammate on basic meshing for simple geometries
4. Created mesh templates for common geometries so others could self-serve
5. Protected focus time — "No mesh requests Thu-Fri unless critical"

**Result:**
Delivered all team meshes on time. Paper accepted at conference. Teammates learned basic meshing. Explicit prioritization + delegation + boundaries enables both individual and team success.

⏱️ **Target time: 75 seconds**

## 📝 Story 6: Coordinating Mock Interview Sessions

**Situation:**
Nobody in our batch was organizing GD and mock interview sessions before company visits. Students were unprepared.

**Task:**
Organize structured practice sessions — design, recruit, and run consistently.

**Action:**
1. Designed weekly 2-hour program — GD (30 min), mock interviews (60 min), feedback (30 min)
2. Created content — GD topics from previous seasons, mock question banks by company type
3. Recruited 3 senior facilitators who had cleared placements
4. Managed logistics — rooms, WhatsApp group, feedback forms
5. Iterated based on weekly feedback

**Result:**
8 sessions, 30+ participants. Multiple credited sessions for their success. Became an annual tradition.

⏱️ **Target time: 75 seconds**

## 📝 Story 7: Knowledge Transfer in Team

**Situation:**
Our lab's only HPC expert was graduating in 2 weeks. They were the only person who knew the cluster setup for CFD runs.

**Task:**
Ensure complete knowledge transfer before they left.

**Action:**
1. Proposed structured handover — 3 sessions over 2 weeks
2. Created handover checklist — access, queue, modules, errors, optimization, contacts
3. Scheduled sessions: basics, job submission, optimization
4. Hands-on approach — I drove first, they drove second, I observed third
5. Created "HPC Quick Start Guide" with screenshots in lab wiki

**Result:**
Smooth transition, zero downtime. Guide became standard onboarding. Knowledge transfer needs documentation + hands-on + verification.

⏱️ **Target time: 75 seconds**

## 📝 Story 8: Building Trust After Conflict

**Situation:**
During a group project, I had a disagreement with a teammate about mesh quality standards. The tension lasted several days and affected collaboration.

**Task:**
Rebuild trust and restore effective collaboration.

**Action:**
1. Initiated private conversation — "I want to make sure we're good"
2. Acknowledged my part — "I could have presented my concerns more diplomatically"
3. Found common ground — "We both want the best result. Let's define quality criteria together"
4. Co-created mesh quality checklist — minimum requirements both agreed on
5. Consistently applied standards and gave positive feedback when quality was met

**Result:**
Checklist became team standard. Teammate thanked me for the direct conversation. Worked together effectively on 2 more projects. Trust is rebuilt through consistent actions.

⏱️ **Target time: 75 seconds**

## 📝 Story 9: Celebrating Team Success

**Situation:**
After 3 months of intense thesis work, our team submitted all deliverables on time. Everyone was exhausted and immediately scattered.

**Task:**
Ensure proper closure and celebration to maintain relationships.

**Action:**
1. Organized closure — "Let's have a proper retrospective and celebration"
2. Structured retro — each shared: proudest moment, biggest learning, appreciation for another member
3. Celebrated with team dinner and group photo
4. Future-proofed — contact sheet with emails, LinkedIn, expertise areas; agreed to be references
5. Documented lessons for future thesis groups

**Result:**
Strong professional network maintained — we still collaborate and refer opportunities. How a team ends determines if relationships last.

⏱️ **Target time: 75 seconds**

## 📝 Story 10: Ensuring Team Communication

**Situation:**
In a group presentation rehearsal, I noticed one member's section had conflicting data with mine — different boundary conditions for the same CFD case.

**Task:**
Resolve the inconsistency before the final presentation without embarrassing anyone.

**Action:**
1. Flagged immediately — "I think there's a mismatch. Can we sync for 5 min?"
2. Debugged collaboratively — opened both case files side-by-side
3. Found root cause — version control gap, one used an older case setup
4. Fixed together and set up shared Google Drive with versioned files
5. Added "sync check" to pre-presentation checklist

**Result:**
Presentation was consistent and received positive feedback. Version control practice adopted by other groups. Proactive communication prevents public errors.

⏱️ **Target time: 75 seconds**
