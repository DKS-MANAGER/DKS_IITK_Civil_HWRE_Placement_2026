# 🧠 Situational Judgment Questions — 25 Scenarios with Answer Frameworks

> **Situational judgment questions ("What would you do if...") test your judgment, ethics, and decision-making. Use the SOAR framework: Situation → Options → Action → Result.**

---

## How to Answer Situational Questions

### The SOAR Framework
```
S — Situation: Restate the scenario briefly (shows you understood)
O — Options: Consider 2-3 alternatives (shows analytical thinking)
A — Action: State what you'd do and WHY (shows judgment)
R — Result: Expected outcome + what you'd learn (shows foresight)
```

### Key Principles
1. **Show reasoning, not just the answer** — "I'd consider X, Y, Z, and choose X because..."
2. **Balance empathy and firmness** — especially for people-related scenarios
3. **Reference your values** — "I'd maintain integrity because..."
4. **Show you'd seek input** — "I'd consult my manager/team before deciding"
5. **End with learning** — "I'd reflect on what this taught me"

## Category 1: Team & Collaboration (7 Scenarios)

### S1: Team member not pulling their weight
**Scenario:** A teammate consistently misses deadlines and delivers poor work. The project is at risk.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Confront them publicly, (2) Ignore it and do their work, (3) Have a private conversation to understand the root cause
- **A:** "I'd have a private conversation first — maybe there's a personal issue or unclear expectations. I'd clarify expectations, offer support, and set a clear timeline. If it continues, I'd escalate to the supervisor with documentation."
- **R:** "This addresses the root cause while maintaining the relationship. If escalation is needed, I have evidence."

### S2: Team member takes credit for your work
**Scenario:** A colleague presents your joint work as primarily their own in a meeting.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Confront publicly, (2) Stay silent, (3) Private conversation + transparent documentation
- **A:** "I'd have a private conversation to clarify contributions, then suggest a contribution log so everyone's work is visible. If it continues, I'd ensure my contributions are documented and visible to the supervisor."
- **R:** "This addresses the issue directly while preventing recurrence through transparency."

### S3: Disagreement on technical approach
**Scenario:** You and a teammate disagree on the best technical approach for a project.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Insist on your approach, (2) Give in to avoid conflict, (3) Data-driven comparison
- **A:** "I'd propose running both approaches on a small test case and comparing against benchmarks. Data resolves disagreements better than opinions."
- **R:** "This is objective, respects both views, and leads to the best technical outcome."

### S4: New team member struggling to integrate
**Scenario:** A new teammate is struggling to understand the project and is falling behind.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Let them figure it out, (2) Assign them simple tasks, (3) Mentor them proactively
- **A:** "I'd offer to mentor them — share documentation, walk them through the workflow, and check in regularly. I did this at IIT Kanpur with juniors learning OpenFOAM."
- **R:** "They integrate faster, and the team benefits from their contribution sooner."

### S5: Team member with different communication style
**Scenario:** A teammate's direct communication style is causing friction with others who prefer indirect feedback.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Ignore it, (2) Confront the direct person, (3) Establish team communication norms
- **A:** "I'd facilitate a team discussion to establish norms — 'feedback on work = direct, feedback on behavior = private and kind.' I'd model the behavior myself."
- **R:** "Explicit norms prevent style clashes and improve team communication."

### S6: You're assigned a task outside your expertise
**Scenario:** Your manager assigns you a task you've never done before, with a tight deadline.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Say you can't do it, (2) Struggle silently, (3) Acknowledge the gap and create a learning plan
- **A:** "I'd acknowledge the gap honestly, ask clarifying questions about expectations, and create a structured learning plan. I'd seek help from experienced colleagues and check in regularly."
- **R:** "I learn the skill, deliver the task, and build a new capability. I did this when I learned MATLAB in 1 week for a project."

### S7: Team morale is low
**Scenario:** Your team is demotivated after a project setback. Productivity is declining.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Push harder, (2) Ignore it, (3) Address the root cause and rebuild momentum
- **A:** "I'd first understand the root cause — is it unclear goals, lack of recognition, or burnout? Then I'd break tasks into smaller wins, celebrate progress, and model positive energy."
- **R:** "Small wins rebuild momentum. I used this approach during our thesis crisis."

## Category 2: Ethics & Integrity (6 Scenarios)

### S8: Asked to do something unethical
**Scenario:** Your manager asks you to manipulate data to make a project look better.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Comply, (2) Refuse outright, (3) Refuse + explain + offer alternatives
- **A:** "I'd respectfully decline and explain why: 'I'm not comfortable manipulating data — our credibility depends on transparency.' I'd offer alternatives: analyze why the data is unfavorable, improve the methodology, or present limitations honestly."
- **R:** "I maintain integrity, protect the company from future risk, and offer constructive alternatives."

### S9: Found an error in published work
**Scenario:** You discover an error in a report that was already submitted to a client.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Hide it, (2) Fix it silently, (3) Report it immediately with a fix plan
- **A:** "I'd report it immediately to my manager with a clear explanation and a fix plan. Transparency builds trust — hiding errors creates bigger problems later."
- **R:** "The client appreciates the honesty, and the issue is resolved before it escalates."

### S10: Conflict of interest
**Scenario:** A friend's company is bidding for a project you're evaluating.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Evaluate anyway, (2) Recuse yourself, (3) Disclose and let the team decide
- **A:** "I'd disclose the relationship immediately and recuse myself from the evaluation to avoid any appearance of bias. Transparency protects everyone."
- **R:** "The process stays fair, and my integrity is preserved."

### S11: Pressure to cut corners
**Scenario:** A deadline is tight, and your manager suggests skipping a quality check to save time.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Skip the check, (2) Refuse, (3) Propose a compromise
- **A:** "I'd quantify the risk: 'Skipping validation could lead to errors that cost more time to fix later.' I'd propose a compromise — do the critical checks, defer non-critical ones, or get help."
- **R:** "Quality is maintained, the deadline is met, and the manager sees the value of the check."

### S12: Confidential information request
**Scenario:** A friend asks you to share confidential company information.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Share it, (2) Refuse, (3) Refuse + explain + offer alternatives
- **A:** "I'd decline and explain: 'This is confidential — I can't share it.' I'd offer to help with what I can share publicly."
- **R:** "I protect confidentiality and maintain trust. I've done this with thesis code before publication."

### S13: Witnessing a mistake being covered up
**Scenario:** You see a colleague covering up a mistake that could affect project quality.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Ignore it, (2) Confront publicly, (3) Private conversation + escalate if needed
- **A:** "I'd first have a private conversation with the colleague — maybe it's unintentional. If they don't address it, I'd escalate to the supervisor with the facts."
- **R:** "The mistake is addressed, quality is protected, and I've handled it professionally."

## Category 3: Pressure & Deadlines (6 Scenarios)

### S14: Impossible deadline
**Scenario:** You're given a task with an unrealistic deadline.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Say it's impossible, (2) Work frantically and deliver poor quality, (3) Communicate, prioritize, and propose a realistic plan
- **A:** "I'd communicate the constraint honestly, propose a prioritized plan — what can be done well vs. what needs more time — and ask for help if needed."
- **R:** "The manager sees my realism and problem-solving. We deliver the most critical parts well."

### S15: Multiple urgent tasks
**Scenario:** Three urgent tasks arrive simultaneously, all marked "high priority."

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Do them in order received, (2) Panic, (3) Prioritize by impact and dependency
- **A:** "I'd use an impact-urgency matrix — which task has the highest impact and is most time-sensitive? I'd communicate my prioritization to stakeholders and set expectations."
- **R:** "The most important work gets done first, and stakeholders are informed."

### S16: You make a mistake that affects the project
**Scenario:** You realize your error has caused a project delay.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Hide it, (2) Blame others, (3) Own it, fix it, and learn
- **A:** "I'd own the mistake immediately, explain what happened, propose a fix, and work to recover the timeline. I'd document the lesson to prevent recurrence."
- **R:** "Trust is maintained through honesty, and the project recovers. I've admitted mistakes before — it builds more trust than hiding them."

### S17: Client is unhappy with your work
**Scenario:** A client is dissatisfied with a deliverable and demands a redo.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Defend your work, (2) Apologize and redo blindly, (3) Understand the gap and fix it
- **A:** "I'd first understand exactly what the client expected vs. what we delivered. Then I'd propose a specific fix plan with a timeline and check in regularly."
- **R:** "The client sees responsiveness and professionalism. The relationship is strengthened."

### S18: You don't know the answer
**Scenario:** In a meeting, you're asked a question you can't answer.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Bluff, (2) Say "I don't know" and stop, (3) Acknowledge + commit to finding out
- **A:** "I'd say: 'I don't have that information right now, but I'll find out and get back to you by [time].' Then I'd follow through."
- **R:** "Honesty + follow-through builds trust. Bluffing risks credibility."

### S19: Working under a difficult manager
**Scenario:** Your manager is demanding, gives unclear feedback, and changes requirements frequently.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Complain, (2) Suffer silently, (3) Proactively manage expectations and communication
- **A:** "I'd focus on understanding expectations clearly, asking clarifying questions, and documenting decisions. I'd communicate proactively and deliver quality work."
- **R:** "Clear communication reduces friction. Difficult managers often push you to grow."

## Category 4: Career & Judgment (6 Scenarios)

### S20: Offered a role different from what you applied for
**Scenario:** The company offers you a different role than the one you applied for.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Accept blindly, (2) Reject outright, (3) Evaluate fit and discuss
- **A:** "I'd ask clarifying questions about the role, growth path, and how it uses my skills. If it's a reasonable fit, I'd consider it. If not, I'd discuss honestly."
- **R:** "I make an informed decision that aligns with my career goals."

### S21: Two offers — one higher pay, one better fit
**Scenario:** You have two offers: one pays more, the other is a better role fit.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Take the money, (2) Take the fit, (3) Evaluate long-term value
- **A:** "I'd evaluate long-term value — which role offers better growth, learning, and alignment with my goals? I'd consider the total package, not just salary."
- **R:** "I make a decision aligned with my long-term career, not just short-term pay."

### S22: You disagree with a company policy
**Scenario:** You disagree with a company policy that affects your work.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Ignore it, (2) Complain, (3) Raise it constructively
- **A:** "I'd raise it constructively with my manager — present the issue, its impact, and a suggested alternative. If it's a firm policy, I'd comply while noting my concern."
- **R:** "I show initiative and respect for the organization. Even if unchanged, my concern is on record."

### S23: Asked to relocate to an undesirable location
**Scenario:** Your company asks you to relocate to a remote location for a project.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Refuse, (2) Accept reluctantly, (3) Evaluate the opportunity
- **A:** "I'd evaluate the opportunity — remote postings often offer unique growth and field experience. I'd discuss the duration and support with my manager. If it's a genuine opportunity, I'd accept."
- **R:** "I show flexibility and commitment while making an informed decision."

### S24: You're passed over for a promotion
**Scenario:** A colleague gets promoted instead of you, despite similar performance.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Get resentful, (2) Leave, (3) Seek feedback and improve
- **A:** "I'd seek constructive feedback on what I can improve, understand the decision criteria, and create a development plan. I'd continue performing well and position myself for future opportunities."
- **R:** "I grow professionally and maintain a positive attitude. Resentment helps no one."

### S25: You realize you're in the wrong role
**Scenario:** After joining, you realize the role isn't what you expected.

**Answer Framework:**
- **O:** (1) Leave immediately, (2) Suffer silently, (3) Discuss with manager and explore options
- **A:** "I'd first give the role a fair chance — initial impressions can be misleading. I'd discuss my concerns with my manager, explore how the role could better use my skills, and give it time before deciding."
- **R:** "I make a thoughtful decision based on evidence, not impulse."
