# Quantitative Aptitude Basics

## Overview

Quantitative aptitude measures your numerical reasoning, problem-solving speed, and familiarity with core arithmetic and algebraic concepts. Placement tests typically assess the following topic clusters:

### Arithmetic & Number Theory
- **Number System**: Natural, whole, integer, rational, irrational, prime, composite, and co-prime classifications; unit digit cyclicity; remainders; factors and divisibility.
- **HCF & LCM**: Methods to compute highest common factors and lowest common multiples; relation $HCF \times LCM = \text{Product of two numbers}$.
- **Simplification**: BODMAS/PMDAS order of operations, algebraic identities, surds, indices, and approximation.
- **Progressions**: Arithmetic (AP), Geometric (GP), and Harmonic (HP) sequences with $n^{\text{th}}$ term and sum formulas.

### Commercial Mathematics
- **Percentages**: Fraction-to-percentage conversions, percentage change, successive changes, population growth, and depreciation.
- **Ratio & Proportion**: Direct and inverse variation, componendo/dividendo, mean proportional, partnership profit sharing.
- **Profit, Loss & Discount**: Cost price, selling price, marked price, successive discounts, and dishonest dealer problems.
- **Simple & Compound Interest**: SI and CI formulas, compounding periods, and difference between CI and SI.

### Time, Work, and Motion
- **Time & Work**: Work equivalence, efficiency, man-day-hour chain rule, pipes and cisterns with inlet/outlet pipes.
- **Speed, Time & Distance**: Relative speed, average speed, train crossing problems, upstream/downstream boat problems.
- **Problems on Ages**: Present/past/future age relations, ratio of ages, invariance of age difference.

### Advanced Quantitative Topics
- **Averages & Mixtures**: Arithmetic mean, weighted average, alligation rule, repeated dilution.
- **Permutations & Combinations**: Factorial, $nPr$ and $nCr$, multiplication/addition principles, circular permutations.
- **Probability**: Sample space, events, mutually exclusive and independent events, addition and multiplication theorems.
- **Data Interpretation**: Tables, caselets, bar/line graphs, pie charts, data sufficiency.
- **Mensuration**: 2D and 3D area, perimeter, volume, surface area for standard geometric shapes.
- **Clock & Calendar**: Angle between hands, day of week, leap year rules.

## Key Concept Summary

| Topic | Core Idea |
| :--- | :--- |
| Number System | Classify numbers; apply cyclicity and remainder theorems; count factors via prime factorization. |
| Averages | $\text{Average} = \frac{\text{Sum}}{\text{Count}}$; weighted average for grouped data; alligation for mixture ratios. |
| Percentages | Base value is the denominator in change formulas; successive changes compound multiplicatively. |
| Ratio & Proportion | $a:b = c:d \implies ad = bc$; partnership profits split in the ratio of $\text{Capital} \times \text{Time}$. |
| Profit & Loss | $\text{Profit}\% = \frac{SP - CP}{CP} \times 100$; successive discounts use $(d_1 + d_2 - \frac{d_1 d_2}{100})\%$. |
| Time & Work | Efficiency $\propto \frac{1}{\text{Time}}$; combined rate $= \sum \text{individual rates}$; net pipe rate $= \frac{1}{x} - \frac{1}{y}$. |
| Speed, Time & Distance | $\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}$; relative speed adds for opposite directions and subtracts for same direction. |
| Probability | $P(E) = \frac{n(E)}{n(S)}$; for independent events $P(A \cap B) = P(A) \times P(B)$. |
| Permutations | $nPr = \frac{n!}{(n-r)!}$ (order matters). |
| Combinations | $nCr = \frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!}$ (order does not matter). |

## Worked Examples (15 Problems)

### Example 1: Number System — Unit Digit
**Problem:** Find the unit digit of $7^{123} \times 3^{45}$.

**Solution:**
1. Unit digit of $7^n$: cycle 7, 9, 3, 1 (length 4). $123 \mod 4 = 3$ → unit digit = 3.
2. Unit digit of $3^n$: cycle 3, 9, 7, 1 (length 4). $45 \mod 4 = 1$ → unit digit = 3.
3. $3 \times 3 = 9$.
4. **Answer**: **9**.

### Example 2: HCF & LCM
**Problem:** The HCF of two numbers is 12 and their LCM is 720. If one number is 60, find the other.

**Solution:**
1. $HCF \times LCM = \text{Product of numbers}$.
2. $12 \times 720 = 60 \times x$.
3. $x = \frac{12 \times 720}{60} = 144$.
4. **Answer**: **144**.

### Example 3: Simplification
**Problem:** Simplify: $\frac{3\frac{1}{2} \times 2\frac{2}{3}}{1\frac{1}{4} + 1\frac{1}{2}}$

**Solution:**
1. Numerator: $\frac{7}{2} \times \frac{8}{3} = \frac{56}{6} = \frac{28}{3}$.
2. Denominator: $\frac{5}{4} + \frac{3}{2} = \frac{5+6}{4} = \frac{11}{4}$.
3. Result: $\frac{28}{3} \div \frac{11}{4} = \frac{28}{3} \times \frac{4}{11} = \frac{112}{33} = 3\frac{13}{33}$.
4. **Answer**: **$3\frac{13}{33}$**.

### Example 4: AP/GP
**Problem:** Find the sum of first 20 terms of AP: 5, 8, 11, 14, ...

**Solution:**
1. $a = 5$, $d = 3$, $n = 20$.
3. $S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n-1)d] = 10[10 + 19 \times 3] = 10[67] = 670$.
4. **Answer**: **670**.

### Example 5: Percentage — Successive Change
**Problem:** A number is increased by 20% and then decreased by 20%. Net change?

**Solution:**
1. Net change $= 20 - 20 + \frac{20 \times (-20)}{100} = -4\%$.
2. **Answer**: **4% decrease**.

### Example 6: Ratio & Proportion — Partnership
**Problem:** A invests Rs. 50,000 for 12 months, B invests Rs. 75,000 for 8 months. Profit = Rs. 45,000. Find B's share.

**Solution:**
1. A's capital-time = $50000 \times 12 = 600000$.
2. B's capital-time = $75000 \times 8 = 600000$.
3. Ratio = $1:1$. B's share = $45000/2 = 22500$.
4. **Answer**: **Rs. 22,500**.

### Example 7: Profit & Loss — Dishonest Dealer
**Problem:** A dealer sells at CP but uses 900g for 1kg. Profit %?

**Solution:**
1. Error = 100g. True value = 1000g.
2. Gain % $= \frac{100}{1000-100} \times 100 = \frac{100}{900} \times 100 = 11.11\%$.
3. **Answer**: **$11\frac{1}{9}\%$**.

### Example 8: Simple & Compound Interest
**Problem:** Difference between CI and SI on Rs. 10,000 for 2 years at 10% p.a.?

**Solution:**
1. $SI = \frac{10000 \times 10 \times 2}{100} = 2000$.
2. $CI = 10000(1.1)^2 - 10000 = 2100$.
3. Difference = 100.
4. Shortcut: $CI - SI = SI \times r/100 = 2000 \times 10/100 = 200$? Wait: $CI - SI = P(r/100)^2 = 10000 \times 0.01 = 100$.
4. **Answer**: **Rs. 100**.

### Example 9: Time & Work — Pipes
**Problem:** Pipe A fills in 6 hrs, B in 8 hrs, C empties in 12 hrs. All opened together. Time to fill?

**Solution:**
1. Rates: A = 1/6, B = 1/8, C = -1/12.
2. Net rate $= \frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{8} - \frac{1}{12} = \frac{4+3-2}{24} = \frac{5}{24}$.
3. Time $= \frac{24}{5} = 4.8$ hrs = 4 hrs 48 min.
4. **Answer**: **4 hours 48 minutes**.

### Example 10: Speed, Time, Distance — Trains
**Problem:** Train 200m at 72 km/h crosses train 300m at 54 km/h in same direction. Time?

**Solution:**
1. Relative speed $= 72 - 54 = 18$ km/h $= 5$ m/s.
2. Total length $= 500$ m.
3. Time $= 500/5 = 100$ seconds.
4. **Answer**: **100 seconds**.

### Example 11: Problems on Ages
**Problem:** Father is 3 times son's age. 5 years ago, he was 4 times. Find present ages.

**Solution:**
1. Let son = $x$, father = $3x$.
2. 5 years ago: $3x-5 = 4(x-5) \implies 3x-5 = 4x-20 \implies x = 15$.
3. Father = 45, Son = 15.
4. **Answer**: **Father 45, Son 15**.

### Example 12: Averages & Mixtures
**Problem:** 40L of 30% alcohol mixed with 60L of 50% alcohol. Final concentration?

**Solution:**
1. Alcohol = $40 \times 0.3 + 60 \times 0.5 = 12 + 30 = 42$ L.
2. Total = 100 L. Concentration = 42%.
5. **Answer**: **42%**.

### Example 13: Permutations & Combinations
**Problem:** In how many ways can 5 boys and 3 girls sit in a row if no two girls sit together?

**Solution:**
1. Arrange 5 boys: $5! = 120$ ways. Creates 6 gaps.
2. Choose 3 gaps for girls: $^6C_3 = 20$.
3. Arrange 3 girls: $3! = 6$.
4. Total $= 120 \times 20 \times 6 = 14400$.
5. **Answer**: **14,400 ways**.

### Example 14: Probability
**Problem:** Two dice thrown. Probability sum is 7 or 11?

**Solution:**
1. Total outcomes = 36.
2. Sum 7: (1,6),(2,5),(3,4),(4,3),(5,2),(6,1) = 6 ways.
3. Sum 11: (5,6),(6,5) = 2 ways.
3. Total favorable = 8. $P = 8/36 = 2/9$.
4. **Answer**: **2/9**.

### Example 15: Mensuration
**Problem:** A sphere of radius 6 cm is melted and recast into a cylinder of radius 4 cm. Find cylinder height.

**Solution:**
1. Volume sphere $= \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3 = \frac{4}{3}\pi \times 216 = 288\pi$.
2. Volume cylinder $= \pi r^2 h = \pi \times 16 \times h$.
3. $16\pi h = 288\pi \implies h = 18$ cm.
4. **Answer**: **18 cm**.

## Speed Tricks Summary

| Trick | Formula | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| Square ending in 5 | $(n5)^2 = n(n+1)25$ | $35^2 = 3 \times 4 = 12 \rightarrow 1225$ |
| Square near 50 | $(50 \pm x)^2 = 2500 \pm 100x + x^2$ | $48^2 = 2500 - 200 + 4 = 2304$ |
| Square near 100 | $(100 \pm x)^2 = 10000 \pm 200x + x^2$ | $97^2 = 10000 - 600 + 9 = 9409$ |
| Multiply by 11 | $ab \times 11 = a(a+b)b$ | $47 \times 11 = 4(11)7 = 517$ |
| Multiply by 5 | $n \times 5 = n \times 10 / 2$ | $48 \times 5 = 240$ |
| % to fraction | $12.5\% = 1/8$, $16.67\% = 1/6$, $33.33\% = 1/3$ | Quick mental math |
| Product stability | $A \uparrow x\% \implies B \downarrow \frac{x}{1+x/100}\%$ | Area constant |
| LCM method (work) | Total work = LCM of times | Avoids fractions |

## Exam Strategy Tips

1. **Master shortcuts first**: Percentage change, product stability, and ratio scaling shortcuts reduce solving time dramatically.
2. **Use LCM method for work problems**: Assign total work as the LCM of individual completion times to avoid fractions.
3. **Verify with substitution**: After solving, plug your answer back into the original conditions to catch arithmetic errors.
4. **Practice mixed sets**: DI problems combine percentages, averages, and ratios; weak spots in any single topic will slow you down.
5. **Time management**: Target < 1 min for simple, < 2 min for moderate, < 3 min for complex.
