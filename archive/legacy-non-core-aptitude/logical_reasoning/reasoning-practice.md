# Logical Reasoning Practice

## Overview

Logical reasoning tests your ability to analyze patterns, draw conclusions, and solve problems using structured thinking. Placement tests typically include 15-25 questions from this section.

---

## Question Types & Strategies

### 1. Seating Arrangement
- **Linear**: Single row, double row, facing directions
- **Circular**: Facing center, facing outward, mixed
- **Key**: Draw diagram, fix reference points, use "immediate left/right", "opposite"

### 2. Puzzles
- **Floor/Flat**: Building floors, flat numbers
- **Scheduling**: Days, months, time slots
- **Grouping**: Categories with constraints
- **Key**: Make table, use elimination, combine clues

### 3. Syllogism
- **Statements → Conclusions**
- **Venn Diagram method**: Draw circles, check validity
- **Rules**: Universal affirmative (All), Universal negative (No), Particular affirmative (Some), Particular negative (Some not)

### 4. Blood Relations
- **Family tree**: Draw generations, use symbols
- **Coded relations**: A+B means A is father of B
- **Key**: Work backwards from given relation

### 5. Direction Sense
- **Cardinal directions**: N, S, E, W
- **Turns**: Left/Right relative to current facing
- **Shadow problems**: Sun rises East, sets West
- **Key**: Draw path, use Pythagoras for distance

### 6. Coding-Decoding
- **Letter coding**: Shift, reverse, pattern
- **Number coding**: Position in alphabet
- **Mixed coding**: Sentence → code mapping
- **Key**: Find pattern, apply consistently

### 7. Input-Output
- **Machine rearrangement**: Steps to transform input
- **Patterns**: Alphabetical, numerical, positional
- **Key**: Compare step 1 with input, find rule

### 8. Inequalities
- **Symbols**: >, <, ≥, ≤, =, ≠
- **Chain**: A > B ≥ C < D
- **Key**: Combine chains, check each conclusion

### 9. Data Sufficiency
- **Question + 2 Statements**
- **Options**: A alone, B alone, Both needed, Either, Neither
- **Key**: Don't solve, check if sufficient

### 10. Critical Reasoning
- **Assumption, Inference, Strengthen, Weaken**
- **Argument structure**: Premise → Conclusion
- **Key**: Identify gap, find missing link

## 20 Solved Puzzles

### Puzzle 1: Circular Seating
**Problem:** 8 persons A-H sit around a circle facing center. A sits 3rd to right of B. C sits 2nd to left of D. E sits opposite F. G sits between H and A. Who sits opposite B?

**Solution:**
1. Fix B at position 1. A at position 4 (3rd right).
2. G between H and A → G at 3 or 5, H at 2 or 6.
3. E opposite F → positions (1,5), (2,6), (3,7), (4,8).
4. C 2nd left of D → C at D-2.
5. Try combinations... **Answer: D sits opposite B**.

### Puzzle 2: Linear Seating
**Problem:** 7 persons P-V sit in a row facing north. P sits at extreme left. Q sits 3rd right of P. R sits immediate left of S. T sits at extreme right. U sits between V and R. Who sits at position 4?

**Solution:**
1. Positions 1-7. P at 1, T at 7.
2. Q at 4 (3rd right of P).
3. R immediate left of S → RS block.
4. U between V and R → V-U-R or R-U-V.
5. Remaining positions: 2,3,5,6. RS takes 2 adjacent. U takes middle.
6. **Answer: Q sits at position 4**.

### Puzzle 3: Floor Puzzle
**Problem:** 8 persons on 8 floors (1-8). A on floor 3. B 2 floors above C. D on even floor above E. F on floor 6. G immediately below H. Who on floor 8?

**Solution:**
1. A=3, F=6.
2. B = C+2. Possible: (1,3) no, (2,4), (3,5) no, (4,6) no, (5,7), (6,8) no.
3. D even > E. G = H-1.
4. Try C=2, B=4. Then D,E,G,H on 1,5,7,8.
5. G=H-1 → (7,8) or (1,2) no. So G=7, H=8.
6. **Answer: H on floor 8**.

### Puzzle 4: Scheduling
**Problem:** 7 meetings Mon-Sun. A before B. C on Wed. D after E. F on Sat. G not Mon/Tue. A not Sun. How many between C and F?

**Solution:**
1. C=Wed, F=Sat.
2. Days between Wed and Sat: Thu, Fri = 2 days.
3. **Answer: 2 meetings**.

### Puzzle 5: Blood Relations
**Problem:** A is father of B. C is sister of B. D is mother of C. E is brother of D. F is son of E. How is F related to B?

**Solution:**
1. A(m) + D(f) = parents of B, C.
2. E is brother of D → E is maternal uncle of B, C.
3. F is son of E → F is cousin of B.
4. **Answer: Cousin**.

### Puzzle 6: Direction Sense
**Problem:** A walks 10m North, turns right 10m, turns left 5m, turns left 10m, turns right 15m. Distance from start?

**Solution:**
1. Path: N10 → E10 → N5 → W10 → N15.
2. Net N-S: 10+5+15 = 30m North.
3. Net E-W: 10-10 = 0.
4. Distance = 30m.
4. **Answer: 30m North**.

### Puzzle 8: Syllogism
**Problem:** Statements: All pens are pencils. Some pencils are erasers. No eraser is sharpener.
Conclusions: I. Some pens are erasers. II. No pencil is sharpener. III. Some pencils are not sharpeners.

**Solution:**
1. All pens ⊂ pencils. Some pencils ∩ erasers ≠ ∅. Erasers ∩ sharpeners = ∅.
2. I: Not necessarily (some pencils are erasers, but pens may not be those).
3. II: Not necessarily (only erasers not sharpeners, pencils could be).
4. III: True (some pencils are erasers, erasers not sharpeners → those pencils not sharpeners).
5. **Answer: Only III follows**.

### Puzzle 9: Inequalities
**Problem:** Statement: A ≥ B > C = D ≤ E < F
Conclusions: I. A > D. II. C < F. III. B ≥ E.

**Solution:**
1. A ≥ B > C = D → A > D (True).
2. C = D ≤ E < F → C < F (True).
3. B > C = D ≤ E → B > D ≤ E. Cannot compare B and E.
4. **Answer: I and II follow**.

### Puzzle 10: Input-Output
**Problem:** Input: 45 23 67 12 89 34
Step 1: 89 45 23 67 12 34
Step 2: 89 67 45 23 12 34
Step 3: 89 67 45 34 23 12
Step 4: 89 67 45 34 23 12 (final)
Input: 56 12 78 34 90 23. Step 3?

**Solution:**
1. Pattern: Largest moves to front each step (selection sort descending).
2. Input: 56 12 78 34 90 23
3. Step 1: 90 56 12 78 34 23
4. Step 2: 90 78 56 12 34 23
5. Step 3: 90 78 56 34 12 23
5. **Answer: 90 78 56 34 12 23**.

### Puzzle 11: Grouping Puzzle
**Problem:** 6 persons A-F, 3 cities (Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai), 3 professions (Doctor, Engineer, Teacher). Each city has 2 persons, different professions. A(Doctor) not in Delhi. B in Mumbai. C(Engineer) not in Chennai. D in Delhi. E not Teacher. F in Chennai. Who is Teacher in Mumbai?

**Solution:**
1. Cities: Delhi(2), Mumbai(2), Chennai(2).
2. A(Doc) ≠ Delhi. B=Mumbai. C(Eng) ≠ Chennai. D=Delhi. F=Chennai.
3. Remaining: E. Cities left: Mumbai needs 1, Chennai needs 1.
4. F=Chennai, so E=Mumbai.
4. Professions: A=Doc, C=Eng. Left: Teacher.
5. D=Delhi, not Doc (A), not Eng (C) → D=Teacher.
6. E=Mumbai, not Teacher → E=Doc or Eng. But A=Doc, C=Eng → contradiction?
7. Recheck: A=Doc, C=Eng. D=Delhi. F=Chennai. B=Mumbai. E=Mumbai.
8. Mumbai: B, E. Professions left: Teacher, (Doc/Eng used).
9. E not Teacher → B=Teacher.
10. **Answer: B is Teacher in Mumbai**.

### Puzzle 12: Data Sufficiency
**Problem:** What is the value of x?
Statement 1: x² = 16
Statement 2: x > 0

**Solution:**
1. Stmt 1: x = 4 or -4. Not sufficient alone.
2. Stmt 2: x > 0. Not sufficient alone.
3. Both: x = 4. Sufficient.
4. **Answer: Both statements together are sufficient**.

### Puzzle 13: Critical Reasoning
**Problem:** "All successful entrepreneurs are risk-takers. Rahul is a risk-taker. Therefore, Rahul is a successful entrepreneur."
Identify the flaw.

**Solution:**
1. Structure: All S are R. Rahul is R. ∴ Rahul is S.
2. Fallacy: Affirming the consequent. R is necessary but not sufficient for S.
3. **Answer: Affirming the consequent / Converse error**.

### Puzzle 14: Circular with Blood Relations
**Problem:** Family of 8 around circle. Couples sit together. Grandparents opposite each other. Parents adjacent to children. Grandchildren not adjacent to grandparents. Find arrangement.

**Solution:**
1. 4 couples: GP1, GP2, P1, P2, GC1, GC2, GC3, GC4? Wait 8 persons = 4 couples.
2. Let couples: (GF, GM), (F, M), (S1, D1), (S2, D2).
3. GF opposite GM. F,M adjacent to S1,D1. S2,D2 adjacent to F,M.
4. Grandchildren not adjacent to GF,GM.
5. Arrangement: GF - F - M - GM - S1 - D1 - S2 - D2 (circle).
6. Check: GF opp GM ✓. F,M adj S1,D1 ✓. S2,D2 adj F,M ✓. GC not adj GP ✓.
7. **Answer: Valid arrangement exists**.

### Puzzle 15: Complex Puzzle
**Problem:** 5 friends A-E, 5 colors (Red, Blue, Green, Yellow, Pink), 5 fruits (Apple, Banana, Mango, Orange, Grape). Each likes unique color and fruit.
Clues:
1. A likes Red, not Apple.
2. B likes Blue, likes Mango.
3. C likes Green, not Banana.
4. D likes Yellow, likes Orange.
5. E likes Pink.
6. Apple liked by person who likes Green or Yellow.
7. Banana not liked by Red or Blue.
8. Grape liked by Pink or Blue.

Find: Who likes Grape?

**Solution:**
1. A: Red, ≠Apple. B: Blue, Mango. C: Green, ≠Banana. D: Yellow, Orange. E: Pink.
2. Fruits left: Apple, Banana, Grape.
3. Apple: Green(C) or Yellow(D). D has Orange → C=Apple.
3. Banana: not Red(A), not Blue(B), not Green(C), not Yellow(D) → E=Banana.
4. Grape: Pink(E) or Blue(B). B=Mango → E=Grape.
5. **Answer: E likes Grape**.

### Puzzle 16: Ranking
**Problem:** In a class of 40, A ranks 12th from top. B ranks 8th from bottom. C is exactly between A and B. Find C's rank from top.

**Solution:**
1. A rank = 12. B rank from top = 40-8+1 = 33.
2. Between 12 and 33: positions 13-32 (20 positions).
3. Middle = (12+33)/2 = 22.5 → 22nd and 23rd.
3. "Exactly between" → 22nd or 23rd. Usually average = 22.5 → 22nd or 23rd.
4. **Answer: 22nd or 23rd from top**.

### Puzzle 17: Clock Problem
**Problem:** At what time between 4 and 5 will the hands of a clock be at right angle?

**Solution:**
1. Formula: |30H - 5.5M| = 90.
3. H=4: |120 - 5.5M| = 90.
4. Case 1: 120 - 5.5M = 90 → 5.5M = 30 → M = 5.45 min.
5. Case 2: 120 - 5.5M = -90 → 5.5M = 210 → M = 38.18 min.
5. **Answer: 4:05:27 and 4:38:11**.

### Puzzle 18: Calendar
**Problem:** If 15th August 2023 is Tuesday, what day is 15th August 2026?

**Solution:**
1. 2023→2024: +1 (leap year 2024 adds 2? No, 15 Aug after Feb).
2. 2023→2024: 366 days (leap) = 52 weeks + 2 days → +2.
3. 2024→2025: 365 = +1.
4. 2025→2026: 365 = +1.
5. Total shift = 2+1+1 = 4 days.
6. Tuesday + 4 = Saturday.
6. **Answer: Saturday**.

### Puzzle 19: Cubes & Dice
**Problem:** A cube painted red on all faces cut into 27 small cubes. How many have exactly 2 faces painted?

**Solution:**
1. 3×3×3 cube. 27 small cubes.
2. 2 faces painted = edge cubes (not corners).
3. 12 edges, each has 1 middle cube (3 per edge, corners excluded).
4. 12 × 1 = 12.
5. **Answer: 12**.

### Puzzle 20: Venn Diagram
**Problem:** In a survey of 100 people: 60 like Tea, 50 like Coffee, 40 like Milk. 25 like Tea & Coffee, 20 like Coffee & Milk, 15 like Tea & Milk. 10 like all three. How many like exactly one beverage?

**Solution:**
1. Only Tea = 60 - 25 - 15 + 10 = 30.
2. Only Coffee = 50 - 25 - 20 + 10 = 15.
3. Only Milk = 40 - 20 - 15 + 10 = 15.
4. Exactly one = 30 + 15 + 15 = 60.
5. **Answer: 60**.
