#### Problem 1.01: Concrete Pour Multi-Pump Work Kinetics
**Question:** Pump A can cast a foundation raft in 15 hours. Pump B can cast the same raft in 20 hours. Both pumps start pumping simultaneously at 06:00 AM. After 5 hours, Pump A encounters a mechanical line blockage and shuts down. At what exact time will Pump B complete the remaining concrete pour?  
**Solution:**
- Rate of Pump A: $R_A = 1/15$ of raft per hour.
- Rate of Pump B: $R_B = 1/20$ of raft per hour.
- Combined rate: $R_{AB} = \frac{1}{15} + \frac{1}{20} = \frac{4 + 3}{60} = \frac{7}{60}$ per hour.
- Work completed in 5 hours: $5 \times \frac{7}{60} = \frac{35}{60} = \frac{7}{12}$ of the raft.
- Remaining work: $1 - \frac{7}{12} = \frac{5}{12}$.
- Time taken by Pump B alone: $T = \frac{5/12}{1/20} = \frac{5}{12} \times 20 = \frac{100}{12} = 8.333\text{ hours} = 8\text{ hours and } 20\text{ minutes}$.
- Completion time: 5 hours (from 06:00 to 11:00 AM) + 8 hours 20 mins = **07:20 PM**.

#### Problem 1.02: Batching Plant Cement-Fly Ash Replacement Ratio
**Question:** A ready-mix concrete batching plant produces M40 concrete using OPC and fly ash in the initial ratio $4:1$ by mass. To optimize costs per green concrete standards, $45\text{ kg}$ of fly ash is added while keeping total cementitious content constant, altering the ratio to $3:2$. What was the original mass of OPC in the mix?  
**Solution:**
- Let original OPC $= 4x$ and Fly Ash $= 1x$. Total cementitious $= 5x$.
- When $45\text{ kg}$ of OPC is replaced by $45\text{ kg}$ of Fly Ash:
  $$\frac{4x - 45}{x + 45} = \frac{3}{2}$$
- Cross-multiplying: $2(4x - 45) = 3(x + 45) \implies 8x - 90 = 3x + 135 \implies 5x = 225 \implies x = 45\text{ kg}$.
- Original OPC mass: $4x = 4 \times 45 = \mathbf{180\text{ kg}}$.

#### Problem 1.03: Transit Mixer Round-Trip Average Velocity
**Question:** A concrete transit mixer travels from a batching plant to an urban elevated viaduct construction site $24\text{ km}$ away at an average laden speed of $30\text{ km/h}$. After discharging, it returns empty along the same route at $40\text{ km/h}$. What is the average speed of the mixer for the entire round trip?  
**Solution:**
- Time taken to site: $T_1 = \frac{24\text{ km}}{30\text{ km/h}} = 0.80\text{ hours} = 48\text{ mins}$.
- Time taken to return: $T_2 = \frac{24\text{ km}}{40\text{ km/h}} = 0.60\text{ hours} = 36\text{ mins}$.
- Total distance: $24 + 24 = 48\text{ km}$. Total time: $0.80 + 0.60 = 1.40\text{ hours}$.
- Average speed:
  $$V_{avg} = \frac{2 v_1 v_2}{v_1 + v_2} = \frac{2 \times 30 \times 40}{30 + 40} = \frac{2400}{70} = \mathbf{34.29\text{ km/h}}$$

#### Problem 1.04: Steel Rebar Price Escalation and Consumption Neutrality
**Question:** During a quarterly review of an EPC highway contract, the market rate of Fe 500D rebar rises by $20\%$. By what percentage must the site project manager reduce non-critical steel inventory holding so that the total steel procurement expenditure increases by only $5\%$?  
**Solution:**
- Let initial price $= P_1$, consumption $= C_1$, initial expenditure $E_1 = P_1 C_1$.
- New price $P_2 = 1.20 P_1$. Target expenditure $E_2 = 1.05 E_1$.
- Let new consumption be $C_2$: $E_2 = P_2 C_2 \implies 1.05 P_1 C_1 = 1.20 P_1 C_2$.
- $C_2 = \frac{1.05}{1.20} C_1 = 0.875 C_1$.
- Percentage reduction: $(1 - 0.875) \times 100\% = \mathbf{12.5\%}$.

#### Problem 1.05: Earthwork Excavation Truck Fleet Sizing
**Question:** 18 tipper trucks working 8 hours a day can clear $7,200\text{ m}^3$ of excavated muck from a metro tunnel portal in 5 days. How many tipper trucks working 10 hours a day will be required to clear $12,000\text{ m}^3$ of muck in 6 days?  
**Solution:**
- Relationship: $\frac{W_1}{N_1 \cdot H_1 \cdot D_1} = \frac{W_2}{N_2 \cdot H_2 \cdot D_2}$ (constant rate per truck-hour).
- Substituting values:
  $$\frac{7200}{18 \times 8 \times 5} = \frac{12000}{N_2 \times 10 \times 6}$$
- Left side: $\frac{7200}{720} = 10\text{ m}^3/\text{truck-hour}$.
- Right side: $\frac{12000}{60 N_2} = 10 \implies 600 N_2 = 12000 \implies N_2 = \mathbf{20\text{ trucks}}$.

#### Problem 1.06: Retaining Wall Drainage Reservoir Leakage
**Question:** A drainage sump for a highway retaining wall has an inlet pipe that fills it in 8 hours and a pump that empties it in 12 hours. If the sump is currently empty and both the inlet pipe and drain pump are operated simultaneously, how long will it take to completely fill the sump?  
**Solution:**
- Filling rate: $+1/8$ per hour. Emptying rate: $-1/12$ per hour.
- Net rate: $\frac{1}{8} - \frac{1}{12} = \frac{3 - 2}{24} = \frac{1}{24}$ per hour.
- Total time required to fill: $1 / (1/24) = \mathbf{24\text{ hours}}$.

#### Problem 1.07: Precast Girder Combinations across Pier Spans
**Question:** An elevated corridor has 6 identical precast I-girders per span. If a yard fabricates 10 girders with varying prestressing strand profiles, in how many distinct ways can a site engineer select 6 girders for a single span?  
**Solution:**
- Combination formula:
  $$C(10, 6) = C(10, 4) = \frac{10 \times 9 \times 8 \times 7}{4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1} = \frac{5040}{24} = \mathbf{210\text{ ways}}$$

#### Problem 1.08: Concrete Test Cube Compressive Strength Probability
**Question:** Quality inspection records from a commercial tower pour show that $4\%$ of concrete test cubes fail the 28-day characteristic strength requirement. If 3 cubes are randomly selected for audit verification, what is the probability that at least one cube is defective?  
**Solution:**
- Probability of a cube being sound: $P(\text{sound}) = 1 - 0.04 = 0.96$.
- Probability that all 3 cubes are sound: $P(\text{all sound}) = 0.96^3 = 0.8847$.
- Probability of at least one defective cube:
  $$P(\ge 1\text{ defective}) = 1 - 0.8847 = \mathbf{0.1153} \quad (11.53\%)$$

#### Problem 1.09: Simple vs Compound Interest in Heavy Equipment Leasing
**Question:** L&T leases a hydraulic rotary drilling rig worth $\text{INR } 80\text{ Lakhs}$ for 2 years at an annual interest rate of $12\%$. How much more interest is paid if interest is compounded annually versus simple interest?  
**Solution:**
- Simple Interest: $SI = P \cdot r \cdot t = 8,000,000 \times 0.12 \times 2 = \text{INR } 19,20,000$.
- Compound Interest: $CI = P [(1 + r)^t - 1] = 8,000,000 [(1.12)^2 - 1] = 8,000,000 [1.2544 - 1] = 8,000,000 \times 0.2544 = \text{INR } 20,35,200$.
- Difference: $CI - SI = 2,035,200 - 1,920,000 = \mathbf{\text{INR } 1,15,200}$.  
  *(Shortcut: Difference for 2 years $= P \cdot r^2 = 8,000,000 \times 0.12^2 = 8,000,000 \times 0.0144 = \text{INR } 1,15,200$).*

#### Problem 1.10: Arithmetic Progression in Bridge Pier Chainage Spacing
**Question:** Piers along a river bridge are constructed at chainages following an arithmetic progression: the 4th pier is at chainage $180\text{ m}$ and the 9th pier is at chainage $390\text{ m}$. What is the chainage of the 1st pier and the uniform span spacing between adjacent piers?  
**Solution:**
- $T_4 = a + 3d = 180$
- $T_9 = a + 8d = 390$
- Subtracting equations: $5d = 210 \implies d = \mathbf{42\text{ m}}$ (pier spacing).
- Substituting $d$: $a + 3(42) = 180 \implies a + 126 = 180 \implies a = \mathbf{54\text{ m}}$ (1st pier chainage).

#### Problem 1.41: Syllogism — Safety and QA/QC Rules
**Statements:**
1. All scaffolding components are safety-certified.
2. Some safety-certified elements are made of aluminum.
3. No aluminum elements are used in marine splash zones.
**Conclusions:**
I. Some scaffolding components are made of aluminum.  
II. No scaffolding components are used in marine splash zones.  
**Analysis:** Statement 1 and 2 indicate overlap between scaffolding and aluminum is possible, but not guaranteed. Statement 3 applies only to aluminum elements. Neither conclusion follows with absolute necessity. **Answer: Neither I nor II follows.**

#### Problem 1.42: Seating Arrangement Around Site Planning Table
**Problem:** Six engineers (A, B, C, D, E, F) sit around a circular table facing inward.
1. A is opposite D.
2. B is to the immediate right of A.
3. C is between A and E.
4. F is to the immediate left of D.
*Who is sitting directly opposite B?*  
**Solution:**
- Place A at 12 o'clock $\implies$ D is at 6 o'clock.
- B is at 11 o'clock (immediate right when facing center).
- C is at 1 o'clock, E is at 2 o'clock.
- F is at 7 o'clock (immediate left of D).
- Opposite B (11 o'clock) is the position at 5 o'clock, which is **E**.

#### Problem 1.43: Direction Sense in Highway Alignment
**Problem:** A survey team sets out from Base Station P. They travel $60\text{ m}$ East, turn right and travel $80\text{ m}$ South, turn right again and travel $120\text{ m}$ West, and finally turn right and travel $160\text{ m}$ North to reach Station Q. What is the shortest displacement and direction of Q from P?  
**Solution:**
- East-West: $+60 - 120 = -60\text{ m}$ (West).
- North-South: $-80 + 160 = +80\text{ m}$ (North).
- Straight-line distance: $d = \sqrt{(-60)^2 + 80^2} = \sqrt{3600 + 6400} = \sqrt{10000} = \mathbf{100\text{ m}}$.
- Direction: **North-West**.

### Part 2.3: Mechanical Reasoning & Data Interpretation (Problems 1.76 to 1.100)

#### Problem 1.76: Gear Ratio and Rotational Speed
**Question:** A tunnel boring machine (TBM) cutterhead drive uses a compound gear train. Gear A (20 teeth) rotates clockwise at $300\text{ rpm}$ and meshes with Gear B (60 teeth). Gear B is keyed to the same shaft as Gear C (15 teeth), which meshes with Gear D (45 teeth). Find the rotational speed and direction of Gear D.  
**Solution:**
- Gear A to B: $\omega_B = 300 \times (20/60) = 100\text{ rpm}$ (counter-clockwise).
- Shaft speed $\omega_C = \omega_B = 100\text{ rpm}$ (counter-clockwise).
- Gear C to D: $\omega_D = 100 \times (15/45) = 100 \times (1/3) = \mathbf{33.33\text{ rpm}}$ (clockwise).

#### Problem 1.77: Crane Pulley Tackle Mechanical Advantage
**Question:** A heavy-lift gantry crane uses a tackle system with 6 rope falls supporting the traveling hook block. Neglecting friction, what tensile line pull must the electric winch drum exert to hoist an $84\text{ ton}$ precast metro segment?  
**Solution:**
- Mechanical Advantage $\text{MA} = 6$.
- Winch rope pull: $F = \frac{\text{Load}}{\text{MA}} = \frac{84\text{ tons}}{6} = \mathbf{14.0\text{ tons}}$ ($137.3\text{ kN}$).

#### Problem 1.78: Hydraulic Jack Pressure and Piston Force
**Question:** A prestressing jack has a hydraulic piston diameter of $160\text{ mm}$. If the hydraulic pump supplies fluid at a pressure of $35\text{ MPa}$ ($35\text{ N/mm}^2$), what jacking force is imparted to the prestressing tendon?  
**Solution:**
- Piston Area $A = \frac{\pi}{4} D^2 = \frac{\pi}{4} \times (160)^2 = 20,106.19\text{ mm}^2$.
- Force $F = P \cdot A = 35\text{ N/mm}^2 \times 20,106.19\text{ mm}^2 = 703,716.7\text{ N} = \mathbf{703.7\text{ kN}}$ ($\approx 71.7\text{ tons}$).

#### Problem 1.79: Unfolded Cube Net Face Adjacency
**Question:** A concrete cube marked with inspection icons on its 6 faces is unfolded into a standard cross net. If the "Passed" stamp is on the top face and "Rejected" is on the bottom face, can the "Passed" face share an edge with the "Rejected" face on the folded 3D cube?  
**Solution:**
- No. Top and bottom faces are opposite and parallel; they share zero common edges or vertices.
