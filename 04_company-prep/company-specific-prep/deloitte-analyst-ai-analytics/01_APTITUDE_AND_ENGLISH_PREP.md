### 1.1 Percentages, Successive Changes & Population Dynamics

#### Core Principles & Formulas
1. **Fraction to Percentage Multipliers**:
   $$\frac{1}{2} = 50\%, \quad \frac{1}{3} = 33.33\%, \quad \frac{1}{4} = 25\%, \quad \frac{1}{5} = 20\%, \quad \frac{1}{6} = 16.67\%, \quad \frac{1}{7} = 14.28\%, \quad \frac{1}{8} = 12.5\%, \quad \frac{1}{9} = 11.11\%, \quad \frac{1}{11} = 9.09\%, \quad \frac{1}{12} = 8.33\%$$
2. **Successive Percentage Change**:
   If a value changes by $+a\%$ and then by $+b\%$, the net percentage change is:
   $$\text{Net Change } (\%) = a + b + \frac{a \times b}{100}$$
   *(Note: Use negative signs for percentage decrements or discounts).*
3. **Product Constancy Concept**:
   If $\text{Expenditure} = \text{Price} \times \text{Consumption}$:
   If price increases by $\frac{x}{y}$ (or $\frac{x}{y} \times 100\%$), consumption must decrease by $\frac{x}{x + y}$ to keep total expenditure constant.
4. **Population Growth & Depreciation**:
   $$P_n = P_0 \left(1 \pm \frac{r}{100}\right)^n$$

#### Worked Example: Successive Discount & Margin
* **Problem**: An enterprise cloud contract is initially quoted at a baseline rate. Due to volume tiers, a client receives successive discounts of $20\%$ and $15\%$. In the final quarter, a compliance surcharge of $10\%$ is added to the discounted price. What is the overall percentage change from the initial quote?
* **Solution**:
  - Successive discount: $a = -20, b = -15$:
    $$\text{Net Discount} = -20 - 15 + \frac{(-20)(-15)}{100} = -35 + 3.0 = -32\%$$
    *(Price multiplier $= 1 - 0.32 = 0.68$)*.
  - Surcharge: Now apply $+10\%$ to $0.68$:
    $$\text{Final Multiplier} = 0.68 \times (1 + 0.10) = 0.68 \times 1.10 = 0.748$$
  - Net Change $= (0.748 - 1.0) \times 100\% = \mathbf{-25.2\%}$ (overall discount of $25.2\%$).

* **Common Pitfall**: Adding percentages directly ($20 + 15 - 10 = 25\%$). Percentages do not sum algebraically when applied to a changing base.

### 1.2 Ratios, Proportions & Partnership Sharing

#### Core Principles & Formulas
1. **Compounded Ratio**: The compounded ratio of $(a : b)$ and $(c : d)$ is $(ac : bd)$.
2. **Mean Proportion & Third Proportion**:
   - Mean proportion between $a$ and $b$: $x = \sqrt{ab}$.
   - Third proportion to $a$ and $b$: $x = \frac{b^2}{a}$.
   - Fourth proportion to $a, b, c$: $x = \frac{bc}{a}$.
3. **Commercial Partnership Profit Sharing**:
   Profit is distributed strictly in proportion to the product of capital invested and the duration of investment:
   $$\text{Profit}_A : \text{Profit}_B : \text{Profit}_C = (C_A \times T_A) : (C_B \times T_B) : (C_C \times T_C)$$

#### Worked Example: Partnership Capital Rebalancing
* **Problem**: Consultant A invests ₹$60,000$ in an analytics venture. After 4 months, Consultant B joins with ₹$90,000$. At the end of 8 months from the start, Consultant A withdraws ₹$20,000$. If the annual net profit is ₹$78,000$, find Consultant B's share.
* **Solution**:
  - Total duration $= 12\text{ months}$.
  - Consultant A's investment:
    $$C_A \times T_A = (60,000 \times 8) + (40,000 \times 4) = 480,000 + 160,000 = 640,000\text{ unit-months}$$
  - Consultant B's investment: B was present for $12 - 4 = 8\text{ months}$:
    $$C_B \times T_B = 90,000 \times 8 = 720,000\text{ unit-months}$$
  - Ratio of profit:
    $$\frac{P_A}{P_B} = \frac{640,000}{720,000} = \frac{64}{72} = \frac{8}{9}$$
  - Sum of ratio parts $= 8 + 9 = 17\text{ parts}$.
  - Consultant B's share:
    $$P_B = \frac{9}{17} \times 78,000 = 9 \times 4,588.235 = \mathbf{₹41,294.12}$$
  - Consultant A's share:
    $$P_A = \frac{8}{17} \times 78,000 = 8 \times 4,588.235 = \mathbf{₹36,705.88}$$
  - **Verification**: $₹41,294.12 + ₹36,705.88 = ₹78,000.00$. (Calculation strictly follows the $8 : 9$ product-time ratio; alternate hypothetical scenarios have been removed for mathematical consistency).

### 1.3 Averages, Weighted Means & The Alligation Method

#### Core Principles & Formulas
1. **Weighted Average**:
   $$\bar{X}_w = \frac{n_1 \bar{x}_1 + n_2 \bar{x}_2 + \dots + n_k \bar{x}_k}{n_1 + n_2 + \dots + n_k}$$
2. **The Alligation Cross (Rule of Mixtures)**:
   When two ingredients of unit costs/values $C_1$ (cheaper) and $C_2$ (dearer) are mixed to produce a mixture of mean value $M$:
   $$\frac{\text{Quantity of Cheaper } (Q_1)}{\text{Quantity of Dearer } (Q_2)} = \frac{C_2 - M}{M - C_1}$$

```
    Cheaper Value (C1)           Dearer Value (C2)
                  \             /
                   \           /
                 Mean Value (M)
                   /           \
                  /             \
           (C2 - M)      :      (M - C1)
       = Quantity (Q1)   :   Quantity (Q2)
```

3. **Repeated Dilution / Replacement Formula**:
   If a container initially holds $V$ units of pure liquid, and $x$ units are drawn off and replaced with water, repeated $n$ times:
   $$\text{Quantity of Pure Liquid Remaining} = V \left(1 - \frac{x}{V}\right)^n$$

#### Worked Example: Multi-Grade Solution Blending
* **Problem**: An industrial lab has a $40\%$ glycol solution and a $70\%$ glycol solution. How many liters of the $70\%$ solution must be mixed with $60\text{ liters}$ of the $40\%$ solution to produce a $52\%$ glycol mixture?
* **Solution**:
  - $C_1 = 40\%, \quad C_2 = 70\%, \quad M = 52\%$.
  - $\frac{Q_{40}}{Q_{70}} = \frac{70 - 52}{52 - 40} = \frac{18}{12} = \frac{3}{2}$.
  - We know $Q_{40} = 60\text{ L}$.
  - Therefore, $\frac{60}{Q_{70}} = \frac{3}{2} \implies Q_{70} = \frac{60 \times 2}{3} = \mathbf{40\text{ liters}}$.

### 1.4 Profit, Loss, Markups, Discounts & Commercial Math

#### Core Principles & Formulas
1. **Profit & Loss**:
   $$\text{Profit } \% = \frac{\text{SP} - \text{CP}}{\text{CP}} \times 100, \qquad \text{Loss } \% = \frac{\text{CP} - \text{SP}}{\text{CP}} \times 100$$
2. **Markup & Marked Price (MP)**:
   $$\text{MP} = \text{CP} \left(1 + \frac{\text{Markup } \%}{100}\right)$$
3. **Discount**:
   $$\text{SP} = \text{MP} \left(1 - \frac{\text{Discount } \%}{100}\right)$$
4. **Direct Relation between CP, MP, Profit, and Discount**:
   $$\frac{\text{MP}}{\text{CP}} = \frac{100 + \text{Profit } \%}{100 - \text{Discount } \%}$$
5. **Dishonest Merchant (False Weights)**:
   $$\text{Gain } \% = \left(\frac{\text{Error}}{\text{True Value} - \text{Error}}\right) \times 100 = \left(\frac{\text{Claimed Weight} - \text{Actual Weight}}{\text{Actual Weight}}\right) \times 100$$

#### Worked Example: Markup and Discount Combination
* **Problem**: A hardware retailer marks an analytics server $40\%$ above its cost price and allows a discount of $25\%$ on cash payment. What is the net profit or loss percentage?
* **Solution**:
  - Let $\text{CP} = 100$.
  - $\text{MP} = 100 \times 1.40 = 140$.
  - $\text{SP} = 140 \times (1 - 0.25) = 140 \times 0.75 = 105$.
  - $\text{Net Profit} = 105 - 100 = +5\%$.
  - Or using formula: $\frac{140}{100} = \frac{100 + P}{100 - 25} \implies 1.40 \times 75 = 100 + P \implies 105 = 100 + P \implies P = \mathbf{5\%}$.

### 1.5 Simple and Compound Interest (Annual, Semi-Annual, Continuous)

#### Core Principles & Formulas
1. **Simple Interest (SI)**:
   $$\text{SI} = \frac{P \times R \times T}{100}, \qquad A = P + \text{SI} = P\left(1 + \frac{RT}{100}\right)$$
2. **Compound Interest (CI)**:
   $$A = P \left(1 + \frac{R}{100 \times k}\right)^{k \times T}$$
   *(where $k = 1$ for annual, $k = 2$ for semi-annual, $k = 4$ for quarterly compounding).*
   $$\text{CI} = A - P = P \left[\left(1 + \frac{R}{100}\right)^T - 1\right]$$
3. **Difference between CI and SI**:
   - For $T = 2\text{ years}$:
     $$D_2 = \text{CI}_2 - \text{SI}_2 = P \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2$$
   - For $T = 3\text{ years}$:
     $$D_3 = P \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2 \left(3 + \frac{R}{100}\right) = D_2 \times \left(3 + \frac{R}{100}\right)$$
4. **Rule of 72**: Approximate years to double money at $R\%$ compound interest $\approx \frac{72}{R}$.

#### Worked Example: 2-Year Difference Calculation
* **Problem**: The difference between the compound interest (compounded annually) and simple interest on a certain principal at $8\%$ per annum for 2 years is ₹$640$. What is the principal?
* **Solution**:
  - $D_2 = P \left(\frac{R}{100}\right)^2$.
  - $640 = P \left(\frac{8}{100}\right)^2 = P \left(\frac{64}{10,000}\right)$.
  - $P = \frac{640 \times 10,000}{64} = 10 \times 10,000 = \mathbf{₹1,00,000}$.

### 1.6 Time & Work, Man-Day Equivalence, Pipes & Cisterns

#### Core Principles & Formulas
1. **Unitary Work Rate Concept**:
   If person A finishes a task in $D$ days, their 1-day work rate is $\frac{1}{D}$.
2. **Combined Work**:
   If A takes $D_A$ days and B takes $D_B$ days:
   $$\text{Combined Daily Rate} = \frac{1}{D_A} + \frac{1}{D_B} \implies \text{Time Taken } T = \frac{D_A D_B}{D_A + D_B}$$
3. **Efficiency Ratios**:
   $$\frac{\text{Efficiency}_A}{\text{Efficiency}_B} = \frac{\text{Time}_B}{\text{Time}_A}$$
4. **Man-Days Equation (Chain Rule)**:
   $$\frac{M_1 \times D_1 \times H_1}{W_1} = \frac{M_2 \times D_2 \times H_2}{W_2}$$
   *(where $M = \text{workers}, D = \text{days}, H = \text{hours/day}, W = \text{work units completed})$.*
5. **Pipes and Cisterns (Inlets & Outlets)**:
   Inlets have positive work rate; leakages/outlets have negative work rate:
   $$\text{Net Rate} = \sum \frac{1}{T_{\text{inlet}}} - \sum \frac{1}{T_{\text{outlet}}}$$

#### Worked Example: Alternating Work with Leakage
* **Problem**: Pipe A can fill an oil storage tank in $12\text{ hours}$, Pipe B can fill it in $16\text{ hours}$, and Drain Pipe C can empty it in $24\text{ hours}$. If Pipe A is opened at 08:00 AM, Pipe B is opened at 09:00 AM, and Drain C is opened at 10:00 AM, at what time will the tank be full?
* **Solution**:
  - Let total tank capacity be $\text{LCM}(12, 16, 24) = 48\text{ units}$.
  - Efficiency of A $= +4\text{ units/hr}$.
  - Efficiency of B $= +3\text{ units/hr}$.
  - Efficiency of C $= -2\text{ units/hr}$.
  - Step-by-step filling:
    - 08:00 to 09:00 (1 hr, only A): $1 \times 4 = 4\text{ units filled}$.
    - 09:00 to 10:00 (1 hr, A + B): $1 \times (4 + 3) = 7\text{ units filled}$.
    - Total filled by 10:00 AM $= 4 + 7 = 11\text{ units}$. Remaining $= 48 - 11 = 37\text{ units}$.
    - From 10:00 AM onward (A + B + C): net rate $= 4 + 3 - 2 = 5\text{ units/hr}$.
    - Time required $= \frac{37}{5} = 7.4\text{ hours} = 7\text{ hours } 24\text{ minutes}$.
  - Final time $= 10:00\text{ AM} + 7\text{h } 24\text{m} = \mathbf{05:24\text{ PM}}$.

### 1.7 Time, Speed, Distance, Relative Velocity & Circular Motion

#### Core Principles & Formulas
1. **Fundamental Units**:
   $$1\text{ km/h} = \frac{5}{18}\text{ m/s}, \qquad 1\text{ m/s} = \frac{18}{5}\text{ km/h} = 3.6\text{ km/h}$$
2. **Average Speed**:
   $$\text{Average Speed} = \frac{\text{Total Distance}}{\text{Total Time}}$$
   *(If equal distances are covered at speeds $v_1$ and $v_2$, $\text{Avg Speed} = \frac{2 v_1 v_2}{v_1 + v_2}$).*
3. **Relative Velocity**:
   - Moving in opposite directions: $V_{\text{rel}} = v_1 + v_2$.
   - Moving in same direction: $V_{\text{rel}} = |v_1 - v_2|$.
4. **Trains Passing Objects**:
   - Train of length $L_T$ passing stationary point (pole/person): Distance $= L_T$.
   - Train of length $L_T$ passing bridge/platform of length $L_P$: Distance $= L_T + L_P$.
   - Two trains of lengths $L_1, L_2$: Distance $= L_1 + L_2$; Speed $= v_1 \pm v_2$.
5. **Boats and Streams**:
   - Let boat speed in still water $= u$, stream speed $= v$.
   - Downstream speed $v_d = u + v$; Upstream speed $v_u = u - v$.
   - $u = \frac{v_d + v_u}{2}, \quad v = \frac{v_d - v_u}{2}$.

#### Worked Example: Boats and Stream Round Trip
* **Problem**: A data collection drone boat travels $36\text{ km}$ downstream and returns to the starting point in a total time of $5\text{ hours}$. If the speed of the river current is $3\text{ km/h}$, find the boat's speed in still water.
* **Solution**:
  - $\frac{36}{u + 3} + \frac{36}{u - 3} = 5$.
  - $36 \left(\frac{u - 3 + u + 3}{(u+3)(u-3)}\right) = 5 \implies 36 \left(\frac{2u}{u^2 - 9}\right) = 5$.
  - $72u = 5u^2 - 45 \implies 5u^2 - 72u - 45 = 0$.
  - Factoring: $5 \times (-45) = -225$. Factors summing to $-72$ are $-75$ and $+3$:
    $$5u^2 - 75u + 3u - 45 = 0 \implies 5u(u - 15) + 3(u - 15) = 0 \implies (5u + 3)(u - 15) = 0$$
  - Since speed must be positive: $u = \mathbf{15\text{ km/h}}$.

### 1.8 Number Systems, Divisibility Rules & Remainder Theorems

#### Core Principles & Formulas
1. **Divisibility Tests**:
   - **2**: Last digit is even.
   - **3**: Sum of digits is divisible by 3.
   - **4**: Last two digits form a number divisible by 4.
   - **8**: Last three digits form a number divisible by 8.
   - **9**: Sum of digits is divisible by 9.
   - **11**: Difference between sum of odd-positioned digits and even-positioned digits is 0 or multiple of 11.
2. **Remainder Arithmetic**:
   $$(a + b) \pmod m = (a \pmod m + b \pmod m) \pmod m$$
   $$(a \times b) \pmod m = ((a \pmod m) \times (b \pmod m)) \pmod m$$
3. **Finding Unit Digits (Cyclicity of Powers)**:
   - Cyclicity of 2: $[2, 4, 8, 6]$ (period 4).
   - Cyclicity of 3: $[3, 9, 7, 1]$ (period 4).
   - Cyclicity of 7: $[7, 9, 3, 1]$ (period 4).
   - Cyclicity of 8: $[8, 4, 2, 6]$ (period 4).
   - Cyclicity of 4: $[4, 6]$ (period 2); Cyclicity of 9: $[9, 1]$ (period 2).
   - Cyclicity of 0, 1, 5, 6 is always 1.
4. **HCF and LCM**:
   $$A \times B = \text{HCF}(A, B) \times \text{LCM}(A, B)$$
   $$\text{HCF of Fractions} = \frac{\text{HCF of Numerators}}{\text{LCM of Denominators}}, \qquad \text{LCM of Fractions} = \frac{\text{LCM of Numerators}}{\text{HCF of Denominators}}$$

#### Worked Example: Finding Unit Digit
* **Problem**: What is the unit digit of $(7^{95} - 3^{58})$?
* **Solution**:
  - For $7^{95}$: Divide exponent 95 by 4: $95 = 23 \times 4 + 3$. Remainder $= 3$.
    Unit digit of $7^3 = 343 \implies 3$.
  - For $3^{58}$: Divide exponent 58 by 4: $58 = 14 \times 4 + 2$. Remainder $= 2$.
    Unit digit of $3^2 = 9$.
  - Expression: $3 - 9$. Since the first term is much larger than the second, borrow 10:
    $$\text{Unit Digit} = 13 - 9 = \mathbf{4}$$.

### 1.9 Linear & Quadratic Equations, Inequations & Progression (AP/GP)

#### Core Principles & Formulas
1. **Quadratic Equations**: $a x^2 + b x + c = 0$
   $$x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$$
   - Roots $\alpha, \beta$: $\alpha + \beta = -\frac{b}{a}$, $\alpha \beta = \frac{c}{a}$.
   - Discriminant $\Delta = b^2 - 4ac$: Real & distinct ($\Delta > 0$), real & equal ($\Delta = 0$), complex ($\Delta < 0$).
2. **Arithmetic Progression (AP)**:
   $$T_n = a + (n - 1)d, \qquad S_n = \frac{n}{2}[2a + (n - 1)d] = \frac{n}{2}(a + l)$$
3. **Geometric Progression (GP)**:
   $$T_n = a r^{n-1}, \qquad S_n = \frac{a(r^n - 1)}{r - 1} \quad (r > 1), \qquad S_\infty = \frac{a}{1 - r} \quad (|r| < 1)$$

### 1.10 Permutations, Combinations & Probability Distributions

#### Core Principles & Formulas
1. **Permutations ($^n P_r$) & Combinations ($^n C_r$)**:
   $$^n P_r = \frac{n!}{(n - r)!}, \qquad ^n C_r = \frac{n!}{r! (n - r)!}, \qquad ^n C_r = ^n C_{n-r}$$
2. **Arranging Identical Items**:
   Number of distinct ways to arrange $N$ items where $p$ are of type 1, $q$ of type 2:
   $$\frac{N!}{p! \, q! \, r!}$$
3. **Circular Permutation**:
   - Distinct items around a round table: $(n - 1)!$
   - Beads on a necklace (flip symmetry): $\frac{(n - 1)!}{2}$
4. **Probability**:
   $$P(A) = \frac{\text{Favorable Outcomes}}{\text{Total Sample Space}}, \qquad P(A \cup B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \cap B)$$
   - Conditional Probability: $P(A | B) = \frac{P(A \cap B)}{P(B)}$
   - Independent Events: $P(A \cap B) = P(A) \times P(B)$.

#### Worked Example: Constrained Committee Selection
* **Problem**: A consulting team of 5 members is to be formed from 6 Data Analysts and 4 Business Consultants. What is the probability that the team contains at least 3 Data Analysts?
* **Solution**:
  - Total ways to select 5 from 10: $^10 C_5 = \frac{10 \times 9 \times 8 \times 7 \times 6}{5 \times 4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1} = 252$.
  - Favorable cases (at least 3 Analysts):
    - Case 1: 3 Analysts and 2 Business Consultants:
      $$^6 C_3 \times ^4 C_2 = 20 \times 6 = 120$$
    - Case 2: 4 Analysts and 1 Business Consultant:
      $$^6 C_4 \times ^4 C_1 = 15 \times 4 = 60$$
    - Case 3: 5 Analysts and 0 Business Consultants:
      $$^6 C_5 \times ^4 C_0 = 6 \times 1 = 6$$
  - Total Favorable $= 120 + 60 + 6 = 186$.
  - Probability $= \frac{186}{252} = \frac{31}{42} \approx \mathbf{0.7381}$ ($73.81\%$).

### 1.11 Data Interpretation (DI) — Tables, Bar Graphs, Pie Charts & Line Trends

### 2.1 Number, Letter & Alphanumeric Series

#### Pattern Recognition Taxonomy
1. **Arithmetic Difference Patterns**:
   - Single difference: $+3, +5, +7, +9 \dots$ (consecutive odd numbers).
   - Double difference: Calculate difference between successive differences.
2. **Geometric & Exponential Progressions**:
   - $\times 2 + 1, \times 3 - 2, \times 4 + 3 \dots$
   - Squares and cubes with offsets: $n^2 \pm 1, n^3 \pm n, n^3 \pm 1$.
3. **Alternating & Interleaved Series**:
   - Two independent series interwoven into alternating positions (Indices $1, 3, 5 \dots$ and $2, 4, 6 \dots$).
4. **Letter Position Reference Table**:
   $$A=1, B=2, C=3, D=4, E=5, F=6, G=7, H=8, I=9, J=10, K=11, L=12, M=13$$
   $$N=14, O=15, P=16, Q=17, R=18, S=19, T=20, U=21, V=22, W=23, X=24, Y=25, Z=26$$
   - **Reverse Position Rule**: $\text{Forward Rank} + \text{Reverse Rank} = 27$. (e.g., $A(1) \leftrightarrow Z(26)$, $B(2) \leftrightarrow Y(25)$).
   - **Mnemonic**: `EJOTY` $(5, 10, 15, 20, 25)$.

#### Worked Example: Double Difference Series
* **Problem**: Identify the missing number in the series: $4, 11, 25, 48, 82, ?$
* **Solution**:
  - 1st Differences:
    $$11 - 4 = 7$$
    $$25 - 11 = 14$$
    $$48 - 25 = 23$$
    $$82 - 48 = 34$$
  - 2nd Differences:
    $$14 - 7 = 7$$
    $$23 - 14 = 9$$
    $$34 - 23 = 11$$
  - The second difference increases by $+2$: next 2nd difference $= 11 + 2 = 13$.
  - Next 1st difference $= 34 + 13 = 47$.
  - Next term $= 82 + 47 = \mathbf{129}$.

### 2.2 Direction Sense, Compass Navigation & Pythagoras Distances

#### Core Coordinate Rules
```
                    NORTH (0°)
                        |
       NORTH-WEST       |       NORTH-EAST
          (315°)        |         (45°)
                        |
WEST (270°) ------------+------------ EAST (90°)
                        |
       SOUTH-WEST       |       SOUTH-EAST
          (225°)        |        (135°)
                        |
                    SOUTH (180°)
```
1. **Turn Mechanics**:
   - Turning "Right" from facing North $\implies$ East (clockwise $90^\circ$).
   - Turning "Left" from facing South $\implies$ East (counter-clockwise $90^\circ$).
2. **Shortest Distance (Pythagoras Theorem)**:
   $$D = \sqrt{(\Delta x)^2 + (\Delta y)^2}$$
3. **Shadow Rules**:
   - **Morning (Sunrise in East)**: All shadows fall toward the **West**.
   - **Evening (Sunset in West)**: All shadows fall toward the **East**.
   - **Noon (12:00 PM)**: The sun is overhead; no shadow (or minimum shadow along North-South meridian).

#### Worked Example: Direction with Angle Turns
* **Problem**: An IT field engineer walks $12\text{ m}$ North, turns $90^\circ$ right and walks $8\text{ m}$, then turns $135^\circ$ clockwise and walks $10\sqrt{2}\text{ m}$. In which direction and at what distance is the engineer relative to the starting point?
* **Solution**:
  - Vector breakdown:
    - Segment 1: $(0, +12)$.
    - Segment 2: Turn right $\implies$ facing East. Walk $8\text{ m} \implies (+8, +12)$.
    - Segment 3: Facing East. Turn $135^\circ$ clockwise: East is $90^\circ$, so $90^\circ + 135^\circ = 225^\circ$ (facing South-West).
    - Walking $10\sqrt{2}\text{ m}$ in South-West direction:
      $$\Delta x = -10\sqrt{2} \cos 45^\circ = -10\sqrt{2} \times \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = -10$$
      $$\Delta y = -10\sqrt{2} \sin 45^\circ = -10\sqrt{2} \times \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} = -10$$
  - Final Coordinates:
    $$x_{\text{final}} = 8 - 10 = -2\text{ m} \quad (2\text{ m West})$$
    $$y_{\text{final}} = 12 - 10 = +2\text{ m} \quad (2\text{ m North})$$
  - Shortest Distance $= \sqrt{(-2)^2 + 2^2} = \sqrt{4 + 4} = \sqrt{8} = \mathbf{2\sqrt{2}\text{ m}}$ in the **North-West** direction.

### 2.3 Blood Relations & Coded Family Lineage

#### Notational Shorthand for Fast Tree Construction
* Male $= [+]$ or $\Box$; Female $= [-]$ or $\bigcirc$.
* Marriage / Couple $= [A = B]$ (horizontal double bond).
* Siblings $= [A - B]$ (horizontal single bond).
* Generational Descent $= [A \downarrow B]$ (vertical downward branch).

#### Standard Relational Nomenclature
* Father's/Mother's Brother = Maternal/Paternal Uncle.
* Father's/Mother's Sister = Maternal/Paternal Aunt.
* Son/Daughter of Uncle/Aunt = Cousin.
* Brother's/Sister's Son = Nephew; Brother's/Sister's Daughter = Niece.

#### Worked Example: Coded Relations
* **Problem**: Given expressions:
  - $P + Q$ means $P$ is the mother of $Q$.
  - $P - Q$ means $P$ is the brother of $Q$.
  - $P \times Q$ means $P$ is the father of $Q$.
  - $P \div Q$ means $P$ is the sister of $Q$.
  Which of the following shows that **A is the paternal aunt of B**?
  - (1) $A \div C \times D + B$
  - (2) $A \div C \times B$
  - (3) $A + C \times B$
  - (4) $A - C \div B$
* **Solution**:
  - Paternal aunt means: A is a female, and A is the sister of B's father.
  - Test Option (2): $A \div C \times B$:
    - $A \div C \implies A$ is the sister of $C$ (A is female).
    - $C \times B \implies C$ is the father of $B$.
    - Since $C$ is the father of $B$, and $A$ is the sister of $C$, $A$ is the sister of $B$'s father $\implies$ **paternal aunt**.
  - **Correct Answer**: **(2)**.

### 2.4 Seating Arrangements (Linear Single/Double Row, Circular & Rectangular)

#### Systematic Method
1. **Anchor Clues First**: Look for clues that give definitive absolute positions (e.g., *"P sits at the extreme left end"* or *"K sits exactly in the middle"*).
2. **Directional Conventions**:
   - In a row facing **North**: Left is to your left, Right is to your right.
   - In a row facing **South**: Left is to your right, Right is to your left.
   - Circular table facing **Center**: Clockwise is to the Left, Counter-Clockwise is to the Right.
   - Circular table facing **Outside**: Clockwise is to the Right, Counter-Clockwise is to the Left.
3. **Branching (Case Generation)**: When an anchor clue offers two possible states, draw Case 1 and Case 2 in parallel. Eliminate the invalid case as contradictory clues emerge.

#### Worked Example: 6-Person Circular Table
* **Problem**: Six analysts (A, B, C, D, E, F) sit around a circular table facing the center:
  1. A sits second to the left of C.
  2. B sits to the immediate right of D.
  3. E sits opposite to C.
  4. F is not adjacent to C.
  Who sits between A and E?
* **Solution**:
  - Number 6 circular positions $1$ to $6$ clockwise.
  - Place C at Position 1.
  - Facing center $\implies$ clockwise is left. A sits second to left of C $\implies$ Position 3.
  - E sits opposite C $\implies$ Position 4 (since $1+3=4$ in a 6-circle).
  - So far: Pos 1: C, Pos 3: A, Pos 4: E.
  - F is not adjacent to C $\implies$ F cannot be at Pos 2 or Pos 6. The only remaining position for F is Pos 5!
  - Now positions 2 and 6 remain for B and D.
  - Clue 2: B sits to the immediate right of D.
    - If D is at Pos 2, facing center $\implies$ counter-clockwise is right $\implies$ right of D is Pos 1 (already occupied by C).
    - If D is at Pos 6, right of D is Pos 5? No, counter-clockwise from 6 is 1. Wait: in circular facing center:
      - For person at Pos 6, facing center: their Right is toward Pos 1, their Left is toward Pos 5.
      - Let's verify: Stand at Pos 6 looking at center: your right hand points to Pos 1.
      - If D is at Pos 2 looking at center: your right hand points to Pos 1.
      - Let's check relative positions: B is immediate right of D means D is immediate left of B.
      - Pos 2 and Pos 6 are left. Adjacent to Pos 6 are Pos 1 and Pos 5.
      - Looking at the circle: Pos 1(C), Pos 2(?), Pos 3(A), Pos 4(E), Pos 5(F), Pos 6(?).
      - Position 5 is F, Position 4 is E, Position 3 is A.
      - Who is between A (Pos 3) and E (Pos 4)? Nobody!
      - Between A (Pos 3) and E (Pos 4) along the other path is Pos 2, 1, 6, 5. But directly between A and E adjacent: Pos 3 and Pos 4 are adjacent!
      - Let's check Pos 5 (F): between E (Pos 4) and D/B. F is adjacent to E (Pos 4).

### 2.5 Categorical Syllogisms & Deductive Logic (Venn & Rules)

#### The Four Classical Categorical Statements
1. **A-Type (Universal Affirmative)**: *"All A are B"* (Subject distributed, Predicate undistributed).
2. **E-Type (Universal Negative)**: *"No A is B"* (Both Subject and Predicate distributed).
3. **I-Type (Particular Affirmative)**: *"Some A are B"* (Neither distributed).
4. **O-Type (Particular Negative)**: *"Some A are not B"* (Predicate distributed).

#### Rules for Valid Deductions
* From two negative premises (No + No, No + Some Not), **no valid conclusion follows**.
* From two particular premises (Some + Some), **no valid conclusion follows**.
* If one premise is negative, the conclusion **must be negative**.
* If one premise is particular, the conclusion **must be particular**.
* **Either/Or Condition (Complementary Pairs)**:
  Two conclusions form an "Either/Or" choice if:
  1. Both conclusions share the same Subject and Predicate.
  2. Both conclusions are individually uncertain/unproven.
  3. They form a complementary pair: **(Some + No)** OR **(All + Some Not)**.

### 2.8 Critical Reasoning: Assumptions, Inferences, Cause-Effect & Data Sufficiency

#### Conceptual Definitions
* **Implicit Assumption**: An unstated premise required for the author's argument to logically hold. An assumption must be strictly necessary; if negated, the argument collapses.
* **Inference**: A factual statement that is $100\%$ logically derived from the text without requiring outside assertions.
* **Data Sufficiency Framework**:
  - **(A)** Statement (1) ALONE is sufficient, but Statement (2) alone is not sufficient.
  - **(B)** Statement (2) ALONE is sufficient, but Statement (1) alone is not sufficient.
  - **(C)** BOTH statements TOGETHER are sufficient, but NEITHER alone is sufficient.
  - **(D)** EACH statement ALONE is sufficient.
  - **(E)** Statements (1) and (2) TOGETHER are NOT sufficient.

# 3. English Language and Verbal Ability

### 3.1 Sentence Correction & Advanced Grammatical Rules

#### High-Yield Rules
1. **Subject-Verb Agreement with Prepositional Phrases**:
   The verb agrees with the true subject, never with the intervening prepositional noun:
   - *Incorrect*: *"The degradation of coastal dyke embankments and breakwaters were alarming."*
   - *Correct*: *"The degradation [singular] of coastal dyke embankments and breakwaters **was** [singular] alarming."*
2. **Neither / Either & Indefinite Pronouns**:
   *Each, everyone, everybody, neither, either, anyone* are strictly singular:
   - *Correct*: *"Neither of the proposed predictive models **demonstrates** superior convergence."*
3. **Correlative Conjunctions (Either...or, Neither...nor, Not only...but also)**:
   The verb agrees with the **closer subject**:
   - *Correct*: *"Neither the project director nor the lead data engineers **were** available."*
   - *Correct*: *"Neither the lead data engineers nor the project director **was** available."*

---

### 3.2 Subject-Verb Agreement, Modifiers & Parallelism

1. **Dangling and Misplaced Modifiers**:
   An introductory modifying phrase must immediately precede the actual noun it modifies:
   - *Incorrect*: *"Running the regression pipeline overnight, the results surprised the consulting team."* (Did the results run the pipeline?)
   - *Correct*: *"Running the regression pipeline overnight, the consulting team **was** surprised by the results."*
2. **Parallelism in Lists and Comparisons**:
   Items in a series or comparison must share identical grammatical form:
   - *Incorrect*: *"The consultant is responsible for data cleaning, running SQL scripts, and to present the executive summary."*
   - *Correct*: *"The consultant is responsible for data cleaning, running SQL scripts, and **presenting** the executive summary."*

---

### 3.3 Tenses, Conditionals & Subjunctive Mood

1. **Past Perfect vs. Simple Past**:
   When two events occurred in the past, the earlier event takes the **past perfect** ($had + V_3$), while the later event takes the **simple past**:
   - *Correct*: *"The team **had validated** the data before the client **called** the meeting."*
2. **Conditional Clauses**:
   - **First Conditional (Real Present/Future)**: If + Present Simple, will + base verb.
     *(If the query runs, the dashboard will refresh).*
   - **Second Conditional (Hypothetical Present)**: If + Past Simple, would + base verb.
     *(If we had more cloud storage, we would process raw video frames).*
   - **Third Conditional (Unreal Past)**: If + Past Perfect, would have + past participle.
     *(If the team had applied cross-validation, they would have caught the overfitting).*
3. **Subjunctive Mood**: Expresses wishes or hypothetical conditions using *"were"*:
   - *Correct*: *"If the dataset **were** clean, analysis would take two hours."* (Never *"If the dataset was clean"* in formal English).

---

### 3.4 Contextual Vocabulary, Confusing Words & Collocations

* **Affect (Verb)** vs. **Effect (Noun / Verb)**:
  - *"Feature scaling affects gradient descent speed."* (Affect = influence).
  - *"The model had a measurable positive effect on supply chain efficiency."* (Effect = outcome).
* **Discreet (Cautious/Confidential)** vs. **Discrete (Separate/Distinct)**:
  - *"Consultants must maintain discreet handling of client proprietary secrets."*
  - *"A Poisson distribution models discrete events occurring in fixed intervals."*
* **Complement (Enhances/Completes)** vs. **Compliment (Praise)**:
  - *"The LLM summarization tool complements the relational reporting engine."*
* **Adverse (Unfavorable/Harmful)** vs. **Averse (Opposed/Disinclined)**:
  - *"Adverse market volatility prompted defensive hedging."*
  - *"The client is risk-averse regarding cloud migration."*

### 3.5 Reading Comprehension (RC) — Strategy, Question Types & Inference Extraction

#### Strategic Reading Architecture for Timed Assessments
1. **Passage Mapping**: Spend 45–60 seconds reading the first sentence of each paragraph and the concluding sentence. Note the structural flow:
   - Paragraph 1: Problem statement / Background.
   - Paragraph 2: Technical/Commercial challenge or counter-evidence.
   - Paragraph 3: Proposed solution or modern framework.
2. **Question Types**:
   - **Direct Factual**: Answer is explicitly in the text. Locate key nouns in question and scan for the matching sentence.
   - **Inference**: Must be true based on the passage, but stated with rephrased vocabulary. Beware of options that use extreme words (*always, never, completely, solely*) unless the passage explicitly used them.
   - **Author's Tone**: Objective, critical, skeptical, analytical, advocating, neutral.
   - **Main Idea / Primary Purpose**: Must encompass the whole passage, not just one supporting body paragraph.

---

### 3.6 Para Jumbles, Sentence Ordering & Coherence

#### Standard Solving Protocol
1. **Locate the Independent Opening Sentence**:
   - The opening sentence introduces a concept or noun for the first time.
   - Sentences starting with pronouns (*he, she, they, this, these*) or contrasting conjunctions (*however, furthermore, therefore, on the other hand*) cannot open the paragraph.
2. **Identify Mandatory Pairs**:
   - **Full Name followed by Acronym/Pronoun**: *"Deloitte US-India Offices (USI)..."* must precede *"The firm..."* or *"USI..."*.
   - **Cause and Effect**: A sentence stating a trigger must precede the sentence detailing the consequence.
   - **Chronology**: Historical dates / stages must flow sequentially.

---

### 3.7 Error Spotting & Sentence Improvement

#### Systematic 5-Step Error Spotting Algorithm
1. **Find Subject and Primary Verb**: Check number and person across intervening phrases (*"The portfolio of multi-cloud transformation accounts **was** (not were) approved"*).
2. **Verify Pronoun Case and Antecedents**: Confirm objective case after prepositions (*"between you and **me**"*, not *"between you and I"*) and eliminate ambiguous pronoun references.
3. **Inspect Modifiers for Misplacement**: Ensure introductory participial phrases immediately precede the intended agent (*"Analyzing the telemetry data, the engineer detected anomalies"*, not *"Analyzing the telemetry data, anomalies were detected"*).
4. **Examine Parallelism**: Verify that conjunctions (*either... or*, *neither... nor*, *not only... but also*) connect structurally identical grammatical forms.
5. **Check Idiomatic Prepositions and Diction**: Verify standard collocations tested in corporate assessments (*conform **to***, *comply **with***, *refrain **from***, *indifferent **to***).

---

### 3.8 Professional & Business Communication Standards

#### Key Principles of Consulting Communication
1. **The Minto Pyramid Principle**: State the core conclusion or recommendation first, followed by logically grouped supporting arguments, and finally the granular data.
2. **Active vs. Passive Voice**: Use active voice for clarity and ownership (*"The data engineering team automated the pipeline"* vs. *"The pipeline was automated by the team"*), reserving passive voice only when the actor is unknown or when softening client defect reports.
3. **Clarity, Brevity & Precision**: Eliminate corporate jargon, tautologies (*"revert back" $\to$ "reply"*, *"past history" $\to$ "history"*), and vague quantifiers in business communication.

#### Question 1 (Percentages & Revenue)
A consultant at Deloitte analyzes a SaaS client's pricing. The price of a software license increased by 20%, resulting in a 10% decrease in the number of licenses sold. What was the net percentage change in total revenue?
- A) 10% increase
- B) 8% increase
- C) 8% decrease
- D) No change

**Answer: B) 8% increase**
**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. Let initial license price = $P_0$ and initial quantity sold = $Q_0$.
2. Initial revenue $R_0 = P_0 \times Q_0$.
3. New price $P_1 = 1.20 P_0$.
4. New quantity $Q_1 = 0.90 Q_0$.
5. New revenue $R_1 = (1.20 P_0) \times (0.90 Q_0) = 1.08 P_0 Q_0 = 1.08 R_0$.
6. Net percentage change = $\frac{1.08 R_0 - R_0}{R_0} \times 100\% = +8\%$.

#### Question 2 (Profit & Loss / Successive Discounts)
An IT hardware vendor supplies laptops to Deloitte with a marked price of Rs. 80,000. The vendor offers successive trade discounts of 15% and 10%. Furthermore, if payment is settled within 10 days, an additional cash discount of 5% on the net amount is applied. What is the final amount payable?
- A) Rs. 58,140
- B) Rs. 60,000
- C) Rs. 61,200
- D) Rs. 56,400

**Answer: A) Rs. 58,140**
**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. Price after 1st discount (15%): $80,000 \times (1 - 0.15) = 80,000 \times 0.85 = 68,000$.
2. Price after 2nd discount (10%): $68,000 \times (1 - 0.10) = 68,000 \times 0.90 = 61,200$.
3. Price after cash discount (5%): $61,200 \times (1 - 0.05) = 61,200 \times 0.95 = 58,140$.

#### Question 3 (Ratio & Proportion / Partnership)
Three consultants, Aman, Bina, and Charu, invest in a technical startup venture. Aman invests Rs. 50,000 for 12 months, Bina invests Rs. 70,000 for 8 months, and Charu invests Rs. 90,000 for 6 months. If the total annual profit generated is Rs. 85,000, what is Bina's share of the profit?
- A) Rs. 30,000
- B) Rs. 28,000
- C) Rs. 27,000
- D) Rs. 32,000

**Answer: B) Rs. 28,000**
**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. Ratio of profit shares = Ratio of (Capital $\times$ Time duration).
2. Aman's product: $50,000 \times 12 = 600,000$.
3. Bina's product: $70,000 \times 8 = 560,000$.
4. Charu's product: $90,000 \times 6 = 540,000$.
5. Ratio = $600,000 : 560,000 : 540,000 = 60 : 56 : 54 = 30 : 28 : 27$.
6. Sum of ratio terms = $30 + 28 + 27 = 85$.
7. Bina's share = $\frac{28}{85} \times 85,000 = \text{Rs. } 28,000$.

#### Question 4 (Time & Work / Efficiency)
Analyst A can complete an analytics sprint in 16 days, and Analyst B can complete the same sprint in 24 days. With the assistance of Senior Analyst C, they finish the sprint together in 6 days. How many days would Senior Analyst C alone take to complete the sprint?
- A) 12 days
- B) 16 days
- C) 18 days
- D) 14 days

**Answer: B) 16 days**
**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. Total work = LCM(16, 24, 6) = 48 units.
2. Efficiency of A = $\frac{48}{16} = 3$ units/day.
3. Efficiency of B = $\frac{48}{24} = 2$ units/day.
4. Combined efficiency of A + B + C = $\frac{48}{6} = 8$ units/day.
5. Efficiency of C alone = $8 - (3 + 2) = 3$ units/day.
6. Days required by C alone = $\frac{48}{3} = 16$ days.

#### Question 5 (Pipes & Cisterns / Negative Rate)
An ingestion pipeline has two intake pipes P1 and P2 that fill an analytics data warehouse staging table in 20 minutes and 30 minutes respectively. A cleanup process P3 purges records at a rate equivalent to emptying the table in 40 minutes. If all three operate concurrently, how long does it take to fully ingest the required data volume?
- A) 15 minutes
- B) 17.14 minutes (120/7 min)
- C) 18.5 minutes
- D) 21.33 minutes

**Answer: B) 17.14 minutes (120/7 min)**
**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. Let capacity of buffer = LCM(20, 30, 40) = 120 units.
2. Inflow rate of P1 = $\frac{120}{20} = +6$ units/min.
3. Inflow rate of P2 = $\frac{120}{30} = +4$ units/min.
4. Outflow rate of P3 = $\frac{120}{40} = -3$ units/min.
5. Net rate of filling = $6 + 4 - 3 = +7$ units/min.
6. Time required = $\frac{120}{7} \approx 17.14$ minutes.

#### Question 6 (Time, Speed & Distance / Relative Speed)
Two client data centers are located 360 km apart. Two field delivery engineers start driving towards each other from these data centers simultaneously. Engineer X travels at 40 km/h, and Engineer Y travels at 50 km/h. How many hours after departure will they meet, and at what distance from Engineer X's starting point?
- A) 4 hours, 160 km
- B) 4 hours, 200 km
- C) 4.5 hours, 180 km
- D) 3.5 hours, 140 km

**Answer: A) 4 hours, 160 km**
**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. Since they are moving in opposite directions towards each other, relative speed = $S_X + S_Y = 40 + 50 = 90$ km/h.
2. Time until meeting = $\frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Relative Speed}} = \frac{360}{90} = 4$ hours.
3. Distance traversed by Engineer X = $\text{Speed} \times \text{Time} = 40 \text{ km/h} \times 4 \text{ h} = 160$ km.

#### Question 7 (Trains & Relative Motion)
A train 180 meters long travelling at a uniform speed of 72 km/h crosses a bridge in 24 seconds. What is the length of the bridge?
- A) 240 m
- B) 300 m
- C) 320 m
- D) 360 m

**Answer: B) 300 m**
**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. Convert train speed to m/s: $72 \times \frac{5}{18} = 20$ m/s.
2. Total distance covered in 24 seconds = $\text{Speed} \times \text{Time} = 20 \times 24 = 480$ m.
3. Total distance = Train length + Bridge length:

#### Question 8 (Boats & Streams)
A motorized inspection boat can travel at 15 km/h in still water. It travels 36 km downstream along a river and returns upstream to the starting point in a total time of 5 hours. What is the speed of the river current?
- A) 2 km/h
- B) 3 km/h
- C) 4 km/h
- D) 5 km/h

**Answer: B) 3 km/h**
**Step-by-Step Solution:**
1. Let speed of water current = $c$ km/h.
2. Speed downstream = $15 + c$; Speed upstream = $15 - c$.
