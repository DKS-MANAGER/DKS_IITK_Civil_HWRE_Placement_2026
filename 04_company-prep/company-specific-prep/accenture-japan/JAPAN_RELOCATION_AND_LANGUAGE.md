## 3. Japanese Language Acquisition Strategy

### Official Requirement Breakdown (`[JD VERIFIED]`)
* **At Time of Employment**: **Japanese is NOT required**. English must be at native level.
* **After Joining the Company**: **Japanese will be required**, as consultants directly serve Japanese enterprise clients.
* **Mindset Requirement**: Having an open mindset toward change and actively learning Japanese is essential.

### Realistic Language Progression Roadmap

```text
┌──────────────────────┬──────────────────────┬──────────────────────┐
│ PHASE 1: PRE-JOINING │ PHASE 2: YEAR 1      │ PHASE 3: YEAR 2      │

├──────────────────────┼──────────────────────┼──────────────────────┤
│ • Complete Hiragana/ │ • Daily Workplace    │ • Professional       │

└──────────────────────┴──────────────────────┴──────────────────────┘
```

#### Phase 1: Pre-Joining Foundation (11 Months Pre-Departure)
1. **Writing Systems**: Master Hiragana & Katakana within 2 weeks.
2. **Grammar & Vocabulary**: Complete *Genki I & II* or *Minna no Nihongo* chapters 1–50 (targets JLPT N4 level).
3. **Accenture Sponsored Course (JPY 664,658)**: Actively complete all assigned modules, vocabulary sets, and live conversational tutoring sessions.
4. **Daily Target**: 45 minutes daily active spaced repetition (Anki flashcards) + 20 minutes listening practice (comprehensible Japanese podcasts).

#### Phase 2: Year 1 In Tokyo (Analyst Immersion)
* **Workplace Communication**: Greeting colleagues (*Ohayou gozaimasu*), understanding project status emails, participating in informal lunch discussions.
* **Internal Team Discussions**: Understanding technical terminology in Japanese (e.g., *Youken Teigi* = Requirements Definition, *Sekkei* = Design, *Unyou* = Operations).
* **Target Milestone**: Pass JLPT N3 within 12 months in Tokyo.

#### Phase 3: Year 2 (Consultant Progression)
* Conducting requirement interviews with client IT managers in Japanese.
* Preparing bilingual deliverables and executive slide decks.
* Target Milestone: JLPT N2 (Business Working Proficiency).

## 4. Japanese Business Culture & Professional Practices

In interviews with the Managing Director (Round 2), demonstrating awareness of Japanese corporate culture is a major differentiator:

### 1. *Ho-Ren-So (報・連・相)* — Transparent Teamwork
* **Hokoku (報告 - Report)**: Proactively report progress and completion of assigned tasks to your Manager without waiting to be asked.
* **Renraku (連絡 - Inform)**: Keep all stakeholders immediately updated on changes, schedule adjustments, or emerging risks.
* **Sodan (相談 - Consult)**: Consult seniors and peers early when facing technical ambiguity or roadblocks rather than silently struggling.

### 2. *Nemawashi (根回し)* — Informal Alignment
* In Japanese organizations, formal meetings are rarely places where decisions are debated for the first time.
* *Nemawashi* is the process of informally sharing proposals with key stakeholders beforehand, gathering input, addressing concerns, and ensuring consensus before the official executive meeting.
* **Consulting Application**: Before presenting an enterprise cloud architecture recommendation to a C-suite committee, align with the client's IT leads and security team to ensure zero surprises.

### 3. *Kaizen (改善)* & Extreme Quality Focus
* Meticulous attention to detail in data validation, presentation formatting, and client deliverables.
* Double-checking quantitative calculations and ensuring every chart footnote has verifiable evidence.
