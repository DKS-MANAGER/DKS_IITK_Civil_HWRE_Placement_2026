## 3. Product Launch Trajectory & The First 6 Months Rule

Extensive research by the IQVIA Institute demonstrates a stark commercial reality:
> **The First 6 Months Predict Peak Sales with Over 85% Accuracy.**  
> If a newly launched drug fails to hit its initial adoption benchmarks within the first two quarters, subsequent promotional spend rarely alters its long-term market share trajectory.

### 3.1 Overcoming Launch Resistance: The 3 Bottlenecks
1. **Awareness Bottleneck**: Community physicians unaware of the new indication $\to$ Resolved via Key Opinion Leader (KOL) webinars, journal reprints, and sales-force detailing.
2. **Access Bottleneck**: High copays or restrictive payer step-therapy $\to$ Resolved via manufacturer **Co-Pay Cards** (reducing patient out-of-pocket costs to \$5–\$10) and bridging sample programs.
3. **Adoption Inertia**: Clinicians comfortable with established standard-of-care $\to$ Resolved via head-to-head Phase III superiority data and simple digital dosing algorithms.

## 5. Loss of Exclusivity (LOE) & Patent Cliff Defense Strategies

When a brand's primary composition-of-matter patent expires, generic manufacturers launch identical small-molecule copies at an $80\text{–}90\%$ discount. Originator brands typically lose **$80\text{ to }90\%$ of their volume within 6 to 12 months**.

### 5.1 Strategic Playbook to Defend Brand Value Post-LOE
1. **Authorized Generic (AG)**: The originator partners with a generic firm (or launches through its own generic subsidiary) to market an authorized generic during the 180-day Hatch-Waxman exclusivity window, capturing generic profits while maintaining brand prestige.
2. **Next-Generation Formulation / Lifecycle Extension**: Launching an extended-release (once-daily vs three-times-daily) or fixed-dose combination therapy 18 to 24 months *prior* to patent cliff, aggressively transitioning patients before generic entry.
3. **Over-the-Counter (OTC) Switch**: Reformulating suitable small molecules for direct retail pharmacy sales without a prescription (e.g., Prilosec, Claritin, Allegra).
4. **Indication Expansion (Patent Thicket)**: Securing secondary patents for novel pediatric formulations, new orphan indications, or proprietary manufacturing delivery devices (e.g., autoinjectors).
