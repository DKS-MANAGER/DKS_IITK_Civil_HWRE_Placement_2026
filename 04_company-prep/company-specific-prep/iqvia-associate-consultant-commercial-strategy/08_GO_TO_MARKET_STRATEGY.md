## 2. Global Launch Sequencing: The "Germany-First" European Rule

When launching an innovative drug globally, biopharma companies sequence national entries to prevent **International Reference Pricing (IRP)** erosion:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                    GLOBAL PHARMACEUTICAL LAUNCH SEQUENCING                  │
│                                                                             │
│ WAVE 1: UNITED STATES (Month 0)                                             │
│ • Free market pricing; highest realized launch price and fastest adoption.  │
│                               ▼                                             │
│ WAVE 2: GERMANY (Month 6–9)                                                 │
│ • Free pricing permitted for the first 12 months under AMNOG regulations.   │
│ • Critical benchmark price referenced by the rest of Europe.                │
│                               ▼                                             │
│ WAVE 3: UK, FRANCE, ITALY, SPAIN (Month 12–18)                              │
│ • Formal HTA reviews (NICE, HAS, AIFA) demanding statutory price discounts. │
│                               ▼                                             │
│ WAVE 4: JAPAN, CANADA, AUSTRALIA, EMERGING MARKETS (Month 18–36)            │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 3. Channel Selection: Specialty Pharmacy vs Hospital Buy-and-Bill

* **Specialty Pharmacy Distribution**: Used for complex, high-cost oral oncology pills and self-administered subcutaneous autoinjectors (\$5,000–\$20,000/month). Specialty pharmacies manage cold-chain logistics, patient adherence tracking, prior authorization approvals, and co-pay card processing.
* **Hospital "Buy-and-Bill" Distribution**: Used for physician-administered intravenous (IV) infusions in oncology clinics. The hospital purchases the drug upfront from a wholesaler, infuses it into the patient, and bills the patient's health insurance, earning a percentage handling markup.

## 4. End-to-End GTM Case Study: Launching an Oral TNBC Oncology Asset

### 4.1 Client Context & Strategic Challenge
A European clinical-stage biotech has received FDA breakthrough therapy designation for **OncoOral**, a novel oral small-molecule PARP-inhibitor for metastatic Triple-Negative Breast Cancer (TNBC). The client has zero commercial sales infrastructure in the United States and has engaged IQVIA Consulting to design its US Go-to-Market strategy.

### 4.2 Structured Issue Tree for GTM Evaluation
```
                                        ┌── 1. Where to Play? (Patient Funnel & Prescriber Universe)
                                        │
OncoOral US Go-to-Market Strategy ──────┼── 2. How to Win? (Value Proposition vs SOC IV Chemotherapy)
                                        │
                                        └── 3. How to Execute? (Sales Model: Direct Build vs Co-Promote)
```

### 4.3 Quantitative Market Sizing & Launch Funnel
1. **Total US Breast Cancer Incidence**: 310,000 new cases/year.
2. **TNBC Proportion ($15\%$)**: $310,000 \times 0.15 = 46,500$ patients.
3. **Metastatic / Advanced Setting ($30\%$)**: $46,500 \times 0.30 = 13,950$ patients.
4. **Target Biomarker Positive ($40\%$)**: $13,950 \times 0.40 = 5,580$ eligible patients/year.
5. **Pricing & Peak Share**:
   * Net Realized Annual Price: \$120,000/patient.
   * Projected Peak Market Share ($35\%$): $5,580 \times 0.35 = 1,953$ treated patients.
   * **Projected Peak US Revenue**: $1,953 \times \$120,000 \approx \mathbf{\$234.4\text{ Million / year}}$.

### 4.4 Commercial Channel & Field-Force Strategy
* **Prescriber Universe**: Oncology prescribing is highly concentrated. IQVIA Xponent® data reveals that **2,200 oncologists** in US academic cancer centers and large community networks treat $78\%$ of metastatic TNBC patients.
* **Sales Force Sizing**:
  * Targeting 2,200 oncologists with an average call frequency of 12 calls/year $= 26,400\text{ details/year}$.
  * A full-time oncology sales rep conducts 4 details/day $\times 200\text{ days} = 800\text{ calls/year}$.
  * Required Sales Rep Headcount $= 26,400 / 800 = \mathbf{33\text{ Sales Representatives}}$.
* **Commercial Model Recommendation**: Build a lean internal 35-person specialized sales force targeting top Tier 1 academic medical centers, and partner with a Contract Commercial Organization (CCO) or deploy digital omnichannel for outlying community practices.
