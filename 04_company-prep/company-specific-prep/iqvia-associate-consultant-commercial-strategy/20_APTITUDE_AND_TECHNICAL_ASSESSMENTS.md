#### Q01: Gross-to-Net (GTN) Waterfall Margin
**Question**: A specialty oncology drug has a Wholesale Acquisition Cost (WAC) list price of \$12,000 per vial. The biopharma manufacturer provides a mandatory statutory Medicaid rebate of 23.1%, a negotiated commercial PBM rebate of 14.0% on WAC, and incurs 3.5% in distributor service fees and copay assistance. What is the realized Net Price per vial, and what is the overall GTN discount percentage?  
**Solution**:
1. Total GTN Deductions percentage:
   $$\text{Total Discount \%} = 23.1\% + 14.0\% + 3.5\% = 40.6\%$$
2. Dollar deduction per vial:
   $$\text{Deduction} = \$12,000 \times 0.406 = \$4,872$$
3. Realized Net Price:
   $$\text{Net Price} = \$12,000 - \$4,872 = \mathbf{\$7,128}$$
4. Overall GTN discount rate = $\mathbf{40.6\%}$ (or 59.4% net realization rate).

#### Q02: Compound Annual Growth Rate (CAGR)
**Question**: Global sales of GLP-1 receptor agonists in obesity were \$3.2 Billion in 2021 and expanded to \$10.8 Billion in 2024. Calculate the 3-year CAGR.  
**Solution**:
1. CAGR Formula:
   $$\text{CAGR} = \left(\frac{V_{\text{final}}}{V_{\text{initial}}}\right)^{1/n} - 1 = \left(\frac{10.8}{3.2}\right)^{1/3} - 1$$
2. Ratio:
   $$\frac{10.8}{3.2} = 3.375$$
3. Cube root:
   $$3.375^{1/3} = 1.50 \quad (\text{since } 1.5 \times 1.5 \times 1.5 = 3.375)$$
4. $\text{CAGR} = 1.50 - 1 = \mathbf{50.0\% \text{ per year}}$.

#### Q03: Loss-of-Exclusivity (LOE) Volume Erosion
**Question**: Prior to patent expiration, a branded blockbuster generated 400,000 monthly 30-day equivalent prescriptions (TRx) at \$500 per prescription. In Year 1 post-LOE, generic entrants capture 85% of physical volume. The branded product drops its price by 20% to retain remaining share, while generics price at an 80% discount to original brand price. What is the total dollar market value of the category in Month 12 post-LOE?  
**Solution**:
1. Total category physical volume = 400,000 prescriptions.
2. Branded physical volume = $400,000 \times 15\% = 60,000 \text{ TRx}$.
3. Generic physical volume = $400,000 \times 85\% = 340,000 \text{ TRx}$.
4. Post-LOE Branded Price:
   $$\$500 \times (1 - 0.20) = \$400$$
   $$\text{Branded Dollar Sales} = 60,000 \times \$400 = \$24,000,000$$
5. Post-LOE Generic Price:
   $$\$500 \times (1 - 0.80) = \$100$$
   $$\text{Generic Dollar Sales} = 340,000 \times \$100 = \$34,000,000$$
6. Total Category Dollar Market Value:
   $$\text{Total Value} = \$24\text{M} + \$34\text{M} = \mathbf{\$58.0 \text{ Million / month}}$$
   *(Down from \$200M/month pre-LOE, representing a 71% market contraction).*

#### Q04: Price Elasticity of Demand in Private Healthcare
**Question**: An Indian private hospital network reduces the out-of-pocket package price of a robotic total knee replacement from ₹3,50,000 to ₹2,80,000. Monthly procedure volume rises from 120 to 168 cases. Calculate the price elasticity of demand ($\epsilon$) using the mid-point (arc elasticity) method.  
**Solution**:
1. Price change:
   $$\Delta P = 2,80,000 - 3,50,000 = -70,000$$
   $$P_{\text{avg}} = \frac{3,50,000 + 2,80,000}{2} = 3,15,000$$
   $$\% \Delta P = \frac{-70,000}{3,15,000} = -\frac{2}{9} \approx -22.22\%$$
2. Quantity change:
   $$\Delta Q = 168 - 120 = +48$$
   $$Q_{\text{avg}} = \frac{120 + 168}{2} = 144$$
   $$\% \Delta Q = \frac{+48}{144} = +\frac{1}{3} \approx +33.33\%$$
3. Price Elasticity:
   $$\epsilon = \frac{+33.33\%}{-22.22\%} = \frac{1/3}{-2/9} = -\frac{9}{6} = \mathbf{-1.50}$$
   *(Demand is elastic: a 1% price cut drives a 1.5% volume expansion).*

#### Q05: Break-Even Sales Representative Productivity
**Question**: Deploying a specialized oncology sales representative costs a biopharma company \$280,000 annually (salary, bonus, fleet, travel, compliance). The drug being promoted generates a net contribution margin of \$1,400 per 30-day patient prescription course. How many incremental annualized patient treatment months must each representative generate to break even?  
**Solution**:
$$\text{Break-Even Courses} = \frac{\text{Annual Fully Loaded Cost}}{\text{Contribution Margin per Unit}} = \frac{\$280,000}{\$1,400} = \mathbf{200 \text{ treatment months / year}}$$
*(If average treatment duration is 8 months per patient, the rep must convert $200 / 8 = 25$ net new patients annually).*

#### Q06: Weighted Average Cost of Capital (WACC) for Valuation
**Question**: A biopharma company is evaluating an in-licensing opportunity. Its capital structure is 70% Equity and 30% Debt. The cost of equity ($K_e$) is 11.5%, pre-tax cost of debt ($K_d$) is 6.0%, and corporate tax rate is 25%. Calculate the firm's WACC.  
**Solution**:
1. After-tax cost of debt:
   $$K_{d,\text{after-tax}} = 6.0\% \times (1 - 0.25) = 4.5\%$$
2. WACC Formula:
   $$\text{WACC} = (w_e \times K_e) + (w_d \times K_{d,\text{after-tax}})$$
   $$\text{WACC} = (0.70 \times 11.5\%) + (0.30 \times 4.5\%) = 8.05\% + 1.35\% = \mathbf{9.40\%}$$

#### Q07: Commercial Cannibalization Rate
**Question**: Brand A currently generates \$600M annual revenue with a 60% gross margin. The company launches an upgraded next-generation Brand B (80% gross margin). In Year 1, Brand B achieves \$200M revenue, but 40% of Brand B's sales were cannibalized directly from Brand A. Calculate the net change in corporate gross profit dollars.  
**Solution**:
1. Sales cannibalized from Brand A = $\$200\text{M} \times 40\% = \$80\text{M}$.
2. Lost Gross Profit from Brand A:
   $$\text{Lost GP} = \$80\text{M} \times 60\% = \$48\text{M}$$
3. Gross Profit gained from Brand B:
   $$\text{Gained GP} = \$200\text{M} \times 80\% = \$160\text{M}$$
4. Net Change in Corporate Gross Profit:
   $$\Delta \text{GP} = \$160\text{M} - \$48\text{M} = \mathbf{+\$112.0 \text{ Million}}$$

#### Q09: Expected Monetary Value (EMV) of Clinical Pipeline
**Question**: A Phase IIb oncology trial has a 60% chance of success (advancing to Phase III, yielding an NPV of \$450M) and a 40% chance of failure (asset abandoned, NPV = -\$40M sunk cost). What is the Expected Monetary Value (EMV)?

$$\text{EMV} = (0.60 \times \$450\text{M}) + (0.40 \times -\$40\text{M}) = \$270\text{M} - \$16\text{M} = \mathbf{\$254.0 \text{ Million}}$$

#### Q10: Inventory Days of Sales (DSO / DOI)
**Question**: A pharmaceutical wholesaler holds \$45 Million in average finished goods inventory. Its annual Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) is \$360 Million. Calculate Days of Inventory Outstanding (DIO) based on a 365-day year.  
**Solution**:
$$\text{Inventory Turnover} = \frac{\text{COGS}}{\text{Avg Inventory}} = \frac{\$360\text{M}}{\$45\text{M}} = 8.0 \times \text{ per year}$$
$$\text{DIO} = \frac{365}{\text{Turnover}} = \frac{365}{8.0} = \mathbf{45.625 \text{ days}} \approx \mathbf{46 \text{ days}}$$

#### Q12: Patient Co-Pay Threshold & Prescription Abandonment
**Question**: An econometric study reveals that prescription abandonment at retail pharmacies rises by 3.2% for every \$10 increase in patient copay above \$25. If baseline abandonment is 8.0% at a \$25 copay, what will the abandonment rate be if a payer moves the drug to Tier 3 with an \$85 copay?

   $$\Delta \text{Abandonment} = 6 \times 3.2\% = 19.2\%$$
4. Final Abandonment Rate:
   $$\text{Final Rate} = 8.0\% + 19.2\% = \mathbf{27.2\%}$$

#### Q13: Operating Leverage & Margin Expansion
**Question**: A biopharma company has fixed commercial OPEX of \$150 Million and a variable cost ratio of 25% of sales. If revenue grows by 20% from \$400 Million to \$480 Million, what is the degree of operating leverage (DOL) and the percentage growth in operating profit (EBIT)?  
**Solution**:
1. Baseline (Revenue = \$400M):
   - Variable Costs = $400 \times 0.25 = \$100\text{M}$.
   - Contribution Margin = $400 - 100 = \$300\text{M}$.
   - $\text{EBIT}_1 = 300 - 150 = \$150\text{M}$.
2. Year 2 (Revenue = \$480M):
   - Variable Costs = $480 \times 0.25 = \$120\text{M}$.
   - Contribution Margin = $480 - 120 = \$360\text{M}$.
   - $\text{EBIT}_2 = 360 - 150 = \$210\text{M}$.
3. EBIT Growth:
   $$\% \Delta \text{EBIT} = \frac{210 - 150}{150} \times 100\% = \frac{60}{150} \times 100\% = \mathbf{+40.0\%}$$
4. Degree of Operating Leverage:
   $$\text{DOL} = \frac{\% \Delta \text{EBIT}}{\% \Delta \text{Revenue}} = \frac{40\%}{20\%} = \mathbf{2.0}$$

#### Q14: Clinical Trial Sample Size Power Estimation
**Question**: A clinical trial requires an expected 240 confirmed progression-free survival (PFS) events to achieve 80% statistical power ($\beta = 0.20$) at $\alpha = 0.05$ two-sided. If the historical annual progression rate is 30% per patient-year and the study duration is 2 years, what is the minimum enrolled patient sample size assuming 10% loss-to-follow-up?  
**Solution**:
1. Expected event rate per enrolled patient over 2 years:
   $$P(\text{Event}) = 1 - (1 - 0.30)^2 = 1 - 0.49 = 0.51 \text{ (or 51\%)}$$
2. Patients required to observe 240 events:
   $$N_{\text{evaluable}} = \frac{240}{0.51} \approx 470.6 \text{ patients}$$
3. Adjusting for 10% loss-to-follow-up:
   $$N_{\text{total}} = \frac{470.6}{1 - 0.10} = \frac{470.6}{0.90} \approx \mathbf{523 \text{ patients}}$$

#### Q15: Risk-Adjusted Net Present Value (eNPV)
**Question**: An asset requires \$50M Phase III investment at $t=0$. Approval probability is 60%. If approved, commercial cash flows are \$80M/year for 5 years starting at $t=1$. Cost of capital is 10%. What is the project eNPV?  
**Solution**:
1. Present Value of 5-year annuity of \$80M at 10%:
   $$\text{PVIFA}_{10\%, 5} = \frac{1 - (1.10)^{-5}}{0.10} = 3.7908$$
   $$\text{PV}_{\text{success}} = \$80\text{M} \times 3.7908 = \$303.26\text{M}$$
2. Risk-adjusted commercial PV:
   $$\text{Risk-PV} = 0.60 \times \$303.26\text{M} = \$181.96\text{M}$$
3. eNPV after deducting upfront \$50M CapEx:
   $$\text{eNPV} = \$181.96\text{M} - \$50.0\text{M} = \mathbf{\$131.96 \text{ Million}}$$

#### Q32: Correlation vs. Causation in Marketing Mix

**Scenario**: A client considers acquiring a clinical-stage biotech based on three pieces of evidence:

2. An *in vitro* rodent binding affinity assay.
3. Key Opinion Leader (KOL) enthusiasm from two prominent academic investigators.  
**Question**: As an IQVIA commercial consultant, how should you evaluate this evidence?  
**Answer**: **High Binary Risk / Low Evidence Rigor**. Single-arm, open-label trials lack a randomized control comparator and are susceptible to investigator bias and patient selection artifacts. Pre-clinical binding assays frequently fail to translate to clinical human bioavailability. A commercial consultant must recommend a risk-adjusted milestone earnout rather than an upfront cash buyout.

#### Q35: Fallacy of Composition in Biopharma
**Premise**: If Company A increases its oncology sales force by 20%, it expands its share of physician detailing and increases prescription volume.  
**Fallacy**: Therefore, if all five competing biopharma companies in the oncology market increase their sales forces by 20%, total industry profitability will rise.  
**Question**: Name and explain this consulting economic dynamic.  
**Answer**: **The Detailing Arms Race (Prisoner's Dilemma)**. When all competitors expand sales forces simultaneously, detailing clutter increases while the total patient pool remains unchanged. Physician access declines, field productivity drops, and fixed commercial OPEX expands across the industry, compressing operating margins for all players.

## Section 4: Quantitative Estimation & Mental Math Drills (Q46 – Q50)

#### Q46: Rapid Estimation of Infusion Chair Capacity
**Question**: A cancer center operates 24 infusion chairs, open 10 hours a day, 250 days a year. An IV chemo regimen requires 2.5 hours per chair (including cleaning and setup). If chair utilization is 80%, how many infusions are delivered annually?  
**Solution**:
1. Total daily hours available = $24 \times 10 = 240 \text{ chair-hours/day}$.
2. Effective hours at 80% utilization = $240 \times 0.80 = 192 \text{ hours/day}$.
3. Infusions per day = $192 / 2.5 = 76.8 \text{ infusions/day}$.
4. Annual Infusions = $76.8 \times 250 = \mathbf{19,200 \text{ infusions / year}}$.

#### Q47: Population Cohort Estimation
**Question**: In a country of 68 Million with a birth rate of 11 per 1,000 and life expectancy of 82 years, estimate the number of annual births and the size of the infant (< 1 year) cohort.  
**Solution**:
$$\text{Annual Births} = 68,000,000 \times \frac{11}{1,000} = 68,000 \times 11 = \mathbf{748,000 \text{ births / year}}$$

#### Q48: Rule of 72 in Healthcare Inflation
**Question**: If US specialty drug spending compounds at an annual rate of 8.0%, in approximately how many years will total specialty drug expenditure double?  
**Solution**:
$$\text{Doubling Time} \approx \frac{72}{\text{Growth Rate}} = \frac{72}{8.0} = \mathbf{9.0 \text{ years}}$$

#### Q49: Rapid Division Shortcut
**Question**: Calculate $4,320 / 36$ without a calculator.  
**Solution**:
$$4,320 / 36 = (3,600 + 720) / 36 = 100 + 20 = \mathbf{120}$$

#### Q50: Weighted Contribution Margin
**Question**: A portfolio consists of Drug X (60% volume share, \$800 margin) and Drug Y (40% volume share, \$1,400 margin). What is the portfolio weighted average contribution margin?  
**Solution**:
$$\text{Weighted Margin} = (0.60 \times \$800) + (0.40 \times \$1,400) = \$480 + \$560 = \mathbf{\$1,040 \text{ per unit}}$$
