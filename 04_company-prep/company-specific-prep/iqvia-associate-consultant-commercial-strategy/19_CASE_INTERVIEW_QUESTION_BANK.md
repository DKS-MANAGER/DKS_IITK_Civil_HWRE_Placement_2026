## Category 1: Market Entry & Geographic Expansion

### Case 01: US Pediatric Rare Neuromuscular Gene Therapy
- **Prompt**: A European biotech company has developed a one-time adeno-associated virus (AAV) gene therapy for a fatal pediatric motor neuron disorder. FDA accelerated approval is anticipated in 9 months. The CEO has hired IQVIA to determine: (1) Addressable US patient volume and market size; (2) Optimal pricing structure; and (3) Distribution and treatment center strategy.
- **Clarifying Data**:
  - US annual live births = 3.6 Million.
  - Disease incidence = 1 in 10,000 live births (360 incident patients/year).
  - Historic untreated life expectancy is < 2 years.
  - Genetic screening: 60% of US states include condition on Universal Newborn Screening (NBS) panels, detecting 216 infants at birth; remaining 40% are diagnosed symptomatically at 6–9 months (144 infants), but 25% suffer irreversible motor neuron death before diagnosis, leaving 108 candidates.
  - Total clinically eligible patient pool = $216 + 108 = 324 \text{ infants/year}$.
  - Current Standard of Care (SoC): Chronic intrathecal antisense oligonucleotide requiring 3 annual spinal lumbar punctures costing \$375,000/year for life.
- **Quantitative Solution**:
  - Total Addressable Patients = 324 infants/year.
  - Lifetime Cost of SoC over 10-year horizon: $10 \times \$375,000 = \$3.75 \text{ Million}$ (undiscounted).
  - Gene Therapy Pricing: One-time upfront price set at \$2.2 Million, representing a 40% cost saving relative to 10-year chronic maintenance therapy.
  - Annual US Addressable Market:
    $$\text{TAM} = 324 \text{ infants} \times \$2.2 \text{ Million} = \mathbf{\$712.8 \text{ Million / year}}$$
- **Strategic Recommendation**:
  1. *Commercial Model*: Implement an **annuity-based outcomes warranty contract** (5 annual installments of \$440,000, forgiven if the child fails to achieve independent sitting milestone).
  2. *Distribution*: Restrict administration to a qualified network of 25 pediatric Centers of Excellence (CoEs) equipped with apheresis and ICU facilities to manage vector-induced hepatotoxicity.

### Case 02: Japan Direct Commercial Build vs. Partnering
- **Prompt**: A US mid-sized biopharma with a novel Phase III targeted oncology oral agent must decide whether to build its own commercial sales organization in Japan or out-license the asset to a domestic Japanese pharma (e.g., Takeda, Daiichi Sankyo).
- **Clarifying Data**:
  - Projected Japan peak sales = ¥30 Billion (~$200 Million USD).
  - Direct Build: Capital expenditure of \$40M upfront, annual SG&A OPEX of \$30M, capturing 100% of net margin (75% operating margin = \$150M/year at peak).
  - Out-Licensing Deal: \$30M upfront milestone, zero OPEX, and a flat 22% net sales royalty (\$44M/year at peak).
  - Breakeven analysis indicates direct commercial infrastructure achieves higher cumulative NPV if the client intends to launch $\ge 2$ subsequent pipeline assets in Japan within 5 years.
- **Strategic Recommendation**: If this is an isolated, single-asset entry, out-license to a domestic partner with established *Yakkyoku* and hospital relationships. If Japan is a designated long-term corporate hub with 2+ fast-following pipeline indications, invest in a direct specialized sales force (40 key account managers).

### Case 03: European TNF Biosimilar Market Entry
- **Prompt**: A biosimilar manufacturer is preparing to launch a biosimilar adalimumab in the EU-5 upon originator patent expiry. The client must select which national markets to prioritize and determine the optimal tendering rebate strategy.
- **Clarifying Data**:
  - Germany operates via regional physician prescription quotas (Bio-quotas $\ge 60\%$), allowing retail pharmacy substitution.
  - UK operates through NHS England centralized hospital multi-winner national tenders.
  - Italy operates through regional Consortia tenders awarding exclusive volume to the lowest 3 bidders.
- **Strategic Recommendation**: Prioritize Germany (rapid physician-driven retail volume uptake at moderate 35% discounts) and the UK (guaranteed NHS tender volume allocation). Avoid severe price degradation in Italy unless manufacturing scale guarantees sub-€8 per syringe marginal cost.

## Category 2: Commercial Product Launch & TPP Optimization

### Case 04: Dual-Acting Incretin in Obesity Commercial GTM
- **Prompt**: A biopharma company is launching a novel dual GLP-1/GIP co-agonist demonstrating 22% mean body weight reduction (vs. 15% for incumbent semaglutide). Formulate the US commercial GTM and payer access strategy.
- **Clarifying Data**:
  - Target US population: 42% obesity rate in adults; ~110M individuals.
  - Employer-sponsored commercial plans currently opt out of anti-obesity medications (AOM) in 55% of plan designs due to budget impact fears.
  - Net monthly price target: \$800/month (\$9,600/year).
- **Strategic Recommendation**: Focus GTM on self-insured Fortune 500 employers by presenting an **actuarial health-economic model** demonstrating a 3-year return on investment: 22% weight reduction drives a 40% reduction in sleep apnea, cardiovascular events, and knee arthroplasty costs, offsetting drug expense.

### Case 05: First-in-Class MASH/NASH Pricing Strategy
- **Prompt**: An oral THR-$\beta$ agonist has secured FDA approval for Metabolic Dysfunction-Associated Steatohepatitis (MASH) with liver fibrosis stage F2/F3. Determine the launch pricing and diagnostic positioning.
- **Clarifying Data**:
  - US F2/F3 MASH prevalence = ~5.0 Million patients.
  - Historical diagnosis required invasive liver biopsy (only 5% diagnosed).
  - Emerging non-invasive tests (NITs: FibroScan, VCTE, FIB-4 index, ELF blood test) expand diagnostic rate to 25% (1.25M patients).
  - ICER cost-effectiveness benchmark suggests \$15,000 to \$20,000 annually.
- **Strategic Recommendation**: Price at \$18,500/year WAC (\$13,500 Net). Tie commercial detailing directly to gastroenterology and hepatology clinics equipped with FibroScan technology to remove the biopsy diagnostic bottleneck.

### Case 06: Subcutaneous Reformulation Delivery Switch
- **Prompt**: An oncology blockbuster currently administered as a 2-hour IV infusion faces generic erosion in 3 years. The company has developed a 5-minute subcutaneous (SC) autoinjector utilizing recombinant human hyaluronidase. Design the lifecycle brand-migration strategy.
- **Clarifying Data**:
  - Hospital infusion suites face severe chair capacity constraints (utilization > 92%).
  - Physician administration billing code: IV infusion generates \$180 facility fee; SC injection generates \$45 fee (hospital margin reduction).
- **Strategic Recommendation**: Target community oncology practices and hospital systems facing infusion suite nurse shortages. Emphasize chair-turnover capacity expansion (treating 3 patients in the time of 1 IV infusion) and offer volume-based institutional purchase rebates to offset lower administration fees. Target migrating 70% of volume to SC prior to IV LOE.

## Category 3: Commercial Due Diligence & M&A / In-Licensing

### Case 07: Private Equity Acquisition of Biopharma CDMO
- **Prompt**: A private equity sponsor is conducting commercial due diligence on a mid-sized Contract Development & Manufacturing Organization (CDMO) specializing in sterile injectable fill-finish for biologics and GLP-1 peptides. Assess market attractiveness and downside risks.
- **Core Diligence Findings**:
  - Market CAGR: 11.5% driven by booming peptide and antibody pipelines.
  - Capacity Bottleneck: Sterile fill-finish high-speed isolator lines have a 3-year lead time from equipment vendors (Syntegon, Groninger). Target CDMO has 4 fully operational, validated lines with 45% available capacity.
  - Customer Concentration: Top 2 biopharma clients account for 62% of revenue.
- **Recommendation**: Proceed with acquisition at a 12.5× EBITDA multiple, but structure deal with an earnout tied to diversifying customer base below 40% concentration and securing 5-year take-or-pay capacity reservation contracts.

### Case 08: In-Licensing Phase IIb Multiple Myeloma Asset
- **Prompt**: Evaluate whether to in-license a BCMA $\times$ CD3 bispecific T-cell engager (BiTE) for relapsed/refractory multiple myeloma (RRMM) at an upfront cost of \$150M plus \$300M in regulatory/commercial milestones.
- **Financial Valuation**:
  - Target patient population: 4th-line RRMM US patients = 12,000 patients/year.
  - Expected peak share = 22% (2,640 patients).
  - Net annual price = \$280,000.
  - Peak Net Revenue = $2,640 \times \$280,000 = \$739.2 \text{ Million}$.
  - Probability of technical & regulatory success (PTRS) from Phase IIb to approval = 65%.
  - Risk-adjusted 10-Year eNPV at 10% discount rate = **\$520 Million**.
- **Recommendation**: In-license. The \$520M eNPV comfortably exceeds the \$150M upfront + \$300M milestone commitments. Ensure risk mitigation against cytokine release syndrome (CRS) through outpatient step-up dosing protocols.

### Case 09: Divestiture of Mature Legacy Respiratory Brand
- **Prompt**: A biopharma company wishes to divest a mature, cash-generating Dry Powder Inhaler (DPI) asthma brand generating \$180M revenue with declining sales (-6% CAGR) to free capital for oncology R&D. Value the asset and identify prospective acquirers.
- **Valuation Analysis**:
  - Operating margin = 58% (\$104.4M EBITDA).
  - Stable cash flow profile with low CapEx requirements.
  - Valued at 5.0× EBITDA for mature cash cows = **\$520 Million enterprise value**.
- **Recommendation**: Divest to mature-brand specialty aggregators (e.g., Viatris, Organon, Karo Pharma) who can extract operational synergies through centralized supply chain management.

## Category 4: Portfolio Prioritization & Lifecycle Management

### Case 10: $400M Pipeline R&D Capital Allocation Matrix
- **Prompt**: A pharmaceutical executive committee has a fixed \$400M Phase III clinical development budget over the next 3 years. Five pipeline assets are competing for capital. Prioritize the portfolio.

```
┌───────┬───────────────────────────────┬────────────┬──────────┬─────────────┬──────────────┬────────────┐
│ Asset │          Indication           │ Phase 3 Cost│   PTRS   │ Peak Sales  │ Unrisked NPV │ Risk eNPV  │
├───────┼───────────────────────────────┼────────────┼──────────┼─────────────┼──────────────┼────────────┤
│ A     │ 1L Metastatic Pancreatic      │   $140M    │   35%    │   $1,200M   │    $950M     │   $332.5M  │
│ B     │ Severe Ulcerative Colitis     │   $110M    │   65%    │     $850M   │    $680M     │   $442.0M  │
│ C     │ Pediatric Rare Metabolic      │    $60M    │   80%    │     $350M   │    $420M     │   $336.0M  │
│ D     │ Alzheimer's Disease           │   $220M    │   25%    │   $3,500M   │  $2,100M     │   $525.0M  │
│ E     │ Diabetic Macular Edema        │    $90M    │   70%    │     $600M   │    $490M     │   $343.0M  │
└───────┴───────────────────────────────┴────────────┴──────────┴─────────────┴──────────────┴────────────┘
```

#### Optimization Analysis:
Calculate Productivity Index (PI = Risk-adjusted eNPV / Phase 3 Cost):
- Asset B: $\$442\text{M} / \$110\text{M} = \mathbf{4.02}$
- Asset C: $\$336\text{M} / \$60\text{M} = \mathbf{5.60}$
- Asset E: $\$343\text{M} / \$90\text{M} = \mathbf{3.81}$
- Asset A: $\$332.5\text{M} / \$140\text{M} = 2.38$
- Asset D: $\$525\text{M} / \$220\text{M} = 2.39$

#### Recommendation:
Fund **Asset C (\$60M) + Asset B (\$110M) + Asset E (\$90M) + Asset A (\$140M) = \$400M Total**. This combination maximizes portfolio PI and secures an aggregate risk-adjusted eNPV of **\$1,453.5 Million**, while rejecting the high-binary-risk Alzheimer's asset (Asset D) which would consume >55% of the total budget.

### Case 11: Indication Sequencing for Pan-Tumor ADC
- **Prompt**: An antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) targeting TROP2 shows efficacy across triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), and urothelial bladder carcinoma. Determine the commercial indication launch sequence.
- **Recommendation**:
  1. *First Wave*: TNBC (Unmet medical need is severe, fastest FDA breakthrough designation, small trial size, establishing high benchmark pricing).
  2. *Second Wave (18 months later)*: 2L NSCLC (Massive commercial volume, expands franchise revenue).
  3. *Third Wave*: Urothelial Carcinoma (Label expansion to maximize patent tail).

### Case 12: LOE Generic Defense Strategy for Anticoagulant
- **Prompt**: An oral factor Xa inhibitor anticoagulant generating \$1.8B US annual sales faces patent expiration in 18 months. Design an integrated loss-of-exclusivity defense.
- **Recommendation**:
  1. *Authorized Generic (AG)*: Launch an in-house authorized generic through a distribution partner on Day 1 of patent cliff to capture 35% of generic market share and retain gross margin.
  2. *Formulation Switch*: Launch a once-daily extended-release pediatric suspension and fixed-dose combination with aspirin 12 months pre-LOE.
  3. *Copay Deflection*: Launch cash-pay direct-to-consumer pharmacy fulfillment (e.g., via Mark Cuban Cost Plus or retail subscription) matching generic out-of-pocket costs.

## Category 5: Revenue Stagnation & Turnaround Diagnosis

### Case 13: Severe Asthma Biologic Plateau Post-Launch
- **Prompt**: An anti-IL-5 biologic for severe asthma reached \$300M revenue by Year 2, but sales have plateaued flat at \$310M in Year 3 despite a growing total market. Diagnose the root cause and recommend tactical remedies.
- **Diagnostic Findings**:
  - New Patient Starts (NBRx) are down 18%.
  - High-prescribing allergists and pulmonologists (Deciles 8–10) report that while efficacy is high, **Prior Authorization denial rates jumped from 12% to 34%** due to major PBMs enforcing step-therapy through generic high-dose inhalers.
  - Average time from script submission to first injection increased from 11 days to 38 days, causing 40% patient drop-off at retail.
- **Recommendation**: Deploy a dedicated **Field Reimbursement Manager (FRM)** team to assist clinic coordinators with PA appeals, and fund a 30-day "Bridge Free Drug Program" so patients initiate therapy immediately while insurance coverage is resolved.

### Case 14: Primary Care Sales Force Incentive Overhaul
- **Prompt**: A commercial team of 250 sales reps promoting an SGLT2 inhibitor in primary care is missing sales targets by 22% across Midwest territories. Diagnose the breakdown.
- **Diagnostic Findings**:
  - Quotas were distributed uniformly based on regional population rather than historical Medicare Part D decile density.
  - Incentive compensation heavily penalized reps for territory formulary exclusions outside their control.
  - Rep detailing frequency was spent 60% on low-prescribing general practitioners (Deciles 1–3) because of easy clinic access.
- **Recommendation**: Re-segment territory quotas using IQVIA Xponent decile data. Restructure incentive compensation to reward **New-to-Brand Prescriptions (NBRx) in Deciles 7–10**, and mandate that 75% of sales calls target verified Tier 2 insured prescribers.

### Case 15: Rare Hematology Buy-and-Bill Access Failure
- **Prompt**: A hospital-administered IV enzyme for atypical Hemolytic Uremic Syndrome (aHUS) costing \$25,000 per vial is experiencing low hospital re-ordering despite strong physician clinical interest.
- **Diagnostic Findings**:
  - Community hospital CFOs are refusing to stock the drug due to financial carrying risk: Medicare Part B reimbursement is $\text{ASP} + 6\%$, but hospital acquisition payment terms are 30 days while Medicare claims adjudication takes 75 days.
  - Hospitals experience a temporary \$100k cash deficit per treated patient.
- **Recommendation**: Establish a **consignment inventory model** where the biopharma client retains legal title to the drug vials stocked in the hospital pharmacy until the infusion is administered and billed, eliminating the hospital's upfront inventory carrying risk.
