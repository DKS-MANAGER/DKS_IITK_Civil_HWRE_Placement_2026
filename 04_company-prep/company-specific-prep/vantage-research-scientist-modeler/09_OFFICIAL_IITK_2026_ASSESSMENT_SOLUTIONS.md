### Q01. Chemical Kinetics: Rate of Formation of Species D

**Problem Statement**:  
Five chemical species are denoted by $A, B, AB, C,$ and $D$, with molar concentrations denoted by square brackets. They participate in the coupled reaction network:
$$A + B \rightleftharpoons AB \longrightarrow 2C + D$$
$$C + A \rightleftharpoons 2D$$
All forward and reverse reaction rate constants are explicitly stated to be unity ($k = 1$). Find the instantaneous rate of formation of species $D$, denoted by $\frac{d[D]}{dt}$.

**Original Answer Choices** (transcribed from assessment screen):
1. $[AB] - [D] + 2[C][A]$
2. $[AB] - [D]^2 - \frac{1}{2}[C][A]$
3. $[AB] - 2[D] - [C][A]$
4. $[AB] - [D]^2 + 2[C][A]$

**Expected Assessment Selection**: **Option 4**, but subject to an important stoichiometric inconsistency in standard mass-action formulation.

**Detailed Step-by-Step Derivation & Mechanistic Analysis**:
1. **Deconstruct the Reaction Network into Elementary Steps**:
   * Reaction 1 (Forward): $A + B \xrightarrow{k_1 = 1} AB$ (Does not involve $D$).
   * Reaction 1 (Reverse): $AB \xrightarrow{k_{-1} = 1} A + B$ (Does not involve $D$).
   * Reaction 2 (Irreversible step): $AB \xrightarrow{k_2 = 1} 2C + D$.
     * Stoichiometric coefficient of $D$: $+1$.
     * Reaction rate: $v_2 = k_2 [AB] = [AB]$.
     * Contribution to $\frac{d[D]}{dt}$: $+v_2 = +[AB]$.
   * Reaction 3 (Forward): $C + A \xrightarrow{k_3 = 1} 2D$.
     * Stoichiometric coefficient of $D$: $+2$.
     * Reaction rate: $v_3 = k_3 [C][A] = [C][A]$.
     * Contribution to $\frac{d[D]}{dt}$: $+2 v_3 = +2 [C][A]$.
   * Reaction 3 (Reverse): $2D \xrightarrow{k_{-3} = 1} C + A$.
     * Stoichiometric coefficient of $D$: $-2$ (two molecules of $D$ consumed per reaction event).
     * By the Law of Mass Action for an elementary bimolecular step, the reaction velocity is:
       $$v_{-3} = k_{-3} [D]^2 = [D]^2$$
     * The net rate of consumption of $D$ is $\nu_D \times v_{-3} = -2 \times [D]^2 = -2 [D]^2$.
2. **Canonical Mass-Action Rate Equation**:
   $$\frac{d[D]}{dt} = + v_2 + 2 v_3 - 2 v_{-3} = \mathbf{[AB] + 2[C][A] - 2[D]^2}$$
3. **Assessment Discrepancy & Intent Analysis**:
   * None of the 4 provided options contains the factor $2$ on the $[D]^2$ term.
   * Option 4 reads: $[AB] - [D]^2 + 2[C][A]$. It possesses the exact correct positive contributions ($+[AB]$ and $+2[C][A]$) and correct quadratic dependence on $[D]$ ($- [D]^2$), but lacks the stoichiometric multiplier $2$ on the consumption rate.
   * This is a known pedagogical inconsistency in certain recruitment screening databases (where reaction velocity is occasionally substituted directly for the species net rate of change). In the assessment, **Option 4** is the unambiguously intended choice.

### Q03. Differentiability and Continuity of a Piecewise Function

**Problem Statement**:  
Let the real-valued function $f(x)$ be defined piecewise by:
$$f(x) = \begin{cases} x^2, & x \ge 0 \\ -x^2, & x < 0 \end{cases}$$
Which of the following statements is **TRUE**?

**Answer Choices**:
1. $f$ is differentiable, but its first derivative is not differentiable at $x = 0$.
2. $f$ is discontinuous at $x = 0$.
3. $f$ is differentiable, but its first derivative is not continuous at $x = 0$.
4. $f$ is continuous but not differentiable at $x = 0$.

**Answer**: **Option 1**.

**Detailed Step-by-Step Derivation**:
1. **Continuity of $f(x)$ at $x = 0$**:
   * Left-hand limit: $\lim_{x \to 0^-} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 0^-} (-x^2) = 0$.
   * Right-hand limit: $\lim_{x \to 0^+} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 0^+} (x^2) = 0$.
   * Function value: $f(0) = 0^2 = 0$.
   * Since $\lim_{x \to 0^-} f(x) = \lim_{x \to 0^+} f(x) = f(0) = 0$, $f(x)$ is continuous everywhere on $\mathbb{R}$.
2. **Differentiability of $f(x)$ at $x = 0$**:
   * Apply the first-principles definition of the derivative:
     $$f'(0) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(h) - f(0)}{h}$$
   * Right-hand derivative:
     $$f'_+(0) = \lim_{h \to 0^+} \frac{h^2 - 0}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0^+} h = 0$$
   * Left-hand derivative:
     $$f'_-(0) = \lim_{h \to 0^-} \frac{-h^2 - 0}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0^-} (-h) = 0$$
   * Because $f'_+(0) = f'_-(0) = 0$, $f'(0)$ exists and equals $0$. Thus, $f(x)$ is **differentiable** at $x = 0$.
3. **First Derivative Function $f'(x)$**:
   $$f'(x) = \begin{cases} 2x, & x > 0 \\ 0, & x = 0 \\ -2x, & x < 0 \end{cases} = 2|x|$$
   * $\lim_{x \to 0^+} f'(x) = \lim_{x \to 0^+} (2x) = 0$.
   * $\lim_{x \to 0^-} f'(x) = \lim_{x \to 0^-} (-2x) = 0$.
   * Since both limits equal $f'(0) = 0$, the first derivative $f'(x)$ is **continuous** at $x = 0$. (This immediately eliminates Option 3).
4. **Differentiability of $f'(x)$ at $x = 0$ (Second Derivative)**:
   * Evaluate the derivative of $f'(x) = 2|x|$ at $x = 0$:
     $$f''_+(0) = \lim_{h \to 0^+} \frac{f'(h) - f'(0)}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0^+} \frac{2h - 0}{h} = +2$$
     $$f''_-(0) = \lim_{h \to 0^-} \frac{f'(h) - f'(0)}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0^-} \frac{-2h - 0}{h} = -2$$
   * Because $f''_+(0) = +2 \ne -2 = f''_-(0)$, the second derivative $f''(0)$ does not exist.
   * Conclusion: $f(x)$ is differentiable at $x = 0$, but its first derivative $f'(x)$ is **not differentiable** at $x = 0$.

### Q04. Stability of a Two-Dimensional Linear Dynamical System

**Problem Statement**:  
An autonomous linear physical system in $\mathbb{R}^2$ is governed by the state equations:
$$\dot{x} = -x - y$$
$$\dot{y} = 2x - 4y$$
If the state vector is slightly perturbed from the origin $(0, 0)$, what describes its long-term trajectory as $t \to \infty$?

**Answer Choices**:
1. It oscillates around $(0, 0)$ forever.
2. It moves away from $(0, 0)$.
3. The behavior depends on the size of the perturbation.
4. It returns to $(0, 0)$.

**Answer**: **Option 4** — It returns to $(0, 0)$.

**Detailed Step-by-Step Derivation**:
1. **Formulate the System Matrix**:
   $$\begin{pmatrix} \dot{x} \\ \dot{y} \end{pmatrix} = \mathbf{A} \begin{pmatrix} x \\ y \end{pmatrix}, \quad \text{where } \mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix} -1 & -1 \\ 2 & -4 \end{pmatrix}$$
2. **Compute Matrix Invariants**:
   * Trace: $\tau = \text{tr}(\mathbf{A}) = (-1) + (-4) = -5$.
   * Determinant: $\Delta = \det(\mathbf{A}) = (-1)(-4) - (-1)(2) = 4 + 2 = 6$.
3. **Characteristic Polynomial and Eigenvalues**:
   $$\lambda^2 - \tau \lambda + \Delta = \lambda^2 + 5\lambda + 6 = 0$$
   $$(\lambda + 2)(\lambda + 3) = 0 \implies \mathbf{\lambda_1 = -2, \quad \lambda_2 = -3}$$
4. **Phase-Plane Stability Classification**:
   * Both eigenvalues are strictly real, distinct, and strictly negative:
     $$\lambda_2 < \lambda_1 < 0 \implies -3 < -2 < 0$$
   * The equilibrium point at $(0, 0)$ is a **Stable Node (Sink)**.
   * The general solution is:
     $$\mathbf{x}(t) = c_1 \mathbf{v}_1 e^{-2t} + c_2 \mathbf{v}_2 e^{-3t}$$
   * As $t \to \infty$, $e^{-2t} \to 0$ and $e^{-3t} \to 0$ for any perturbation $(c_1, c_2)$.
   * Therefore, the trajectory asymptotically **returns to $(0, 0)$**.

### Q05. Transcendental Exponents & Trigonometric Root Finding

**Problem Statement**:  
Given the transcendental equation:
$$81^{\sin^2 x} + 81^{\cos^2 x} = 30, \quad \text{for } 0 \le x \le \frac{\pi}{4}$$
Find the value of the quotient $\frac{\pi}{x}$.

**Answer Format**: Numeric response (Integer entry).  
**Answer**: $\mathbf{6}$.

**Detailed Step-by-Step Derivation**:
1. **Apply the Pythagorean Trigonometric Identity**:
   * $\cos^2 x = 1 - \sin^2 x$.
   * Substitute into the second term:
     $$81^{\cos^2 x} = 81^{1 - \sin^2 x} = \frac{81^1}{81^{\sin^2 x}} = \frac{81}{81^{\sin^2 x}}$$
2. **Transform to an Algebraic Quadratic**:
   * Define intermediate variable $u = 81^{\sin^2 x}$.
   * The equation becomes:
     $$u + \frac{81}{u} = 30 \implies u^2 - 30u + 81 = 0$$
3. **Solve the Quadratic Equation**:
   * Factor: $(u - 3)(u - 27) = 0 \implies u = 3 \quad \text{or} \quad u = 27$.
4. **Constrain by the Specified Domain**:
   * The domain is restricted to $x \in \left[0, \frac{\pi}{4}\right]$.
   * In this interval, $0 \le \sin x \le \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}} \implies 0 \le \sin^2 x \le \frac{1}{2}$.
   * Therefore, $81^0 \le 81^{\sin^2 x} \le 81^{1/2} \implies 1 \le u \le \sqrt{81} = 9$.
   * Since $u \le 9$, the root $u = 27$ is rejected.
   * Hence, we must have $u = 3$.
5. **Back-Substitute for $x$**:
   * $81^{\sin^2 x} = 3$.
   * Express 81 as a power of 3: $81 = 3^4$.
   * $(3^4)^{\sin^2 x} = 3^1 \implies 3^{4 \sin^2 x} = 3^1$.
   * Equate exponents:
     $$4 \sin^2 x = 1 \implies \sin^2 x = \frac{1}{4} \implies \sin x = \frac{1}{2} \quad (\text{since } x \in [0, \pi/4])$$
   * The principal angle is:
     $$x = \arcsin\left(\frac{1}{2}\right) = \frac{\pi}{6}$$
6. **Compute Target Quotient**:
   $$\frac{\pi}{x} = \frac{\pi}{\pi / 6} = \mathbf{6}$$

### Q06. Von Bertalanffy Allometric Tumor Growth Equilibrium

**Problem Statement**:  
An allometric model of solid tumor growth is governed by the differential equation:
$$\frac{dx}{dt} = a x^\alpha - b x^\beta$$
where $a, b, \alpha, \beta > 0$, $x(t) \ge 0$ represents the tumor volume or cell population, and $\alpha \ne \beta$. At what non-zero stationary tumor size does growth arrest ($\frac{dx}{dt} = 0$)?

**Answer Choices**:
1. $x = \left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta - \alpha}}$
2. The tumor never stops growing.
3. $x = \left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{\alpha - \beta}$
4. Not enough information is provided.

**Answer**: **Option 1**.

**Detailed Step-by-Step Derivation**:
1. **Set the Net Growth Rate to Zero**:
   $$\frac{dx}{dt} = 0 \implies a x^\alpha - b x^\beta = 0 \implies a x^\alpha = b x^\beta$$
2. **Solve for Non-Zero Equilibrium $x_* > 0$**:
   * Divide both sides by $b x^\alpha$:
     $$\frac{x^\beta}{x^\alpha} = \frac{a}{b} \implies x^{\beta - \alpha} = \frac{a}{b}$$
   * Take the $\frac{1}{\beta - \alpha}$-th power of both sides:
     $$\mathbf{x_* = \left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^{\frac{1}{\beta - \alpha}}}$$
3. **Biological & Physical Context (QSP Modeling)**:
   * In classical Von Bertalanffy growth, $\alpha = 2/3$ (metabolic intake scaling with surface vascularization area) and $\beta = 1$ (cellular catabolic maintenance scaling with volume).
   * For those specific exponents: $\beta - \alpha = 1 - 2/3 = 1/3 \implies x_* = (a/b)^3$, representing the asymptotic carrying capacity plateau.

### Q07. General Solution of a Coupled 2-Compartment Linear ODE System

**Problem Statement**:  
Find the general solution vector $\begin{pmatrix} x(t) \\ y(t) \end{pmatrix}$ for the coupled continuous dynamical system:
$$\dot{x} = x + 4y$$
$$\dot{y} = 2x + 3y$$

**Answer Choices**:
1. $A e^{-5t} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} + B e^t \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$
2. $A e^{5t} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} + B e^{-t} \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$
3. $A e^t \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 4 \end{pmatrix} + B e^{3t} \begin{pmatrix} 2 \\ 3 \end{pmatrix}$
4. $A e^{5t} \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} + B e^{-t} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$

**Answer**: **Option 2**.

**Detailed Step-by-Step Derivation**:
1. **Formulate Characteristic Equation**:
   $$\mathbf{A} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 4 \\ 2 & 3 \end{pmatrix}$$
   $$\det(\mathbf{A} - \lambda \mathbf{I}) = (1 - \lambda)(3 - \lambda) - (4)(2) = \lambda^2 - 4\lambda + 3 - 8 = \lambda^2 - 4\lambda - 5 = 0$$
   $$(\lambda - 5)(\lambda + 1) = 0 \implies \mathbf{\lambda_1 = 5, \quad \lambda_2 = -1}$$
2. **Find Eigenvector for $\lambda_1 = 5$**:
   $$(\mathbf{A} - 5\mathbf{I}) \mathbf{v}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} -4 & 4 \\ 2 & -2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} v_{11} \\ v_{12} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \implies -4 v_{11} + 4 v_{12} = 0 \implies v_{11} = v_{12}$$
   $$\mathbf{v}_1 = \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$$
3. **Find Eigenvector for $\lambda_2 = -1$**:
   $$(\mathbf{A} - (-1)\mathbf{I}) \mathbf{v}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} 2 & 4 \\ 2 & 4 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} v_{21} \\ v_{22} \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 \\ 0 \end{pmatrix} \implies 2 v_{21} + 4 v_{22} = 0 \implies v_{21} = -2 v_{22}$$
   $$\mathbf{v}_2 = \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$$
4. **Construct General Vector Solution**:
   $$\mathbf{x}(t) = A e^{\lambda_1 t} \mathbf{v}_1 + B e^{\lambda_2 t} \mathbf{v}_2 = \mathbf{A e^{5t} \begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix} + B e^{-t} \begin{pmatrix} -2 \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}}$$
   * Matches **Option 2**.

### Q08. Geometric Classification of a Differential Equation Solution Curve

**Problem Statement**:  
Given the initial value problem:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{3y}{x} + 7, \quad \text{with } y(1) = -\frac{7}{2}$$
What geometric curve type describes the solution $y(x)$ for $x > 0$?

**Answer Choices**:
1. Quadratic
2. Linear
3. Cubic
4. Exponential

**Answer**: **Option 2 — Linear**.

**Detailed Step-by-Step Derivation**:
1. **Standard Form of First-Order Linear ODE**:
   $$\frac{dy}{dx} - \frac{3}{x} y = 7$$
2. **Calculate Integrating Factor**:
   $$I(x) = \exp\left(\int -\frac{3}{x} dx\right) = \exp(-3 \ln x) = x^{-3} = \frac{1}{x^3} \quad (\text{for } x > 0)$$
3. **Multiply and Integrate**:
   $$\frac{d}{dx} \left[ y \cdot x^{-3} \right] = 7 x^{-3}$$
   $$y \cdot x^{-3} = \int 7 x^{-3} dx = 7 \left( \frac{x^{-2}}{-2} \right) + C = -\frac{7}{2x^2} + C$$
4. **Solve for Explicit $y(x)$**:
   $$y(x) = x^3 \left( -\frac{7}{2x^2} + C \right) = -\frac{7}{2} x + C x^3$$
5. **Apply Initial Condition $y(1) = -\frac{7}{2}$**:
   $$-\frac{7}{2} = -\frac{7}{2}(1) + C(1)^3 \implies -\frac{7}{2} = -\frac{7}{2} + C \implies \mathbf{C = 0}$$
6. **Resulting Solution**:
   $$y(x) = -\frac{7}{2} x$$
   * This is strictly the equation of a **straight line** passing through the origin with slope $m = -3.5$.
   * *Answer*: **Linear** (Option 2).

### Q09. Probability That the Final Remaining Ball is Black

**Problem Statement**:  
An urn contains $m$ white balls and $n$ black balls ($m, n \ge 1$). Balls are sequentially extracted one-by-one uniformly at random without replacement until exactly one single ball remains in the urn. What is the probability that the final remaining ball is black?

**Answer Choices**:
1. $\frac{n}{m+n}$
2. $\frac{1}{m+n}$
3. $\frac{m}{m+n}$
4. $0.5$

**Answer**: **Option 1**.

**Detailed Step-by-Step Derivation**:
1. **Symmetry & Random Permutation Argument**:
   * Drawing $m + n - 1$ balls without replacement until 1 remains is mathematically equivalent to placing all $m + n$ balls into a completely random permutation and looking at the ball occupying the $(m + n)$-th position.
   * Under a uniform random permutation, every individual ball in the initial set of $m + n$ balls has an identical, equal likelihood of occupying the final position:
     $$P(\text{A specific ball is last}) = \frac{1}{m + n}$$
2. **Sum Across Favorable Outcomes**:
   * There are exactly $n$ indistinguishable black balls in the total pool.
   * By the additivity of mutually exclusive events:
     $$P(\text{Final ball is black}) = \sum_{k=1}^n \frac{1}{m + n} = \mathbf{\frac{n}{m + n}}$$
* **Alternative Backward Inspection Principle**: The ball left behind at the end has the exact same probability distribution as a single ball sampled uniformly at random on the first draw.

### Q10. Coupled Ordinary Differential Equations for Animal–Plant Interaction

**Problem Statement**:  
Let $A(t)$ denote an animal population and $P(t)$ denote a plant biomass population. The biological dynamics are described by:
* Animals reproduce at a rate proportional to plant availability with coefficient $\alpha$.
* Animals die at a per-capita rate proportional to their own population with coefficient $\beta$.
* Plants are consumed by animals at a rate proportional to animal population with coefficient $\gamma$.
* Plants regenerate and grow at a rate proportional to their own biomass with coefficient $\delta$.
All coefficients $\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta > 0$. Identify the governing system of differential equations.

**Answer Choices**:
1. $A' = \alpha A + \beta P, \quad P' = \gamma A - \delta P$
2. $A' = \alpha P + \beta A, \quad P' = \gamma A + \delta P$
3. $A' = \alpha P - \beta A, \quad P' = -\gamma A + \delta P$
4. $A' = \alpha A - \beta P, \quad P' = \gamma P - \delta A$

### Q24. Discrete Number Theory: Consecutive Integers & Sum of Squares Digit Puzzle

**Problem Statement**:  
Let $P, Q, R, S, X, Y$ denote six distinct decimal digits from the set $\{0, 1, 2, \dots, 9\}$. The two-digit numbers $\overline{PQ}$ and $\overline{RS}$ are consecutive positive integers ($\overline{RS} = \overline{PQ} + 1$). The sum of their squares equals a three-digit integer $\overline{XYP}$:
$$(\overline{PQ})^2 + (\overline{RS})^2 = \overline{XYP}$$
where the units digit of the resulting sum is equal to the tens digit $P$ of the first number. Determine the numerical value of digit $Y$.

**Answer Choices**:
1. 5
2. 4
3. 3
4. 6

**Answer**: **Option 4 — 6**.

**Detailed Step-by-Step Derivation**:
1. **Analyze Constraints**:
   * $\overline{PQ} = 10P + Q$.
   * $\overline{RS} = \overline{PQ} + 1$.
   * $(\overline{PQ})^2 + (\overline{PQ} + 1)^2 = \overline{XYP}$.
   * Range: For the sum of squares of two consecutive two-digit numbers to be a 3-digit number ($\le 999$):
     $$2 N^2 < 1000 \implies N^2 < 500 \implies N \le 22$$
   * Also, since $N \ge 10$: $10 \le N \le 22$.
2. **Test Candidates in the Valid Range**:
   * If $N = 19$:
     $$\overline{PQ} = 19 \implies P = 1, \quad Q = 9$$
     $$\overline{RS} = 20 \implies R = 2, \quad S = 0$$
   * Compute sum of squares:
     $$19^2 + 20^2 = 361 + 400 = 761$$
   * Examine resulting digits $\overline{XYP} = 761$:
     $$X = 7, \quad Y = 6, \quad P = 1$$
   * Verify all constraints:
     * Are the six digits distinct? The set of digits is $\{P, Q, R, S, X, Y\} = \{1, 9, 2, 0, 7, 6\}$. All 6 digits are completely distinct!
     * Does the units digit of 761 match $P$? Yes, $1 = P$.
3. **Identify Target Digit $Y$**:
   * Tens digit $Y = \mathbf{6}$.
   * Matches **Option 4**.

### Q25. Differential Equations: Classification of Linearity & Separability

**Problem Statement**:  
Classify the mathematical properties of the differential equation:
$$e^{3t} \frac{dy}{dt} + 5y = t^5 y$$

**Answer Choices**:
1. Linear and separable
2. Linear but not separable
3. Separable and not linear
4. Neither linear nor separable

**Answer**: **Option 1 — Linear and separable**.

**Detailed Step-by-Step Derivation**:
1. **Test for Linearity**:
   * An ODE is linear if it can be written in the canonical form:
     $$\frac{dy}{dt} + P(t) y = Q(t)$$
   * Rearrange the given equation:
     $$e^{3t} \frac{dy}{dt} + (5 - t^5)y = 0$$
   * Divide by $e^{3t} \ne 0$:
     $$\frac{dy}{dt} + e^{-3t}(5 - t^5)y = 0$$
   * Here $P(t) = e^{-3t}(5 - t^5)$ and $Q(t) = 0$.
   * Since $y$ and its derivative appear only to the first power and are not composed with non-linear functions, the equation is strictly **Linear** (homogeneous linear ODE).
2. **Test for Separability**:
   * An ODE is separable if it can be factored as $\frac{dy}{dt} = g(t) h(y)$.
   * From our rearrangement:
     $$\frac{dy}{dt} = e^{-3t}(t^5 - 5) \cdot y$$
   * For $y \ne 0$, separate the variables:
     $$\frac{1}{y} dy = e^{-3t}(t^5 - 5) dt$$
   * The equation is strictly **Separable**.
3. **Conclusion**:
   * The differential equation is both **Linear and Separable**.
   * Matches **Option 1**.
