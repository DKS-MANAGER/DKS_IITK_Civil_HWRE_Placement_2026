### Step-by-Step Derivations

#### M1-Q01: (D)
* For laminar Hagen-Poiseuille pipe flow, the velocity profile is parabolic: $u(r) = u_{\max}\left(1 - \frac{r^2}{R^2}\right)$.
* Integrating across the circular cross-section: $V = \frac{1}{\pi R^2} \int_0^R u(r) 2\pi r dr = \frac{1}{2} u_{\max}$.
* Therefore, $\frac{u_{\max}}{V} = \mathbf{2.00}$.

#### M1-Q02: (B)
* Unit discharge: $q = \frac{Q}{b} = \frac{18.0}{6.0} = 3.0\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$.
* Critical depth: $y_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{q^2}{g}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{3.0^2}{9.81}} = \sqrt[3]{\frac{9.0}{9.81}} = \sqrt[3]{0.9174} = \mathbf{0.9717\text{ m}} \approx \mathbf{0.97\text{ m}}$.

#### M1-Q03: (B)
* Bélanger equation:
  $$\frac{y_2}{y_1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \mathrm{Fr}_1^2} - 1\right) = \frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8(25)} - 1\right) = \frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{201} - 1\right) = \frac{1}{2}(14.177 - 1) = 6.588$$
  $$y_2 = 6.588 \times 0.30\text{ m} = \mathbf{1.976\text{ m}} \approx \mathbf{1.98\text{ m}}$$.

#### M1-Q04: (A)
* The viscous sublayer where molecular viscosity dominates and Reynolds turbulent shear stress is negligible occurs strictly for dimensionless wall distance $y^+ < 5$.

#### M1-Q05: (B)
* Joukowsky surge:
  $$\Delta P_{\max} = \rho c \Delta V = (860\text{ kg/m}^3)(1100\text{ m/s})(2.5\text{ m/s}) = 2,365,000\text{ Pa} = \mathbf{2.365\text{ MPa}} \approx \mathbf{2.37\text{ MPa}}$$.

#### M2-Q01: (B)
* Management of Change (MOC) is the mandatory formal PSM protocol required prior to executing any modifications to process technology, equipment, or operating limits.

#### M2-Q02: (C)
* Gas hydrates are ice-like inclusion compounds formed thermodynamically under **high pressure** ($>30\text{ bar}$) and **low temperature** ($<15^\circ\text{C}$).

#### M2-Q03: (B)
* Absolute temperatures: $T_H = 327 + 273 = 600\text{ K}$, $T_L = 27 + 273 = 300\text{ K}$.
* Carnot efficiency: $\eta = 1 - \frac{300}{600} = 0.50$ (50%).
* Work output: $W = \eta Q_{\text{in}} = 0.50 \times 1200\text{ kJ} = \mathbf{600\text{ kJ}}$.

#### M2-Q04: (C)
* In the Hierarchy of Controls, automated engineering controls (closed-loop vapor recovery and flare interlocks) eliminate human dependency and isolate the hazard, ranking far above administrative controls or PPE.

#### M3-Q01: (C)
* Tank capacity $= \text{LCM}(10, 15, 12) = 60\text{ units}$.
* Rates: Rate A $= +6\text{ u/hr}$, Rate B $= +4\text{ u/hr}$, Pump C $= -5\text{ u/hr}$.
* Net rate $= 6 + 4 - 5 = +5\text{ units/hr}$.
* To fill remaining half of the tank ($30\text{ units}$):
  $$\text{Time} = \frac{30\text{ units}}{5\text{ units/hr}} = \mathbf{6.0\text{ hours}}$$.

#### M3-Q02: (C)
* Let catalogue price $= 100$.
* Contractor's purchase cost $= 100 \times 0.75 = 75$.
* Contractor's selling price $= 100 \times 0.90 = 90$.
* Profit margin based on cost:
  $$\frac{90 - 75}{75} \times 100\% = \frac{15}{75} \times 100\% = \mathbf{20.0\%}$$.

#### M3-Q03: (C)
* Sum of all 5 values $= 5 \times 160 = 800$.
* Sum of 4 known values $= 120 + 145 + 160 + 185 = 610$.
* Value of $X = 800 - 610 = \mathbf{190}$.
