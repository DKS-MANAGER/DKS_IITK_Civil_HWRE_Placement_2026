## Section 1.1: Fluid Properties, Units & Continuum Mechanics

### 1. Physical Intuition
A fluid is a substance that deforms continuously under the application of a shear (tangential) stress, no matter how small that shear stress may be. Under the **continuum hypothesis**, macroscopic fluid properties (density, velocity, pressure) are treated as continuously distributed fields, valid whenever the characteristic flow dimension $L$ is vastly larger than the molecular mean free path $\lambda$ (Knudsen number $\mathrm{Kn} = \lambda / L < 0.001$).

### 2. Governing Definitions & Mathematical Formulation
* **Mass Density ($\rho$):** Mass per unit volume $[kg/m^3]$.
  $$\rho = \lim_{\delta \mathcal{V} \to \delta \mathcal{V}^*} \frac{\delta m}{\delta \mathcal{V}}$$
* **Specific Weight ($\gamma$):** Gravitational force exerted per unit volume $[N/m^3]$.
  $$\gamma = \rho g$$
* **Dynamic Viscosity ($\mu$):** Constant of proportionality relating shear stress to velocity gradient $[Pa \cdot s = N \cdot s/m^2 = kg/(m \cdot s)]$.
  $$\tau = \mu \frac{du}{dy}$$
* **Kinematic Viscosity ($\nu$):** Ratio of dynamic viscosity to density, representing the molecular diffusivity of momentum $[m^2/s]$.
  $$\nu = \frac{\mu}{\rho}$$
* **Bulk Modulus of Elasticity ($K$):** Resistance of fluid to isotropic volumetric compression $[Pa]$:
  $$K = - \mathcal{V} \frac{dP}{d\mathcal{V}} = \rho \frac{dP}{d\rho}$$
* **Vapor Pressure ($P_v$):** The equilibrium pressure exerted by fluid vapor molecules upon the liquid surface at a given temperature. If local static pressure drops to $P \le P_v$, boiling and cavitation bubbles nucleate immediately.

### 3. Worked Numerical Example: Viscous Shear Resistance
**Problem Statement:** A flat rectangular plate of dimensions $0.50\text{ m} \times 0.80\text{ m}$ slides horizontally at a steady velocity $U = 1.20\text{ m/s}$ over a stationary flat surface separated by an oil film of thickness $h = 1.50\text{ mm}$ ($0.0015\text{ m}$). The oil has specific gravity $\mathrm{SG} = 0.88$ and kinematic viscosity $\nu = 2.50 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2/\text{s}$. Compute:
1. Dynamic viscosity $\mu$ of the lubricating oil.
2. The shear stress $\tau$ developed on the plate surface.
3. The horizontal pulling force $F$ and mechanical power $\mathcal{P}$ dissipated.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
* *Step 1: Compute density and dynamic viscosity.*
  $$\rho = \mathrm{SG} \times \rho_{\text{water}} = 0.88 \times 1000\text{ kg/m}^3 = 880\text{ kg/m}^3$$
  $$\mu = \rho \cdot \nu = (880\text{ kg/m}^3) \times (2.50 \times 10^{-4}\text{ m}^2/\text{s}) = 0.220\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s}$$
* *Step 2: Linear velocity profile assumption.*
  Because the gap $h = 1.5\text{ mm}$ is extremely narrow, the velocity gradient is linear:
  $$\frac{du}{dy} = \frac{U}{h} = \frac{1.20\text{ m/s}}{0.0015\text{ m}} = 800.0\text{ s}^{-1}$$
  $$\tau = \mu \frac{du}{dy} = (0.220\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s})(800.0\text{ s}^{-1}) = 176.0\text{ N/m}^2\text{ (Pa)}$$
* *Step 3: Compute pulling force and power.*
  $$A = 0.50\text{ m} \times 0.80\text{ m} = 0.40\text{ m}^2$$
  $$F = \tau \times A = (176.0\text{ N/m}^2)(0.40\text{ m}^2) = 70.40\text{ N}$$
  $$\mathcal{P} = F \times U = (70.40\text{ N})(1.20\text{ m/s}) = 84.48\text{ Watts}$$

## Section 1.2: Fluid Statics & Hydrostatic Thrust on Gates

### 1. Governing Law & Pressure Distribution
In a static fluid at rest ($\nabla \mathbf{V} = 0$), shear stresses are identically zero. The Navier-Stokes equation reduces to the **hydrostatic differential equation**:
$$\nabla P = \rho \mathbf{g} \implies \frac{dP}{dz} = -\rho g = -\gamma$$
Integrating for an incompressible fluid with free surface at $z = 0$ ($P = P_{\text{atm}}$):
$$P(h) = P_{\text{atm}} + \rho g h \implies P_{\text{gauge}} = \rho g h$$

### 2. Hydrostatic Forces on Submerged Surfaces
* **Plane Surface:** Total resultant thrust equals the pressure at the area centroid multiplied by total submerged area:
  $$F_R = \bar{P} A = (\rho g \bar{h}) A$$
  The point of application—the **center of pressure ($h_{cp}$)**—always lies strictly below the centroid due to the trapezoidal pressure profile:
  $$h_{cp} = \bar{h} + \frac{I_{xx,c}}{\bar{h} A}$$
  where $I_{xx,c}$ is the second moment of area about the horizontal centroidal axis.
* **Curved Surface:**
  - Horizontal component $F_H$: Equal to the hydrostatic thrust on the vertical projection of the curved surface: $F_H = \rho g \bar{h}_v A_v$.
  - Vertical component $F_V$: Equal to the weight of the fluid volume directly above the curved surface extending vertically up to the free surface: $F_V = \rho g \cdot \mathcal{V}_{\text{fluid}}$.

## Section 1.3: Fluid Kinematics & Conservation of Mass

### 1. Velocity Field & Material Derivative
Fluid motion is described via Eulerian velocity field $\mathbf{V}(x, y, z, t) = u\hat{i} + v\hat{j} + w\hat{k}$. The total acceleration of a fluid parcel following the flow is the **material (substantial) derivative**:
$$\frac{D\mathbf{V}}{Dt} = \frac{\partial \mathbf{V}}{\partial t} + (\mathbf{V} \cdot \nabla)\mathbf{V} = \underbrace{\frac{\partial \mathbf{V}}{\partial t}}_{\text{Local Unsteady Accel.}} + \underbrace{\left(u\frac{\partial \mathbf{V}}{\partial x} + v\frac{\partial \mathbf{V}}{\partial y} + w\frac{\partial \mathbf{V}}{\partial z}\right)}_{\text{Convective Advective Accel.}}$$

### 2. Derivation of the Continuity Equation
Applying conservation of mass to an infinitesimal control volume $\delta x \delta y \delta z$:
$$\text{Rate of mass accumulation inside CV} = \text{Net mass inflow rate across faces}$$
$$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} \delta x \delta y \delta z = - \left[\frac{\partial (\rho u)}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial (\rho v)}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial (\rho w)}{\partial z}\right] \delta x \delta y \delta z$$
Dividing by the volume:
$$\frac{\partial \rho}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (\rho \mathbf{V}) = 0$$
For **steady, incompressible flow** ($\rho = \text{constant}$):
$$\nabla \cdot \mathbf{V} = \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial v}{\partial y} + \frac{\partial w}{\partial z} = 0$$
For 1D pipe flow: $Q = A_1 V_1 = A_2 V_2 = \text{constant}$.

## Section 1.4: Control-Volume Linear Momentum & Forces

Applying Newton's second law to a macroscopic control volume yields the **Reynolds Transport Theorem for Linear Momentum**:
$$\sum \mathbf{F}_{\text{ext}} = \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\iiint_{\mathrm{CV}} \rho \mathbf{V} \, d\mathcal{V} + \iint_{\mathrm{CS}} \mathbf{V} (\rho \mathbf{V} \cdot d\mathbf{A})$$
For steady flow with uniform properties across inlets and outlets:
$$\sum \mathbf{F}_{\text{ext}} = \sum_{\text{out}} \dot{m}_{\text{out}} \mathbf{V}_{\text{out}} - \sum_{\text{in}} \dot{m}_{\text{in}} \mathbf{V}_{\text{in}}$$
where $\sum \mathbf{F}_{\text{ext}} = \mathbf{F}_{\text{pressure}} + \mathbf{F}_{\text{gravity}} + \mathbf{R}_{\text{structural reaction}}$.

### Worked Numerical Example: Force on a 90° Pipe Bend
**Problem Statement:** A horizontal $90^\circ$ reducing pipe bend deflects water flow ($\rho = 1000\text{ kg/m}^3$) in the horizontal plane from the $+x$ to $+y$ direction.
* Inlet diameter $D_1 = 300\text{ mm}$ ($A_1 = 0.07069\text{ m}^2$), inlet pressure $P_1 = 250\text{ kPa gauge}$.
* Outlet diameter $D_2 = 150\text{ mm}$ ($A_2 = 0.01767\text{ m}^2$), outlet pressure $P_2 = 210\text{ kPa gauge}$.
* Volumetric discharge $Q = 0.25\text{ m}^3/\text{s}$.
Compute the external anchoring reaction forces $R_x$ and $R_y$ required to hold the bend rigidly in place.

**Step-by-Step Solution:**
* *Step 1: Compute velocities.*
  $$V_1 = \frac{Q}{A_1} = \frac{0.25}{0.07069} = 3.537\text{ m/s} \quad (\text{along } +x)$$
  $$V_2 = \frac{Q}{A_2} = \frac{0.25}{0.01767} = 14.148\text{ m/s} \quad (\text{along } +y)$$
  $$\dot{m} = \rho Q = 1000 \times 0.25 = 250.0\text{ kg/s}$$
* *Step 2: Pressure forces acting ON the fluid CV.*
  $$F_{p1, x} = + P_1 A_1 = (250,000\text{ Pa})(0.07069\text{ m}^2) = +17,672.5\text{ N}$$
  $$F_{p2, y} = - P_2 A_2 = -(210,000\text{ Pa})(0.01767\text{ m}^2) = -3,710.7\text{ N}$$
* *Step 3: Momentum balance in $x$-direction.*
  $$F_{p1, x} + R_x = \dot{m}(V_{2, x} - V_{1, x})$$
  $$17,672.5 + R_x = 250.0(0 - 3.537) = -884.25\text{ N}$$
  $$R_x = -884.25 - 17,672.5 = \mathbf{-18,556.75\text{ N} = -18.56\text{ kN} \quad (\text{directed in } -x)}$$
* *Step 4: Momentum balance in $y$-direction.*
  $$F_{p2, y} + R_y = \dot{m}(V_{2, y} - V_{1, y})$$
  $$-3,710.7 + R_y = 250.0(14.148 - 0) = +3,537.0\text{ N}$$
  $$R_y = +3,537.0 + 3,710.7 = \mathbf{+7,247.7\text{ N} = +7.25\text{ kN} \quad (\text{directed in } +y)}$$
* *Total resultant thrust on anchor block:*
  $$R = \sqrt{(-18.56)^2 + (7.25)^2} = \sqrt{344.47 + 52.56} = \sqrt{397.03} = \mathbf{19.93\text{ kN}}$$

## Section 1.5: Bernoulli Equation & Mechanical Energy Line

Integrating Euler’s equation along a streamline for steady, incompressible, frictionless (inviscid) flow gives:
$$\frac{p_1}{\rho g} + \frac{V_1^2}{2g} + z_1 = \frac{p_2}{\rho g} + \frac{V_2^2}{2g} + z_2 = H = \text{constant}$$

### Extended Mechanical Energy Equation (With Losses and Work):
In practical industrial piping, shaft work is added by pumps ($h_p$), extracted by turbines ($h_t$), and dissipated by viscous friction ($h_L = h_f + h_m$):
$$\frac{p_1}{\rho g} + \alpha_1 \frac{V_1^2}{2g} + z_1 + h_p = \frac{p_2}{\rho g} + \alpha_2 \frac{V_2^2}{2g} + z_2 + h_t + h_L$$
* **Energy Grade Line (EGL):** Total mechanical head available: $\mathrm{EGL} = z + \frac{p}{\rho g} + \alpha \frac{V^2}{2g}$.
* **Hydraulic Grade Line (HGL):** Piezometric head level: $\mathrm{HGL} = z + \frac{p}{\rho g}$.
* $\mathrm{EGL} - \mathrm{HGL} = \frac{V^2}{2g}$ (Velocity head).
* *Sub-Atmospheric Warning:* If the HGL drops below the physical pipe centerline elevation ($z_{\text{pipe}} > \mathrm{HGL}$), negative gauge pressure (vacuum) develops, creating a severe siphon cavitation risk.

## Section 1.6: Viscous Pipe Flow, Friction Factors & Moody Regimes

### 1. Darcy-Weisbach Equation
Frictional head loss in a circular conduit of diameter $D$ and length $L$:
$$h_f = f \frac{L}{D} \frac{V^2}{2g}$$

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                            CRITICAL CONVENTION: DARCY VS FANNING                              |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| • Darcy friction factor f: hf = f * (L/D) * (V^2 / 2g). Laminar: f = 64 / Re.                 |
| • Fanning friction factor f_F: hf = 4 * f_F * (L/D) * (V^2 / 2g). Laminar: f_F = 16 / Re.     |
| • Conversion Rule: f_Darcy = 4 * f_Fanning                                                    |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 2. Flow Regimes in Pipes
* **Laminar ($\mathrm{Re} \le 2000$):** Frictional resistance is purely viscous. Profile is parabolic ($u_{\max} = 2V$).
  $$f = \frac{64}{\mathrm{Re}} \implies \Delta P_f = \frac{128 \mu L Q}{\pi D^4} \quad (\text{Hagen-Poiseuille Law})$$
* **Turbulent ($\mathrm{Re} \ge 4000$):** Governed by Colebrook-White equation:
  $$\frac{1}{\sqrt{f}} = -2.0 \log_{10}\left(\frac{\epsilon/D}{3.7} + \frac{2.51}{\mathrm{Re}\sqrt{f}}\right)$$
* **Fully Rough Turbulent ($\mathrm{Re} \to \infty$):** Viscous sublayer destroyed by wall roughness; $f$ is independent of $\mathrm{Re}$:
  $$\frac{1}{\sqrt{f}} = 1.14 - 2.0 \log_{10}\left(\frac{\epsilon}{D}\right)$$

## Section 1.7: Pipe Networks, Minor Losses & Hydraulic Transients

### 1. Minor Energy Losses
Disruptions to the streamline pattern generate eddy turbulence:
$$h_m = K_L \frac{V^2}{2g}$$
* Sudden Expansion (Borda-Carnot): $h_L = \frac{(V_1 - V_2)^2}{2g} = \left(1 - \frac{A_1}{A_2}\right)^2 \frac{V_1^2}{2g}$.
* Sudden Contraction: $h_L = \left(\frac{1}{C_c} - 1\right)^2 \frac{V_2^2}{2g} \approx 0.5 \frac{V_2^2}{2g}$.

### 2. Acoustic Water Hammer Surge (Joukowsky Equation)
Rapid valve closure creates an acoustic shock wave propagating at celerity $c = \sqrt{\frac{K/\rho}{1 + (K/E)(D/t)}}$.
* Critical round-trip time: $T_c = \frac{2 L}{c}$.
* **Rapid Closure ($T_{\text{valve}} \le T_c$):**
  $$\Delta P_{\max} = \rho c \Delta V \implies \Delta H_{\max} = \frac{c \Delta V}{g}$$
* **Slow Closure ($T_{\text{valve}} > T_c$):** Reflected relief waves mitigate peak surge:
  $$\Delta H \approx \frac{2 L \Delta V}{g T_{\text{valve}}}$$

## Section 1.8: Pumps, System Head Curves & Cavitation NPSH

* **System Head Curve:**
  $$H_{\text{sys}}(Q) = H_{\text{static}} + K_{\text{sys}} Q^2 = (z_{\text{discharge}} - z_{\text{suction}}) + \frac{\Delta P}{\rho g} + \sum \left(f\frac{L}{D} + K_L\right)\frac{8 Q^2}{\pi^2 g D^4}$$
* **Operating Point:** Intersection $H_{\text{pump}}(Q) = H_{\text{sys}}(Q)$.
* **Cavitation & Net Positive Suction Head Available ($\mathrm{NPSH}_A$):**
  $$\mathrm{NPSH}_A = \frac{P_{\text{suction, abs}}}{\rho g} + \frac{V_s^2}{2g} - \frac{P_v}{\rho g} = \frac{P_{\text{atm}}}{\rho g} + z_{\text{source}} - z_{\text{pump}} - h_{L,\text{suction}} - \frac{P_v}{\rho g}$$
  - *Design Criterion:* $\mathrm{NPSH}_A \ge \mathrm{NPSH}_R + 1.0\text{ m}$ to prevent catastrophic impeller pitting.

---

## Section 1.9: Boundary Layers, Wall Turbulence & Law of the Wall

* **Friction Velocity:** $u_* = \sqrt{\frac{\tau_w}{\rho}}$.
* **Dimensionless Wall Distance:** $y^+ = \frac{y u_*}{\nu}$.
* **Sublayer Structure:**
  1. *Viscous Sublayer ($y^+ < 5$):* Molecular viscosity governs; $u^+ = y^+$.
  2. *Buffer Layer ($5 < y^+ < 30$):* Transition layer.
  3. *Log-Law Layer ($30 < y^+ < 300$):* Turbulent eddy mixing governs:
     $$u^+ = \frac{1}{\kappa}\ln(y^+) + B \quad (\kappa \approx 0.41, B \approx 5.2)$$

---

## Section 1.10: Open-Channel Hydraulics, Critical Flow & Choking

* **Froude Number:** $\mathrm{Fr} = \frac{V}{\sqrt{g D_h}} = \frac{V}{\sqrt{g (A/T)}}$.
* **Specific Energy:** $E = y + \frac{Q^2}{2g A^2}$.
* **Critical Depth in Rectangular Channel ($q = Q/b$):**
  $$\frac{dE}{dy} = 1 - \mathrm{Fr}^2 = 0 \implies y_c = \sqrt[3]{\frac{q^2}{g}}, \quad E_{\min} = 1.5 y_c, \quad V_c = \sqrt{g y_c}$$
* **Channel Choking:** If channel width is contracted below $b_{\min} = \frac{Q}{\sqrt{g (2/3 E_1)^3}}$ or a bed hump $\Delta z > E_1 - E_c$ is introduced, flow chokes, forcing upstream depth to rise ($y_1' > y_1$).

---

## Section 1.11: Hydraulic Jump Dynamics & Energy Dissipation

Transition from supercritical ($\mathrm{Fr}_1 > 1$) to subcritical ($\mathrm{Fr}_2 < 1$) flow forms a standing hydraulic jump.
* **Bélanger Equation (Horizontal Rectangular Channel):**
  $$\frac{y_2}{y_1} = \frac{1}{2}\left(\sqrt{1 + 8 \mathrm{Fr}_1^2} - 1\right)$$
* **Energy Head Dissipation:**
  $$\Delta E_L = E_1 - E_2 = \frac{(y_2 - y_1)^3}{4 y_1 y_2}$$
  Mechanical power destroyed in the turbulent roller: $\mathcal{P} = \rho g Q \Delta E_L$.

---

## Section 1.12: Gradually Varied Flow (GVF) & Profile Computation

The differential equation of gradually varied steady flow:
$$\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{S_0 - S_f}{1 - \mathrm{Fr}^2}$$
* $S_0$ = bed slope; $S_f = \frac{n^2 V^2}{R^{4/3}}$ = friction slope.
* **Direct Step Method Integration:**
  $$\Delta x = \frac{E_2 - E_1}{S_0 - \bar{S}_f}$$
* **Profile Types:**
  - $M_1$ profile ($y > y_n > y_c$): Subcritical backwater curve behind dams/weirs ($\frac{dy}{dx} > 0$).
  - $M_2$ profile ($y_n > y > y_c$): Drawdown curve approaching free outfall ($\frac{dy}{dx} < 0$).

---

## Section 1.13: Hydrology, Hydrographs & Flood Risk Modeling

* **Rational Method (Industrial Catchments $A < 50\text{ ha}$):**
  $$Q_p = \frac{C \cdot I \cdot A}{360} \quad (\text{with } Q_p\text{ in m}^3/\text{s}, I\text{ in mm/hr}, A\text{ in hectares})$$
* **Unit Hydrograph Convolution:**
  $$Q_n = \sum_{m=1}^n P_m U_{n - m + 1}$$
* **Hydrologic Encounter Risk ($R_e$):** Probability that a design flood of return period $T$ will be exceeded at least once during facility operational life $N$:
  $$R_e = 1 - \left(1 - \frac{1}{T}\right)^N$$

## Section 1.14: Sediment Transport, Shields Criterion & Riverbed Scour

* **Bed Shear Stress:** $\tau_b = \rho g R S_0$.
* **Dimensionless Shields Parameter ($\tau_*$):**
  $$\tau_* = \frac{\tau_b}{(\rho_s - \rho) g d_{50}}$$
  Incipient motion initiates when $\tau_* > \tau_{*c} \approx 0.045$.
* **Exner Equation (Morphodynamic Conservation of Sediment):**
  $$(1 - \lambda_p) \frac{\partial z_b}{\partial t} + \frac{\partial q_s}{\partial x} = 0$$
* **Bridge Pier Scour (FHWA HEC-18):**
  $$\frac{y_s}{y_1} = 2.0 \, K_1 K_2 K_3 K_4 \left(\frac{a}{y_1}\right)^{0.65} \mathrm{Fr}_1^{0.43}$$

## Section 1.15: Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD), FVM & Numerical V&V

* **Finite Volume Method (FVM):** Solves conservation laws over polyhedral control volumes using Gauss' Divergence Theorem:
  $$\int_V \frac{\partial \mathbf{U}}{\partial t} dV + \oint_A \mathbf{F} \cdot d\mathbf{A} = \int_V \mathbf{S} dV$$
  Enforces exact cell-by-cell mass and momentum conservation.
* **Courant-Friedrichs-Lewy (CFL) Condition:**
  $$C = \frac{u \Delta t}{\Delta x} \le 1.0$$
* **ASME V&V 20 Grid Convergence Index (GCI):**
  $$\mathrm{GCI}_{\text{fine}} = \frac{1.25 \, e_a^{21}}{r_{21}^p - 1}$$
  Confirms spatial grid independence when $\mathrm{GCI} < 3-5\%$.
